ThePerfectHacker
By the theorem of chord in a circle we have that,
$n^2=r(2r-h)$
But, $n=r\sin(s/2r)$
Thus, we have,
$r^2\sin(s/2r)=r(2r-h)$[/tex]
Thus,
$r\sin(s/2r)=2r-h$
----------------------
If you know calculus
Thus, you need to find the zero's of the function,
$f(x)=x\sin(s/2x)-2x+h$
Use Newton's method
