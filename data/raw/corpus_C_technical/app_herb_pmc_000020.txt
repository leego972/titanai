Antioxidant Activities</title><p>Nkobole et al. [<xref rid="B72-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>] evaluated antioxidant activities of acetone root extracts of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> using 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl radical (DPPH) free radical assay. The DPPH scavenging activity of the plant extract was 94.4 ± 0.01% which was comparable to 95.8 ± 0.01% demonstrated by the control, Vitamin C. Nkobole et al. [<xref rid="B72-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>] also evaluated antioxidant activities of the compounds lupeol <bold>2</bold> and β-sitosterol <bold>16</bold> isolated from the stem bark of <italic toggle="yes">Terminalia sericea</italic> using DPPH free radical assay. The compound lupeol <bold>2</bold> demonstrated high radical activity, exhibiting half maximal effective concentration (EC<sub>50</sub>) values of 3.66 µM, which was comparable to the EC<sub>50</sub> values of 2.52 µM demonstrated by the control, Vitamin C [<xref rid="B72-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>]. Lall et al. [<xref rid="B67-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>] evaluated antioxidant activities of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> ethanolic shoot extracts using theDPPH free radical assay. The IC<sub>50</sub> value of the extracts against DPPH free radical was found to be 22.55 ± 2.93 µg/mL against 4.34 ± 0.48 µg/mL exhibited by the control, ascorbic acid [<xref rid="B67-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>]. These results obtained by Lall et al. [<xref rid="B67-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>] and Nkobole et al. [<xref rid="B72-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>] are important as intake of antioxidant rich herbal medicines scavenge free radicals and modulate oxidative stress-related degenerative effects.</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot4-molecules-22-02128"><title>5.4. Antiplasmodial and Larvicidal Activities</title><p>Clarkson et al. [<xref rid="B73-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>] evaluated antiplasmodial activities of aqueous, dichloromethane, dichloromethane and methanol (1:1) root and stem extracts of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> against <italic toggle="yes">Plasmodium falciparum</italic> using the parasite lactate dehydrogenase assay. <italic toggle="yes">Euclea natalensis</italic> dichloromethane and methanol (1:1) root and leaf extracts showed promising activities with IC<sub>50</sub> values of 5.1 µg/mL and 5.3 µg/mL, respectively [<xref rid="B73-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>]. The antiplasmodial properties demonstrated by <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> imply that the species could be a promising candidate for further investigation as plant-based antimalarial agent. Historically, some of the antimalarial drugs have been derived from herbal medicines or from structures modelled on medicinal plant lead compounds and these include the quinoline-based antimalarials as well as artemisinin and its derivatives. Maharaj et al. [<xref rid="B74-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>] evaluated larvicidal activities of roots and stem dichloromethane extracts of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> by exposing the third instar <italic toggle="yes">Anopheles arabiensis</italic> larvae to the extracts with acetone and distilled water as controls. The root and stem extracts exhibited 100% mortality after 48 and 96 h of exposure, respectively [<xref rid="B74-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>]. These results provide a scientific basis to the traditional uses of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> as herbal medicine for malaria in East Africa [<xref rid="B16-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>], Mozambique [<xref rid="B15-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>], Tanzania [<xref rid="B7-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>,<xref rid="B19-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>] and Zimbabwe [<xref rid="B20-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>].
