Far-ultraviolet laser ablation of atherosclerotic lesions.

Linsker R, Srinivasan R, Wynne JJ, Alonso DR.

Far-ultraviolet (far-UV) (193 nm) laser radiation ablates arterial wall tissue, 
including noncalcified atherosclerotic lesions, with no apparent thermal damage 
to remaining tissue. This effect contrasts sharply with the thermal damage 
produced by visible-wavelength laser irradiation. The mechanism by which far-UV 
radiation interacts with tissue is predominantly photochemical rather than 
photothermal. Potential clinical applications include those in which 
geometrically precise removal of tissue, without thermal damage to the remaining 
substrate, is desired. Ultraviolet laser catheterization appears practical with 
respect to the availability of fiberoptic materials and high-pulse-rate excimer 
lasers.
