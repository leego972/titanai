The remaining five studies were associated with concerns regarding bias in certain domains.<fig id="Fig5" position="float" orientation="portrait"><label>Fig. 5</label><caption><p>Results of risk bias</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" id="MO5" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="12903_2024_4179_Fig5_HTML.jpg"/></fig></p></sec></sec><sec id="Sec17"><title>Discussion</title><sec id="Sec18"><title>Summary of findings</title><p id="Par47">According to the World Health Organization, OLK is one of the most common precancerous lesions since as early as 1972, being defined as “a morphologically altered tissue that has a greater potential for developing cancer than the corresponding normal-appearing tissue” [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR37">37</xref>]. While surgical excision remains the gold standard for managing OLK, the associated trauma and postoperative pain deter many patients, impacting their comfort and decreasing their willingness to seek treatment. Additionally, the recurrence rate after surgical intervention has been reported inconsistently [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR38">38</xref>]. Therefore, identifying appropriate treatment modalities for OLK and enhancing patient compliance are crucial for effectively preventing and intercepting the development of OSCC [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR39">39</xref>].</p><p id="Par48">The present systematic review and network meta-analysis, encompassing 1138 OLK lesions in 917 patients, explored the similarities and differences in postoperative recurrence rates, intraoperative hemorrhage, and postoperative pain scores across various laser and conventional surgical interventions. The results revealed that Er,Cr:YSGG laser, CO<sub>2</sub> laser combined with PDT, and Er:YAG laser demonstrated superior efficacy compared to surgical excision in preventing OLK recurrence. Additionally, all the laser-based treatments outperformed surgical excision in minimizing intraoperative hemorrhage and postoperative pain scores on the first postoperative day, with Er,Cr:YSGG and Er:YAG lasers demonstrating the highest rankings in these respective categories. Notably, none of the included studies reported severe adverse events with any treatment modality.</p><p id="Par49">Currently, several lasers are employed to manage OLK, including CO<sub>2</sub> lasers, erbium lasers (Er,Cr:YSGG with a wavelength of 2780 nm and Er:YAG with a wavelength of 2940 nm), and semiconductor lasers. CO<sub>2</sub> lasers primarily function through vaporization and heat coagulation, leading to intraoperative pain and carbonized tissue remnants [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR40">40</xref>]. This thermal damage prevents the surgeon from accurately assessing the depth of involvement and compromises the postoperative aesthetic outcome [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR41">41</xref>].
