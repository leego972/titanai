Evidence-Based Toxicology</title><sec id="sec7dot1-molecules-21-00331"><title>7.1. Safety and Toxicity</title><p>Although <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> has been used in traditional medicine for generations in Malaysia, it was only in the late 1990s that researchers started to pay more attention to its safe dosage and toxicity profile. Safety studies carried out thus far showed that Tongkat Ali (<italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic>) concentrations used therapeutically (2.5 µg·mL<sup>−1</sup>) appear not to have any detrimental effects on human spermatozoa <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> [<xref rid="B194-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">194</xref>]. However, at concentrations higher than 100 µg·mL<sup>−1</sup>, cytotoxic effects might occur [<xref rid="B36-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>,<xref rid="B160-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">160</xref>] supporting <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> data by Tambi and Kadir, that the extract is not toxic [<xref rid="B9-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. In animal studies, no negative effect on the offspring could be found, either in terms of malformations or of any effect on body weight or the number of the offspring [<xref rid="B124-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">124</xref>]; yet an acute toxicity study done by Satayavivad <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> has found that the oral Lethal Dose 50 (LD<sub>50</sub>) of the alcoholic extract of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> in mice is between 1500–2000 mg/kg, while the oral LD<sub>50</sub> of the aqueous extract form is more than 3000 mg/kg [<xref rid="B194-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">194</xref>]. These authors further showed that dosages of 200 mg·kg<sup>−1</sup> body weight of the ethanolic extract and 300 mg·kg<sup>−1</sup> of the aqueous extract daily were not toxic. Only at dosages above 1200 mg·kg<sup>−1</sup> body weight, were significant hepatotoxic effects shown in the rat [<xref rid="B195-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">195</xref>]. The acute toxicity studies in mice found that the n-butanol fraction of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> was the most toxic, mainly due to eurycomanone [<xref rid="B191-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">191</xref>].</p><p>It simply means that as the composition of ethanolic, n-butanolic- and aqueous-based fractions of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> differs, therefore, LD<sub>50</sub> as well daily effective doses are also varied among fractions. The water-based fraction of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> is considered the safest among others, as its LD<sub>50</sub> value is comparatively high (&gt;3000 mg/kg) than other fractions, so this needs attention when using different fractions of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> and proper reference of the corresponding range of LD<sub>50</sub>.</p><p>Choudhary <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, investigated the acute, subacute and subchronic toxicity of the standardized aqueous <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> extract (Physta<sup>®</sup>) in a rat model. Male and female Wistar rats were treated for 90 days with <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> concentrations from 250 mg·kg<sup>−1</sup> body weight to 2000 mg·kg<sup>−1</sup> body weight. Results clearly show no significant changes in blood chemistry and haematological parameters. There were also no histopathological changes and even in acute toxicity tests, no changes in mortality or in the behaviour of the animals was seen [<xref rid="B196-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">196</xref>].</p><p>With reference to the prostate, the Endocrine Society recommends that prostate cancer (PCa) has to be regarded as a contraindication for any testosterone treatment [<xref rid="B197-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">197</xref>]. Considering that <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> extract increases the serum testosterone concentrations, there might be a potential risk from its treatment in elderly men, which might cause prostatic problems. On other hands, a randomized double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial by Ismail <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, revealed no difference between the placebo and the verum group for serum prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels [<xref rid="B114-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>].
