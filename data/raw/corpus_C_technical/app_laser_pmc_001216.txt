With phase stable OCT data, functional time-resolved changes from blood cells in the microvasculature of the retina can be calculated to create an <italic toggle="yes">en face</italic> angiogram without the need for any injected contrast or fluorescent dye.<xref rid="r23" ref-type="bibr"><sup>23</sup></xref> This is technically difficult to achieve in a handheld, since these techniques are typically limited by the phase stability of the optical system and the optical fiber connected to the handheld probe, as well as the inherent added motion of the user holding the probe and the subject being imaged. One handheld probe has been able to overcome these challenges, notably without the use of mydriatic (dilation) drops, to produce volumetric angiograms of subjects (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="f15">Fig. 15</xref>).<xref rid="r114" ref-type="bibr"><sup>114</sup></xref> This system utilized a fast-refocusing tunable lens to quickly lock onto the retina and postacquisition B-scan alignment to correct for motion artifacts from the operator and subject. The sensitivity of the system for imaging capillaries in the retina approaches that of mounted commercial systems and can provide a vessel density map of the retina without the use of any injected contrast agents. This information is useful for clinicians to discern pathological vessel growth in subjects with DBR or other diseases.</p><fig id="f15" orientation="portrait" position="float"><label>Fig. 15</label><caption><p>Handheld OCT angiography probe with automated tunable lens focus adjustment. Very often, it is more practical and sometimes necessary to bring the imaging system to the subject, rather than having the subject approach the benchtop imaging system. In one unique case, an elephant was nearly blind and required cataract removal surgery to improve its quality of life. With assistance from a portable OCT system and handheld probe, surgeons were able to remove the cataracts and restore partial vision to this animal,<xref rid="r115" ref-type="bibr"><sup>115</sup></xref> although a synthetic lens implantation was not possible given existing damage to ocular tissue.
