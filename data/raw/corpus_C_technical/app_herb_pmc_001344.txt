Additionally, <italic toggle="yes">L. angustifolius</italic> seeds exhibited the highest total polyphenol content (696.21 mg GAE/100 g) among four <italic toggle="yes">Lupinus</italic> spp., followed by <italic toggle="yes">L. albus</italic> (614.13 mg GAE/100 g), <italic toggle="yes">L. luteus</italic> (467.78 mg GAE/100 g), and <italic toggle="yes">L. mutabilis</italic> (367.36 mg GAE/100 g) [<xref rid="B155-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">155</xref>].</p><p>In <italic toggle="yes">Lupinus luteus</italic> germinated seeds produced by Plantico (Waganiec, Poland), apigenin and fisetin were identified as the dominant flavonoids. The study also identified 20 different phenolic compounds, including salicylic, ferulic, and sinapinic acids, and highlighted the presence of glucosides, which may influence bioactivity. Polyphenol content increased significantly during germination, with peak concentrations observed after six days, suggesting that germination enhances the bioactive potential of <italic toggle="yes">L. luteus</italic> [<xref rid="B156-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">156</xref>].</p><p>Polyphenolic profiling of <italic toggle="yes">L. mutabilis</italic> seeds provided by the Leguminous Program of the Universidad Nacional Agraria La Molina, Lima, Perú, indicated that flavonoids account for 85.5–99.6% of free phenolic compounds, with key molecules including genistein and its derivatives, apigenin, catechin, and naringenin. Smaller quantities of phenylethanoids (e.g., tyrosol derivatives) and cinnamic acid derivatives were also identified. Among different ecotypes, the H6 INIA BP variety exhibited the highest total free phenolic concentration (1393.32 mg/kg DM) [<xref rid="B153-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">153</xref>].</p><p>The roots of <italic toggle="yes">Lupinus polyphyllus</italic> were found to contain a diverse range of phenolic compounds, including (−)-epicatechin (213.05 µg/mL), (+)-catechin (208.06 µg/mL), and (−)-epigallocatechin (154.95 µg/mL). The HPLC-based quantification confirmed that 1.972% of root biomass consists of phenolic compounds, indicating the presence of condensed tannins, which contribute to antioxidant activity [<xref rid="B157-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">157</xref>].</p><p>Beyond profiling, several studies examined structural stability and bioactivity modulation of <italic toggle="yes">Lupinus</italic> polyphenols. Research on heat-treated <italic toggle="yes">L. albus</italic> seeds showed that thermal processing slightly increased total polyphenol content (from 6142.50 mg GAE/100 g to 6418.44 mg GAE/100 g), demonstrating that heat exposure does not significantly degrade polyphenols. Furthermore, the study highlights the presence of macromolecular polyphenols, which contribute to greater antioxidant capacity [<xref rid="B158-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">158</xref>]. Key phenolic acids distributed across <italic toggle="yes">Lupinus</italic> spp. include p-hydroxybenzoic acid, protocatechuic acid, vanillic acid, syringic acid, and hydroquinone, alongside simpler phenols like catechol, orcinol, phloroglucinol, and pyrogallol. These compounds are essential in understanding flavonoid biosynthesis in <italic toggle="yes">Lupinus</italic> [<xref rid="B159-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">159</xref>].</p><p>The polyphenol composition of <italic toggle="yes">Lupinus</italic> spp. varies significantly across species, plant parts, and environmental conditions. The identification and quantification of polyphenols using advanced analytical methods have provided valuable insights into their structural diversity, antioxidant potential, and functional properties. Notably, flavonoids such as apigenin, fisetin, genistein, catechin, and naringenin play a dominant role in bioactivity, while thermal processing and germination influence polyphenol stability and concentration. These findings establish <italic toggle="yes">Lupinus</italic> as a promising source of dietary polyphenols, with applications in functional foods, nutraceuticals, and health-related formulations.</p></sec><sec id="sec8dot2-nutrients-17-01749"><title>8.2. Peptides</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Lupinus</italic>-derived peptides have been extensively studied for their structural properties, isolation techniques, and biochemical functions, revealing their potential in diverse biological applications. The enzymatic hydrolysis of <italic toggle="yes">L. mutabilis</italic> proteins, performed using pancreatin, papain, and endopeptidase, has allowed for the extraction of peptides with distinct molecular weights and bioactivities. These peptides were fractionated into Lupin Protein Hydrolysate (LPH) 1 (&gt;10 kDa), LPH2 (3-10 kDa), and LPH3 (&lt;3 kDa), with LPH3 demonstrating the highest functional activity [<xref rid="B160-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">160</xref>]. The identification and structural characterization of these peptides have been achieved using mass spectrometry (MS) and high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), providing valuable insights into their sequence composition and biochemical behavior.</p><p>Among the many lupin seed-derived bioactive peptides identified, AVPFWM, YSGWLGL, AHAGFGMLY, and FFSMKVM emerged as the most bioactive [<xref rid="B161-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">161</xref>].
