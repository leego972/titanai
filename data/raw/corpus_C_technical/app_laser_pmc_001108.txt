Abbreviations: as with Figures <xref rid="cxo12847-fig-0001" ref-type="fig">1</xref>, <xref rid="cxo12847-fig-0002" ref-type="fig">2</xref>, <xref rid="cxo12847-fig-0003" ref-type="fig">3</xref>, <xref rid="cxo12847-fig-0004" ref-type="fig">4</xref>.</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" id="nlm-graphic-9" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="CXO-102-242-g005.jpg"/></fig><p>Early detection is vital to the prevention of vision loss and due to its high sensitivity OCT, as an adjunct to fundus examination for DR, is now widely regarded as indispensable for the detection of DMO and monitoring response to treatment. The International Council of Ophthalmology Guidelines for Diabetic Eye Care, updated in 2017, emphasise that the definition and classification of DMO should take into account OCT findings, if available.<xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0066" ref-type="ref">66</xref> However, the high costs and training required preclude its use outside of high‐resource settings. There are also a number of proposed OCT‐based classification systems for DMO, not yet widely adopted, which in turn evaluate various parameters including retinal thickness and/or volume, presence/absence of hard exudates, location, morphology, vitreomacular interface changes and microstructural alterations.<xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0063" ref-type="ref">63</xref>, <xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0064" ref-type="ref">64</xref>, <xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0067" ref-type="ref">67</xref>, <xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0068" ref-type="ref">68</xref>, <xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0069" ref-type="ref">69</xref>
</p><p>The term subclinical DMO was first described using time domain OCT (Stratus, Zeiss) by the Diabetic Retinopathy Clinical Research Network (<ext-link xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" ext-link-type="uri" xlink:href="http://drcr.net">DRCR.net</ext-link>) as central retinal thickness beyond 225 μm (not greater than 299 μm) in the absence of centre‐involving macular oedema detectable using slitlamp fundus examination.<xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0070" ref-type="ref">70</xref> Of importance, 38 per cent of patients with these subclinical findings progressed to DMO or required treatment by two years. Subclinical DMO was later redefined by Diabetic Retinopathy Clinical Research Network using spectral domain OCT (Cirrus, Zeiss) as retinal thickness &gt; 260 μm and &lt; 290 μm in women and &gt; 275 μm and &lt; 305 μm in men.<xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0071" ref-type="ref">71</xref> Directly comparing measurements from different OCT imaging technologies is problematic, due to differences in scan resolution, speed of acquisition and algorithms for locating the boundary of the outer retina, bringing into question the clinical usefulness of a numerical definition of DMO or subclinical macular oedema for eye care professionals in clinical practice.<xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0072" ref-type="ref">72</xref> Nevertheless, increased central retinal thickness in the absence of other structural abnormalities may provide a biomarker for future progression to DMO.</p><p>Structural OCT has limited value in evaluating signs of severe non‐proliferative DR such as venous calibre abnormalities and intra‐retinal microvascular abnormalities; however, OCT may assist in the differential diagnosis of cotton wool spots, seen as a hyper‐reflective thickening of the retinal nerve fibre layer, as well as in confirming the presence of neovascularisation and determining the location of vitreous and pre‐retinal haemorrhage.<xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0073" ref-type="ref">73</xref>, <xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0074" ref-type="ref">74</xref> Using OCT, neovascularisation may appear as pre‐retinal hyper‐reflective material on the retinal surface or proliferating along the posterior vitreous interface. Vitreous haemorrhage can be seen as hyper‐reflective dots within the vitreous cavity and pre‐retinal (or sub‐hyaloid) haemorrhage may present as an area of hyper‐reflectivity trapped between the internal limiting membrane of the retina and the posterior vitreous interface.<xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0074" ref-type="ref">74</xref>
</p></sec><sec id="cxo12847-sec-0013"><title>The emerging role and limitations of OCT in DR</title><p>OCT‐A has shown promise as a non‐invasive method of ascertaining the morphology of intraretinal microvascular abnormalities and neovascularisation,<xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0061" ref-type="ref">61</xref>, <xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0075" ref-type="ref">75</xref> but outside the posterior pole the technique is limited by field of view and difficulty in off‐axis image acquisition.
