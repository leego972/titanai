Answer: <p>If you're specifically after parsing links out of web-pages, then Perl's <a href="http://search.cpan.org/perldoc?WWW::Mechanize" rel="nofollow">WWW::Mechanize</a> module will figure things out for you in a very elegant fashion.  Here's a sample program that grabs the first page of Stack Overflow and parses out all the links, printing their text and corresponding URLs:</p>

<pre><code>#!/usr/bin/perl
use strict;
use warnings;
use WWW::Mechanize;

my $mech = WWW::Mechanize-&gt;new;

$mech-&gt;get("http://stackoverflow.com/");

$mech-&gt;success or die "Oh no! Couldn't fetch stackoverflow.com";

foreach my $link ($mech-&gt;links) {
    print "* [",$link-&gt;text, "] points to ", $link-&gt;url, "\n";
}
</code></pre>

<p>In the main loop, each <code>$link</code> is a <a href="http://search.cpan.org/perldoc?WWW::Mechanize::Link" rel="nofollow">WWW::Mechanize::Link</a> object, so you're not just constrained to getting the text and URL.</p>
