With the huge medicinal success of herbs, the development of plant-based drugs will continue.</p><p>The genus <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus</italic> of the Rhamnaceae family contains over 58 accepted species of thorny shrubs and small trees growing mostly in arid and semi-arid regions. Species of this genus are traditionally known for their health benefits, nutritional values, and therapeutic properties, as described by various cultures around the world, specifically in India, Pakistan, China and the Middle East [<xref rid="B4-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B5-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>,<xref rid="B6-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>]. Although research on the health benefits of <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus</italic> is still evolving, its potential uses range from reducing inflammation, improving gastrointestinal health, controlling diabetes, preventing or treating neurological diseases, treating dry skin and sunburns, promoting wound healing, reducing wrinkles, and having an anti-infective and anti-cancer potential. The <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus</italic> species are in fact a rich source of bioactive compounds. Analysis of their phytochemical composition revealed about 431 chemical constituents so far, including, alkaloids (particularly cyclopeptide alkaloids), flavonoids, terpenoids, saponins, and other minor compounds such as cholinergic acids, aromatic or polyaromatic compounds, steroids, cerebrosides and nucleosides [<xref rid="B4-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>]. </p><p><italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus jujuba</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus mauritiana</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus spina-christi</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus lotus</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus xylopyrus</italic> are the most studied species of the genus. The literature describes, more or less extensively, their ethnobotanical, phytochemical and pharmacological attributes. Of particular interest to this review is <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic>, which is a small thorny branched bush native to India, Pakistan, and Iran, growing mainly in arid and dry areas (<xref rid="molecules-27-04240-f001" ref-type="fig">Figure 1</xref>). It is widely used in traditional folk medicine. Its fruit is used as food; it has a cooling effect and removes biliousness [<xref rid="B7-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>], and its ripened fruit powder has been reported to treat constipation [<xref rid="B8-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>]. Its leaves are used in the treatment of scabies and other diseases of the skin [<xref rid="B5-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>], conjunctivitis [<xref rid="B9-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>], diarrhea [<xref rid="B9-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>], gastrointestinal spams [<xref rid="B10-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>], and helminthiasis [<xref rid="B5-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>]. The roots are used for the treatment of dysentery [<xref rid="B11-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>] and the stem bark is used for alleviating joint pain as well as sore throat and bleeding gums [<xref rid="B12-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>,<xref rid="B13-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>]. There have also been reports on its beneficial role in treating insomnia, anxiety and fever, although these have not yet been supported by scientific proof [<xref rid="B14-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>]. </p><p>The therapeutic value of <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> was reported by several other reviews, where various biological properties were reported such as its antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, antiproliferative, hypoglycemic and hypolipidemic activities [<xref rid="B4-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B15-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>,<xref rid="B16-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>,<xref rid="B17-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>]. Moreover, numerous phytochemicals have been isolated from various parts of the plant, including tannins, flavonoids, steroids, glycosides, and alkaloids [<xref rid="B18-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>]. This richness in bioactive molecules justifies its use in the prevention and/or treatment of a wide spectrum of pathological conditions as described above, and calls for further investigation into the impending benefits of <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic>. This review aims to provide a comprehensive overview of the phytochemical properties of <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> and its pharmacological activities based on an up-to-date examination of the literature, shedding light on the cellular and molecular mechanisms involved.
