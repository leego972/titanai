xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" content-type="pdf" xlink:href="file:LSM-58-311.pdf"/><abstract><title>ABSTRACT</title><sec id="lsm70119-sec-0010"><title>Background</title><p>Field cancerization is a major concern in photodamaged skin due to its risk of progression to squamous cell carcinoma. The 1927‐nm thulium laser has emerged as a promising therapy, but its isolated effects on this condition remain underexplored.</p></sec><sec id="lsm70119-sec-0020"><title>Objective</title><p>To evaluate clinical and histopathological changes in field cancerization treated with the 1927‐nm thulium laser.</p></sec><sec id="lsm70119-sec-0030"><title>Methods</title><p>It was a prospective, observational, and interventional clinical study. Twenty‐three patients (age average of 61.3 years), phototype I–III, with facial photodamage and multiple actinic keratosis underwent 4 monthly sessions of thulium laser, followed by a 3‐month evaluation. Outcomes included lesion counts and a photoaging scale at each session, along with comprehensive histological analysis of pre‐ and post‐treatment biopsies from 20 patients.</p></sec><sec id="lsm70119-sec-0040"><title>Results</title><p>There was a significant reduction in AK count (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.001) and photoaging parameters (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.001). Histologically, we observed improvement in vertical extension of keratinocyte atypia (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> = 0.046), increased dermal thickness (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> = 0.012), and fibroplasia (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.001). No severe adverse effects occurred.</p></sec><sec id="lsm70119-sec-0050"><title>Conclusion</title><p>The 1927‐nm thulium laser is a safe, well‐tolerated and has promising results as a field‐directed therapy for actinic keratosis, promoting both clinical and histopathological improvement.</p></sec></abstract><counts><fig-count count="5"/><table-count count="2"/><page-count count="10"/><word-count
