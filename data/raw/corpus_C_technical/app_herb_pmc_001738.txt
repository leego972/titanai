Anticancer</title><p>Elansary et al. [<xref rid="B13-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>] screened their methanolic leaf extracts and identified compounds (<xref rid="molecules-28-04396-t005" ref-type="table">Table 5</xref>) against cancerous cell lines such as colon adenocarcinoma (HT-29), cervical adenocarcinoma (HeLa), breast adenocarcinoma cultures (MCF-7), and human T-cell lymphoblasts (Jurkat) in the 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyl-tetrazolium bromide (MTT) assay. The results indicated that the extract showed strong antiproliferative effects against the tested cell lines with the most potent activity against HeLa (IC<sub>50</sub> = 33 μg/mL) &gt; HT-29 (IC<sub>50</sub> = 36 μg/mL) &gt; MCF-7 (IC<sub>50</sub> = 59 μg/mL) &gt; Jurkat (IC<sub>50</sub> = 62 μg/mL). The identified compounds also exhibited antiproliferative activities against the tested cell lines in the extract. For instance, rutin <bold>6</bold>, hyperoside <bold>9</bold>, quercetin <bold>3</bold>, miquelianin <bold>7</bold>, and <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-coumaric acid <bold>37</bold> showed inhibition of HeLa with IC<sub>50</sub> values 4, 7, 5, 5, and 7 μg/mL, respectively, compared to the positive control vinblastine sulphate (IC<sub>50</sub> = 2 μg/mL). The author suggested that the observed cytotoxic effects in the crude extract was due to rutin <bold>6</bold>, hyperoside <bold>9</bold>, quercetin <bold>3</bold>, quercertin-3-glucuronide <bold>7</bold>, and <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-coumaric acid <bold>37</bold>. Both rutin <bold>6</bold> and hyperoside <bold>9</bold> have been known to have antiproliferative and cytotoxic properties against cancer cells [<xref rid="B72-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>,<xref rid="B125-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">125</xref>,<xref rid="B126-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">126</xref>].</p><p>Gedara et al. [<xref rid="B25-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>] reported the cytotoxic activity of isolated compounds from the methanolic leaf extract against liver cancer cells (HEPG2) in a sulphorhodamine B staining method [<xref rid="B127-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">127</xref>]. The study used adriamycin as a positive control (IC<sub>50</sub> = 0.54 mg/mL). Spirostane saponin <bold>55</bold> inhibited cell growth with an IC<sub>50</sub> of 2.8 μg/mL, while erythrodiol <bold>53</bold> (IC<sub>50</sub> = 11 μg/mL) and <bold>54</bold> showed moderate activities (IC<sub>50</sub> = 11.2 μg/mL) (<xref rid="molecules-28-04396-f003" ref-type="fig">Figure 3</xref>). On the other hand, flavonoid glycosides such as myricitrin <bold>11</bold>, quercitrin <bold>4</bold>, and biflavonoid <bold>33</bold> (<xref rid="molecules-28-04396-f002" ref-type="fig">Figure 2</xref>) exhibited weak cytotoxic activities with IC<sub>50</sub> values of 14, 17.2, and 17.8 μg/mL, respectively.</p></sec></sec><sec id="sec4-molecules-28-04396"><title>4.
