Anti-Inflammatory and Antioxidant Effects</title><p>In recent years, the oxidative stress and its associated factors have gained importance in human health. This occurs due to the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) (hydroxyl radicals, superoxide anion radicals and hydrogen peroxide) when the body is under stress [<xref rid="B179-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">179</xref>]. This production results in imbalance processes, cell damage and health problems due to the overload of ROS that endogenous enzymatic and non-enzymatic antioxidant substances are not able to cope with [<xref rid="B180-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">180</xref>]. This process often results in, among others, inflammatory diseases. Currently, inflammation is also one of the major researched areas for biomedical researchers [<xref rid="B181-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">181</xref>]. The incorporation of antioxidant agents in diet from consumable natural plants can be used as preventive medicine for these disorders [<xref rid="B181-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">181</xref>].</p><p>The ethanol and ethyl acetate extracts of <italic toggle="yes">C. ciliaris</italic> rhizome showed anti-inflammatory activities due to inhibition of cyclooxygenase-1 (COX-1) and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) [<xref rid="B176-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">176</xref>]. Compounds typical extracted by polar solvents, such as ethyl acetate, with anti-inflammatory activities were not reported in <italic toggle="yes">C. ciliaris</italic>, with the exception of cholest-22-ene-21-ol; hence, it is not possible to infer a secure relationship between the known chemical composition and the reported activity.</p><p><italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic>’s ethanol extracts reduced significantly paw edema in rats (300 mg/kg), revealing its anti-inflammatory activity [<xref rid="B182-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">182</xref>], while the roots’ methanol (70%) extract showed ROS scavenging activities assessed by H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub> radical scavenging assay at concentrations of 50 μg/mL, 100 μg/mL, 200 μg/mL, 300 μg/mL, and 400 μg/mL [<xref rid="B183-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">183</xref>]. These effects may be due to the presence of coumarin umbelliferone [<xref rid="B53-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>] and flavonol quercetin [<xref rid="B53-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>], which are present in the polar extracts of <italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic> and normally related to these activities [<xref rid="B143-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">143</xref>,<xref rid="B167-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">167</xref>]. Regarding antioxidant activity, this could also be related to quercetin as well as their glucoside derivatives (quercetin 3-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-glucoside and quercetin 7-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-glucoside), known for its antioxidant properties [<xref rid="B184-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">184</xref>,<xref rid="B185-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">185</xref>]. Similarly, the methanol and aqueous extracts of <italic toggle="yes">E. crus-galli</italic> showed strong antioxidant activity [<xref rid="B168-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">168</xref>], assessed by DPPH (2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl) radical scavenging assay at concentrations of 50–500 μg/mL. This can also be attributed to quercetin and their 3-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-glucoside derivative but also to ferulic acid [<xref rid="B186-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">186</xref>] and 5,7-dihydroxy-3′,4′,5′-trimethoxy flavone [<xref rid="B187-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">187</xref>] known for their antioxidant properties. In the case of <italic toggle="yes">C. gayana</italic>, its isopropyl alcohol (3:2) extract showed free radical scavenging activity, concentration-dependent, in ABTS (2,2′-azino-bis(3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulfonic acid) and DPPH assays and Fe<sup>3+</sup> reducing potential until 500 µg/mL [<xref rid="B68-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>]. This can be related to the presence of the several flavonoids in its polar extracts [<xref rid="B68-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>], metabolites that are known for their antioxidant capacities [<xref rid="B188-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">188</xref>,<xref rid="B189-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">189</xref>,<xref rid="B190-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">190</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">I.
