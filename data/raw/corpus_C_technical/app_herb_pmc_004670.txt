There is no robust evidence-based intervention to improve post-stroke fatigue. However, some evidence suggests that pharmaceutical interventions may become promising treatments.</p><p>Some botanicals, particularly adaptogens, including <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> L. (Arctic Root) and <italic toggle="yes">Withania somnifera</italic> Burge roots (Ashwagandha) preparations, improve cognitive functions and prevent fatigue in human subjects under stressful conditions.</p><p>Meanwhile, multi-target network pharmacology systems biology concepts and innovative drug discovery tools, such as transcriptome-wide microarray gene expression profiling in vitro testing and artificial intelligence in silico, provided theoretical models that propose using botanical adaptogens to treat stress-induced disorders, including brain fatigue and related neurological diseases.</p><p>Recent studies of gene expression in neuroglia and neuronal cells unveiled the polyvalent and synergistic actions of botanical hybrid preparations (BHP) in stress-induced disorders, suggesting that adaptogens exhibited multitarget and pleiotropic effects via HPA- and GPCR-mediated signaling pathways.</p><p>Specifically, the BHP of Arctic Root and Ashwagandha, Adaptra<sup>®</sup>, shows a potential activation of neuronal development by specifically deregulating 22 genes in neuroglia cell culture, including nine product-specific synergistically deregulated genes: <italic toggle="yes">PRKCZ</italic> protein kinase C, <italic toggle="yes">GRIN3A</italic> glutamate ionotropic receptor, <italic toggle="yes">ADGRL1</italic> adhesion G protein-coupled receptor, <italic toggle="yes">CDK5R1</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">CDKL3</italic> cyclin-dependent kinases, <italic toggle="yes">CHRNB2</italic> cholinergic receptor, and <italic toggle="yes">ROR2</italic> receptor tyrosine kinase-like orphan receptor, suggesting beneficial effects in neurogenesis, neuroplasticity, and cognitive recovery, which are beneficial for rehabilitation in post-stroke long-lasting brain fatigue. Where Do We Go in Drug Discovery?</title><p>Herbs, used as food and a source of drugs for centuries, have been extensively studied chemically and pharmacologically during the last decades, with two main aims. One of them was to identify and purify pharmacologically active compounds following the development of new, more active compounds and analogs targeting a selective receptor free of adverse effects. Another aim was to combine several plants or their purified ingredients into new multitarget hybrid combinations with more effective pharmacological activity.</p><p>The first approach was successfully applied in orthodox Western medicine, resulting in the discovery of morphine, salicin, papaverine, paclitaxel, etc., and their synthetic analogs and the current drug development platform, which is based mainly on the pharmacodynamics and pharmacokinetics of botanicals and accrues knowledge of interactions of biologically active molecules of plant extracts with receptor molecules, primarily proteins, involved in cellular and organism responses to achieve a therapeutic effect and maintain organismal homeostasis. In the meantime, the stimulus-response coupling is often mediated by complex intra- and extracellular communications and feedback regulations within networks of signaling pathways and pharmacological systems, including neuroendocrine-immune complex, cardiovascular, gastrointestinal, and other systems.</p><p>The second approach was used mainly in traditional Chinese medicine based on empirical knowledge and undergoing modernization by applying the current understanding of pharmacological systems and network pharmacology of metabolomics, proteomics, transcriptomics, genomics, and microbiomes (collectively—OMICS).</p><p>In this review, we discussed to some extent the following:<list list-type="bullet"><list-item><p>The trends and pitfalls in pharmacognosy from galenic formulations to evidence-based medicine and artificial intelligence in network analysis of the systems pharmacology;</p></list-item><list-item><p>Multitarget and pleiotropic effects of adaptogens: unveiling of the polyvalent and synergistic actions of botanical hybrid preparations in stress-induced disorders;</p></list-item><list-item><p>Effects of botanical hybrid preparations on crosstalk of neuroendocrine-immune, cardiovascular, and other regulatory systems and the integrative response of intracellular and extracellular communications in stroke.</p></list-item></list></p><p>Our knowledge of biochemical mechanisms underlying brain fatigue, discussed in <xref rid="sec3-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="sec">Section 3</xref>, is based on the fundamentals of the physiology of organisms and molecular biology, which conventional methods of pharmacology and biology have accrued.
