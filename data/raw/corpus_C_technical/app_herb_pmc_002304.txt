Accurate identification of these compounds is essential for assessing the nutritional value of senescent leaves. Furthermore, commercial practices such as the fermentation of leaves from the same species (e.g., <italic toggle="yes">C. sinensis</italic>) have been shown to improve the sensory and nutritional properties of final products [<xref rid="B63-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>]. Although the effect of fermentation on the carotenoid content of <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> leaves has not been widely studied, it is possible that this process alters the carotenoid composition, either by degrading them or, in some cases, by releasing bound forms that could increase the extractable fraction. For example, it has been established that during tea processing in <italic toggle="yes">C. sinensis</italic>, carotenoids undergo complex transformations, including autoxidation, photooxidation, thermal degradation, and enzymatic cleavage catalyzed by carotenoid cleavage dioxygenases. These processes play a key role in determining both the flavor, through the formation of volatile carotenoid derivatives, and the quality, as reflected in the color of the final product [<xref rid="B63-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>]. Therefore, exploring the controlled fermentation of <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> leaves could offer dual benefits: improving organoleptic properties and possibly affecting carotenoid availability, warranting further experimental investigation.</p></sec></sec><sec id="sec3dot3-nutrients-17-03382"><title>3.3. Terpenoids</title><p>Terpenoids constitute another significant class of secondary metabolites present in <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> (<xref rid="nutrients-17-03382-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>). These compounds are characterized by their involvement in the plant’s defense and adaptation mechanisms and, therefore, have a high bioactive potential. This group of molecules is made up of a broad class of organic compounds derived from isoprene units, whose repetition gives rise to the different subclasses of terpenoids [<xref rid="B64-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>]. In the case of <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic>, the subclasses identified were monoterpenoids, triterpenoids, sesquiterpenoids, and saponins. The most abundant compounds were the triterpenoids, particularly squalene and lupeol, which stand out due to their high concentrations and demonstrated anticancer and anti-inflammatory potential [<xref rid="B50-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>]. Significant concentrations of saponins, comprising over 10% of the dry weight of <italic toggle="yes">Camellia</italic> material, are also noteworthy for their bioactive potential. More than 38 distinct saponins have been identified in this species [<xref rid="B20-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>,<xref rid="B65-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>]. Among these saponins, the camellidin family is particularly notable for its high antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antifungal potential [<xref rid="B51-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>]. This type of molecules is exclusive to the <italic toggle="yes">Camellia</italic> genus. They are conformed with 3<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>-hydroxy-18<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>-acetoxy-28-norolean-12-en-16-one (camellidin I) or 3<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>, 8<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>-dihydroxy-28-norolean-12-en-16-one (camellidin II) as possible aglycon moieties, and d-glucuronic acid, d-glucose and two moles of d-galactose as the sugar moieties [<xref rid="B66-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>]. Other saponins found in <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> include camellisoides (A, B, E, and G), molecules that are also exclusive to the <italic toggle="yes">Camellia</italic> genus [<xref rid="B20-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>]. Monoterpenoids, such as eucalyptol and 3-cyclohexene-1-methanol, were also identified, reaching concentrations of up to 1.18%. Additionally, sesquiterpenoids, including patchouli alcohol, santalol, epicurzerenone, caryophyllene, and isoledene, were detected [<xref rid="B50-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>]. Patchouli alcohol was determined as the majority sesquiterpene in <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> (3.49%) and is characterized by its anti-inflammatory potential [<xref rid="B50-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>].</p><p>In conclusion, the terpene profile of <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> reveals a diverse array of monoterpenoids, triterpenoid, and sesquiterpenoids, which contribute significantly to the plant’s bioactive properties.
