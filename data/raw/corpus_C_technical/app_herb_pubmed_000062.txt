Hop (Humulus lupulus)-derived bitter acids as multipotent bioactive compounds.

Van Cleemput M(1), Cattoor K, De Bosscher K, Haegeman G, De Keukeleire D, 
Heyerick A.

Author information:
(1)Laboratory of Pharmacognosy and Phytochemistry, Ghent University-UGent, Gent, 
Belgium.

Hop acids, a family of bitter compounds derived from the hop plant (Humulus 
lupulus), have been reported to exert a wide range of effects, both in vitro and 
in vivo. They exhibit potential anticancer activity by inhibiting cell 
proliferation and angiogenesis, by inducing apoptosis, and by increasing the 
expression of cytochrome P450 detoxification enzymes. Furthermore, hop bitter 
acids are effective against inflammatory and metabolic disorders, which makes 
them challenging candidates for the treatment of diabetes mellitus, 
cardiovascular diseases, and metabolic syndrome. This review summarizes the 
current knowledge on hop bitter acids, including both phytochemical aspects, as 
well as the biological and pharmacological properties of these compounds.
