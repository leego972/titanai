PPAR Cascade</title><p>PPARs are responsible for the regulation of metabolism and inflammation. The subtypes of PPARs include PPAR <italic toggle="yes">α</italic>, PPAR <italic toggle="yes">β</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">δ</italic>, and PPAR-<italic toggle="yes">γ</italic>. Activation of PPARs is followed by the formation of heterodimers with the retinoid X receptor (RXR). Heterodimerization recruits PGC-1<italic toggle="yes">α</italic> and then binds to DNA-specific sequences called PPAR response elements (PPRE) and, consequently, allows the target gene transcription (CPT-1, FAT/CD36, PDK4, and GLUT-4 et al.). Of the three isoforms, PPAR <italic toggle="yes">α</italic> and PPAR <italic toggle="yes">β</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">δ</italic> express at high levels in the heart, while PPAR-<italic toggle="yes">γ</italic> enriched in adipose tissue shows a lower expression. According to Finck et al. [<xref rid="B43" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>] myocardial FA oxidation rates increased while glucose uptake and oxidation decreased in MHC-PPAR <italic toggle="yes">α</italic> mice, accompanied by the ventricular hypertrophy and systolic ventricular dysfunction. The MHC-PPAR <italic toggle="yes">α</italic> mice displayed metabolic phenotype and structure alteration similar to that of the diabetic heart. Burkart et al. [<xref rid="B44" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>] further showed that in contrast to MHC-PPAR <italic toggle="yes">α</italic> mice, MHC-PPAR <italic toggle="yes">β</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">δ</italic> mice did not develop cardiomyopathy, even in the context of a high-fat diet, owing to upregulation of GLUT-4 and enhanced rate of myocardial glucose uptake and utilization but without increased FA oxidation. Interestingly, in their study, both FAT/CD36 expression and GLUT-4 mRNA levels increased in MHC-PPAR-<italic toggle="yes">γ</italic> mice, suggesting that PPAR-<italic toggle="yes">γ</italic> shares the characteristic with both PPAR <italic toggle="yes">α</italic> and PPAR <italic toggle="yes">β</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">δ</italic> in regulation metabolism in diabetic hearts. Therefore, their findings provide the evidence that selective activation of PPAR <italic toggle="yes">β</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">δ</italic> is a promising therapeutic strategy for DCM. PGC-1<italic toggle="yes">α</italic> is the coactivator of PPARs enriched in the myocardium. PGC-1<italic toggle="yes">α</italic> regulates the mitochondrial biogenesis, FA oxidation, and glucose oxidative metabolism. PDK4 is a downstream molecule of PGC-1<italic toggle="yes">α</italic>. Selective overexpression of PDK4 in the heart of mice results in a remarkable decrease in glucose oxidation and an increase in FA oxidation. Overexpression of PGC-1<italic toggle="yes">α</italic> is associated with heart failure linked to increased FA oxidation. However, knockdown PGC-1<italic toggle="yes">α</italic> also leads to heart failure due to depletion of energy production in mitochondrion. According to Botta et al. [<xref rid="B45" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>] short-time exercise attenuated DCM in aged diabetic heart in db/db mice by activating PGC-1<italic toggle="yes">α</italic>. Wang et al. [<xref rid="B46" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>] showed that excise ameliorated DCM through activation of PGC-1<italic toggle="yes">α</italic> and Akt signaling.</p><p>All the three isoforms of PPARs exert anti-inflammation in the development of DCM for physical interaction with the p65 subunit of NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B and inhibit the activation of certain members of the MAPK signaling pathway [<xref rid="B47" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>–<xref rid="B49" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>]. Enhanced physical interaction between p65 and PGC-1<italic toggle="yes">α</italic> contributes to the decreased activation of PGC-1<italic toggle="yes">α</italic>.</p></sec><sec id="sec3.4"><title>3.4. Phosphatidylinositol 3-Kinase (PI3K)/PKB/Akt Cascade</title><p>PI3K takes a crucial role in insulin pathway and cardiac adaptation including protein synthesis, FA and glucose metabolisms, and cell survival regulation. Activation of PI3K subsequently targets to the upregulation of downstream effectors including PKB/Akt, glycogen synthase kinase (GSK)-3Β, and mTOR.
