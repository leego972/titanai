Title: Addressing the impacts of climate change on human rights through adaptation-focused litigation: Cases before the African Union’s human rights bodies

Abstract:
Abstract This article examines the limited engagement of African human rights bodies with the nexus between climate change and human rights and proposes adaptation-focused litigation as a more viable pathway for climate justice in Africa. Applying a doctrinal method and a human rights-based approach, the article argues that the prevailing mitigation-focused arguments, even if successful, would not achieve the most impact, given the African context, which requires more adaptation measures due to the rapidly increasing negative impacts of climate change. Instead, the article makes a case for framing climate litigation based on the interconnections between the unequal impacts of climate change and recognized human rights norms—political, civil, economic, social, cultural, and environmental rights—already enshrined in African human rights treaties. The article contends that, by focusing on the adverse effects of climate change on these guaranteed rights—such as the rights to life, health, food, and a satisfactory environment—adaptation-focused litigation can effectively hold African governments accountable for addressing climate harms and protection of vulnerable populations. The article concludes by highlighting the untapped potential of the African human rights system in advancing ambitious climate action through this distinctive legal approach and suggests areas for further research.
