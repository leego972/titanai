BY</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><sec sec-type="intro"><title>Introduction</title><p>Phytochemicals are secondary metabolites specifically synthesized by plants, exerting substantial effects on human health (<xref rid="ref-9" ref-type="bibr">Arora &amp; Suvartan, 2020</xref>). Additionally, they play a crucial role in safeguarding plants against diseases and contribute significantly to the development of color, flavor, and fragrance in plants (<xref rid="ref-31" ref-type="bibr">Koche, Shirsat &amp; Kawale, 2016</xref>). Phytochemicals exhibiting biological activities (bioactivities) are termed “biologically active compounds”. Over the years, numerous studies have been conducted to investigate the significance of various phytochemicals in plants. For instance, research has underscored the economic value of purple maize, attributing it to its abundance in flavonoids, phenolic compounds, and anthocyanin. This plant serves essential roles in the health sector, functioning as an anti-mutagenic, antioxidant, anti-angiogenic, anti-inflammatory, and anti-carcinogenic agent (<xref rid="ref-30" ref-type="bibr">Jayaprakash et al., 2023</xref>). Additionally, Mexican oregano essential oils contain a wealth of biologically active compounds with diverse properties, including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, antifungal, ultraviolet defense, anti-glycemic, and cytotoxic activities (<xref rid="ref-12" ref-type="bibr">Bautista-Hernández et al., 2021</xref>). In essence, the presence of bioactive phytochemicals is integral to both human and plant health, as they are associated with significant activities that contribute to overall well-being.</p><p>The <italic toggle="yes">Solanum</italic> genus, a large genus within the Solanaceae family, comprises approximately 2,800 species (<xref rid="ref-70" ref-type="bibr">Wiart, 2006</xref>). It has been a subject of significant interest in both chemical and biological studies over a long period. Numerous plants belonging to the <italic toggle="yes">Solanum</italic> genus have exhibited the presence of diverse phytochemicals responsible for various bioactivities. For instance, <italic toggle="yes">Solanum lycopersicum</italic> is abundant in phenolic compounds, flavonoids, carotenoids, and nucleosides, rendering it valuable as nutraceuticals and anti-mutagenic plants (<xref rid="ref-4" ref-type="bibr">Alam et al., 2019</xref>) The fruits of <italic toggle="yes">Solanum anguivi</italic> are suggested to possess antidiabetic properties, potentially attributed to a range of phytochemicals, including saponins, phenolics, alkaloids, ascorbic acid, and flavonoids (<xref rid="ref-42" ref-type="bibr">Nakitto et al., 2021</xref>).
