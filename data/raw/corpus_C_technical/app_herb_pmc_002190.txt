Cardiovascular Protection</title><p>Cardiovascular diseases remain a major threat to public health and human life, and it is caused by various pathological factors such as oxidative stress and inflammation [<xref rid="B82" ref-type="bibr">82</xref>]. It has been reported that <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> exerted cardiovascular-protective effects mainly by defending against oxidative stress, reducing the apoptosis of cardiomyocytes, and suppressing inflammation. The polysaccharides of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> protected cardiomyocytes against oxidative stress-induced apoptosis by reducing ROS production, restoring mitochondrial membrane potential, regulating apoptosis-related protein, and increasing the activity of antioxidant enzymes, and these effects were possibly associated with the regulation of phosphoinositide 3-kinases (PI3K)/Akt and MAPK pathways [<xref rid="B82" ref-type="bibr">82</xref>, <xref rid="B83" ref-type="bibr">83</xref>]. Moreover, <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> extracts had protective potential against diabetic cardiomyopathy in STZ-induced diabetic mice, which inhibited oxidative stress, decreased cardiac lipid accumulation as well as deposition of collagen, downregulated the expression of several proinflammatory cytokines, and reduced cardia fibrosis [<xref rid="B84" ref-type="bibr">84</xref>]. Furthermore, <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> could ameliorate the aberrant cardio condition through the regulation of metabolism. In the rat model of unhealthy diet-induced metabolic hypertension, <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> could alleviate hypertension by reducing lipid abnormalities and improving the function of gastrointestine as well as vascular endothelial relaxation, which may be mediated by activating the SCFA-GPCR 43/41 pathway [<xref rid="B75" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>, <xref rid="B85" ref-type="bibr">85</xref>]. Besides, the water-soluble extracts of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> alleviated cardiac injury and fibrosis in HFD/STZ-induced diabetic mice with a 12-week daily administration, which was potentially implicated in increasing lipid transport, reducing insulin resistance, and inhibiting the EMT signaling pathway [<xref rid="B86" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec4.5"><title>4.5. Liver Protection</title><p>
<italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> could also confer protection against liver injuries and improve liver functions against different forms of liver injuries, such as drug-, chemical-, and acute alcohol-induced injuries and nonalcoholic fatty liver diseases (NAFLD). The polysaccharides from <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> could attenuate the acetaminophen-induced hepatotoxicity in mice by reducing the oxidative stress and activating the Nrf2-Keap1 signaling pathway, in which the levels of alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), ROS, MDA, and myeloperoxidase (MPO) were decreased; the levels of GSH, CAT, and T-AOC were increased, and the Nrf2 nuclear translocation was activated [<xref rid="B87" ref-type="bibr">87</xref>]. Additionally, alcoholic liver diseases are characterized by disrupted ethanol metabolism and stimulated oxidative stress. The NIR fluorescence imaging showed that the polysaccharides from <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> could protect against acute alcoholic liver injury in vivo by increasing the antioxidant levels, in which the level of GSH was balanced in the liver [<xref rid="B88" ref-type="bibr">88</xref>]. In addition, the alcohol-induced liver injury could be mitigated by the extracts of the <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> flower, which was associated with its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activities.
