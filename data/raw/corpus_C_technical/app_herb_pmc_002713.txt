This seasonal food is a tradition in many places in northeastern Mexico. Another edible product with high demands is the <italic toggle="yes">aguamiel</italic> (sap) of the maguey (<italic toggle="yes">Agave</italic> spp.). Rayones’s residents recommend drinking this sweet sap instead of sugar (after boiling it) to diabetics. People do not recommend drinking the sap uncooked, as it cause diarrhea. Two and a half litters of sap are sold in $30.00-$50.00 pesos (about 3–3.5 dollars). Several autochthonous wild or cultivated species with edible fruits such as <italic toggle="yes">Crateagus greggiana</italic> var. <italic toggle="yes">greggiana, Cucurbita moschata, C. ficifolia</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Persea americana</italic> var. <italic toggle="yes">drymifolia</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Physalis philadelphica</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Capsicum annum</italic>, and other species with edible leaves such as <italic toggle="yes">Tagetes lucida, Monarda citriodora</italic> var. <italic toggle="yes">austromontana</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Litsea glauscecens</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Lippia graveolens</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Hedeoma drummondii</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus palmeri</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Croton suaveolens, Portulaca mundula</italic>, are frequently gathered to prepare salads, beverages or used as condiments. Roasted and raw roots of several <italic toggle="yes">Hechtia</italic> species are not commonly used for food in Rayones, but for the making of shampoo, while in northwestern Mexico, these roots are commonly roasted for food [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B40">40</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Begonia</italic> is a common plant cultivated in gardens as an ornamental in Nuevo León, however in Puebla, the petioles of at least six different species of <italic toggle="yes">Begonia</italic> are commonly used as food [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B41">41</xref>]. Other species such as <italic toggle="yes">Echinocactus platyacanthus</italic> (candy barrel cactus) widely distributed in the Chihuahua Desert are used in different states such as Hidalgo, Querétaro, and Oaxaca to make candy or used as forage for cattle [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B42">42</xref>], in Nuevo León this species is used in a similar way. Several edible Cucurbitaceae found in Nuevo León [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B11">11</xref>] are also widely used as food or soap substitutes, such as <italic toggle="yes">Cucumis anguria</italic> (fruits), <italic toggle="yes">Cucurbita foetidissima</italic> (fruits). The quelites (<italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus</italic> spp.) are a weed species in Nuevo León, however, its leaves and inflorescences are widely used as food [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B43">43</xref>], in Rayones, <italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus palmeri</italic> is one of the most common foods along with nopales (<italic toggle="yes">Opuntia</italic> spp.).</p></sec><sec><title>Wood, fuel, tools and construction</title><p>Almost 18% of the flora is used for wood and construction purposes. Because of their size, thickness and hardness, <italic toggle="yes">Prosopis glandulosa</italic> var <italic toggle="yes">torreyana</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Helietta parvifolia</italic>, are by far, the most frequently used species for house construction and fences; both of them have a strong wood, suitable for these activities. According to information gathered from the residents (men) (Figure <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F9">9</xref>), fences made from <italic toggle="yes">Helietta parvifolia</italic> logs last for about 30 years. Other species such as <italic toggle="yes">Acacia berlandieri</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">A. farnesiana</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Havardia pallens</italic> are commonly used in the construction of houses, roofs, fences, but also, they are an excellent source for charcoal, part of tools (hammer handle, handle hoes, sickle handle) and firewood. The wood is not marketed locally; they use them to meet their own needs. Besides these uses, some species are multipurpose such as <italic toggle="yes">Carya illinoiensis</italic> (fruits, wood, fuel, construction and shampoo), <italic toggle="yes">Eysenhardtia texana</italic> (forage, medicinal and fuel)<italic toggle="yes">, Juglans major</italic> (fruits, wood, fuel, construction and shampoo). Besides being used for food, mezquite and alien species are used in similar way by the aborigines in northwestern Mexico and southwestern USA [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B44">44</xref>]. In Tamaulipas, <italic toggle="yes">Prosopis glandulosa, Acacia farnesiana, Havardia pallens, Helietta parvifolia</italic>[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B29">29</xref>] are commonly used for making fences or used as fuel (<italic toggle="yes">Ebenopsis ebano</italic>), the same species and the same uses are practiced by residents of Rayones.
