The Oleic / Linoleic ratio was 2.21 which is similar to the range reported in the literature [<xref rid="b0330" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="s0085"><label>4.9</label><title>Ultrasound effect on herbal carbohydrates</title><p id="p0175">Herbal polysaccharides, as natural macromolecules, have been demonstrated to have significant bioactivities, such as anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and antioxidant activities [<xref rid="b0525" ref-type="bibr">107</xref>]. The application of US can enhance the medicinal properties of polysaccharides but the extent of improvement is dependent on the treated herbs. For instance, Zhao, Xia, Lin, Xiong, Tang and Liao [<xref rid="b0210" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>] showed that US has a significant effect on polysaccharides extracted from dried leaves of Chinese herb <italic toggle="yes">Turpiniae Folium</italic> and optimum extraction conditions for high yield were 200 W at 30 °C for 35 min.</p><p id="p0180">US (45 kHz) at different power levels (40–100 W) were used extraction of polysaccharides from <italic toggle="yes">Acantho panaxsenticosus</italic> herb and their antioxidant activity have been reported by Zhao, Xu, Ye and Dong [<xref rid="b0530" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>]. An extraction time of 75 min at 80 °C, and 100 W US power resulted in the greatest yield (10.9 mg/g). The obtained polysaccharide by US treatment possessed considerable antioxidant activity against DPPH, hydroxyl, and superoxide free radicals [<xref rid="b0530" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>]. Similarly, the highest yield of polysaccharides from hibiscus leaves was obtained using US power of 93.59 W for 25.71 min at 93.18 °C. Under these conditions, the extracted polysaccharide content was increased by 10% [<xref rid="b0535" ref-type="bibr">109</xref>]. A study used US (25 kHz, 50–70 W, 50–70 °C, and time range of 10–30 min) for extraction of bioactive polysaccharides from mulberry (<italic toggle="yes">Morus Alba</italic>) leaves proved higher yields and lower water/raw material ratio compared to micro-wave-assisted extraction method [<xref rid="b0540" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>].</p><p id="p0185">For cellulose carbohydrate molecule, Nakayama and Imai [<xref rid="b0215" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>] found that US pretreatment (20 kHz, 200 W, 10 min) can enhance the enzymatic hydrolysis of kenaf herb (<italic toggle="yes">Hibiscus cannabinus</italic>) cellulose to produce glucose sugar. In which, the authors reported that US induced a higher association of cellulase and cellulose by removing the covering materials on the kenaf cellulose, as could be seen in <xref rid="f0025" ref-type="fig">Fig. 5</xref>.<fig id="f0025" position="float" orientation="portrait"><label>Fig. 5</label><caption><p>Scanning electron microscope image of Kenaf: (a) Before ultrasonic irradiation (b) After ultrasonic irradiation (20 kHz, 200 W, 10 min) [<xref rid="b0215" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>] (License Number: 4958670625442).</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="gr5.jpg"/></fig></p><p id="p0190">Joshi and Gogate [<xref rid="b0250" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>] reported that US horn (20 kHz, 100 W, and 60 °C) enhanced tea acid hydrolysis for the production of reducing sugars. In which, it reduced the reaction time from 120 to 60 min with a high yield of reducing sugars (24.75 g/L). Also, they reported that US combined with oxidants such as H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub> effectively decreased the acid hydrolysis time of the tea polysaccharides through facilitating the breakage of lignin, which increased the rate of reducing sugars production from tea powder [<xref rid="b0250" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="s0090"><label>4.10</label><title>Effect of US on herbal extract activity against pathogenic bacteria</title><p id="p0195">Intense US treatment and long contact times are required to inactivate microorganisms. For instance, to inhibit <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic>, a US treatment of 187 min and 150 W, 20 kHz is required [<xref rid="b0545" ref-type="bibr">111</xref>]. Also, Kazibwe, Kim, Chun and Gopal [<xref rid="b0550" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>] studied the US (20, 60 kHz; 200, 300 W; 2 min) assisted extraction effects of <italic toggle="yes">Tagetes erecta</italic> herb on their antimicrobial activity.
