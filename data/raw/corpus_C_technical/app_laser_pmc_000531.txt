Our data show that the HIDPPNPs displayed remarkable fluorescent expression after treatment with the secondary antibody (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="f2">Fig. 2-II</xref>; inset image A) and that the level of fluorescence was four-fold (<italic toggle="yes">P</italic> = 0.008) higher than that obtained from the HIDPPNPs without the secondary antibody (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="f2">Fig. 2-II</xref>, inset image a). These results clearly show that the HIDPPNPs, rather than the IDPPNPs, have an affinity to the secondary antibody, indicating that the anti-HER2-mAbs were definitely bound on the surface of the HIDPPNPs and were able to provide intact bioconjugation activity after the carbodiimide-mediated crosslinking reactions.
