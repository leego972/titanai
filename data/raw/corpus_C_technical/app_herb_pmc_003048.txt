</p></sec><sec id="Sec12"><title>Major human ailments and associated medicinal plants</title><p id="Par73">In the Karamara forest patches, 85 medicinal plant species were used to treat 54 human ailments (Table <xref rid="Tab2" ref-type="table">2</xref>). The most commonly addressed health problems included wounds, malaria, toothache, snake bites, abdominal pain, and kidney disorders, with wounds treated by the highest number of species, followed by malaria, digestive disorders, snake bites, and eye problems (Fig. <xref rid="Fig6" ref-type="fig">6</xref>).</p><p id="Par74">MPs were employed across a wide range of conditions, including gastrointestinal, respiratory, dermatological, urogenital, parasitic, musculoskeletal, and neurological disorders.</p><p id="Par75">Gastrointestinal and digestive ailments were the most frequently treated. Species such as <italic toggle="yes">Acacia nilotica</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Acacia nubica</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Acacia senegal</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Crotalaria jijigensis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Ehretia cymosa</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Ocimum lamiifolium</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus spina-christi</italic> were used for gastritis, abdominal pain, constipation, diarrhea, and stomachache.</p><p id="Par76">Dermatological disorders and wounds were managed with <italic toggle="yes">Abutilon fruticosum</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Cadaba glandulosa</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Cynoglossum amabile</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Euphorbia abyssinica</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Jasminum grandiflorum</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Solanum coagulans</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Tagetes minuta</italic>, typically applied topically for wounds, rashes, ringworm, and insect bites.</p><p id="Par77">Respiratory ailments, including coughs and colds, were treated with <italic toggle="yes">Aloe megalacantha</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Jatropha aceroides</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Ocimum lamiifolium</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Withania somnifera</italic>.</p><p id="Par78">Urogenital and reproductive issues were addressed using <italic toggle="yes">Vachellia bussei</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Otostegia modesta</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Maytenus arbutifolia</italic>, for urogenital infections, menstrual disorders, and kidney problems.</p><p id="Par79">Parasitic and microbial infections were managed with <italic toggle="yes">Carica papaya</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Dovyalis abyssinica</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Psidium guajava</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Opuntia ficus-indica</italic>, targeting intestinal worms, amoebiasis, and ectoparasites.</p><p id="Par80">Musculoskeletal and neurological conditions were treated with <italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus dubius</italic> for swollen joints, <italic toggle="yes">Commiphora myrrha</italic> for brittle bones, and <italic toggle="yes">Withania somnifera</italic> for epilepsy, highlighting remedies for chronic or systemic ailments.</p><p id="Par81">Additional common treatments included malaria, using <italic toggle="yes">Aloe megalacantha</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Aloe pirottae</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Cleome kelleriana</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Eucalyptus camaldulensis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Tamarindus indica</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Lawsonia inermis</italic>; toothache, with <italic toggle="yes">Acacia nilotica</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Clerodendrum myricoides</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Solanum spina-christi</italic>; headache, with <italic toggle="yes">Commiphora hodai</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Kleinia neriifolia</italic>; and poisoning or spiritually attributed conditions, with <italic toggle="yes">Calotropis procera</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Punica granatum</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Datura stramonium</italic>.</p><p id="Par82">
