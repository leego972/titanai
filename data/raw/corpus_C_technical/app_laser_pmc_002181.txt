In this context, analysis of the response of fibroblasts cells crucial for repair processes allows for the assessment of the dose-response relationship and the identification of the parameters promoting a pro-regenerative response. The results of our study suggest that the response of fibroblasts to laser irradiation was dependent on the applied parameters. The most favourable effects, reflected by an increased number of adherent metabolically active cells, were observed for the 100 mW/10 J/cm² and 200 mW/2 J/cm² configurations. In the wound healing assay, a parameter-dependent pattern of gap closure dynamics was noted. The most pronounced and sustained effect was observed for cells treated with the 200 mW/2 J/cm² LLLT (Table <xref rid="Tab2" ref-type="table">2</xref>). The fact that only two of the four parameter sets were effective supports the concept of a therapeutic bioenergetic window [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR27">27</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR28">28</xref>]. This phenomenon is most likely related to the activation of mitochondrial complex IV (cytochrome c oxidase, CCO), leading to an increase in ATP production, activation of the PI3K/Akt pathway and inhibition of NF-κB activity, which promotes the proliferation and migration of fibroblasts, and improves their survival [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR30">30</xref>]. <table-wrap id="Tab2" position="float" orientation="portrait"><label>Table 2</label><caption><p> The effect of LLLT on the cellular response of fibroblasts of the Hs680Tr. cell line</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="anchor" id="MO8" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="10103_2026_4873_Tab2_HTML.jpg"/></table-wrap></p><p id="Par39">The dependence of the observed effect on radiation parameters was confirmed by Anders et al., who demonstrated that irradiation with a wavelength of 810nm (10 mW; 5 J/cm²) increased the mitochondrial metabolism of fibroblasts, while 980 nm (0.2–5 J/cm²) decreased its activity. In turn, in neurons, irradiation with a wavelength of 980 nm induced a dose-dependent effect; low doses (2–200 mJ/cm²) stimulated neurite growth, while higher doses had no pro-regenerative effects [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR31">31</xref>]. A different response profile was observed in the case of RAW 264.7 macrophages irradiated at a wavelength of 808 nm (100 and 200 mW; 5 and 10 J/cm²), where a pulsed beam of 200 mW and a dose of 5 J/cm² increased cell adhesion, their viability and secretion of NO and TOS, while reduced the levels of TNF-α, monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 (MCP-1/CCL2) and MMP-9 [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR20">20</xref>]. Brondon et al. also showed that at a wavelength of 670nm (5 J/cm² per treatment; total dose 50 J/cm²), dose fractionation affected the proliferation and metabolism of HEp-2 epithelial cells and L-929 fibroblasts, with the strongest response observed in the case of two exposures per day [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR32">32</xref>]. In another study of LLLT treatment, a wavelength of 670 nm (5 J/cm²/24 h) and a pulse frequency of 6–600 Hz differentiated the response of HEp-2 cells, with the maximum proliferation being observed at a frequency of 100 Hz [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR33">33</xref>]. This means that not only the total dose of LLLT, but also the method of its application determines the biological effect.</p><p id="Par40">In numerous studies, wavelength- and dose-dependent effects of LLLT on fibroblast adhesion, migration, and viability have been confirmed. Karimi et al. (660, 808, 915 nm; 150–250 mW; 2 and 4 J/cm²) observed increased adhesion of HGF fibroblasts at 660 and 915 nm, whereas at 808 nm the effect was observed only at 2 J/cm² [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR34">34</xref>]. Basso et al. demonstrated enhanced migration and proliferation of HGF fibroblasts following irradiation with 780nm laser light (40 mW; 0.5 and 3 J/cm²) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR35">35</xref>]. Similarly, Shaikh-Kader et al. reported increased migration of WS1 fibroblasts after exposure to a 660 nm laser beam (100 mW; 5 J/cm²) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR36">36</xref>]. Ladiz showed that irradiation at 810 nm (100 mW; 0.5 J/cm²) increased the viability of HGF fibroblasts, whereas 940 nm at the same dose decreased it [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR37">37</xref>]. In contrast, Harorli et al., irradiating fibroblasts with light at a wavelength of 940nm (40–90 mW; 0.84–1.97 J/cm²), found no effect on cell viability [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR38">38</xref>]. Collectively, these findings further support the non-linear and parameter-specific character of the LLLT response.</p><p id="Par41">In our study, LLLT not only enhanced ATP-dependent metabolic activity of fibroblasts, but also influenced their membrane integrity and inflammatory status, as evidenced by a decrease in AK and NO release (Table <xref rid="Tab2" ref-type="table">2</xref>).
