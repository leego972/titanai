Different approaches can stimulate and influence this process, including chemical treatments, physical stimulation (mechanical forces, ultrasound, shock waves, stimulation of different nature), photostimulation and cell therapies. The latter approach, currently considered the most effective, consists of using mesenchymal stem cells to increase the bone regeneration process, as this cell line has excellent self-renewal and pluripotency properties. MSCs are easily obtained from several tissues, including bone marrow, adipose tissue, skin, umbilical cord and placenta, but the best regenerative results have been obtained from bone marrow MSCs. However, this cellular type also has some negative aspects, among which are high variability in cell quality and concentration, due to the different tissue sources from which they were obtained, as well as challenging costs and times. The presence of these limitations in the use of MSCs has led to the search for new treatments to enhance bone regeneration, and among these is the LLLT. Photostimulation would seem to influence several biological activities, including mitochondrial respiration, ATP production and ROS levels in cells, causing a possible alteration of some cellular biological processes [<xref rid="B17-ijms-24-07094" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>]. Therefore, several studies have been conducted to understand the effects of LLLT, both in vitro, mainly on BMSC, HUVEC, MSC cells and osteoblasts, and in vivo, especially on rat and rabbit.</p><p>Below are some studies, conducted both in vivo and in vitro, showing the possible effects of LLLT.</p></sec><sec id="sec3dot3-ijms-24-07094"><title>3.3. In Vitro Studies</title><p>It has been observed that LLLT interacts mainly in the initial phases of bone regeneration process; it stimulates the following signaling pathways (<xref rid="ijms-24-07094-f002" ref-type="fig">Figure 2</xref> and <xref rid="ijms-24-07094-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>):</p><sec id="sec3dot3dot1-ijms-24-07094"><title>3.3.1. Inflammatory Phase</title><list list-type="bullet"><list-item><p>WNT/β-catenin is a signaling pathway that plays a crucial role in the progression of bone regeneration; in particular, its activation stabilizes the cytoplasmic β-catenin, allowing its nuclear translocation and degrades the protein complex formed by axin, adenomatosis polyposis coli (APC), protein phosphatase 2A (PP2A), glycogen synthase kinase 3 (GSK3) and casein kinase 1 α (CK1α) responsible for the destruction of ß-catenin [<xref rid="B20-ijms-24-07094" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>]. Once activated and translocated to the nucleus, β-catenin interacts with several DNA-bound proteins, including LEF and TFC, to induce the expression of osteogenic markers and the differentiation of pre-osteoblasts [<xref rid="B35-ijms-24-07094" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>]. The inflammatory process negatively affects this signaling pathway, since the presence of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including TNF, stimulates the expression of Dickkopf-related protein 1 (DKK-1), an antagonist of the WNT signaling pathway that prevents its activation [<xref rid="B36-ijms-24-07094" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>]. Laser treatment, particularity with a wavelength of 830 nm, increases the translocation of β-catenin from the cytoplasm to the nucleus, increasing the activation of the pathway and accelerating the bone regeneration process [<xref rid="B25-ijms-24-07094" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>].</p></list-item><list-item><p>NF-kB is a transcription factor present in the cytoplasm, which, following the phosphorylation of its inhibitor complex IkBα, can translocate into the nucleus and through gene transcription, promoting the inflammation process. Treatment, with a red laser with a wavelength of 660 nm of adipose-derived stem cells (ADSCs), showed a reduction in phosphorylated IkBα and NF-kB concentration in the nucleus, highlighting the ability of LLLT to negatively regulate the process of inflammation via NF-kB inhibition [<xref rid="B27-ijms-24-07094" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>].</p></list-item></list></sec><sec id="sec3dot3dot2-ijms-24-07094"><title>3.3.2. Angio-Mesenchymal Phase</title><list list-type="bullet"><list-item><p>VEGF is one of the main regulators of angiogenesis, able to promote the migration and proliferation of endothelial cells. A subclass of endothelial vessels, termed H-type vessels due to high expression of CD31 and endomucin, positively influence osteogenesis by resisting surrounding osteoprogenitors that express high levels of <italic toggle="yes">osterix</italic>, a promoter of bone formation. It has been reported through the literature that irradiation of human bone marrow mesenteric stem cells with an 808 nm laser increased both VEGF expression and H-type vessel formation, leading to an overall enhancement of the angiogenic process [<xref rid="B16-ijms-24-07094" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>,<xref rid="B29-ijms-24-07094" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>].</p></list-item><list-item><p>FGF contributes to the regulation of several important cellular processes; by activating the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway, it inhibits cell apoptosis by promoting its proliferation, while by stimulating the MAPK/ERK pathway it induces osteoblast differentiation.
