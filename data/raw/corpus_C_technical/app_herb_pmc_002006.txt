The multitarget profile of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> suggests potential benefits in complex pathologies such as postmenopausal osteoporosis, where inflammation, oxidative stress, and hormonal dysregulation coexist.</p><p>Its principal active compounds, triterpenoid saponins (e.g., PFS), flavonoids, and polyacetylenes, are implicated in these diverse biological effects.</p><p>In particular, saponins may modulate RANKL-induced transcription, while polyacetylenes could contribute to anti-inflammatory signaling inhibition.</p><p>However, challenges remain, particularly concerning the limited oral bioavailability of saponins and the potential for endocrine modulation at high doses. Author’s Perspective on the Pharmacological Potential of Polyscias fruticosa</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Polyscias fruticosa</italic> emerges as a pharmacologically versatile medicinal plant whose broad spectrum of biological activities—ranging from antidiabetic and anti-inflammatory to neuroprotective, antimicrobial, and osteoprotective—has been increasingly substantiated by preclinical evidence [<xref rid="B10-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>,<xref rid="B11-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>,<xref rid="B13-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>,<xref rid="B15-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>,<xref rid="B17-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>,<xref rid="B18-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>,<xref rid="B19-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>,<xref rid="B20-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>,<xref rid="B21-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>]. Unlike many botanical agents that exert isolated effects, <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> demonstrates a multitarget pharmacodynamic profile, largely attributed to its diverse phytochemical composition, including triterpenoid saponins, polyacetylenes, flavonoids, phenolic acids, and sterols [<xref rid="B3-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B4-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B6-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="B10-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>,<xref rid="B11-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>,<xref rid="B16-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>].</p><p>A critical comparative analysis with related medicinal plants—such as <italic toggle="yes">Panax ginseng</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Morus alba</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Momordica charantia</italic>—highlights <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>’s unique advantages, including dual enzyme inhibition in diabetes management, mitochondrial stabilization in neurodegeneration, and superior antibacterial activity due to polyacetylene-rich fractions [<xref rid="B10-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>,<xref rid="B11-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. Furthermore, its adaptogenic and immunomodulatory properties provide a theoretical basis for its use in hormone-sensitive conditions, such as reproductive disorders and postmenopausal osteoporosis.</p><p>However, despite these promising findings, significant knowledge gaps remain. First, many pharmacological studies rely on ethanolic or crude extracts without standardization, making reproducibility and dose extrapolation challenging. Second, the <italic toggle="yes">oral bioavailability and pharmacokinetics</italic> of key compounds, particularly saponins, remain poorly defined.
