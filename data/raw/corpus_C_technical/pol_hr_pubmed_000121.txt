The ethical obligations of the military medical practitioner.

Niemann G(1).

Author information:
(1)School of Law, Flinders University South Australia. 
grant.niemann@flinders.edu.au

International humanitarian law requires medical practitioners to be given 
"respect and protection" when serving as medical practitioners in the military. 
A component of this legal assurance is that when military medical personnel base 
their decisions on their medical code of ethics, that decision will be respected 
and protected. Although the "respect" that has been afforded by international 
humanitarian law has been part of the law for a considerable period of time, it 
is not always clear that military command or the courts are sufficiently aware 
of the ambit of this prescription.
