Flavonoids such as quercetin <bold>3</bold> (0.0037), kaempferol <bold>22</bold> (0.0011), and rutin <bold>6</bold> (0.0016) were also detected. </p><p>Four different extracts from individual parts (flowers, leaves, and bark) of Australian <italic toggle="yes">A. saligna</italic> were obtained by our group using a sequential polarity-based extraction (<xref rid="molecules-28-04396-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>). Bioactive extracts were determined using in vitro antioxidant DPPH assays [<xref rid="B26-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>]. Methanolic extracts from barks, leaves, and flowers were the most active. Compared to vitamin C (IC<sub>50</sub> = 49.97 µg/mL), the bark extract was the most active, with an IC<sub>50</sub> of 94.24 µg/mL, followed by the leaf (IC<sub>50</sub> = 190.1 µg/mL) and then the flower (IC<sub>50</sub> = 331.5 µg/mL). Pure compound isolation of the flower extract by column chromatography provided (reported in % <italic toggle="yes">w</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">w</italic> of dried flowers) naringenin <bold>42</bold> (0.183)<bold>,</bold> quercitrin <bold>4</bold> (0.432), naringenin-7-O-<italic toggle="yes">α</italic>-<italic toggle="yes">L</italic>-arabinofuranoside <bold>47</bold> (0.27), <italic toggle="yes">D</italic>-(+)-pinitol <bold>48</bold> (0.262), and isosalipurposide <bold>1</bold> (0.159). The leaf extract yielded (reported in % <italic toggle="yes">w</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">w</italic> of dried leaves) (–)-epicatechin <bold>51</bold> (0.0.91), quercitrin <bold>4</bold> (0.29), myricitrin <bold>11</bold> (0.507), 2,4-di-<italic toggle="yes">t</italic>-butylphenol <bold>50</bold> (0.101), (−)-pinitol <bold>49</bold> (0.913), and (3<italic toggle="yes">S</italic>,5<italic toggle="yes">S</italic>)-3-hydroxy-5-(2-aminoethyl)-dihydrofuran-2(3H)-one <bold>52</bold> (0.507). Isolation of the bark extract using column chromatography (reported in % <italic toggle="yes">w</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">w</italic> of dried bark) yielded (−)-epicatechin <bold>51</bold> (0.185), <italic toggle="yes">D-</italic>(+)-pinitol <bold>48</bold> (1.303), and sucrose (6.87).</p><p>The isolated compounds from each active extract were screened in the same DPPH assay. Myricitrin <bold>11</bold> (IC<sub>50</sub> = 199.9 µM), (–)-epicatechin <bold>51</bold> (IC<sub>50</sub> = 278 µM), and quercitrin <bold>4</bold> (IC<sub>50</sub> = 322.6 µM) were the three active compounds among the isolated compounds. In similar DPPH methods, myricitrin <bold>11</bold> was reported to be active, with IC<sub>50</sub> values ranging from 2.8 to 165.75 µM [<xref rid="B40-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>,<xref rid="B41-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>]. The (–)-epicatechin <bold>51</bold> was active with IC<sub>50</sub> values ranging from 10.8 to 103.4 µM [<xref rid="B42-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>,<xref rid="B43-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>]. Similarly, the works by Li et al. [<xref rid="B44-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>] and Hong et al. [<xref rid="B45-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>] showed that quercitrin <bold>4</bold> had IC<sub>50</sub> values of 4.45 and 107.5 µM. Naringenin <bold>42</bold>, naringenin-7-O-<italic toggle="yes">α</italic>-<italic toggle="yes">L</italic>-arabinofuranose <bold>47</bold>, and 2,4-di-<italic toggle="yes">t</italic>-butylphenol <bold>50</bold> showed poor activity against the DPPH, with undetectable IC<sub>50</sub> values at the highest tested concentration of 10 mM. Similarly, this result was also observed for compound <bold>52</bold>. Naringenin <bold>42</bold>, as well as compounds <bold>47</bold>, <bold>49</bold>, <bold>50,</bold> and <bold>52</bold> performed poorly due to their lack of π-π conjugation OH groups required to donate hydrogen or electrons, which may have led to the possibility of forming radical resonance intermediates [<xref rid="B46-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>,<xref rid="B47-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>]. Naringenin <bold>42</bold> was also found to have poor activity by Cai et al. [<xref rid="B46-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>], where it yielded an IC<sub>50</sub> of 2 mM. Isosalipurposide <bold>1</bold> was reported to be moderately active, with an IC<sub>50</sub> of 81.9 µM in the DPPH assay [<xref rid="B11-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. These findings reiterate that IC<sub>50</sub> values were considerably variable.</p><p>The presence of quercitrin <bold>4</bold> and perhaps naringenin <bold>42</bold> and isosalipurposide <bold>1</bold> (1.52% <italic toggle="yes">w</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">w</italic>) in the flower extract was responsible for the activity observed in this extract. The presence of three active antioxidants, namely, (–)-epicatechin <bold>51</bold>, quercitrin <bold>4</bold>, and myricitrin <bold>11,</bold> was reported to be supportive of the activity of the leaf extract.
