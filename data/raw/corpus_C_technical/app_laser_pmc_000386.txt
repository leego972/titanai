Skin Cancer</title><p>Skin cancer is a main common form of cancer, with non-melanoma types (NMSC), basal cell carcinoma (BCC) and squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), being the most frequent types [<xref rid="B1-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B2-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>]. Although death from NMSC is rare, treatment of NMSC results in a considerable burden on the health-care system [<xref rid="B3-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B4-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B5-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>].</p><p>One of the most important etiologic factors is sun exposure, particularly, ultraviolet radiation (UVR) and especially ultraviolet B (UVB). In fact, exposure to UVR is associated with approximately 90% of NMSCs. This is particularly applicable to people who have lighter complexion (skin types I–III) [<xref rid="B6-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>] and some predisposing genetic factors [<xref rid="B7-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>].</p><p>BCC is the most common cancer in fair skinned individuals and its incidence is still rising [<xref rid="B6-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>]. The incidence is also increasing among younger people (&lt;40 years), however the average age of first diagnosis is 60. Metastasis is unusual (incidence of 0.0028%–0.55%), however, the invasive growth pattern can destroy vital structures [<xref rid="B8-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>]. It mainly appears in the chronically photoexposed skin, even though superficial BCC is also frequent on the trunk. Other risk factors include skin phototype, immunosuppression, and hereditary disorders such as nevoid basal cell carcinoma syndrome (Gorlin-Goltz syndrome) and xeroderma pigmentosum. Recently, it has been proposed that 90% of superficial BCC evolve from interfollicular epidermal basal stem cells, whereas the rest of types evolve from hair follicle (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="ijms-16-25912-f001">Figure 1</xref>) [<xref rid="B9-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>].</p><p>According to both clinical and histological criteria, the most common subtypes of BCC are nodular, infiltrative (sclerodermiform and micronodular) and multicentric-superficial. Aggressive ulcerative and destructive forms and pigmented BCCs are other variants [<xref rid="B6-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="B8-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>].</p><p>SCC derives from the keratinocytes of the spinous layer of the epidermis (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="ijms-16-25912-f001">Figure 1</xref>).
