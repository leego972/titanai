TEM analysis revealed that when the nanoparticles were introduced into a bacterium, they completely destroyed the cell wall, resulting in bacterial cell death.<xref rid="b40-ijn-9-001" ref-type="bibr">40</xref></p><p>Honokiol (HN) is a constituent of the Chinese medicinal plant <italic toggle="yes">Magnolia officinalis</italic> (Magnoliaceae): 3′,5-di(2-propenyl)-1,1′-biphenyl-2,4′-diol. It has several pharmacological effects, including anti-inflammatory, antithrombotic, antirheumatic, antioxidant, with anxiolytic, central nervous system depressant, and muscle relaxant activities. In addition, it has potent antitumor activity. Again, this compound’s hydrophobic properties represent an obstacle, because high hydrophobicity prevents vascular administration. However, when this active constituent was loaded in polymeric nanoparticles, vascular administration was possible, as shown by Zheng et al. They developed a new formulation containing HN-loaded polymeric nanoparticles for vascular administration, obtaining better results, relative to free HN.<xref rid="b41-ijn-9-001" ref-type="bibr">41</xref></p><p>Khuda-Bukhsh et al formulated polymeric nanocapsules containing coumarin (7-hydroxy-6-methoxy) (HMC), which was isolated from an ethanolic extract of <italic toggle="yes">Gelsemium sempervirens</italic> J. St.-Hil (Gelsemiaceae). According to the in vitro evaluations, HMC has a pronounced anti-tumoral activity, but further studies were discontinued because of its hydrophobicity. A new formulation containing HMC-loaded polymeric nanoparticles demonstrated much better bioavailability than the free active constituent.<xref rid="b31-ijn-9-001" ref-type="bibr">31</xref></p><p><italic toggle="yes">Harungana madagascariensis</italic> Lam. Ex Poir (Hypericaceae) is widely known for its antibacterial, antifungal, and antiviral properties. Moulari et al evaluated and compared the in vitro and ex vivo antibacterial activity of ethanolic extract of <italic toggle="yes">H. madagascariensis</italic> leaf (HLE), incorporated into poly(D,L-lactide-co-glycolide) (PLG) nanoparticles (PLG-NPs) (HLE-PLG-NP). Two concentrations of HLE (500 μg/mL and 1,000 μg/mL) were evaluated for the in vivo assay; one concentration (500 μg/mL) was tested ex vivo, against two gram-positive bacteria (<italic toggle="yes">S. epidermidis</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Micrococcus luteus</italic>) and one gram-negative bacterial strain strain (<italic toggle="yes">Moraxella sp</italic>.). Ex vivo antibacterial activity was evaluated for <italic toggle="yes">S. epidermidis</italic> CIP 55109 using an artificial contamination method. The microorganism was inoculated for 12 hours on human skin fragment surfaces treated with HLE-PLG-NP, empty PLG-NPs, or HLE solution. In vitro studies revealed that both formulations completely reduced the bacterial growth of all strains tested, at 1,000 μg/mL concentrations. However, the 500 μg/mL HLE solution did not have a significant antibacterial activity against <italic toggle="yes">S. epidermidis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">M. luteus</italic>, or <italic toggle="yes">Moraxella sp</italic>., relative to HLE-PLG-NP. During ex vivo evaluation, 4 hours after artificial contamination, HLE-PLG-NP (500 μg/mL) showed better antibacterial activity than HLE solution. A thin-layer chromatographic analysis revealed that, of the seven components found in the chromatogram of the HLE solution, only two were found in the HLE-loaded nanoparticles, including the flavonoid fraction, which is responsible for the antibacterial properties. Once again, results improved when extracts were loaded into polymeric nanoparticles.<xref rid="b42-ijn-9-001" ref-type="bibr">42</xref></p><p>In 2006, Moulari et al investigated the in vitro antibacterial activity of the ethyl acetate extract from <italic toggle="yes">H. madagascariensis</italic> leaves against the major oral bacterial strains responsible for dental caries and gingivitis. To enhance antibacterial activity, HLE was loaded within PLG-NPs. Antibacterial activity was assessed via a dilution technique, using microplates, and polymeric nanoparticles were formulated using an interfacial polymer deposition solvent diffusion method. Free HLE displayed good results against most of the bacterial strains tested, with a 500 μg/mL MIC. The exception was <italic toggle="yes">Lactobacillus casei</italic>.
