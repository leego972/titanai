Carbohydrates and Amino Acids</title><p>Carbohydrates, 13 in total (<bold>102</bold>–<bold>114</bold>), were reported to be present in eight halophytes from the Poaceae: <italic toggle="yes">A. donax</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">C. dactylon</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">D. spicata</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">I. cylindrica</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">P. maritima</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">S. spontaneum</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">S. anglica</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">S. pyramidalis</italic> (<xref ref-type="app" rid="app1-ijms-20-01067">Table S1</xref>). These compounds belong to several subclasses such as aldoses, ketoses, disaccharides, trisaccharides, tetrasaccharides, polysaccharides, and aldonic acids. <italic toggle="yes">A. donax</italic> has the most diverse carbohydrates reported (11 compounds), being the richest species regarding this class of compounds [<xref rid="B43-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>,<xref rid="B106-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>] (<xref ref-type="app" rid="app1-ijms-20-01067">Table S1</xref>). <italic toggle="yes">I. cylindrica</italic> [<xref rid="B107-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">107</xref>] and <italic toggle="yes">S. pyramidalis</italic> [<xref rid="B108-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>] contain 6 carbohydrates (<xref ref-type="app" rid="app1-ijms-20-01067">Table S1</xref>), while in both <italic toggle="yes">P. maritima</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">S. anglica</italic> fructose, glucose and sucrose were reported [<xref rid="B109-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">109</xref>]. Furthermore, in both <italic toggle="yes">C. dactylon</italic> [<xref rid="B64-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>] and <italic toggle="yes">S. spontaneum</italic> [<xref rid="B59-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>], only mannose was reported. At last, in <italic toggle="yes">D. spicata</italic>, sucrose was the only carbohydrate described [<xref rid="B110-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>]. These compounds were retrieved, in all cases from hydroalcoholic extracts. Sugars constitute a class of compounds which are energetic sources and add flavor to plants. Additionally, several health benefits are also being attributed to vegetal-derived sugars such as trehalose [<xref rid="B111-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">111</xref>]. The presence of these molecules adds value to halophytic grasses and reveals their potential as functional foods.</p><p>Regarding amino acids (<bold>115</bold>–<bold>131</bold>), 17 were reported in three halophytic grasses, specifically in <italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">S. anglica</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">P. maritima.</italic> All 17 amino acids were reported in <italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic> perchloric acid (5% (<italic toggle="yes">v</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">v</italic>)) extract [<xref rid="B110-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>], while only two were described in <italic toggle="yes">S. anglica</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">P. maritima</italic>’s hydroalcoholic extracts [<xref rid="B109-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">109</xref>] (<xref ref-type="app" rid="app1-ijms-20-01067">Table S1</xref>). Amino acids are important compounds from a nutritional point of view since they are crucial to a balanced diet (maintaining optimal levels of essential amino acids) [<xref rid="B112-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>]. Some of the essential ones were reported in <italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic>, explicitly histidine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, and valine [<xref rid="B110-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>].
