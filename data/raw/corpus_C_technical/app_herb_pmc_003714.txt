They found that the polysaccharides derived from <italic toggle="yes">L. barbarum</italic> showed a lot of beneficial effects for elderly people, including increased immune-enhancing activity, proliferation ability, and increased phagocytosis capacity. The prepared extract may be suitable for addition to fluid food for elderly people who have a problem with dysphagia [<xref rid="B72-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>]. On the other hand, Wang et al. presented exosomes from <italic toggle="yes">L. barbarum</italic>, in which isoliquiritigenin was encapsulated and then incorporated into 3D-printed hydrogel scaffolds. The obtained material exhibited neurogenic differentiation and anti-inflammatory properties. Therefore, it was used for the regeneration of spinal cord injuries. The product promoted regeneration, the recovery of motion function, and the inhibition of inflammation; therefore, the new route of delivery of insoluble drugs via plant exosomes was promoted [<xref rid="B73-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>]. Interestingly, <italic toggle="yes">L. barbarum</italic> leaf flavonoids together with collagen were successfully used as a lutein emulsifier, prolonged its stability, and protected the compound from premature release and destruction, simultaneously enhancing its bioavailability [<xref rid="B74-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>]. Similarly, whey protein <italic toggle="yes">L. barbarum</italic> L. leaves flavonoids were also used as a functional carrier of β-carotene. The prepared emulsion showed good salt tolerance, enhanced light and thermal stability, as well as enhanced bioavailability of the encapsulated compound [<xref rid="B75-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">Schisandra chinensis</italic> is a very valuable plant in traditional Chinese Medicine. It contains in its structure valuable chemical compounds, including oils, polysaccharides, acids, lignans, and flavonoids. It is used in hypnosis and to protect the liver, exhibit therapeutic, antifungal, and antitumor effects, and support preventing asthma and other lung diseases. Long et al. discussed the antifungal effect of <italic toggle="yes">S. chinensis</italic>, which inhibited the growth of <italic toggle="yes">Alternaria alternata</italic> on apples, after the harvest season. The fungus is responsible for most of the losses of fruit and causes black spot disease. An ethanolic extract of <italic toggle="yes">S. chinensis</italic> fruit strongly inhibited the growth of fungus with an EC50 of 1882.00 mg/L [<xref rid="B76-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>]. Polysaccharides of <italic toggle="yes">S. chinensis</italic> show a great potential for the protection of nerves. Therefore, it may be useful in preventing and treating Alzheimer’s disease. They can improve the memory ability and learning skills of mice as well as moderate the damage to the neuron cells in the hippocampus and reduce the abnormally deposited Aβ-protein [<xref rid="B77-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>]. On the other hand, polysaccharides of <italic toggle="yes">S. chinensis</italic> have the ability to protect the kidney and liver from side effects caused by cyclosporine A, an immunosuppressive drug, prescribed after organ transplantation, which may affect important organs. The polysaccharides of <italic toggle="yes">S. chinensis</italic> improve fibrosis degree protect liver cells from function injury and promote the LX-2 cells proliferation [<xref rid="B78-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">78</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">S. chinensis</italic> shows not only protection on the liver but also the brain and can protect the organs from neurological and behavioral disorders which can be caused by alcohol through moderation of ethanol-induced neurotoxicity. Therefore, the water extract can also protect nerves from ethanolic damage. It was also tested on Caenorhabditis elegans worms, and it was concluded that the extract reduced the concentration of ethanol in its tissues, reduced oxidative stress, and alleviated the motility loss of organisms [<xref rid="B79-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">79</xref>]. Interestingly, subarachnoid hemorrhage can lead to early brain injury; therefore, Jin and co-workers extracted Schizandrin A from <italic toggle="yes">S. chinensis</italic> and studied its mechanism to contain the side effects of subarachnoid hemorrhage. It was described that the presented extract alleviated inflammation and protected neurons against injury, neurological problems, and brain edema. Schizandrin A can be used as a novel and natural therapeutic compound [<xref rid="B80-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">S. chinensis</italic> extracts also exhibit antidepressant effects and, therefore, may affect the noradrenergic, dopaminergic, and serotonergic systems [<xref rid="B81-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>].</p><p>In China, there are a lot of <italic toggle="yes">Sophora japonica</italic> trees which are very beneficial in economic and ecological sectors.
