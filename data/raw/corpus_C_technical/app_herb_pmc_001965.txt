Agitation and aeration ensure uniform cell proliferation and support metabolite biosynthesis, independent of environmental fluctuations [<xref rid="B61-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>]. This approach has been validated in numerous medicinal plants, including <italic toggle="yes">Taxus</italic> spp., <italic toggle="yes">Panax</italic> spp., and <italic toggle="yes">Catharanthus roseus</italic>, for the production of taxanes, ginsenosides, and alkaloids, respectively [<xref rid="B62-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>,<xref rid="B63-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>]. Suspension cultures provide high reproducibility and process standardization, while allowing the synthesis of both native and novel compounds via metabolic reprogramming [<xref rid="B63-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>].</p><p>Nevertheless, these systems face certain limitations, such as genetic instability, low metabolite yields, and microbial contamination. Optimization strategies—such as the selection of productive cell lines, modulation of plant growth regulators, elicitor treatments, and bioreactor scaling—have been employed to overcome these challenges. Within the Araliaceae family, <italic toggle="yes">Polyscias fruticosa</italic> has emerged as a promising candidate for cell suspension culture due to its ability to biosynthesize bioactive triterpenoid saponins [<xref rid="B62-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>,<xref rid="B63-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>].</p><p>In an early study, Kim et al. [<xref rid="B25-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>] established a suspension culture from 30 d-old stem-derived callus. Cultures were maintained in MS medium with 30 g·L<sup>−1</sup> sucrose, 1.0 mg·L<sup>−1</sup> BAP, and 0.5 mg·L<sup>−1</sup> 2,4-D under a 12 h/12 h light/dark cycle at 25 ± 2 °C and 120 rpm. After 16 days, dry biomass reached 0.43 g per flask (a 3.7-fold increase), and oleanolic acid accumulated to 25.4 mg·L<sup>−1</sup>, nearly five times higher than in in vitro leaves (5.2 mg/g DW) [<xref rid="B25-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>]. Further experiments by the same group [<xref rid="B26-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>] revealed that sucrose (30 g·L<sup>−1</sup>) outperformed other carbon sources in biomass promotion, while elicitors like yeast extract and silver nitrate induced a shift towards secondary metabolism at the expense of biomass. This suggests that a trade-off exists between growth and metabolite accumulation, which must be optimized depending on the intended application. In comparative studies, the application of malonyl ginsenosides (M-GS) as elicitors in two cell lines—6a (<italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>) and VDK (<italic toggle="yes">P. filicifolia</italic>)—revealed contrasting responses [<xref rid="B27-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>]. The 6a line showed a 79.7% increase in polyscioside E and a 70.7% rise in ladyginoside A, despite a ~45% reduction in biomass. In contrast, the VDK line exhibited lower biomass, but a 63.3% increase in only polyscioside A, highlighting cell-line-specific responses.</p><p>Scalability was demonstrated by Titova et al. [<xref rid="B17-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>], who used a 20 L bubble-type bioreactor operated semi-continuously. Peak biomass reached 6.8 g·L<sup>−1</sup> DW, with productivity of 0.32 g·L<sup>−1</sup>·d<sup>−1</sup>and over 70% cell viability. Saponin content significantly increased: PFS (3-O-[β-D-glucopyranosyl-(1→4)-β-D-glucuronopyranosyl] oleanolic acid 28-O-β-D-glucopyranosyl ester) reached 0.91 mg·L<sup>−1</sup> DW (82% higher than in flask cultures), while ladyginoside A reached 0.77 mg·L<sup>−1</sup> DW (3.5-fold increase). The extracts exhibited antimicrobial activity (MIC = 250–2000 µg·mL<sup>−1</sup>) against <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic>, MRSA, <italic toggle="yes">Pseudomonas aeruginosa</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic>, outperforming leaf-derived extracts (MIC = 4000 µg·mL<sup>−1</sup>). Antioxidant activity was also confirmed via DPPH and TEAC assays, indicating the retention of pharmacologically active compounds under in vitro conditions [<xref rid="B17-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>].</p><p>The suspension culture system of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> provides a biotechnologically robust platform for triterpenoid saponin production, with documented scalability and responsiveness to elicitation. The observed trade-off between growth and metabolite synthesis, alongside cell-line-specific metabolic behavior, underlines the need for tailored optimization. Bioreactor systems, when properly configured, enhance both productivity and phytochemical enrichment, positioning <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> as a viable candidate for pharmaceutical-scale bioproduction.</p></sec></sec><sec id="sec4dot3-molecules-30-03460"><title>4.3.
