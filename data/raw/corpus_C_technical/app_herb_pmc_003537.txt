This study demonstrates that ethnomedicinal plants are not merely healthcare tools, but foundational components of ecological resilience, cultural identity, and community livelihoods.</p></sec><sec id="Sec29"><title>Comparative analysis of medicinal plants from Sheikh district and other east African dryland areas</title><p id="Par213">The Jaccard Similarity Index (JSI) was employed to compare medicinal plant species documented in Sheikh District with those reported from ecologically comparable areas in Eastern Ethiopia and neighboring dryland regions of Eastern Africa (Table <xref rid="Tab14" ref-type="table">14</xref>). In response to the reviewer’s comment, the comparison has been refined to emphasize arid and semi-arid ecosystems and pastoral communities, while reducing emphasis on humid forest regions that are ecologically dissimilar.</p><p id="Par214">High similarity values were recorded with geographically proximate and environmentally comparable dryland areas, including Babile (83.33%), Dire Dawa (65.71%), Jigjiga town (62.86%), Turkana (58.1%), Jigjiga district (58.33%), Dirre (49.12%), Marsabit (48.8%), Tabora region (46.94%), Raya Kobo (42.19%), and Melit area (42.00%) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR32">32</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR39">39</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR47">47</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR56">56</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR57">57</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR66">66</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR75">75</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR103">103</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR106">106</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR128">128</xref>]. These areas are largely characterized by semi-arid to arid climates and savannah vegetation, which likely account for the substantial overlap in medicinal plant composition. The relatively high similarity with Turkana and Marsabit is particularly notable, as these counties form part of the wider East African dryland pastoral belt and share comparable ecological conditions and plant adaptations.</p><p id="Par215">Moderate similarity indices (20–40%) were observed in Yalo (39.73%), North-eastern Tanzania (37.25%), Wakiso (34.62%), Algoz area (29.11%), Randa (27.85%), and the Marakwet Community (27.78%) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR39">39</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR42">42</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR48">48</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR93">93</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR102">102</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR107">107</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR120">120</xref>]. These findings indicate partial floristic overlap influenced by transitional ecological zones and differences in cultural practices. While certain savannah elements are shared, variation in ethnic composition, knowledge transmission systems, and therapeutic preferences likely contributes to the observed divergence.</p><p id="Par216">Low similarity values (&lt; 20%) were documented in Sheka (8.17%), Sekela (9.56%), Nyamira (9.84%), Fofa (10.98%), Menz Keya (11.54%), Andracha (11.48%), Ensaro (12.59–15.79%), Bita (12.60%), Mana Angetu (14.72%), Sodo (14.29%), and Yeki (14.02%) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR24">24</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR33">33</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR35">35</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR44">44</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR81">81</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR87">87</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR101">101</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR146">146</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR172">172</xref>]. Many of these areas are situated in humid highland or forested agro-ecological zones that differ substantially from the dryland ecology of Sheikh District.
