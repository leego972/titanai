Skin care and skin whitening</title><p>Studies have reported the potential for using <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> extract in skin care and wound healing, and that <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> extract modulates skin melanogenesis. <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> extract/ L-carnosine-associated compound (RCAC) treatment for sensitive skin reduced transepidermal water loss in humans, thereby improving skin barrier function [<xref rid="b65-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>]. RCAC treatment also exhibited a positive subjective response in patients with sensitive skin, promoted the release of proopiomelanocortin peptides, and returned to normal levels the increased number of neuropeptides and cytokines produced by keratinocytes exposed to ultraviolet radiation [<xref rid="b65-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola imbricata</italic>-treated wounds healed more rapidly than control group, and the plant extract promoted cellular proliferation and collagen synthesis at the wound site in SD rats [<xref rid="b38-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola imbricata</italic> extract increased DNA, protein, hydroxyproline, and hexosamine expression in granulation cells more than providone–iodine ointment [<xref rid="b38-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>]. The extract also exhibited antioxidant activity and reduced lipid peroxidation. Thus, it exhibits wound healing activity.</p><p><italic toggle="yes">Propionibacterium acnes</italic>, a Gram-positive bacterium, is critical in the pathogenesis of acne vulgaris [<xref rid="b66-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Propionibacterium acnes</italic> is capable of biofilm formation, and the decreased antimicrobial susceptibility of biofilm-associated cells may hamper the effective treatment of <italic toggle="yes">Propionibacterium acnes</italic> infection. <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola crenulata</italic> extract exhibited antibiofilm activity against <italic toggle="yes">Propionibacterium acnes</italic> [<xref rid="b66-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>].</p><p>Melanin is responsible for skin color and plays a major role in the defense against harmful external factors such as ultraviolet irradiation [<xref rid="b67-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>–<xref rid="b70-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>]. Tyrosinase is the rate-limiting step of tyrosine hydroxylation and is responsible for the critical steps of melanogenesis [<xref rid="b70-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>,<xref rid="b71-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>]. The mechanisms of the action of skin hypopigmenting agents are thought to be based on the ability of an agent to inhibit the activity of tyrosinase and, thus, down-regulate melanin synthesis [<xref rid="b70-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>,<xref rid="b72-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>,<xref rid="b73-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>]. Studies have shown that the acetone extract of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> exhibits antityrosinase activity [<xref rid="b74-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>] and that the hydroalcoholic extract of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> extract and its hydrolysate inhibited melanin synthesis and tyrosinase activity in B16F0 cells (mouse melanoma cells) [<xref rid="b75-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> extract also inhibited the gene and protein expression of melanocortin 1 receptor (MC1R), inhibited c-AMP response element binding protein phosphorylation, suppressed the activation of AKT and glycogen synthase kinase-3 beta (GSK3b), and inhibited the expression of microphthalmia-associated transcription factor and tyrosinase-related protein 1 (TRP-1) [<xref rid="b70-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>,<xref rid="b76-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>,<xref rid="b77-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>]. The hydrolysate of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> inhibited the phosphorylation of CREB, activation of AKT and GSK3β, and expression of MITF and tyrosinase.</p><p>In one study, tyrosol and its glycoside, salidroside, exhibited antimelanogenesis activity [<xref rid="b78-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">78</xref>]. Treating B16F0 cells with tyrosol and salidroside reduced melanin content and the inhibition oftyrosinase activity and its expression[<xref rid="b78-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">78</xref>]. Tyrosolsuppressed α-MSH-induced MC1R and TRP-1 expression, but salidroside did not. Neither tyrosol nor salidroside affected MITF or TRP-2 expression. The compounds exhibited metal-coordinating interactions with copper ions in molecular docking with tyrosinase [<xref rid="b78-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">78</xref>].
