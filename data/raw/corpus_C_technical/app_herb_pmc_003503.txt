The ICF for each ailment category was calculated using the following formula:<disp-formula id="Equd"><tex-math id="d33e1037">\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\mathrm{I}\mathrm{C}\mathrm{F}\:=\frac{\mathrm{N}\mathrm{u}\mathrm{r}-\mathrm{N}\mathrm{t}}{\mathrm{N}\mathrm{u}\mathrm{r}-1}$$\end{document}</tex-math></disp-formula></p><p id="Par57">Where: Nur = Number of use-reports for a particular illness category, Nt = Number of species used for that illness category. ICF values range from 0 to 1. Values approaching 1 indicate a high level of agreement among informants, meaning that a limited number of plant species are repeatedly used to treat a specific ailment. Such high consensus suggests strong cultural validation and highlights species that may have higher therapeutic relevance. In contrast, low ICF values indicate low agreement, with informants reporting a wide variety of species for the same ailment, reflecting more individualized or less standardized treatment practices. In this study, ailment categories with high ICF values were interpreted as culturally salient and were used to guide the identification of priority species for further ethnopharmacological evaluation.</p></sec><sec id="Sec7"><title>Jaccard similarity index</title><p id="Par58">The Jaccard Similarity Index (JSI) was applied to measure the overlap in medicinal plant knowledge among different groups or regions. This index quantifies the proportion of species shared between two datasets and is particularly useful for comparing knowledge among traditional healers, elders, general community members, or across distinct geographic areas [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR37">37</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR39">39</xref>]. This metric helps to understand the extent to which traditional medicinal plant knowledge is shared or varies across different communities and ecological settings.</p></sec><sec id="Sec8"><title>Rahman’s similarity index (RSI)</title><p id="Par60">Rahman’s Similarity Index (RSI) was used to evaluate the cultural similarity of medicinal plant use across different study areas.
