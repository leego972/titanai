Likewise, the ethyl acetate root extract of <italic toggle="yes">P. australis</italic> was reported to have antidiabetic activity related to activation of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPARy). Nonetheless, the active principles and the exact mechanism of action are not known [<xref rid="B199-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">199</xref>]. Ong and co-workers (2017) demonstrated the anti-obesity activity of <italic toggle="yes">E. indica</italic>’s methanol extract using obese rats; its properties seem to be due to pancreatic lipase inhibition (27.01 ± 5.68%) [<xref rid="B200-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">200</xref>,<xref rid="B201-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">201</xref>]. Anticarcinogenic Activity</title><p>Cancer has become the second single cause of death, taking over six million lives every year around the world [<xref rid="B203-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">203</xref>]. Recently, an urge to find new anticancer drugs from natural products has become evident [<xref rid="B203-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">203</xref>] and investigations in plants have led to the discovery of many valuable compounds [<xref rid="B203-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">203</xref>].</p><p>The methanol extract of <italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic> roots displayed dose-dependent anticarcinogenic activity (between 25–400 µg/mL) against human cervical cancer cell lines (HeLa), human laryngeal epithelial carcinoma cells (HEp-2), and mouse embryo fibroblast cells (NIH 3T3) [<xref rid="B204-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">204</xref>]. The anticarcinogenic activity of the alkaloid umbelliferone [<xref rid="B147-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">147</xref>] as well as of the flavonols kaempferol [<xref rid="B205-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">205</xref>] and quercetin-3-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-glucoside [<xref rid="B184-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">184</xref>] are well described. These compounds are present in <italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic>’s polar extract, which ultimately could contribute to the reported activity. <italic toggle="yes">C. ciliaris</italic> alcohol and successive extracts of both aerial and root parts were active against lung (A-549), intestinal (CACO), colon (HCT-116), cervical (Hela), hepatocellular (HepG-2) and breast (MCF-7) cancer cell lines with IC<sub>50</sub> values between 11.1 ± 0.3 and 267 ± 0.8 µg/mL [<xref rid="B206-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">206</xref>]. These activities might be related to the presence of lupeol and other sterols known for their anticarcinogenic activities and described on the polar extracts of <italic toggle="yes">C. ciliatis</italic> [<xref rid="B117-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">117</xref>]</p><p>The ethanol extract of <italic toggle="yes">E. crus-galli</italic>’s seeds presented cytotoxic activity against four human cancer cell lines: colon (HCT-116) (IC<sub>50</sub> = 11.2 ± 0.11µg/mL), cervical (HeLa) (IC<sub>50</sub> = 12.0 ± 0.11 µg/mL), liver (HEPG-2) (IC<sub>50</sub> = 14.2 ± 0.11 µg/mL) and breast (MCF-7) (IC<sub>50</sub> = 18.9 ± 0.12 µg/mL) [<xref rid="B90-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">90</xref>].
