Although the polar components of this species are not well known, the activity could be related to ferulic acid [<xref rid="B98-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">98</xref>], present in the polar extracts of <italic toggle="yes">L. multiflorum</italic> [<xref rid="B84-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">84</xref>]</p><p>The ethanol root extract of <italic toggle="yes">S. spontaneum</italic> has the ability to scavenge free radicals (IC<sub>50</sub> 488μg/mL) and therefore act as an antioxidant [<xref rid="B193-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">193</xref>,<xref rid="B194-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">194</xref>], which can also be due to the presence of quercetin [<xref rid="B195-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">195</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Z. aquatica</italic> is claimed to hold antioxidant effects; therefore, Sumczynski and co-workers (2017) identified and evaluated the compounds that ultimately contribute to this property [<xref rid="B92-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">92</xref>]. In their study, the activity was attributed to epigallocatechin, epicatechin, quercetin, and rutin as well as to ferulic acid, sinapic acid and other phenolic acids (<xref ref-type="app" rid="app1-ijms-20-01067">Table S1</xref>) [<xref rid="B92-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">92</xref>].</p><p>Through the data exposed above, it is possible to conclude that eight species of halophytic grasses were studied regarding their anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activities. Isoeugenin was the only compound directly linked to this effect even though the mechanisms of action were only grasped. Concerning antioxidant activity, only one study performed in <italic toggle="yes">Z. aquatica</italic> was able to enlighten the compounds that contribute the most to this effect. These investigations allow us to perceive the immense potential of halophytic grasses to treat and/or prevent inflammatory and oxidative-related disorders.</p></sec><sec id="sec3dot4-ijms-20-01067"><title>3.4. Anti-Diabetic and Anti-Obesity Activities</title><p>Recently, diabetes type 2 has been developing into a worldwide epidemic, mostly due to rapid economic growth and related lifestyle changes in the last 50 years [<xref rid="B196-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">196</xref>]. This disorder is intimately related to obesity. It seems like the fast food culture allied with the sedentary lifestyle are the major causes of obesity, which contributes to insulin resistance and diabetes type 2 [<xref rid="B197-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">197</xref>].</p><p>The methanol fraction of the ethanol extract from <italic toggle="yes">D. aegyptium</italic> (at a dose of 50 mg/kg) decreased both hyperglycemia and ameliorated oxidative stress, which contributed to its antidiabetic activities [<xref rid="B198-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">198</xref>]. These could be related to insulinimimetic and antioxidant effects of the extract [<xref rid="B198-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">198</xref>].
