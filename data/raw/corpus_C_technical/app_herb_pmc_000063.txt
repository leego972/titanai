Anti-Anxiolytic Effect</title><p>The anti-anxiety effect of various fractions of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> was investigated in mice using various behavioral tests, including the open field (emotional state), elevated plus-maze (anxiolytic and anxiogenic drug effects), and anti-fighting test. The <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> anxiolytic effect was similar to that of the positive control diazepam [<xref rid="B166-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">166</xref>].</p><p>In human, effects of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> hot-water extract was screened for stress hormones and mood state in 63 subjects (32 men and 31 women) for moderate stress, with placebo for 4 weeks, and indicates that daily supplementation with <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> extract improves stress hormone profile and certain mood state parameters [<xref rid="B167-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">167</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot7-molecules-21-00331"><title>5.7. Antidiabetic Effect</title><p>Blood glucose decreased in streptozotocin-induced hyperglycemic adult rats after treatment of 150 mg/kg body weight using aqueous extracts of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic>. Blood-glucose levels decreased 38% (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.05) and 47% (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.001) for two different <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> extracts. In normoglycaemic rats, no significant reduction was noted when the same extracts were used [<xref rid="B168-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">168</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">E</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">longifolia</italic> root extract increased insulin sensitivity through the enhancement of glucose uptake by more than 200% at 50 μg/mL and suppressed lipid accumulation in a concentration-dependent manner, suggesting the ability of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> to suppress lipid production would provide additional benefits in the treatment of diabetes [<xref rid="B169-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">169</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot8-molecules-21-00331"><title>5.8. Osteoporosis Preventive Effect</title><p>Osteoporosis in men is attracting more interest as it is becoming one of the main causes of morbidity and mortality in older men. Approximately 2 million men in the United States suffer from osteoporosis [<xref rid="B170-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">170</xref>]. Worldwide, 1 in 3 women over 50 will experience osteoporotic fractures, as will 1 in 5 men [<xref rid="B171-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">171</xref>,<xref rid="B172-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">172</xref>,<xref rid="B173-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">173</xref>]. According to Tambi and Kamarul, <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> contains high concentrations of superoxide dismutase (SOD), an antioxidant that plays an important role in counteracting oxidative stress [<xref rid="B120-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">120</xref>]. Other components of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic>, such as alkaloids and triterpenes, can also act as antioxidants that may reduce bone loss and maintain the bone formation rate [<xref rid="B123-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">123</xref>].</p><p>Recently, it was established that <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> may be used in the prevention and treatment of osteoporosis, or more specifically, male osteoporosis. Shuid <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, showed that both testosterone replacement and <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> supplementation to orchidectomised rats were able to maintain the bone calcium levels, with the former showing better effects, so <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> prevented bone calcium loss in orchidectomised rats and therefore, has the potential to be used as an alternative treatment for androgen deficient osteoporosis [<xref rid="B174-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">174</xref>]. The bioactive complex polypeptides from the <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> root extract, labelled as eurypeptides, can exert and enhance their effects on the biosynthesis of various androgens [<xref rid="B175-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">175</xref>]. Eurypeptides work by stimulating dihydroepiandosterone (DHEA). DHEA in turn will act on androgen receptors to initiate the conversion of androstenedione and androstenediol to testosterone and estrogen, respectively [<xref rid="B125-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">125</xref>].
