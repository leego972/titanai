# PMC: phytochemical bioactive compound anticancer — Overview

Several halophytic grasses are already known to possess antibacterial, antiviral and antifungal activities. For instance, polar extracts of <italic toggle="yes">C. ciliaris</italic> revealed significant antibacterial and antifungal activities against <italic toggle="yes">Proteus merabilis</italic> (MIC 0.234 mg/mL), <italic toggle="yes">Klebsiella pnemoniae</italic> (MIC 1.21 mg/mL) and <italic toggle="yes">Agerobacterium tumefaciens</italic> (MIC 4.24 mg/mL) [<xref rid="B162-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">162</xref>]. Padalia and co-workers [<xref rid="B163-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">163</xref>], through disk diffusion assay, evaluated different extracts of <italic toggle="yes">S. spontaneum</italic>’s aerial parts and concluded that the petroleum ether, ethyl acetate, acetone, and methanol extracts were active against gram-positive bacteria (inhibition zones of 10.0, 11.0, 12.5 and 10.0 mm, respectively) [<xref rid="B163-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">163</xref>]. Some of the compounds found in <italic toggle="yes">C. ciliaris</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">S. spontaneum</italic> are well known for their antimicrobial activity; however, its non-polar nature [<xref rid="B164-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">164</xref>] prevent their presence in polar extracts. This allowed us to conclude that only cholest-22-ene-21-ol could be present in the polar extract of <italic toggle="yes">C. ciliaris</italic> and thus, be responsible for its activity [<xref rid="B56-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>]. Therefore, more studies need to be conducted in order to identify the responsible compounds for the reported effects. </p><p>The essential oils of <italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic> also exhibited antibacterial activity against <italic toggle="yes">S. aureus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus epidermis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">E. coli</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">P. aeruginosa</italic> (MIC &gt; 4 µg/mL) [<xref rid="B160-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">160</xref>], while its ethanol extract inhibited <italic toggle="yes">K. pneumonae</italic> NCIM 2957 (MIC 0.977 mg/mL), <italic toggle="yes">E. coli</italic> NCIM 2931 (MIC 31.25 mg/mL), <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus cereus</italic> NCIM 2458 (MIC 31.25 mg/mL), <italic toggle="yes">Salmonella typhimurium</italic> NCIM 2501 (MIC 62.5 mg/mL) and <italic toggle="yes">Proteus vulgaris</italic> NCIM 2857 (MIC 62.5 mg/mL) [<xref rid="B165-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">165</xref>]. In addition, the n-butanol extract of this species showed antibacterial activity against <italic toggle="yes">Helicobacter pylori</italic> with a MIC value of 6.25 mg/mL [<xref rid="B166-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">166</xref>]. Kaempferol is reported to be present in the polar extracts of <italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic> [<xref rid="B53-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>] and their antimicrobial activity is widely recognized [<xref rid="B167-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">167</xref>], which could be a clue for the compound(s) responsible for the activity of this extract. <italic toggle="yes">E. crus-galli</italic>’s methanol extract (1% acidified) also displayed antibacterial activity against <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus megaterium</italic> MTCC-428 (inhibition zone of 12 mm), <italic toggle="yes">E. coli</italic> MTCC 443 (inhibition zone of 16 mm) and <italic toggle="yes">P. aeruginosa</italic> MTCC1688 (inhibition zone of 13 mm). In contrast, its ethyl acetate extract inhibited <italic toggle="yes">S. aureus</italic> MTCC96 (inhibition zone of 14 mm) [<xref rid="B168-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">168</xref>]. This activity could be related to <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-coumaric acid and ferulic acid, both well-known antimicrobial agents [<xref rid="B98-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">98</xref>,<xref rid="B169-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">169</xref>] and present in the ethanol extract of <italic toggle="yes">E. crus-galli</italic> [<xref rid="B90-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">90</xref>].</p><p>The methanol extracts of <italic toggle="yes">P. karka</italic> showed activity against <italic toggle="yes">Actinobacter</italic> sp.
