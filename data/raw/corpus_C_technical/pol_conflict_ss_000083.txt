Title: Historical narratives as a political tool of russian information operations against Ukraine

Abstract:
This article offers a comprehensive analysis of scholarly approaches to historical policy, collective memory, and information-psychological influence, framing historical narratives as a structural component of the strategic communications of an aggressor state. It argues that, in the context of the ongoing russian-Ukrainian war, the use of historical narratives as an instrument of information aggression and as an element of hybrid warfare has become particularly significant. 
The study demonstrates that, in russian contemporary information strategy, historical narratives no longer serve as a purely academic field. Instead, it is transformed into a political tool used to justify foreign policy decisions, support military expansion, and promote an alternative interpretation of the past. The article examines key ideologemes in russian information discourse, most notably the concepts of a “common history,” a “one people,” the “russian world” (russkiy mir), and “Novorossiya.” These concepts are employed to legitimize geopolitical claims, undermine Ukrainian statehood, and erode Ukrainian national identity. 
The article highlights that historical myths in russian propaganda serve as symbolic weapons, aiming to influence audiences emotionally and morally, simplify complex historical processes, and shape cognitive frameworks that normalize and legitimize aggressive policies. It also demonstrates that historical argumentation is systematically embedded in information-psychological operations, combining elements of propaganda, memory politics, manipulative techniques, and strategic communications. 
The study demonstrates that historical discourse is used to normalize aggression, shape the image of the enemy, and influence international perceptions of the russian-Ukrainian war. The findings indicate that, within russia’s information aggression, historical narratives operate as a systemic mechanism of influence aimed at undermining Ukraine’s sovereignty in the realms of meaning-making and collective memory. Accordingly, countering historical manipulation requires the development of a coherent state memory policy, the advancement of historical education, the strengthening of strategic communications, support for scholarly historiography, and the reinforcement of information security as a core component of national security.
