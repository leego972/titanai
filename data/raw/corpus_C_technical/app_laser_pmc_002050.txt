2024</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><sec id="Sec1"><title>Introduction</title><p id="Par2">Since the 1960’s, photobiomodulation (PBM) therapy has been used in wound healing [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR1">1</xref>]. This process involves exposing cells and tissue to low levels of light, as compared to the higher energy densities of traditional photothermolysis based laser therapy. In the last several years, major improvements have been made to light emitting diodes (LEDs), and they have begun to replace lasers as a light source for PBM therapy [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR2">2</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR5">5</xref>].</p><p id="Par3">Studies utilizing PBM therapy from 600 to 1072 nm in vitro and in animal models have demonstrated a positive effect on wound healing [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR3">3</xref>]. In humans, normal wound or tissue healing is a sequential process with three phases: inflammatory, proliferative, and matrix remodeling [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR6">6</xref>]. Potential biological effects of PBM have been proposed for all three phases of the wound healing process [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR7">7</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">9</xref>]. Various mechanisms have been proposed including metabolic effects of light therapy on tissue repair and faster resolution of the inflammatory process [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR8">8</xref>]. An in-vitro study by Hawkins et al. demonstrated lower light doses of PBM therapy (2.5 J/cm<sup>2</sup>) led to changes in the respiratory chain in the mitochondria and enhanced DNA and RNA synthesis leading to cell proliferation [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR10">10</xref>]. PBM has also been shown to induce fibroblast proliferation and collagen synthesis aiding the proliferative stage of healing [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR8">8</xref>]. PBM therapy can significantly increase the synthesis of fibroblast growth factor (FGF), which contributes to collagen synthesis [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR8">8</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR10">10</xref>]. Hopkins et al. demonstrated PBM therapy treated wounds showed earlier contraction and subsequent healing. [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR11">11</xref>]. Other studies demonstrated that PBM therapy can induce mast cell degranulation, which through the release of proinflammatory granules, can assist in collagen synthesis [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>]. In addition, PBM therapy has been demonstrated to enhance local blood flow and lymphatic drainage, which can accelerate wound healing by delivering oxygen to the tissues and clearing away waste through the lymphatic system [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR13">13</xref>].</p><p id="Par4">There may be different effects on wound healing for different wavelengths.
