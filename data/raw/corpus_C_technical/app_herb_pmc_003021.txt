Interleukin (IL)−6 is pleiotropic and plays a multifaceted role in mediating host defence, immunity, disease pathogenesis, and inflammation [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR6">6</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR15">15</xref>]. Additionally, IL-8, belonging to the CXC family of chemokines, attracts and activates neutrophils at infection sites, initiating the pathogenic cascade seen in IBD [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR23">23</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">D. lanceolata var. subsessilifolia</italic> extract also significantly suppressed IL-1β in one of the PBMC donors (Fig. <xref rid="Fig5" ref-type="fig">5</xref>E).</p></sec></sec><sec id="Sec18"><title>Conclusion</title><p id="Par39">Globally, medicinal plants and traditional medicine remain important in managing and preventing many chronic diseases. Indigenous Australians have vast ethnomedical plant knowledge and have been using it for millennia. In this study, we have evaluated five medicinal plants used by the Mbabaram Aboriginal community in Atherton Tableland in Far-North Queensland for their phytochemical contents, antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties.</p><p id="Par40">While all five species tested positive for flavonoids, <italic toggle="yes">B. oblongifolia</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Coleus amoenus</italic> did not contain saponins, and only <italic toggle="yes">Cajanus reticulatus</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">E. latifolius</italic> tested positive for alkaloids. The highest TPC was detected in the ethanol extracts of <italic toggle="yes">B. oblongifolia</italic> leaf and the aqueous extracts of <italic toggle="yes">C. amoenus</italic> root extract. Interestingly, <italic toggle="yes">E. latifolius</italic> stem extract showed the best antioxidant (DPPH-free radical scavenging) activity with EC<sub>50</sub> value of 0.024 μg/mL, which is even better than gallic acid (0.634 μg/mL) – a standard used for the assay.</p><p id="Par41">Of the five aqueous crude extracts tested, the <italic toggle="yes">B. oblongifolia</italic> leaf extract showed the best suppression of four pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF), which are known for instigating IBD pathogenesis. This result validated the traditional uses of medicinal plants, which is used for treating inflammation-related conditions including wounds and sores. Since this medicinal plant supported strong cell viability and its crude extract consistently showed good suppression of cytokines, we have identified this plant as a candidate for further investigation, including isolation, characterisation and anti-inflammatory testings of pure compounds.
