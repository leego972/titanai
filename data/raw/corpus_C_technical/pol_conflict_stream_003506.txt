# Coadou-Fleury

Coadou-Fleury, also known as Coadou et Fleury, was an automobile manufacturer based in Trébeurden, France, that operated from 1921 to 1934.

History 

In 1921, a pilot, Marcel Coadou, founded the automobile manufacturing company of Coadou-Fleury, based in Trébeurden, France. Its vehicles were manufactured in Paris, France.

The first vehicle manufactured by the company was a cyclecar. It had a Ruby sidevalve V4 engine with an engine displacement of either 850 cm³ or 903 cm³, and with 20 horsepower drive. The vehicle had a monocoque body. It had failed to attract customers, due to its high price.

In 1927, a further developed version of the Citroën Type B2 overhead valve engine was used, with the 30 horsepower. To vehicle was also equipped with four-wheel brakes. Later, the coupe version was also manufactured.

The last model that was made by the company was a Coadou-Fleury Aérolithe, which had an aerodynamic body. One unit had been preserved to the present day.

The company was dissolved in 1934.
