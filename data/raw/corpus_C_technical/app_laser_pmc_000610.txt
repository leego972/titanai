2021</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><sec id="Sec1"><title>Introduction</title><p id="Par3">With the increasing number incidence of fatal diseases like cancer, there is a growing need for precise diagnosis and efficient therapy [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR1">1</xref>]. Therefore, nanomedicines have been proposed to use nanotechnology to endow both imaging and therapeutic capabilities to achieve cancer theranostics [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR2">2</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR6">6</xref>]. Although a range of nanomaterials have been developed for biomedical applications, persistent luminescence nanoparticles (PLNPs) as unique optical materials have attracted extensive attention for excitation-free optical imaging and cancer therapy due to persistent luminescence (PersL) property and nanocarrier structures [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR7">7</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR8">8</xref>]. PLNPs can store part of the excitation energy and then release the photonic emission for an appreciable time after ceasing excitation. Such continuous luminescence phenomenon is called PersL or afterglow luminescence, which is achieved by forming defects by doping emitter ions in a specific host, and capturing and releasing electrons through these defects [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">9</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR10">10</xref>]. By tuning the host and emitter, it is possible to obtain PLNPs with different emission wavelengths from UV to near-infrared (NIR) region [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">9</xref>].</p><p id="Par4">In recent years, PLNPs exhibit outstanding strengths in the field of tumor diagnosis and treatment. Firstly, compared to traditional fluorescent agents (such as semiconductor quantum dots [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR11">11</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>], upconversion nanoparticles [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR13">13</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>], organic dyes [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR15">15</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR16">16</xref>]), optical imaging with PLNPs can be freed from real-time excitation by external light sources, thus completely avoiding interference of autofluorescence and enabling high-sensitive in vivo imaging.
