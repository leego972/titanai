Miscellaneous Effects</title><sec id="sec5dot9dot1-molecules-21-00331"><title>5.9.1. Hormonal Effects</title><p>A standardized extract of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> Jack containing a high concentration of quassinoids (20% eurycomanone and 4% 13α,21-dihydroeurycomanone) had antiestrogenic effects against 17α-ethynylestradiol (EE)-induced uterotrophy of immature rats [<xref rid="B86-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>]. Another study showed that the <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> plant extract normalized irregular estrous cycles and reduced the follicular morphological damage caused by chronic testosterone administration in the female rats. The reversal effect derived from the anti-estrogenic properties of the plant quassinoids. Further work is required to identify the exact mechanism behind the ameliorative effects of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> [<xref rid="B183-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">183</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot9dot2-molecules-21-00331"><title>5.9.2. Ergogenic Effects</title><p>The ergogenic effects of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> were discussed in a review [<xref rid="B184-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">184</xref>]. The authors reviewed its medicinal properties and studies investigating physiological responses and endurance exercise performance. Increased testosterone, as shown in animal models [<xref rid="B115-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">115</xref>], has been suggested in anecdotal reports as being responsible for <italic toggle="yes">E.</italic>
<italic toggle="yes">longifolia</italic>-induced increases in muscle mass and strength in humans. According to secondary sources, <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> enhances testosterone production by the Leydig cells and frees bound testosterone for use by muscles [<xref rid="B185-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">185</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot9dot3-molecules-21-00331"><title>5.9.3. Insecticidal Effects</title><p><italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic>-containing smoke from mosquito coils resulted in increased knock-down activities of mosquitos, but not increased mortality [<xref rid="B186-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">186</xref>]. One study showed that <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> exhibits the highest anti-protozoal activity at 1.0 mg/mL. The ethyl acetate fraction exhibited a slightly higher percentage of anti-protozoal activity and demonstrated the highest anti-protozoal activity against <italic toggle="yes">Blastocystis</italic> sp. isolates and showed a sizeable reduction in the cell count in comparison to the allopathic drugs [<xref rid="B187-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">187</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot9dot4-molecules-21-00331"><title>5.9.4. Muscular Effects</title><p>In animal research, <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> extracts increased weight of the levator ani muscle (involved in tail wagging) in castrated animals, but not testosterone-treated animals and uncastrated animals [<xref rid="B188-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">188</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot9dot5-molecules-21-00331"><title>5.9.5. Antiulcer Effect</title><p>A bioassay study of <italic toggle="yes">Pasak bumi (E. longifolia)</italic> led to the isolation of four quassinoids, pasakbumin-A, -B, -C, and -D. Both pasakbumin-A (eurycomanone) and pasakbumin-B exhibited potent antiulcer activity [<xref rid="B50-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>]. In one other study, Qodriyah <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, investigation showed that <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> in Radix is as effective as ranitidine in the treatment of ethanol-induced gastric lesions in rats [<xref rid="B189-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">189</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot9dot6-molecules-21-00331"><title>5.9.6.
