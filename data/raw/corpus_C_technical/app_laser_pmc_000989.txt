position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="423_2025_3810_Fig4_HTML.jpg"/></fig> </p><p id="Par18">Figure <xref rid="Fig1" ref-type="fig">1</xref> shows conventional white adipose tissue in proximity to the pancreas. Macroscopically the sample impresses with a homogeneous, shiny, yellow surface with narrow fibrous septa in between (Fig. <xref rid="Fig1" ref-type="fig">1</xref>a/d). Large, homogenous spherical cells with a thin, crescent-shaped nucleus that is squeezed and pushed to the cell’s periphery by lipid droplets are the microscopic hallmarks of adipose tissue. A portion stained with standard HE shows distinct adipocytes. There are tiny membranes between cells (Fig. <xref rid="Fig1" ref-type="fig">1</xref>e). OCT reproduces the architecture of fat cells in detail. This clearly shows the homogeneous round structures with the narrow membranes between the fat cells (Fig. b/c) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>]. Additionally, OCT allows for visualization of the vascular system within the adipose tissue and even shows the content of bigger vessels.</p><p id="Par19">Figure <xref rid="Fig2" ref-type="fig">2</xref> represents an IPMN. Macroscopic impressions show an erased smooth surface, which is rough to touch (Fig. <xref rid="Fig2" ref-type="fig">2</xref>a/d). The intraductal proliferation of columnar mucin-producing cells, which can be of flat or papillary form. These range in size from microscopic folds to clearly apparent projections, what microscopically characterizes IPMNs. IPMNs are categorized as low-grade or high-grade depending on the epitheliums greatest level of cytoarchitectural atypia. Mild to moderate atypia is the hallmark of low-grade IPMNs, which may or may not exhibit papillary projections and mitoses (Fig. <xref rid="Fig1" ref-type="fig">1</xref>c/e). For comparison, severe atypia, papillae with irregular branching and budding, nuclear stratification with loss of polarity, pleomorphism, and conspicuous nucleoli are characteristics of high-grade IPMNs with many mitoses. Furthermore, OCT images replicate the columnar mucin-producing cells in their papillary projection (Fig. <xref rid="Fig2" ref-type="fig">2</xref>b/c) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>].</p><p id="Par20">Figure <xref rid="Fig3" ref-type="fig">3</xref> shows a moderately differentiated (G2) PDAC based on an IPMN. Macroscopic impressions are similar to the IPMN (Fig. <xref rid="Fig2" ref-type="fig">2</xref>a/d) with an erased smooth surface, which is rough to touch (Fig. <xref rid="Fig3" ref-type="fig">3</xref>a/d). However, microscopic architecture of the sample and the correlating OCT-image show similar characteristics. The majority of ductal adenocarcinomas are well to moderately differentiated, forming glandular structures resembling ducts that randomly invade the parenchyma of the pancreas and induce a potent desmoplastic stromal response (differentiation to IPMN, Fig. <xref rid="Fig2" ref-type="fig">2</xref>). The duct-like structures in well-differentiated carcinomas can be difficult to identify as neoplastic glands because they appear next to glands with characteristics more indicative of adenocarcinoma, such as angularly contoured ducts, branching, ruptured glands, and/or multilayered papillary epithelium with cribriform patterns. The most distinctive feature are burst glands that have cellular stroma partially lining them and mucus leaking out of them (Fig. <xref rid="Fig3" ref-type="fig">3</xref>c/e). OCT can visualize the densely elevated brighter desmoplastic stroma in particular. In addition, the darker islets can be distinguished from glandular structures that resemble ducts (Fig. 3b/c) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>]</p><p id="Par21">An intermediately differentiated (G2) pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor (pNET) is illustrated in Fig. <xref rid="Fig4" ref-type="fig">4</xref>. Again, macroscopic impressions are similar to the IPMN and PDAC (Figs. <xref rid="Fig2" ref-type="fig">2</xref> and <xref rid="Fig3" ref-type="fig">3</xref> a/d) with an erased smooth surface, which is rough to touch (Fig. <xref rid="Fig4" ref-type="fig">4</xref>a/d). pNET (G2) has intermediate microscopic features, consisting of cells with mild atypia, organoid patterns, and no necrosis. Trabecular architecture, displaying an intermediate level of differentiation.
