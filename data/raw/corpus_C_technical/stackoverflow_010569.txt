Question: <p>Is there a way to conveniently define a C-like structure in Python? I'm tired of writing stuff like:</p>

<pre><code>class MyStruct():
    def __init__(self, field1, field2, field3):
        self.field1 = field1
        self.field2 = field2
        self.field3 = field3
</code></pre>

Answer: <p>Whenever I need an "instant data object that also behaves like a dictionary" (I <em>don't</em> think of C structs!), I think of this cute hack:</p>

<pre><code>class Map(dict):
    def __init__(self, **kwargs):
        super(Map, self).__init__(**kwargs)
        self.__dict__ = self
</code></pre>

<p>Now you can just say:</p>

<pre><code>struct = Map(field1='foo', field2='bar', field3=42)

self.assertEquals('bar', struct.field2)
self.assertEquals(42, struct['field3'])
</code></pre>

<p>Perfectly handy for those times when you need a "data bag that's NOT a class", and for when namedtuples are incomprehensible...</p>
