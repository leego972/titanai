We checked and updated the species scientific plant names by using The Plant List [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR42">42</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="Sec5"><title>Data analysis</title><p id="Par16">We organized the following information for each recorded specimen in an Excel sheet: family, genus, species and collection number, vernacular name, language, plant part sold, status in Brazil (native or introduced). We verified the spelling of the names, first with the help of the vendors in the market, and later by using Corriente [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR43">43</xref>], Navarro [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR44">44</xref>], DATAPLAMT [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR45">45</xref>], Dicionário ilustrado Tupi Guarani [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR46">46</xref>], and Michaelis Dicionário Brasileiro da Língua Portuguesa [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR47">47</xref>]. The Tupi and Portuguese dictionaries, along with the Online Etymology Dictionary [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR48">48</xref>], Corriente [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR43">43</xref>], Sebba and Corbacho [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR49">49</xref>], and Fernandes and Soares [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR50">50</xref>] were used to determine the etymology of the vernacular plant names. Names with Latin, Greek, Phoenician (in the case of Malaga), and Arabic etymologies were listed as Portuguese, as these words were implemented into Portuguese language prior to entering Brazilian Portuguese vocabulary [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR24">24</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR51">51</xref>].</p><p id="Par17">To check the phytogeographical distribution, its status in Brazil, and the origin of each plant species, we used the Tropical Plant Database [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR52">52</xref>], Missouri Botanical Garden Plant Finder [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR53">53</xref>], GBIF [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR31">31</xref>], PROTA4A [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR54">54</xref>], PROSEA [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR55">55</xref>], Van Andel et al. [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR56">56</xref>], the Flora do Brasil 2020 online checklist [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR32">32</xref>], the Flora of China [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR57">57</xref>], Kew Science Plants of the World online [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR58">58</xref>], the Catalogue of Life Annual Checklist [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR59">59</xref>], Tropicos [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR33">33</xref>], the Naturalis Bioportal [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR60">60</xref>], and Herbarium voucher labels at MG. To compare our results to the VOP ethnobotanical inventory in the 1980s, we updated the taxonomy of the plant species found at the medicinal and religious plant stalls by Van den Berg [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR1">1</xref>] and checked if they were native or introduced. Comparing detailed medicinal or ritual uses of plants between 1984 and 2018 was not part of our research aims.</p></sec><sec id="Sec6"><title>Data sharing</title><p id="Par18">This study was based on the participation of local specialist vendors. Although we compensated them financially by buying their plants, we also discussed our research results with them and followed up their request to provide them with a complete list of the common and scientific names of the identified plants, following Del Arco et al. [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR61">61</xref>]. We also included pictures of some pressed and dried vouchers, our contact information, and the MG location. We decided that this was the best option due to time constraints preventing us from organizing workshops or other benefit-sharing activities.</p></sec></sec><sec id="Sec7"><title>Results</title><sec id="Sec8"><title>Inventory of medicinal and ritual plants at the VOP in 2018</title><p id="Par19">We recorded 155 plant specimens with in total 165 corresponding vernacular names and collected 146 specimens from the medicinal stalls of the VOP (Table <xref rid="Tab1" ref-type="table">1</xref>).
