However, the results indicate that exposing the NIR laser beam to H<sub>2</sub>Ti<sub>3</sub>O<sub>7</sub> NTs and Au-H<sub>2</sub>Ti<sub>3</sub>O<sub>7</sub> NTs-embedded LNCap cells does not cause successful treatment, and even dark conditions cause better results compared to NIR irradiation conditions. Therefore, in the next step, the effects of Nd: YAG laser irradiation at 532 nm with a fluence of 450 J/cm<sup>2</sup> are investigated in vitro on Au-H<sub>2</sub>Ti<sub>3</sub>O<sub>7</sub> NTs-embedded LNCap cells to achieve better treatment conditions for prostate cancer cells. The research was based on varying concentrations of Au-H<sub>2</sub>Ti<sub>3</sub>O<sub>7</sub> nanotubes integrated into LNCap cells and exposure to green laser beams in 24- and 48-hour incubation at 37 °C. The treatment results were recorded with two different concentrations of NTs (62.5 µg/ml and 500 µg/ml) and two different incubation times (24 and 48 h) and optical inverted microscope images of prostate cancer (LNCap cells) including irradiated Au-H<sub>2</sub>Ti<sub>3</sub>O<sub>7</sub> NTs by green laser are shown in Fig. <xref rid="Fig13" ref-type="fig">13</xref>. Also, an optical inverted microscope image of the control group cells is presented in Fig. <xref rid="Fig13" ref-type="fig">13</xref>e to compare.
