Therefore, a procedure needs to be established for the assessment of ovarian reserve in ovarian tissue.</p><p id="Par7">Optical coherence tomography (OCT) is a non-invasive imaging technique that provides high-resolution images for a range of clinical applications [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR13">13</xref>]. By measuring back-scattered light from microstructural features in tissues, OCT images include micron-scale detail to a penetration depth of 1–3 mm [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>]. OCT has recently been used for the imaging of tissues for clinical examination of areas that can be accessed directly or by using an endoscope or catheter. OCT is used in such fields as ophthalmology [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR15">15</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR16">16</xref>] and dentistry [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR17">17</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR18">18</xref>], and to evaluate such areas of the body as the gastrointestinal tract [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR19">19</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR20">20</xref>], coronary blood vessels [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR21">21</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR22">22</xref>], colon [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR23">23</xref>], breast [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR24">24</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR25">25</xref>], and more [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>]. Some researchers have been investigating the application of OCT in evaluating the anatomical features of normal ovarian tissue [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR13">13</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR26">26</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>]. In particular, a study has been reported on the clinical application of OCT for the detection of malignant cells and to search for primordial follicles in formalin-fixed and embedded ovarian tissue for fertility preservation [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR30">30</xref>]. In addition, researchers have recently reported a study using mice ovaries to investigate the possibility of using OCT to assess ovarian reserve [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR31">31</xref>].</p><p id="Par8">The present study investigated the possibility of using OCT to assess the localization of primordial follicles and ovarian reserve in ovarian tissue, using non-fixed human ovarian tissue that had been cryopreserved for fertility preservation due to POI and impending treatment of hematological disease and cancer.</p></sec><sec id="Sec2"><title>Material and methods</title><sec id="Sec3"><title>Preparation of human ovarian tissue</title><p id="Par9">Human ovaries were obtained from 4 patients who underwent ovariectomy for ovarian tissue cryopreservation between November 2013 and February 2017 for fertility preservation or fertility treatment at our university hospital. Ovarian tissue from Patients 1 and 2 was cryopreserved-thawed by vitrification methods using a commercial ovarian cryopreservation kit (Ova Cryo Kit Type M; KITAZATO Biopharma Co., Ltd., Shizuoka, Japan), as previously reported [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR7">7</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR8">8</xref>]. The ovarian tissue from Patients 3 and 4 was fresh. A small (4–8 mm × 2 mm) 1-mm-thick section of ovarian cortex was cut out from part of each ovary. This process was performed in a petri dish (Bacteriological Petri Dish 60 × 15 mm, FALCON 351007; Becton, Dickinson and Company, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA) containing modified HTF (mHTF) culture medium (with HEPES; KITAZATO Biopharma Co., Ltd.). For the present study, in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, all patients provided written, informed consent, and the study was approved by the institutional review board of our university (institutional approval No. 3311, UMIN000023141).</p></sec><sec id="Sec4"><title>Optical coherence tomography examination</title><p id="Par10">The OCT system (Light-CT scanner; LL Tech, Paris, France) was used in the present study. The light source of this equipment is a halogen lamp. The mechanical characteristics of the Light-CT scanner included images with 1-μm resolution in all three dimensions that can be manipulated using a standard DICOM viewer. The wavelength of the OCT examination was 700–800 nm (near-infrared), the width of the wave was 125 nm, and the area of examination was 0.64 mm<sup>2</sup>. Images were obtained to a depth of 100 μm, which was the maximum depth at which high-resolution images could be taken. The mean exposure time during the OCT examination was approximately 2–3 min for each 0.64-mm<sup>2</sup> section. The recorded images were modified using a Gaussian filter to reduce the speckle noise, and then contrast and brightness were adjusted to clarify the follicle boundaries.
