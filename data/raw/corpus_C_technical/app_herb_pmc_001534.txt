A significant association was observed between the antioxidant activity, quantified in mg 100 g<sup>−1</sup> DW Trolox equivalents, and the total polyphenol content, with variations dependent on fermentation length [<xref rid="B76-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>].</p><p>Ecotypes of <italic toggle="yes">C. angustifolium</italic> cultivated in Lithuania showed notable differences in radical scavenging capacity, according to Vilma Kaškonienė et al. [<xref rid="B23-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>]. Key bioactive components, such as rutin, caffeic acid, 3,4-dihydroxybenzoic acid, oenothein B, and chlorogenic acid, were found in their study, which also demonstrated a radical scavenging activity ranging from 110.9 to 174.2 mg/g in analyzed samples. These polyphenolic compounds are vital for scavenging free radicals, reducing oxidative stress, and protecting cells from damage due to their potent antioxidant properties. The potent antioxidant capacity of these extracts increases the likelihood of many health benefits, including anti-inflammatory and disease-preventive qualities against oxidative stress [<xref rid="B3-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B107-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">107</xref>]. The greatest radical scavenging activity was demonstrated by oenothein B in an HPLC-DPPH assay [<xref rid="B23-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>].</p><p>Maruška et al. evaluated the seasonal radical scavenging activity of <italic toggle="yes">C. angustifolium</italic> in relation to its flavonoid content [<xref rid="B66-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>,<xref rid="B82-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">82</xref>]. Leaves harvested at the peak of the blooming phase exhibited the highest flavonoid concentrations (8.71–11.12 mg per 100 g D.M.) and radical scavenging activity [<xref rid="B66-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>]. The antioxidant capacity of <italic toggle="yes">C. angustifolium</italic> leaves has been thoroughly investigated, with applications in food preservation and pharmaceutical development examined [<xref rid="B108-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>,<xref rid="B109-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">109</xref>].</p><p>The antioxidant potential of <italic toggle="yes">C. latifolium</italic> extracts was evaluated usingDPPH radical scavenging and FRAP tests, with both demonstrating significant antioxidant activity. The ethanol extract showed superior activity, with low IC<sub>50</sub> values of 21.31 ± 0.65 μg/mL (DPPH) and 18.13 ± 0.15 μg/mL (FRAP), underscoring its potential as a natural antioxidant source [<xref rid="B9-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>].</p></sec><sec><title>Antimicrobial Activities</title><p>Antimicrobial resistance poses a serious global health threat, driving the search for novel therapeutic alternatives [<xref rid="B110-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>]. Increasingly, researchers are turning to plant-derived natural compounds due to their diverse bioactive properties and unique mechanisms of action [<xref rid="B111-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">111</xref>,<xref rid="B112-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>,<xref rid="B113-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>,<xref rid="B114-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>]. Medicinal plants produce a wide range of secondary metabolites, including alkaloids, terpenoids, and phenolic compounds, which have demonstrated potentc antimicrobial activity [<xref rid="B115-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">115</xref>]. The antibacterial properties of <italic toggle="yes">C. latifolium</italic> extracts have also been assessed using the disc diffusion method against Gram-positive (<italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus cereus</italic>), Gram-negative (<italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Klebsiella pneumoniae</italic>), and fungal (<italic toggle="yes">Candida albicans</italic>) pathogens. Ethanol extracts exhibited broad activity, with inhibition zone diameters (IZD) ranging from 8.53 to 14.27 mm, whereas ethyl acetate extracts were ineffective against bacteria but inhibited <italic toggle="yes">Candida albicans</italic> (IZD: 8.58 mm) [<xref rid="B9-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. The antimicrobial effects of plant-derived compounds are often linked to their ability to disrupt cellular membranes, inhibit enzymatic activity, and interfere with essential cellular processes [<xref rid="B116-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">116</xref>]. Additionally, the synergistic interactions between phytochemicals in plant extracts enhance antimicrobial potency and may reduce the risk of resistance development.
