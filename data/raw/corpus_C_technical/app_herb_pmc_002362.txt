The taxonomic identification of the plant samples was carried out by Prof. Dr. Selami Selvi, a faculty member of the Medicinal and Aromatic Plants Program at Balıkesir University, Altınoluk Vocational School. The voucher specimen number is SV 3455, and it has been deposited in the Balıkesir University Herbarium.</p><p id="Par34">Five grams of plant material were weighed and added to 100 mL of an ethanol/water solution (70:30 v/v). The mixture was homogenized at 5000 rpm x g for 5 min. Subsequently, the cyclodextrin complex was prepared in the ratio 1:1. And 25 mg of cyclodextrin and 25 mg of plant extract were accurately weighed to achieve a final concentration of 1 mg/mL. These were mixed in 50 mL of water and stirred for 24 h in the dark to protect from light-induced degradation. The resulting samples remained in liquid form and were used within three days of preparation, without undergoing any freezing process.</p></sec><sec id="Sec4"><title>Chemicals</title><p id="Par35">The chemicals were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (Darmstadt, Germany) and included: 2,2’-azino-bis(3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulphonic acid (ABTS), 1,1-diphenyl-2-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH), gallic acid, electric eel acetylcholinesterase (AChE) (type-VI-S, EC 3.1.1.7), horse serum butyrylcholinesterase (BChE) (EC 3.1.1.8), galantamine, acetylthiocholine iodide (ATChI), butyrylthiocholine chloride (BTChI), 5,5-dithio-bis(2-nitrobenzoic) acid (DTNB), tyrosinase (EC 1.14.18.1, from mushroom), α-glucosidase (EC 3.2.1.20, from <italic toggle="yes">Saccharomyces cerevisiae</italic>), α-amylase (EC 3.2.1.1, from porcine pancreas), α-cyclodextrin (CAS:10016-20-3), β-cyclodextrin (CAS:7585-39-9, sodium carbonate, Folin-Ciocalteu reagent, hydrochloric acid, sodium hydroxide, trolox, ethylenediaminetetraacetate (EDTA), neocuproine, cupric chloride, ammonium acetate, ferric chloride, 2,4,6-Tris(2-pyridyl)-s-triazine (TPTZ), ammonium molybdate, ferrozine, ferrous sulphate hexahydrate, kojic acid, and acarbose. All chemicals were of analytical grade.</p></sec><sec id="Sec5"><title>HPLC analyses</title><p id="Par36">We conducted detailed chemical analyses to characterize the composition of the pure extract. The measurements were performed with an Agilent 1260 chromatograph equipped with 1260 diode array (DAD)<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR21">21</xref></sup>.</p><p id="Par37">The experimental procedure has been described in our previous study<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR22">22</xref></sup>. Antioxidant activity was evaluated using ABTS, DPPH, FRAP, and CUPRAC<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR22">22</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR25">25</xref></sup> assays, providing complementary insights into radical scavenging and reducing power. Detailed procedure and instrumental specifications, including the thermostable column cabinet, autosampler, secondary syringe pump, and UV-DAD detector configuration, are provided in the supplementary material.</p></sec><sec id="Sec6"><title>Simulated digestion model for human bioavailability testing</title><p id="Par38">The gastrointestinal digestion simulation was carried out following a modified protocol described previously<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR20">20</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR26">26</xref></sup>. To mimic the gastric environment, 2.5 mL of the sample solution was mixed with 17.5 mL of simulated gastric fluid (SGF), containing pepsin enzyme and appropriate electrolytes dissolved in water at pH 2. The mixture was then placed in a shaking water bath at 37 °C for 2 h to mimic peristaltic movement. After this stage, samples were immediately cooled in an ice bath to halt enzyme activity, and a 2 mL aliquot was taken as the “post-gastric” (PG) sample. The simulated digestion model is shown Fig. <xref rid="Fig1" ref-type="fig">1</xref>.</p><p id="Par39">
