Every part of the plant especially the leaves and seeds have been recognized to contain a variety of bioactive compounds, including vitamins, carotenoids, polyphenols and flavonoids (<xref rid="ref-9" ref-type="bibr">Azlan et al., 2022</xref>). These compounds greatly enhance the potential of <italic toggle="yes">M. oleifera</italic> as a nutritional supplement, nutraceutical, and pharmaceutical product. Furthermore, the seeds, flowers, and bark of <italic toggle="yes">M. oleifera</italic> are rich in various phytochemical compounds. Notably, the seeds contain glucosides and isothiocyanates, which exhibit significant anti-cancer, anti-inflammatory, and antibacterial properties (<xref rid="ref-42" ref-type="bibr">Shahbaz et al., 2024</xref>). Previous studies have shown that the concentration of bioactive components and protein expression in leaves and seeds differ in diverse stages of maturity, especially polyphenols and flavonoids. The leaves of mature plants are rich in higher concentrations of these active components and thus have greater medicinal potential (<xref rid="ref-18" ref-type="bibr">Garcia et al., 2019</xref>; <xref rid="ref-36" ref-type="bibr">Proestos &amp; Varzakas, 2017</xref>).</p><p>Antioxidant activity is a vital component of the pharmacological value of <italic toggle="yes">M. oleifera</italic> leaves, which is mainly attributed to its abundance of polyphenols and flavonoids. Antioxidants can protect cells from damage by scavenging free radicals and reducing oxidative stress. Studies have shown that mature leaves exhibit higher activity in scavenging 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) radicals, 2,2′-azino-bis (3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulfonic acid) (ABTS) cationic radicals, and superoxide anions (<xref rid="ref-44" ref-type="bibr">Sreelatha &amp; Padma, 2009</xref>). This antioxidant activity makes <italic toggle="yes">M. oleifera</italic> a potential candidate for application in the prevention of oxidative damage related diseases such as cardiovascular disease, cancer, and diabetes. Previous studies also have revealed the potential neuroprotective properties of <italic toggle="yes">M. oleifera</italic> against neurodegenerative diseases (<xref rid="ref-19" ref-type="bibr">Ghimire et al., 2021</xref>; <xref rid="ref-35" ref-type="bibr">Pandey et al., 2020</xref>). Its cholinesterase inhibition, anti-amyloid aggregation and antioxidant activity have been used to alleviate the symptoms of Alzheimer’s disease (<xref rid="ref-3" ref-type="bibr">Adebayo et al., 2021</xref>; <xref rid="ref-8" ref-type="bibr">Azlan et al., 2023</xref>; <xref rid="ref-32" ref-type="bibr">Nwidu et al., 2018</xref>).</p><p>Metabolomics studies of <italic toggle="yes">M. oleifera</italic> through liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) techniques have revealed the diversity of secondary metabolites and their associated biological activities (<xref rid="ref-40" ref-type="bibr">Saucedo-Pompa et al., 2018</xref>). The metabolites produced by <italic toggle="yes">M. oleifera</italic> leaves during growth included a range of amines, amino acids, phenols, and lipids. These secondary metabolites are not only closely related to plant growth and development but also play an important role in the antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties of plants (<xref rid="ref-48" ref-type="bibr">Vergara-Jimenez, Almatrafi &amp; Fernandez, 2017</xref>). Metabolomics studies provide a more comprehensive understanding of the nutritional value and potential pharmacological properties of <italic toggle="yes">M. oleifera</italic> leaves at various maturation stages, thus providing scientific basis for the development of <italic toggle="yes">M. oleifera</italic>-based products for different purposes.</p><p>However, the contents of bioactive compounds and secondary metabolites in leaves of <italic toggle="yes">M. oleifera</italic> at different maturity stages, as well as the changes of main phytochemical components, are rarely published in recent years. As plant-based products are one of the promising approaches to utilize natural bioactive compounds for medical research, the lack of age-dependent phytochemical and bioactivity profiling of <italic toggle="yes">M. oleifera</italic> leaves has limited its potential in functional food and pharmaceutical applications. Therefore, this study systematically investigates the phytochemical and bioactivity profiles of <italic toggle="yes">M. oleifera</italic> leaves at different maturity stages.
