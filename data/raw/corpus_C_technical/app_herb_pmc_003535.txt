# PMC: ethnobotany medicinal plants Africa Asia — Overview

Local communities rely heavily on these resources for medicinal, construction, and fuel purposes and many species have experienced noticeable declines over recent decades due to overexploitation, limited natural regeneration, and unsustainable harvesting practices. Informants reported that species under the most significant pressure include <italic toggle="yes">Vachellia bussei</italic>,<italic toggle="yes"> Vachellia tortilis</italic>,<italic toggle="yes"> Vachellia nilotica</italic>,<italic toggle="yes"> Balanites aegyptiaca</italic>,<italic toggle="yes"> Eucalyptus camaldulensis</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Dodonaea angustifolia</italic> (Table <xref rid="Tab13" ref-type="table">13</xref>). Although globally assessed as LC, these observations demonstrate that even widely distributed species can face intense localized threats, emphasizing the need for site-specific conservation interventions [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR4">4</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR7">7</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR11">11</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR45">45</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR77">77</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR147">147</xref>].</p><p id="Par203">The patterns in Sheikh District reflect broader regional and global trends. In Nigeria and Cameroon, MPs face similar pressures from fuelwood collection, overgrazing, and agricultural expansion. In South and East Asia, including India, China, and Pakistan, habitat degradation, commercial harvesting, and unsustainable trade have caused declines in culturally valuable species [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR5">5</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR116">116</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR161">161</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR169">169</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR178">178</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR179">179</xref>]. Even in Europe, North America, and Australia, urbanization, invasive species, and climate change increasingly threaten wild MP populations, demonstrating that anthropogenic and environmental pressures on MPs are a globally pervasive concern [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR54">54</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR71">71</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR145">145</xref>].</p><p id="Par204">These findings highlight the urgent need for sustainable management strategies to conserve medicinal plant diversity in Sheikh District. Recommended actions include community-based awareness programs, expansion of ex-situ cultivation in home gardens and protected areas, and integration of indigenous knowledge into formal conservation planning. Prioritizing species with high cultural and therapeutic value, such as <italic toggle="yes">Vachellia bussei</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Balanites aegyptiaca</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Dodonaea angustifolia</italic>, can mitigate local extinction risks while ensuring continued access to essential medicinal resources. Globally, integrating local knowledge with formal conservation strategies has proven effective in safeguarding ethnobotanical resources and maintaining cultural and economic benefits [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR11">11</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR15">15</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR40">40</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR41">41</xref>].</p><p id="Par205">Thus, MPs in Sheikh District face multifaceted threats from human activities and environmental pressures. While many species are classified as LC on a global scale, localized depletion underscores the need for targeted conservation interventions. Aligning community-based management with formal conservation strategies offers a viable pathway to preserve both the biological diversity and rich indigenous knowledge that underpin traditional healthcare systems in the region. These findings resonate with global observations, emphasizing the universal importance of incorporating local perspectives into sustainable conservation frameworks.</p><p id="Par206">
