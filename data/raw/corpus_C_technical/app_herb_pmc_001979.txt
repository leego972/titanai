Sterols and Steroidal Derivatives Identified in Polyscias fruticosa</title><p>Sterols are fundamental components of plant cell membranes, contributing to membrane fluidity, permeability, and receptor-mediated signaling. In medicinal plants, phytosterols are valued for their roles in modulating inflammation, oxidative stress, and lipid metabolism [<xref rid="B116-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">116</xref>,<xref rid="B117-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">117</xref>].</p><p>In <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, recent phytochemical investigations have confirmed the presence of sterols and steroidal derivatives, enhancing the known chemical diversity of the species (<xref rid="molecules-30-03460-t003" ref-type="table">Table 3</xref>). Rarison et al. [<xref rid="B10-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>] identified two sterols, stigmasterol (<bold>40</bold>) and (3β,5α)-stigmasta-7,16-dien-3-ol (<bold>41</bold>), in both the n-hexane (PFLH) and dichloromethane (PFLD) leaf extracts. The identification of these sterols in both moderately and strongly non-polar fractions confirms their high lipophilicity and supports their accumulation in membrane-rich tissues.</p><p>Additionally, Nguyen et al. [<xref rid="B118-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">118</xref>] isolated an unusual sterol, 22-dehydro-24-isopropylcholesterol (<bold>42</bold>), from petroleum ether–diethyl ether (1:1, <italic toggle="yes">v</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">v</italic>) root extracts (<xref rid="molecules-30-03460-t003" ref-type="table">Table 3</xref>). This sterol, previously described mainly in marine taxa, constitutes a rare occurrence in terrestrial flora, suggesting that <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> may possess biosynthetic traits not yet fully characterized within Araliaceae.</p><p>Earlier reviews by Ashmawy et al. [<xref rid="B4-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>] documented the presence of β-sitosterol and 3-O-β-D-glucopyranosyl-sitosterol in other <italic toggle="yes">Polyscias</italic> species, such as <italic toggle="yes">P. fulva</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">P. nodosa</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">P. guilfoyeli</italic> [<xref rid="B4-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B119-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">119</xref>,<xref rid="B120-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">120</xref>]. However, their presence in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> was not confirmed until the reports by Rarison et al. [<xref rid="B10-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>] and Nguyen et al. [<xref rid="B118-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">118</xref>]. The confirmation of these sterols in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> aligns with previous reports in congeneric species and reinforces its position within a phytochemically rich lineage.
