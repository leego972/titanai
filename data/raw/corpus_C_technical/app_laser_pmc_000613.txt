# PMC: laser photodynamic therapy cancer mechanism — Overview

colspan="1" rowspan="1">Short</td><td align="left" colspan="1" rowspan="1">[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR35">35</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR37">37</xref>]</td></tr><tr><td align="left" colspan="1" rowspan="1">Co-synthesis method</td><td align="left" colspan="1" rowspan="1">20–60</td><td align="left" colspan="1" rowspan="1">Medium</td><td align="left" colspan="1" rowspan="1">Medium</td><td align="left" colspan="1" rowspan="1">Lack</td><td align="left" colspan="1" rowspan="1">Long</td><td align="left" colspan="1" rowspan="1">[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR38">38</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR40">40</xref>]</td></tr></tbody></table></table-wrap></p><p id="Par9">Sol–gel method is to first hydrolyze the precursor into sol, and then transform the sol into gel for subsequent calcination (800–1100 °C), resulting in PLNPs with high yield and small size. Scherman et al. synthesized a series of silicate PLNPs by sol–gel method, such as Ca<sub>0.2</sub>Zn<sub>0.9</sub>Mg<sub>0.9</sub>Si<sub>2</sub>O<sub>6</sub>:Eu<sup>2+</sup>,Dy<sup>3+</sup>,Mn<sup>2+</sup> [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR31">31</xref>], CaMgSi<sub>2</sub>O<sub>6</sub>:Eu<sup>2+</sup>,Mn<sup>2+</sup>,Pr<sup>3+</sup> [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR41">41</xref>], Sr<sub>1.6</sub>Mg<sub>0.3</sub>Zn<sub>1.1</sub>Si<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub>:Eu<sup>2+</sup>,Dy<sup>3+</sup> [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR42">42</xref>], Ca<sub>1.86</sub>Mg<sub>0.14</sub>ZnSi<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub>:Eu<sup>2+</sup>,Dy<sup>3+</sup> [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR43">43</xref>], Sr<sub>2</sub>MgSi<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub>:Eu<sup>2+</sup>, Dy<sup>3+</sup>[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR44">44</xref>]. In addition, gallate PLNPs, such as LiGa<sub>5</sub>O<sub>8</sub>:Cr<sup>3+</sup>[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR45">45</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR46">46</xref>], Zn<sub>2.94</sub>Ga<sub>1.96</sub>Ge<sub>2</sub>O<sub>10</sub>:Cr<sup>3+</sup>,Pr<sup>3+</sup> and Zn<sub>1.1</sub>Ga<sub>1.8</sub>Ge<sub>0.1</sub>O<sub>4</sub>:Cr<sup>3+</sup> [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR25">25</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR47">47</xref>] were also synthesized by sol–gel method, respectively. Sol–gel method has become one of the most commonly used synthesis methods of PLNPs. However, the synthetic products still suffer from inhomogeneous morphology, uncontrollable size, and poor dispersion.</p><p id="Par10">Template method is to use mesoporous silica or carbon nanospheres as a template to bind with precursor ions and then obtain monodisperse, regular morphological PLNPs upon low-temperature calcination. The morphology and particle size of PLNPs can be controlled by the template. Zhang and co-workers have done fruitful work in synthesizing PLNPs by template method. They used mesoporous silica, hollow silica, or carbon nanospheres as a template to controlled synthesize PLNPs, respectively, such as SiO<sub>2</sub>@Zn<sub>2</sub>SiO<sub>4</sub>:Mn[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR48">48</xref>], SiO<sub>2</sub>@SrMgSi<sub>2</sub>O<sub>6</sub>:Eu<sub>0.01</sub>,Dy<sub>0.02</sub> [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR33">33</xref>], SiO<sub>2</sub>@CaMgSi<sub>2</sub>O<sub>6</sub>:Eu<sup>2+</sup>,Pr<sup>3+</sup>,Mn<sup>2+</sup> [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR49">49</xref>], SiO<sub>2</sub>@CaTiO<sub>3</sub>:Pr[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR50">50</xref>], Gd<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>@mSiO<sub>2</sub>@CaTiO<sub>3</sub>:Pr [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR51">51</xref>], Zn<sub>1.1</sub>Ga<sub>1.8</sub>Ge<sub>0.1</sub>O<sub>4</sub>:Cr<sup>3+</sup>,Eu<sup>3+</sup> @SiO<sub>2</sub>[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR32">32</xref>], mSiO<sub>2</sub>@Gd<sub>3</sub>Ga<sub>5</sub>O<sub>12</sub>:Cr<sup>3+</sup>,Nd<sup>3+</sup> [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR34">34</xref>], ZnGa<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub>:Cr<sup>3+</sup>@HMS[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR52">52</xref>], ZGOCS@MSNs@Gd<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR53">53</xref>], and ZnGa<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub>:Cr<sup>3+</sup> [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR54">54</xref>]. These PLNPs have regular spherical morphology, good monodispersity, and 50–500 nm particle size, and NIR PersL, which is very suitable for biomedical applications. Although this method has some limitation to prepare ultrasmall PLNPs, it is still considered as a facile way to make nanocarriers for PersL imaging-guided drug delivery.</p><p id="Par11">Hydrothermal/solvothermal method refers to the preparation of nanomaterials by treating the precursors in a sealed heated solution above ambient temperature and pressure [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR55">55</xref>]. This method has the advantages of mild synthesis conditions and low agglomeration. Importantly, the synthesized PLNPs have ultrasmall size and facile surface modification. Han et al. report a direct hydrothermal synthesis route for ZnGa<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub>:Cr<sup>3+</sup>, which has 8 nm size and stable colloidal property [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR56">56</xref>]. Zhang et al. employed this method to synthesize ZnGa<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub>:Cr, Eu [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR36">36</xref>] and Zn<sub>2</sub>SnO<sub>4</sub>:Cr, Eu [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR57">57</xref>] which have rich surface groups and ultrasamll-size (&lt; 10 nm).
