Extraction Strategies for Triterpenoid Saponins</title><p>Given the therapeutic relevance of triterpenoid saponins, considerable efforts have been made to develop efficient extraction methods for <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> plant material. Aqueous ethanol-based protocols remain standard, but recent innovations offer greater selectivity and sustainability.</p><p>Among these, ultrasound-assisted extraction (UAE) has demonstrated notable efficacy. Khoang et al. [<xref rid="B58-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>] applied UAE in combination with response surface methodology (RSM) to optimize the recovery of saponins from <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> roots. Under optimal conditions, i.e., 60 °C, 60% ethanol, 65 min, and 180 W ultrasonic power, the total saponin yield reached 14.5% of the dry root mass, and the extract contained approximately 41 mg·g<sup>−1</sup> of saponins. The extract showed cytotoxicity against A549, HepG2, PC-3, and HeLa cell lines.</p><p>In a complementary study, Chuyen et al. [<xref rid="B72-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>] evaluated the influence of extraction parameters on both saponin recovery and antioxidant activity. Extraction with 40% ethanol at 50 °C for 90 min, using a solvent-to-solid ratio of 40:1, resulted in high yields of saponins and co-extracted phenolic compounds. The obtained extracts demonstrated enhanced antioxidant capacity, suggesting saponin–polyphenol synergy.</p><p>Such optimization supports both pharmacological efficacy and product standardization.</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot1dot3-molecules-30-03460"><title>5.1.3. Pharmacological Potential of <italic toggle="yes">Polyscias fruticosa</italic> Saponins</title><p>Numerous pharmacological studies have validated the traditional uses of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> and demonstrated the broad spectrum of biological activities exerted by its triterpenoid saponins [<xref rid="B4-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B6-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="B7-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>,<xref rid="B14-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>,<xref rid="B16-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>,<xref rid="B17-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>]. One of the most extensively studied compounds is PFS, identified as 3-O-[β-D-glucopyranosyl-(1→4)-β-D-glucuronopyranosyl] oleanolic acid 28-O-β-D-glucopyranosyl ester (<bold>13</bold>). This saponin exhibited significant inhibitory activity against key carbohydrate-hydrolyzing enzymes: porcine pancreatic α-amylase (IC<sub>50</sub> = 27.1 µg·mL<sup>−1</sup>, ~21.2 µM) and yeast α-glucosidase (IC<sub>50</sub> = 440.5 µg·mL<sup>−1</sup>, ~344 µM) [<xref rid="B4-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B57-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>]. In vivo, oral administration of PFS at a dose of 100 mg·kg<sup>−1</sup> significantly reduced postprandial blood glucose levels in mice subjected to an oral sucrose tolerance test, resulting in decreases of 16.6% at 30 min and 27.9% at 60 min, along with a reduced area under the curve (AUC<sub>0–120</sub> min) compared to controls [<xref rid="B57-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>]. These results confirm its potential in metabolic disorder management.</p><p>Another triterpenoid saponin identified in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> is chikusetsusaponin IVa (<bold>19</bold>) [<xref rid="B7-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>], a compound with well-documented pharmacological activity. It possesses anti-inflammatory properties by suppressing the nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) signaling pathway and inhibiting activation of the NOD-like receptor protein 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome, leading to reduced expression of pro-inflammatory mediators such as TNF-α, IL-6, COX-2, and iNOS [<xref rid="B73-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>,<xref rid="B74-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>,<xref rid="B75-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>,<xref rid="B76-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>]. Under oxidative stress conditions, it enhances endogenous antioxidant defenses by upregulating superoxide dismutase (SOD), glutathione (GSH), and catalase (CAT), thereby mitigating cellular oxidative damage [<xref rid="B74-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>,<xref rid="B75-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>]. It also exerts significant antihyperglycemic effects by activating AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), which promotes glucose uptake and fatty acid oxidation via increased GLUT4 translocation and CPT-1 activation in insulin-resistant skeletal muscle cells [<xref rid="B75-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>,<xref rid="B76-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>].
