While research in cardiology began soon after the inception of OCT,<xref rid="r139" ref-type="bibr"><sup>139</sup></xref><sup>,</sup><xref rid="r140" ref-type="bibr"><sup>140</sup></xref> and has continued since,<xref rid="r141" ref-type="bibr"><sup>141</sup></xref> intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) still dominates this clinical application. For example, OCT-guided stent placement and IVUS have been shown to have comparable procedure rates despite the improved resolution of OCT.<xref rid="r142" ref-type="bibr"><sup>142</sup></xref><sup>,</sup><xref rid="r143" ref-type="bibr"><sup>143</sup></xref> However, most physicians appreciate the improved image features and resolution that OCT provides, which is <inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math14" overflow="scroll"><mml:mrow><mml:mo form="prefix">∼</mml:mo><mml:mn>10</mml:mn><mml:mo form="postfix">×</mml:mo></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula> the resolution of a standard IVUS image.<xref rid="r144" ref-type="bibr"><sup>144</sup></xref> With further research demonstrating, the utility of OCT over IVUS,<xref rid="r145" ref-type="bibr"><sup>145</sup></xref> systems demonstrating improved operating speed and ease of use, and especially a reduction in cost, OCT will likely continue to be impactful in cardiology. Most systems used in cardiology applications are catheter based<xref rid="r146" ref-type="bibr"><sup>146</sup></xref> and used primarily for the identification of plaques in the coronary arteries,<xref rid="r147" ref-type="bibr"><sup>147</sup></xref> evidence of atherosclerosis,<xref rid="r148" ref-type="bibr"><sup>148</sup></xref> and for stent placement.<xref rid="r147" ref-type="bibr"><sup>147</sup></xref></p></sec><sec id="sec4.4"><label>4.4</label><title>Dentistry and Dermatology</title><p>There are many applications in dentistry where OCT may be useful in the diagnosis of periodontal diseases,<xref rid="r149" ref-type="bibr"><sup>149</sup></xref> as well as for oral cancer,<xref rid="r150" ref-type="bibr"><sup>150</sup></xref><sup>,</sup><xref rid="r151" ref-type="bibr"><sup>151</sup></xref> and in observing the effectiveness of various therapies for preventing enamel loss<xref rid="r152" ref-type="bibr"><sup>152</sup></xref> or for restoration,<xref rid="r153" ref-type="bibr"><sup>153</sup></xref> among many others. One group in particular prepared a highly detailed guide on engineering efforts to design, construct, and test several iterations of a handheld probe for general dental applications,<xref rid="r154" ref-type="bibr"><sup>154</sup></xref><sup>,</sup><xref rid="r155" ref-type="bibr"><sup>155</sup></xref> reviewing design requirements such as material choices and cost. <xref ref-type="fig" rid="f21">Figure 21</xref> shows the compact and handheld “final prototype” OCT probe developed through this process.
