Influence of Environment on Bioactive Compound Potency</title><p>The extreme environmental pressures of the Andean region not only shape plant survival strategies, but also enhance their bioactive compound potency, making these crops valuable for human health. Studies indicate that quinoa grown at high altitudes accumulates higher levels of flavonoids and phenolic acids, contributing to its anti-inflammatory and antioxidant capacities [<xref rid="B18-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>]. Likewise, amaranth exhibits increased concentrations of bioactive peptides when cultivated under environmental stressors, enhancing its potential applications in functional foods and nutraceuticals [<xref rid="B9-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. Similarly, a study comparing quinoa varieties Regalona-Baer and Titicaca grown in different regions (Chile, Italy, Denmark) found that environmental conditions influenced their phytochemical profiles. Notable differences were observed in phenolic acids and flavonoids—especially vanillic acid, daidzein, and quercetin derivatives—while the total phenolic index was slightly lower in seeds grown in Italy compared to those from their original growing regions [<xref rid="B35-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>]. Also, a recent review selected studies from various research groups across different countries, providing a broad perspective on lupin samples and their quinolizidine alkaloid (QA) profiles. The research concluded that environmental conditions—such as light, temperature, soil pH, and drought stress—significantly affect QA synthesis, and that specific irrigation and nutrient regimes (e.g., low nitrogen, adequate potassium and phosphorus) help reduce QA accumulation [<xref rid="B36-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">Lupin</italic>, particularly <italic toggle="yes">L. mutabilis</italic>, has gained attention for its high protein content and unique phytochemical composition, which includes isoflavones and alkaloids with potential anticancer and lipid-lowering effects [<xref rid="B32-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>]. The Andean environment, with its fluctuating temperatures and nutrient-rich but arid soils, has been shown to drive variations in <italic toggle="yes">L. mutabilis</italic> seed composition, particularly in antioxidant capacity and β-tocopherol content [<xref rid="B28-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>].</p><p>As climate change continues to alter temperature, precipitation, and UV radiation levels, the synthesis of secondary metabolites in Andean plants is expected to intensify. Research suggests that elevated UV exposure leads to the increased production of protective compounds such as carotenoids and terpenoids, further enhancing their therapeutic potential [<xref rid="B37-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>]. Such shifts may also influence agricultural strategies, as traditional cultivation methods must adapt to maintain the bioactive integrity of these crops [<xref rid="B34-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>].</p><p>Furthermore, sustainable farming practices remain essential for preserving the biochemical diversity of Andean crops. Traditional Andean agricultural techniques, such as mixed cropping and rotational farming, play a crucial role in maintaining soil health and promoting phytochemical accumulation in quinoa, amaranth, and lupin [<xref rid="B4-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>]. The integration of indigenous knowledge with modern research provides a promising avenue for optimizing both yield and bioactive content in these crops.</p><p>The Andean ecosystem—defined by high altitudes, intense UV radiation, and nutrient-limited soils—has been instrumental in shaping the phytochemistry of <italic toggle="yes">C. quinoa</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">A. hybridus</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">L. mutabilis</italic>. These crops have evolved distinct biochemical pathways that not only support their resilience to extreme conditions, but also enhance their nutritional and medicinal value. The synthesis of flavonoids, terpenoids, phenolic acids, and other bioactive compounds is a direct result of their adaptation to environmental pressures, positioning them as valuable sources of functional foods and therapeutic agents.</p></sec></sec><sec id="sec4-nutrients-17-01749"><title>4.
