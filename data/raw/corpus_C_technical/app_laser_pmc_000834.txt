If in the ideal case, the laser line is approximated with a Dirac delta function (infinitesimally small linewidth δλ), then the photodetected signal takes the exact shape of the channelled spectrum. A fast Fourier transform of this signal translates the periodicity of the channelled spectrum into peaks of different frequency, related to the OPD. In this way, an A-scan is obtained, as shown at the bottom of <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig05">Figure 5</xref>, when the <italic toggle="yes">Object</italic> is a mirror. Recent progress in the fast tunability of laser sources has revived the interest in SS-OCT. The time required to tune the wavelength determines the time to produce an A-scan. Tuning speeds in excess of 5 MHz (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b27">Wieser <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>., 2010</xref>) makes the SS-OCT the fastest scanning OCT method that has lead to sufficient quality of <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> images acquired from tissue to date (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b14">Klein <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>., 2011</xref>).</p></sec></sec></sec><sec><title>Comparison of TD-OCT and SD-OCT Methods</title><p>There is a fundamental difference between TD and SD methods. TD-OCT methods deliver the reflectivity of a single point in depth, at OPD = 0, whereas SD-low coherence interferometry (LCI) and SD-OCT return reflectivity values for all points along the axial range at once. This confers SD methods superiority in terms of acquisition rate.
