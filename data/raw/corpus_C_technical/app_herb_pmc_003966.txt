Determination of Mature BDNF Protein Level</title><p>SH-SY5Y cells were plated in 10 cm<sup>2</sup> dishes. 24-h postplating, the cells were treated either with DMEM (control condition) or with WS®1375 RRE at 5 ng/ml. Cell lysates were produced after 4 h and 24 h of treatment. The time points were selected according to published data on the detection of BDNF levels in rats and mice [<xref rid="B22" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>, <xref rid="B40" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>, <xref rid="B41" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>] as well as in neuronal cells [<xref rid="B22" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>, <xref rid="B41" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>]. The concentrations of mature BDNF were quantified in duplicate using the Biosensis Mature BDNF Rapid ELISA Kit (Thebarton, Australia) according to the instructions of the manufacturer. Prior to each immunoassay, cell lysates were diluted 1 : 4 using the provided sample buffer. After incubation, the absorbance was measured at 450 nm using the Cytation 3 multimode plate reader, and the levels of mature BDNF were calculated in pg/ml and normalized to 1 mg of total protein level.</p></sec><sec id="sec2.13"><title>2.13. Characterization of the Lead Compounds of RRE WS®1375</title><p>The lead compounds in the RRE WS®1375 were determined by reversed-phase HPLC with UV detection (<xref rid="fig1" ref-type="fig">Figure 1</xref>). The single WS®1375 batch used for the present study contained 1.04% rosarin, 2.63% rosavin, 1.04% rosin (all assessed as rosavins), and 1.16% salidroside (assessed as p-tyrosol) that is in agreement with the concentration range of those lead compound in RREs used for medicinal purposes in other studies [<xref rid="B42" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>] .</p></sec><sec id="sec3.2"><title>3.2. RRE Increases Bioenergetics and Cell Metabolic Activity in SH-SY5Y and HT22 Cells</title><p>To examine the effect of the extract in physiological (healthy) condition, a screening was conducted using the ATP production assay and the cell viability (MTT) assay which is an indicator of increased mitochondrial metabolic activity. Regarding SH-SY5Y cells, all tested concentrations (1, 5, 10, and 100 ng/ml) significantly increased the ATP levels up to 8.3% (<xref rid="fig2" ref-type="fig">Figure 2(a)</xref>). The same effect was observed in the cell viability assay in which all the concentrations increased the MTT values up to 12.1%. The highest increase amounted to 12.1% and 11.5% and was caused by treatment with 5 and 10 ng/ml, respectively (<xref rid="fig2" ref-type="fig">Figure 2(b)</xref>). Upon Pearson correlation of the ATP production with the cell viability assay, a significant positive linear correlation was found in SH-SY5Y cells (<italic toggle="yes">R</italic><sup>2</sup> = 0.9007, <italic toggle="yes">p</italic> = 0.0137) with the most effective RRE concentrations being 5 and 10 ng/ml. The resulting plot allows clear visualization of the most effective concentrations of RRE (<xref rid="fig2" ref-type="fig">Figure 2(c)</xref>). This suggests that the improvement in ATP production was preferentially linked to an increase in metabolic activity of the cells. These results were confirmed in the HT22 cells in which the effects of RRE were like those observed in SH-SY5Y cells. In detail, all tested concentrations (1-100 ng/ml) increased both bioenergetics, in the form of ATP production, and metabolic activity, in the form of cell viability of up to 18.8% and 19.7%, respectively (Figures <xref rid="fig2" ref-type="fig">2(d)</xref> and <xref rid="fig2" ref-type="fig">2(e)</xref>). The RRE concentration with the most prominent effect was 5 ng/ml in both assays which is also apparent in the Pearson correlation plot in 2F.
