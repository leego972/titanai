Numerous investigations in various in vivo and in vitro experimental settings have demonstrated the impact of withanolides on the control of Nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) transcriptional activity [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR116">116</xref>].</p><p id="Par146">According to Ichikawa et al. (2006), withaferin A, viscosalactone, and their acetylated derivatives suppress the phosphorylation and degradation of IκB, which prevents the production of NF-κB-related genes [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR192">192</xref>]. In a MEK1/ERK1 dependent redox mechanism sensitive to thiol alkylation, Kaileh et al. showed that withaferin A directly suppresses IKK kinase activity, which phosphorylates IκB prior to its ubiquitination and destruction [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR116">116</xref>]. Similar to this, withaferin A and withanolide D inhibited the breakdown of IκB and promoted the production of the antioxidant enzyme HO-1 to prevent the angiogenic activity of endothelial cells [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR193">193</xref>]. Among other secondary metabolites, withaferin A has been recognized as a regulator of several cellular settings that may support myogenesis and oxidative metabolism in people.</p><p id="Par147">Ashwagandha supplementation has been associated with possible enhancements in testosterone levels and overall hormonal regulation in both men and women an effect that holds particular relevance for athletic performance, strength development, and muscle growth [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR11">11</xref>]. It is often noted that elevated testosterone levels can improve physical capability, support muscle development, and enhance overall athletic performance.</p><p id="Par148">However, it is important to recognize that research outcomes are inconsistent. While some studies have documented notable increases in testosterone levels [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR194">194</xref>], other clinical trials have reported no statistically significant differences when compared with placebo groups [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR162">162</xref>]. This tendency to highlight only favorable findings may lead to an overly optimistic and biased view of the herb’s effectiveness. Apart from its possible hormonal influences, ashwagandha is well recognized for its traditional role as an adaptogen that helps reduce stress. Research shows that by lowering cortisol levels, it can enhance psychological well-being, which in turn may indirectly support athletic performance and recovery by improving stress regulation and promoting better sleep quality [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR195">195</xref>].</p><p id="Par149">In a mouse model of induced liver damage, Palliyaguru et al. (2016) found that withaferin A is a strong inducer of Nrf2, a transcription factor that controls the production of antioxidant enzymes in response to oxidative stress through activation of the PTEN/PI3K/Akt pathway [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR146">146</xref>]. A 2020 review analyzing five studies reported that supplementation with high doses of Ashwagandha (330–1000 mg) over 8 to 24 weeks led to a significant increase in VO₂max in both trained and untrained participants [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR61">61</xref>]. A higher VO₂max indicates more efficient oxygen utilization during exercise, which is commonly associated with better cardiorespiratory fitness.</p><p id="Par150">Supporting these results, several studies suggest that Ashwagandha supplementation can enhance hematological parameters such as mean corpuscular hemoglobin (MCH), mean corpuscular hemoglobin concentration (MCHC), and mean corpuscular volume (MCV), potentially offering a physiological basis for the improvements observed in endurance performance and VO₂max [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR147">147</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR148">148</xref>].</p><p id="Par151">However, the evidence is not entirely uniform. While the previously mentioned review and studies report beneficial effects, other rigorously controlled trials have found no significant changes in VO₂max after Ashwagandha supplementation [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR57">57</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR132">132</xref>]. Excluding studies with null results in certain discussions may create an overly favorable impression of Ashwagandha’s effectiveness. Thus, although the herb shows potential to improve cardiorespiratory endurance, the overall evidence from the scientific literature is mixed and warrants further investigation.</p><p id="Par152">
