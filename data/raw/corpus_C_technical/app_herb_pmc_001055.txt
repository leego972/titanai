A diet rich in phytosterols is associated with lower risks of osteoporosis, heart disease, breast cancer, among others [<xref rid="B119-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">119</xref>]. For instance, β-sitosterol (<bold>196</bold>) (reported in <italic toggle="yes">A. donax</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">C. dactylon</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">I. cylindrica</italic>) is known for its hypocholesterolemic activity [<xref rid="B120-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">120</xref>] while their derivative β-sitosterol glucoside (<bold>197</bold>) (described in <italic toggle="yes">A. donax</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">I. cylindrica</italic>) have antibacterial activity against <italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic> O157:H7 (EHEC) biofilms (IC<sub>50</sub>: 8.3 µM) [<xref rid="B121-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">121</xref>]. Stigmasterol (<bold>201</bold>), present in <italic toggle="yes">A. donax</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">C. ciliaris</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic> proved its value as an antiasthmatic agent, with suppressive effects on essential features of allergen-induced asthma (dietary administered in guinea pigs, 10, 50, 100 mg/kg) [<xref rid="B122-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">122</xref>].This compound also has the ability to protect pancreatic β-cells from glucotoxicity during diabetes progression through inhibition of early apoptosis, increasing total insulin and promoting insulin secretion [<xref rid="B123-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">123</xref>].</p><p>Concerning monoterpenoids, 5 compounds (<bold>211</bold>–<bold>215</bold>) were reported in <italic toggle="yes">C. ciliaris</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">C. dactylon</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">P. clandestinum</italic>. This subclass is also known for the biological activities of its members [<xref rid="B124-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">124</xref>]. For instance, α-pinene (<bold>215</bold>), only reported in <italic toggle="yes">P. clandestinum</italic>, show anti-inflammatory effects on human chondrocytes which leads to antiosteoarthritic activity [<xref rid="B125-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">125</xref>]. This compound also displays antibacterial, antifungal and antibiofilm activities, with minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) values ranging from 117 μg/mL to 4,150 μg/mL [<xref rid="B126-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">126</xref>]. In the case of diterpenoids, only phytol (<bold>210</bold>) was reported in <italic toggle="yes">C. ciliaris</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">C. dactylon</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">S. spontaneum</italic>. Nonetheless, phytol is well known for its apoptotic effects in human gastric cancer cells [<xref rid="B127-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">127</xref>] as well as for its antioxidant activity [<xref rid="B128-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">128</xref>]. Regarding sesquiterpenoids (<bold>181</bold>–<bold>189</bold>), these were reported in <italic toggle="yes">C. dactylon</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">I. cylindrica</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">P. clandestinum</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">S. spontaneum</italic>.</p><p>Terpenoids was the most studied class among halophytic grasses. The compounds reported are widely diverse among the studied species and are known for their pharmacological activities. Moreover, the inclusion of food with a high quantity of these compounds is associated with chronic diseases’ prevention, highlighting the nutraceutical potential of these species.</p></sec><sec id="sec2dot6-ijms-20-01067"><title>2.6.
