GLS content and profile differ by <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> species and organ, with a high amount in seeds. Generally, <italic toggle="yes">B. nigra</italic> (L.) <italic toggle="yes">W. D. J. Koch</italic> was declared to have the highest GLS content followed by <italic toggle="yes">B. oleracea Alboglabra</italic> with 201.95  and 180.9 µmol/g, respectively [<xref rid="B197-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">197</xref>]. Interestingly, the intake of <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> vegetables and seeds is closely associated with a wide range of therapeutic properties in the prevention and treatment of several chronic diseases, especially cancer risks due to the richness in GLS and their breakdown products [<xref rid="B97-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>,<xref rid="B153-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">153</xref>,<xref rid="B155-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">155</xref>,<xref rid="B164-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">164</xref>]. Conversely, the intake of <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> plants caused death [<xref rid="B198-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">198</xref>] and liver disease in animals [<xref rid="B199-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">199</xref>]. Similarly, the consumption of de-oiled seeds with excess amounts of hydrophilic glucosinolates is deleterious to domestic animals, according to numerous studies [<xref rid="B200-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">200</xref>]. This is mainly due to the accumulation of progoitrin and sinigrin in <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds after oil extraction, leading to a bitter aftertaste responsible for reduced dietary intake [<xref rid="B201-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">201</xref>]. Intact GLSs are nontoxic, while the degradation products generated after myrosinase hydrolysis are more toxic. It has been shown that nitrile progoitrin with 2–3 mmol/kg induced liver, pancreatic, and kidney toxicity in rats [<xref rid="B198-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">198</xref>], and the progoitrin breakdown generates goitrogenic compounds [<xref rid="B200-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">200</xref>]. The principal toxic consequences in animals that have been documented are fetal death in mammals, decreased avian egg production, liver and kidney hypertrophy, and thyroid gland modification [<xref rid="B202-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">202</xref>]. Typically, these harmful and antinutritional effects appear mostly in animal species rather than in humans at high doses. Thus, the removal of maximum GLS without coextraction of proteins in oil-extracted meal was required in animal feed [<xref rid="B203-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">203</xref>]. Furthermore, thiocyanate ions and axazolidin-2-thiones formed from GLS are goitrogenic and cause thyroid cancer [<xref rid="B204-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">204</xref>]. It was proven that <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> vegetable intake was linked to thyroid cancer in a study on 293 women [<xref rid="B205-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">205</xref>]. The ADI set by EFSA is 20 g/kg of body weight, while AITC’s daily intake should be 1 mg without exceeding the ADI of 1.4 mg for an adult of 70 kg of body weight [<xref rid="B206-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">206</xref>]. Therefore, in order to reduce the seed GSL content (SGC) in mature seeds to increase their economic and nutritional value, research has been directed toward the study of the genetic structure of SGC to deeply understand the regulatory genes and their genetic mechanisms controlling both seed GSL synthesis and accumulation [<xref rid="B207-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">207</xref>].</p></sec><sec sec-type="conclusions" id="sec6-molecules-27-06008"><title>6. Conclusions</title><p>Black mustard (<italic toggle="yes">B. nigra</italic>), <italic toggle="yes">B. oleracea</italic>, rapeseed (<italic toggle="yes">B. rapa</italic>), Ethiopian mustard (<italic toggle="yes">B. carinata</italic>), Asian mustard (<italic toggle="yes">B. juncea</italic>), oilseed rape (<italic toggle="yes">B. napus</italic>), African mustard (<italic toggle="yes">B. tournefortii</italic>), and canola plants are nutritionally, economically, medicinally, and pharmaceutically important in the world. They are the major economically important oilseed crops in many countries. <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds have been employed since antiquity as an oil source, as a food condiment, in traditional medicine, and as animal feed. Recently, they have gained increasing interest for the extraction and characterization of their health-promoting compounds.
