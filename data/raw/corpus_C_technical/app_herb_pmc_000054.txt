Male Fertility Enhancement Effect</title><p>Infertility is a major clinical problem, which affects the people medically, economically and psychosocially. Almost, 15% of all couples in the U.S. are infertile, and it is predicted that the male factor is responsible in many of such cases [<xref rid="B104-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">104</xref>]. Male infertility refers to a male’s inability to achieve a pregnancy in a fertile female. In humans, this accounts for 40%–50% of infertility cases [<xref rid="B105-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">105</xref>,<xref rid="B106-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>]. Infertility in males is a multifactorial disease, based on numerous factors including reduced spermatogenesis and also production of dysfunctional sperm, which are the major prevalent underlying characteristic in idiopathic male infertility cases [<xref rid="B107-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">107</xref>,<xref rid="B108-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>]. One meta-analysis of sixty-one studies worldwide reported s downward trend in the sperm count and semen volume over the past fifty years [<xref rid="B109-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">109</xref>,<xref rid="B110-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>].</p><p>Mostly, the water-soluble <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> extracts were reported to be able to enhance male fertility (with regards to higher semen volumes, spermatozoa count, and motility) in rodents [<xref rid="B111-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">111</xref>,<xref rid="B112-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>] and in human trials [<xref rid="B86-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>,<xref rid="B113-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>,<xref rid="B114-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>].</p><p>The standardized extract F2 of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> (25mg/kg <italic toggle="yes">p.o</italic>) and its major quassinoids, especially eurycomanone (250 mg/kg <italic toggle="yes">p.o</italic>) improved rat spermatogenesis by affecting the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis and the potential efficacy may be worthy of further investigation [<xref rid="B111-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">111</xref>].</p><p>Eurycomanone, the major quassinoid in the <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> root extract, significantly increased testosterone production on a dose-dependent manner at 0.1, 1.0 and 10.0 μM (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.05). It enhanced testosterone steroidogenesis at the rat testicular Leydig’s cells by inhibiting aromatase conversion of testosterone to oestrogen, and may also involve in phosphodiesterase inhibition at a high concentration, so authors have suggested that quassinoids from <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> may be worthy for further development as new phytomedicines for the treatment of testosterone-deficient idiopathic male infertility and sterility [<xref rid="B112-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>].
