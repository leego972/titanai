Introduction</title><p>Diabetes mellitus (DM) is a metabolic disorder characterized by chronic hyperglycemia resulting from impaired insulin secretion, impaired insulin action, or a combination of both [<xref rid="B1-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>]. This systemic disorder causes disturbance in carbohydrate, fat, and protein metabolism. Chronic hyperglycemia leads to damage of various structures, including small and large blood vessels, peripheral nerves, and basement membranes in different tissues, resulting in numerous acute and chronic complications [<xref rid="B2-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="B3-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>]. A wide range of clinical manifestations and subjective symptoms are observed in the oral cavity of individuals with diabetes. These patients exhibit a higher prevalence of periodontitis, burning mouth syndrome, and dental caries, along with altered salivary flow and increased susceptibility to opportunistic infections [<xref rid="B4-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B5-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>]. The predisposition to oral infections is primarily attributed to reduced salivary secretion, the diminished buffering capacity of saliva, and poor oral hygiene [<xref rid="B6-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>]. Additionally, in these individuals, there is an increased incidence of denture stomatitis, oral lichen planus, fissured tongue, and angular cheilitis [<xref rid="B7-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>]. Chronic hyperglycemia contributes to structural alterations of oral tissues and impairs wound healing through associated microvascular and macrovascular complications [<xref rid="B8-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>,<xref rid="B9-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. These changes present significant challenges in the fabrication and wearing of removable dental prostheses. Poor adaptation or improper prosthetic design may lead to mucosal microtrauma, erosion, and lesion aggravation, ultimately resulting in chronic non-healing wounds [<xref rid="B10-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>,<xref rid="B11-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. Given their weakened immune response and impaired tissue repair, patients with DM require particularly careful therapeutic planning when undergoing prosthetic rehabilitation [<xref rid="B12-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>,<xref rid="B13-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>].</p><p>Impaired wound healing is among the most significant clinical challenges in patients with DM.
