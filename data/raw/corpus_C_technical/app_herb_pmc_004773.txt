Adaptive homeostasis refers to the transient reversible adjustments of the homeostatic range in response to exposure to challenging signaling molecules or events. Any biological function or measurement oscillates around a mean or median within a homeostatic range considered ’normal’ or physiological. Adaptogens, as shown in the figure, increase the normal homeostatic thresholds (adaptive homeostasis) to a pathological state, thereby enhancing resilience to stress within the adaptive stress system. This system regulates various bodily functions, including the neuroendocrine-immune complex, blood circulatory and digestive systems, organismal development, and aging.</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="pharmaceuticals-18-00261-g005.jpg"/></fig><fig position="float" id="pharmaceuticals-18-00261-f006" orientation="portrait"><label>Figure 6</label><caption><p>Adaptive stress response factors, mediators, and effectors and the effect of adaptogens in stress and inflammaging-induced aging-related diseases. The adaptive stress response involves the activation of intracellular and extracellular signaling pathways and increased expression of antiapoptotic proteins, neuropeptides, antioxidant enzymes, and the defense response of an organism, resulting in increased survival. One primary mechanism of adaptogens’ action is that they trigger adaptive cellular stress response pathways in human brain cells, similar to exercise, dietary restriction, and cognitive stimulation, which may exert their health benefits. Each of these environmental factors induces a mild stress response in nerve cells in the brain, increasing the expression of stress resistance proteins such as heat-shock protein 70 (HSP-70), Hsp32, and nerve cell growth factors, preventing the degeneration of neurons during aging, enhancing learning and memory, and exerting beneficial effects on many different organ systems, including the cardiovascular and glucose-regulating systems.
