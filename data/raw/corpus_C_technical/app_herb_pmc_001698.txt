This group included flavonol aglycones (<italic toggle="yes">e.g</italic>., quercetin, kaempferol, isorhamnetin), glycosylated flavonols (<italic toggle="yes">e.g</italic>., rutin, isoquercitrin, spiraeoside), C-glycosyl flavones (<italic toggle="yes">e.g</italic>., orientin, isovitexin, vicenin-2), and procyanidin B2, a dimeric flavanol. Phenolic compounds, including caffeic acid, chlorogenic acid, p-coumaric acid, and coumarin (a non-flavonoid phenolic), contributed approximately 19%, followed by glucosinolates and their derivatives (<italic toggle="yes">e.g</italic>., rhamnosylated benzyl glucosinolates, glucosinalbin) at 7%. Minor constituents like folic acid and moringyne, each constituting roughly 4%.</p><p>The distribution of these metabolites across different leaf age groups exhibited a non-linear, erratic pattern, suggesting that metabolite accumulation is determined by age-dependent and metabolically dynamic processes. As presented in <xref rid="table-4" ref-type="table">Table 4</xref>, the quantified metabolites showed that some reached peak levels during intermediate maturity stages (day-45), while others predominated in either younger (day-30) or older (day-60) leaf extracts. For example, kaempferol glycoside, the highest quantified metabolite, was most abundant in day-45 extracts, followed by day-60 and day-30 (day-45 &gt; day-60 &gt; day-30), indicating enhanced flavonoid biosynthesis during mid-leaf maturation. Conversely, caffeic acid, the least abundant quantified compound, showed its highest levels in day-60 leaves, followed by day-45 and day-30 (day-60 &gt; day-45 &gt; day-30).</p><table-wrap
