In this respect, several studies have been carried out to assess the antioxidant activity of <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic>. A summary of the analysis of these properties are listed in <xref rid="molecules-27-04240-t005" ref-type="table">Table 5</xref>. The methanolic and acetone extracts from the leaves of <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> showed significant scavenging activities of the free radicals 1,1-diphenyl-2-picryl-hydrazyl (DDPH) and superoxide anion radicals. In addition, a high reducing power was recorded, supporting the strong antioxidant potential of this plant [<xref rid="B70-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>]. In another study, the methanolic extract from the fruit part of <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic>, which contains a high phenolic and flavonoid content, was shown to have a significant antioxidant activity against DDPH and hydrogen peroxide (H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub>) [<xref rid="B71-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>]. Similar results were obtained with the hydro-alcoholic extract from the stem bark, rich in phenolics [<xref rid="B72-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>], and with the methanolic extract of roots [<xref rid="B42-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>]. Finally, an investigation of the antioxidant capacity of methanolic extracts from different <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> genotypes showed that the antioxidant potential was directly correlated with the extract’s phenolic content [<xref rid="B43-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>]. Hence, <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> exhibits potent antioxidant capacities that warrant further investigation. Anti-Inflammatory Effect</title><p>Inflammation is part of the body’s homeostatic and self-defence mechanism, and is always kept under homeostatic regulation. However, when inflammation becomes excessive or chronic, it may contribute to the development of several diseases such as diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and cancer [<xref rid="B73-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>,<xref rid="B74-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>]. Several studies have aimed to explore the reported use of <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> as an anti-inflammatory agent in traditional medicine [<xref rid="B16-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>]. An analysis of the anti-inflammatory activities of <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> is listed in <xref rid="molecules-27-04240-t006" ref-type="table">Table 6</xref>. Using the carrageenan induced paw edema test, the topical application of a gel ethanolic extract from <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> leaves to Wistar albino rats caused a significant reduction in paw edema in a dose-dependent manner, confirming the anti-inflammatory effect of the extract [<xref rid="B75-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>]. It also showed faster wound repair abilities when compared to the marketed formulation Betadine<sup>®</sup> [<xref rid="B75-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>]. Dey Ray et al. orally administered the ethanolic extract from the root bark into mice and found it to cause a significant inhibition of carrageenan and arachidonic acid induced edema, and reduce the formation of granuloma tissue, further confirming the anti-inflammatory properties of the plant [<xref rid="B76-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>]. Moreover, octadecahydro-picene-2,3,14,15-tetranone, a compound isolated from the root bark of <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic>, had a more pronounced anti-inflammatory than the extract, nearly comparable to that of aspirin. It was suggested that this compound controls inflammation through the inhibition of TNF-α and nitric oxide production [<xref rid="B76-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>]. Goyal et al. found that the anti-inflammatory effect of the ethanolic extract of <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> was due to the inhibition of histamine release [<xref rid="B77-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>], which initially causes vasodilation and increases vascular permeability. This extract also inhibited peritoneal leukocyte migration [<xref rid="B77-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>]. Finally, a recent study demonstrated that the ethanolic extract of the leaves of <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> exhibited an anti-inflammatory effect in human aortic smooth muscle cells by decreasing their proliferation, and invasion and migration potentials.
