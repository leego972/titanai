In addition, several groups have demonstrated scopoletin’s ability to inhibit acetyl- and butyryl-cholinesterases <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> (<xref rid="B110" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>, <xref rid="B247" ref-type="bibr">247</xref>, <xref rid="B254" ref-type="bibr">254</xref>–<xref rid="B257" ref-type="bibr">257</xref>, <xref rid="B512" ref-type="bibr">512</xref>) and <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> (<xref rid="B258" ref-type="bibr">258</xref>). Antidepressant, anti-psychotic, and anti-amnesic effects were also observed in behavioral studies in mice (<xref rid="B246" ref-type="bibr">246</xref>, <xref rid="B258" ref-type="bibr">258</xref>–<xref rid="B261" ref-type="bibr">261</xref>). A study investigating anticonvulsant effects of <italic toggle="yes">Benkara malabarica</italic> (Linn.) root extract found that scopoletin inhibited GABA transaminase activity (<xref rid="B262" ref-type="bibr">262</xref>). Consistent with this finding, molecular docking analysis has demonstrated affinity of scopoletin for both GABA transaminase and for the GABA-A receptor (<xref rid="B252" ref-type="bibr">252</xref>). The anticonvulsant drug vigabatrin is an irreversible inhibitor of GABA-T, and there is convincing evidence that it is effective in attenuating anxiety symptoms (<xref rid="B513" ref-type="bibr">513</xref>). Formation of amyloid beta peptide 42 (Aβ42) and α-synuclein fibrils, processes central to the pathogenesis of AD and PD respectively, have both been shown to be inhibited by scopoletin (<xref rid="B109" ref-type="bibr">109</xref>, <xref rid="B110" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>). These actions are likely complementary to the antioxidant mechanisms described above (<xref rid="B227" ref-type="bibr">227</xref>) in combatting neurodegenerative diseases. Finally, scopoletin improves neuronal plasticity, as measured in <italic toggle="yes">ex vivo</italic> electrophysiological assays, and exerts neuroprotective activity in a rat spinal cord injury model (<xref rid="B263" ref-type="bibr">263</xref>, <xref rid="B264" ref-type="bibr">264</xref>).</p><p>In conditions related to metabolic health, scopoletin acts favorably on many pathways <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic>, as well as in various cell types and experimental conditions. In animal models of diet-induced obesity, diabetes, or alcohol-induced metabolic dysfunctions, scopoletin restored insulin sensitivity, reversed disruptions in circulating lipids, glucose, insulin, and inflammatory cytokines, while also attenuating lipid accumulation and fibrosis in liver, restoring adiponectin levels in white adipose tissue, and reducing oxidative stress in the pancreas (<xref rid="B181" ref-type="bibr">181</xref>–<xref rid="B187" ref-type="bibr">187</xref>). Consistent with these <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> observations, scopoletin has been shown to mitigate insulin resistance and improve metabolic functions in cultured hepatocytes, adipocytes, and pancreatic beta-cells (<xref rid="B184" ref-type="bibr">184</xref>, <xref rid="B185" ref-type="bibr">185</xref>, <xref rid="B188" ref-type="bibr">188</xref>, <xref rid="B514" ref-type="bibr">514</xref>). <italic toggle="yes">In vitro</italic> assays have revealed that enzymes involved in glucose homeostasis (protein tyrosine phosphatase 1b, α-glucosidase and α-amylase) or in diabetic complications (aldone reductase) are inhibited by scopoletin (<xref rid="B108" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>, <xref rid="B116" ref-type="bibr">116</xref>, <xref rid="B180" ref-type="bibr">180</xref>, <xref rid="B184" ref-type="bibr">184</xref>, <xref rid="B187" ref-type="bibr">187</xref>, <xref rid="B189" ref-type="bibr">189</xref>, <xref rid="B190" ref-type="bibr">190</xref>). While effects described above are likely to improve cardiovascular health in the context of metabolic syndrome, scopoletin also acts directly on the heart and vasculature. Scopoletin has antihypertensive actions (<xref rid="B126" ref-type="bibr">126</xref>, <xref rid="B191" ref-type="bibr">191</xref>–<xref rid="B193" ref-type="bibr">193</xref>), anti-atherosclerotic capabilities (<xref rid="B132" ref-type="bibr">132</xref>, <xref rid="B194" ref-type="bibr">194</xref>–<xref rid="B196" ref-type="bibr">196</xref>), and vascular spasmolytic/vasodilatory effects (<xref rid="B191" ref-type="bibr">191</xref>, <xref rid="B193" ref-type="bibr">193</xref>, <xref rid="B197" ref-type="bibr">197</xref>–<xref rid="B199" ref-type="bibr">199</xref>).</p><p>Scopoletin is a compound of interest in cancer research, as it has been shown to have apoptotic, cytotoxic, anti-proliferative, anti-angiogenic, and anti-metastatic activities in various cancer cell lines (<xref rid="B97" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>, <xref rid="B203" ref-type="bibr">203</xref>–<xref rid="B212" ref-type="bibr">212</xref>). Efforts are ongoing to develop synthetic derivatives of scopoletin and to characterize and improve its bioavailability and pharmacokinetic properties (<xref rid="B96" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>).
