the accumulation of genetic mutations and histological alterations in chronically sun‐exposed skin, predisposing it to actinic keratoses (AK) and squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) [<xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0001" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>, <xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0002" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>, <xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0003" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>, <xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0004" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>]. First described by Slaughter et al. in 1953 [<xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0003" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>], it represents a continuum of subclinical and clinical epidermal and dermal changes typical of photodamaged skin [<xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0004" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>]. Photodamage combines clinical signs of photoaging with molecular alterations linked to carcinogenesis [<xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0005" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>, <xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0006" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>, <xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0007" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>], including textural, pigmentary, and vascular changes such as wrinkles, lentigines, telangiectasias, and sebaceous gland hypertrophy [<xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0005" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>]. AKs—the most common clinical manifestation—consist of intraepithelial atypical keratinocyte proliferation, presenting as normochromic, erythematous, or brownish papules or plaques [<xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0007" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>]. The progression of AK to SCC ranges from 0.025% to 16% per year, with contiguity reported in up to 97% of cases [<xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0008" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>, <xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0009" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>].</p><p>Chronic UV exposure induces molecular alterations, including DNA damage, oxidative stress, and inflammation [<xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0008" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>, <xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0009" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>, <xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0010" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>], contributing to carcinogenesis [<xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0010" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>, <xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0011" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. Field cancerization is clinically relevant due to its malignant potential [<xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0012" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>]; areas with multiple AKs show alterations similar to invasive SCC [<xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0011" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>, <xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0012" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>, <xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0013" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>]. As a result, management has shifted from treating isolated lesions to addressing the entire field [<xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0014" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>].</p><p>Field‐directed therapies include topical agents, photodynamic therapy, and laser‐based modalities [<xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0013" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>, <xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0014" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>, <xref rid="lsm70119-bib-0015" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>].
