# PMC: herbal medicine pharmacology mechanism review — Overview

A recent study investigated the impact of US on camptothecin (CPT) (plant alkaloid monoterpene produced by <italic toggle="yes">Camptotheca acuminata</italic> herb) and the authors found that US transformed the disulfide bonds to thiol bonds of the molecules. As a result, US modified the molecules from covalently attached linear polymer chains in the β-position to a disulfide moiety (<xref rid="f0015" ref-type="fig">Fig. 3</xref>b) [<xref rid="b0345" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>].<fig id="f0015" position="float" orientation="portrait"><label>Fig. 3</label><caption><p>US mechanochemistry releases camptothecin (CPT) herbal monoterpene. (a) Fluorescent micrograph of <italic toggle="yes">Nothapodytes nimmoniana</italic> stem sample showing autofluorescence of CPT in the epidermal layer. (b) The US mechanochemical cleavage of polymers disulfide bond that releases CPT from its β-carbonate linker presented in 2D and 3D structures (License Number: 5022331159441) [<xref rid="b0345" ref-type="bibr">[71]</xref>, <xref rid="b0365" ref-type="bibr">[72]</xref>].</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="gr3.jpg"/></fig></p><p id="p0105">The position of the disulfide motif in the center of the macromolecular framework (e.g. Hydrocarbon) enabled the efficient mechanochemical scission and the release of this monoterpene molecule [<xref rid="b0335" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>]. A US mechanophore breakage (mechanochemically breakage of the polymer reactive units like cyclic rings) can occur in extracts solutions via the shear stress caused by the collapse of US-induced cavitation bubbles [<xref rid="b0350" ref-type="bibr">[73]</xref>, <xref rid="b0355" ref-type="bibr">[74]</xref>]. These modifications in the structure of extracted compounds caused US can facilitate better bioavailability of the herbal bioactive molecules [<xref rid="b0360" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>]. Patil and Akamanchi [<xref rid="b0365" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>] used US (20 kHz, 150 W, 30 °C) for CPT extraction from the stem part of <italic toggle="yes">Nothapodytes nimmoniana</italic> herb. The application of the US increased the camptothecin yield (1.7-fold) and decreased the extraction time from 6 h to 18 min. The authors suggested that the mechanochemical effects of the US disrupted both the outer and interior parts of stem cell wall structure (<xref rid="f0015" ref-type="fig">Fig. 3</xref>a). The bubble collapse creates high-speed solvent jets that disassociated the disulfide and covalent bonds on the surface and interior parts of the cells which release the CPT [<xref rid="b0365" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>] (<xref rid="f0015" ref-type="fig">Fig. 3</xref>b).</p></sec><sec id="s0045"><label>4.3</label><title>Ultrasound effects on herbal phenolics and polyphenols</title><p id="p0110">Ultrasound has a significant influence on plant phytochemicals, especially polyphenols [<xref rid="b0140" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>]. For example, using US for extraction of gingerols from ginger powder at 50 °C enhanced the extraction of gingerol yield. However, a degradation of the active compound occurred at higher temperatures [<xref rid="b0370" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>]. Boulatov [<xref rid="b0360" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>] reported that the mechanochemical effects of the US results in overstretching of macromolecules polymers (like carbohydrate and protein chains) that lead to their fragmentation. This stretching process helps in releasing small molecules that are bound in the polymer chain. A comparison study of US-assist and conventional extraction methods on herbal tea bioactive compounds showed that total phenols and α-tocopherol were increased by 44% and 20%, respectively. The breakdown of cells cytoarchitecture has been found to release these bioactive compounds [<xref rid="b0375" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>]. According to Ranalli, Malfatti, Lucera, Contento and Sotiriou [<xref rid="b0380" ref-type="bibr">78</xref>], the increase in bioactive components in oil extracted by US could be due to the breakage of cross-links between these compounds and other macromolecules like polysaccharides and protein. Also, Rashed, Tong, Abdelhai, Gasmalla, Ndayishimiye, Chen and Ren [<xref rid="b0385" ref-type="bibr">79</xref>] reported that higher total phenolic content was extracted from <italic toggle="yes">Lavandula pubescent</italic> herb using US compared to a maceration extraction. For most herbs, the US acoustic cavitation can facilitate the flow of solvent into the plant cells, and enhance desorption of bioactives from the matrix of solid samples. Thus, US enhances the efficiency of phenolic extraction. In another study that investigated <italic toggle="yes">Elsholtzia ciliata</italic> herb, Pudziuvelyte, Jakštas, Ivanauskas, Laukevičienė, Ibe, Kursvietiene and Bernatoniene [<xref rid="b0390" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>] reported that US significantly increased the extracted apigenin phenolic yield (855.54 μg/g) compared to the maceration method (141.06 μg/g).
