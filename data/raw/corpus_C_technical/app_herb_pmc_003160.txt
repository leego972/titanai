# PMC: ethnobotany medicinal plants Africa Asia — Overview

Fresh plants were chewed, and the juice was swallowed before a meal for a day. The remedies for some diseases were boiled in the evening, and the filtrate was taken before breakfast in the morning. Some diseases were treated with a combination of routes in both human and livestock treatments (Table <xref rid="Tab1" ref-type="table">1</xref>; Additional files <xref rid="MOESM1" ref-type="media">1</xref> &amp; <xref rid="MOESM2" ref-type="media">2</xref>).<fig id="Fig4" position="float" orientation="portrait"><label>Fig. 4</label><caption><p>Routes of application of treatments</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" id="MO4" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="13002_2017_166_Fig4_HTML.jpg"/></fig>
</p><p id="Par29">The dosages taken orally were measured using a small coffee cup (~5 ml), glass (~20 ml), and tin can (~50 ml) for humans and large tin can (~3lt) for animals. The coffee cup was used to provide remedies to a child and tin cans for adults based on the symptoms and physical condition of the patients. The medication applied as eyedrops, eardrops and through nostrils was five to ten drops while in the treatment of animals thin bamboo stem about ten centimeters long was used. The application of remedies for flu, sleepwalking, and devil illness was inhaling the smoke, fume, and steam the patient is covered by overcoat/blanket or sniffing of crushed or powdered fresh plant parts wrapped with locally weaved close. Sniffing was not administered to children under age of five (Additional files <xref rid="MOESM1" ref-type="media">1</xref> and <xref rid="MOESM2" ref-type="media">2</xref>). The majority of the treatments were given only once in the morning as a single dose or three times in a day or for two to seven days. However, some treatments were given only once for all animals as a vaccine to prevent and stop the transmission of the contagious disease such as CBPP, anthrax, blackleg, and bovine pasteurellosis (Additional file <xref rid="MOESM2" ref-type="media">2</xref>).</p></sec><sec id="Sec15"><title>Fidelity value and informants consensus factor</title><p id="Par30">The medicinal plants reported by few informants (one to two) or that were used only as a treatment for an ailment had FL value of 1.00. <italic toggle="yes">Dorstenia barnimiana</italic> Schweinf. was used as a treatment for schizophrenia, and <italic toggle="yes">Priva curtisiae</italic> Kobuski as a treatment for typhoid/typhus, headache, and fever and had FL value of 1.00 (Table <xref rid="Tab1" ref-type="table">1</xref>). On the other hand, medicinal plants that were used to treat a variety of ailments had lower FL values. <italic toggle="yes">Indigofera articulata</italic> (0.25), <italic toggle="yes">C. farinosa</italic> (0.22), <italic toggle="yes">C. rotundifolia</italic> (0.19), and <italic toggle="yes">A. fruticosa</italic> (0.15) were used to treat diseases ranging from human to livestock and were reported by more than 20 informants.</p><p id="Par31">The informant consensus factor was calculated by pulling to gather the human and animal ailments into ten categories.
