In the EU, as of 2023, processed potatoes hold a market value of around €9.7 billion, constituting roughly 2.1% of the European food industry’s value [<xref rid="B101-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">101</xref>]. Among all the processed products, French fries are the most important, generating a market value of approximately €5.9 billion [<xref rid="B102-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>].</p><p>About 30% of this tuber is discarded during harvesting and storage, due to quality standards such as size, appearance, and pest damage. These discarded tubers, with low added value, are commonly repurposed as animal feed [<xref rid="B99-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>]. During processing, a significant portion of potatoes is peeled, yielding a substantial amount of nutrient-rich wet peel paste, ranging from 15 to 40% of the fresh weight, depending on the peeling technique [<xref rid="B103-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">103</xref>]. Additionally, this industry generates outer layers of pulp, as well as pulp and wastewater from starch extraction [<xref rid="B104-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">104</xref>]. Annually, this processing industry produces between 70 and 140 million tons of peels and 800–2800 million m<sup>3</sup> of wastewater [<xref rid="B105-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">105</xref>]. The disposal of untreated waste poses significant environmental concerns due to its potential for microbial degradation, constraining the storage options [<xref rid="B102-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>]. This substantial amount of waste yields ample opportunities for reuse, as potato peels are a source of bioactive compounds (starch, non-starch polysaccharides, proteins, antioxidants, or fibers, among others), as shown in <xref rid="molecules-31-00996-t004" ref-type="table">Table 4</xref>. These compounds exhibit high recovery potential and find utility across various industries, fostering a more favorable economic balance between industrial processes and the commercialization of new products [<xref rid="B99-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>,<xref rid="B106-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>]. The concentration of these bioactive compounds in the waste depends on the potato variety as well as the agronomic conditions during cultivation and maturation [<xref rid="B102-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>,<xref rid="B103-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">103</xref>].</p><p>Potato peels serve as a source of phenolic compounds, harboring approximately 50% of the compounds found in the potato. Investigations conducted by Brahmi et al., 2022 [<xref rid="B113-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>], reported the total phenolic content (TPC) in peels as ranging from 11 to 2840 gallic acid equivalent (GAE) mg 100 g<sup>−1</sup>, and total flavonoid content (TFC) varying from 780 to 2970 quercetin equivalent (QE) mg 100 g<sup>−1</sup>. In studies by Gomez-Urios et al. 2023 [<xref rid="B114-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>] utilizing UHPLC-MS/MS, 12 phenolic compounds were identified, wherein chlorogenic and trans-cinnamic acids were identified as being the most abundant compounds in free form in peels.</p><p>In addition to phenolic compounds, potato peels are also excellent sources of steroidal alkaloids, such as glycoalkaloids (α-solanine and α-chaconine) and aglycone alkaloids (solanidine and demissidine), as shown in <xref rid="molecules-31-00996-t004" ref-type="table">Table 4</xref>, with α-solanine and α-chaconine glycosides and solanidine constituting about 95% of the total concentration [<xref rid="B115-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">115</xref>].</p><p>Some of the applications of the phytochemical compounds obtained from potato waste are shown in <xref rid="molecules-31-00996-t005" ref-type="table">Table 5</xref>.</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot3-molecules-31-00996"><title>5.3. Banana Waste</title><p>Banana stands out as one of the most popular and consumed fruits worldwide, due to its nutritional content and wide array of uses. It is the second most produced fruit worldwide [<xref rid="B120-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">120</xref>,<xref rid="B121-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">121</xref>] and belongs to the Musaceae family, which includes several hybrids of the genus Musa, with studies suggesting its origin in tropical regions of South Asia [<xref rid="B122-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">122</xref>]. Global average production hovers around 120 million metric tons, whereas in 2023, the production reached 139 million metric tons, due to the significant delay in the reporting of the data, with India, China, and Indonesia being the top producers, accounting for approximately 40% of global output [<xref rid="B100-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">100</xref>].
