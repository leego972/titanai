After treatment, the patient’s HbA1C level was 9.3%, fasting blood sugar level was 189 mg/dL, and 2-h postprandial blood sugar level was 259 mg/dL.</p></sec><sec sec-type="discussion"><title>Discussion</title><p>DFU is among the major causes of morbidity and mortality in patients with DM. The risk of a person with DM to experience DFU is estimated at 19% to 34% [<xref rid="b1-amjcaserep-25-e944106" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>]. Peripheral vascular disease (PVD) and diabetic neuropathy are the 2 main risk factors for DFU [<xref rid="b10-amjcaserep-25-e944106" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>]. Diabetic neuropathy can affect almost 50% of patients with DM [<xref rid="b11-amjcaserep-25-e944106" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. Symptoms can affect sensory, motor, or autonomic functions [<xref rid="b11-amjcaserep-25-e944106" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. Diabetic neuropathy can lead to DFU because there is increased skin atrophy, excessively dry or excessively moist skin, decreased sensation, and decreased balance [<xref rid="b10-amjcaserep-25-e944106" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>].</p><p>PVD in patients with DM can result in decreased blood flow function in the lower extremities, resulting in ischemia [<xref rid="b10-amjcaserep-25-e944106" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>]. Unrecognized trauma due to neuropathy will result in wounds that are difficult to heal, due to ischemia [<xref rid="b12-amjcaserep-25-e944106" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>]. This is exacerbated by immunosuppression due to DM, which can increase the risk of infection [<xref rid="b10-amjcaserep-25-e944106" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>].</p><p>There are 3 stages to the wound-healing process: inflammation, proliferation, and remodeling [<xref rid="b13-amjcaserep-25-e944106" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>]. Growth factors and cytokines, as well as inter- and intracellular signaling, are necessary for cell proliferation, differentiation, migration, and optimal protein synthesis throughout the healing phase of a wound [<xref rid="b14-amjcaserep-25-e944106" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>]. In patients with DM, this process can be disrupted. Wounds can experience healing failure, which will usually remain in the inflammatory phase and will eventually become chronic wounds [<xref rid="b15-amjcaserep-25-e944106" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>]. In patients with DM, there is an impairment of PI3K/AKT signaling that will increase the risk of complications in DM [<xref rid="b16-amjcaserep-25-e944106" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>]. PI3K/AKT signaling has a crucial role in the process of tissue healing, regeneration, remodeling, and re-epithelialization. Therefore, the disturbance of PI3K/AKT regulation can interfere with the wound healing process [<xref rid="b16-amjcaserep-25-e944106" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>].</p><p>Current DFU therapy includes blood sugar control, debridement, antibiotics, dressing, and offloading [<xref rid="b17-amjcaserep-25-e944106" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>]. This series of therapies can give unfavorable results in patients with uncontrolled blood sugar [<xref rid="b18-amjcaserep-25-e944106" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>]. Patients with uncontrolled blood sugar levels can be immunocompromised due to the negative effects of the hyperglycemic state [<xref rid="b19-amjcaserep-25-e944106" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>]. This will result in immune system dysfunction due to impaired neutrophil function, impaired antioxidant system, and humoral immunity [<xref rid="b20-amjcaserep-25-e944106" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>].</p><p>In addition to the above management strategies, rehabilitation management in the form of exercise is also considered to have a positive effect on patients with DFU [<xref rid="b21-amjcaserep-25-e944106" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>]. Although the literature is still sparse, non-weight-bearing exercise has shown a positive effect on reducing wound size in DFU and did not show negative effects during the intervention [<xref rid="b21-amjcaserep-25-e944106" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>].
