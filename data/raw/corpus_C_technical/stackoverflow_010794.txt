Question: <p>I am learning Python for a class now, and we just covered tuples as one of the data types. I read the Wikipedia page on it, but, I could not figure out where such a data type would be useful in practice. Can I have some examples, perhaps in Python, where an immutable set of numbers would be needed? How is this different from a list?</p>

Answer: <p>Tuples make good dictionary keys when you need to combine more than one piece of data into your key and don't feel like making a class for it.</p>

<pre><code>a = {}
a[(1,2,"bob")] = "hello!"
a[("Hello","en-US")] = "Hi There!"
</code></pre>

<p>I've used this feature primarily to create a dictionary with keys that are coordinates of the vertices of a mesh.  However, in my particular case, the exact comparison of the floats involved worked fine which might not always be true for your purposes [in which case I'd probably convert your incoming floats to some kind of fixed-point integer]</p>
