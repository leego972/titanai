While the current study did not evaluate animal models, future research could explore species-specific dosing and nutraceutical integration.</p><p>Although the present study focuses on biochemical and <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> cytotoxicity assays, the biological activities observed are consistent with recent <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> reports demonstrating organ-specific effects of fenugreek extracts. <xref rid="ref-4" ref-type="bibr">Ammari et al. (2025b)</xref> observed renal protective activity with similar sex-dependent patterns. These animal studies suggest that constituents within the combined extract may influence systemic metabolic and antioxidant pathways <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic>, warranting further investigation using whole-organism models.</p></sec><sec><title>Study limitations</title><p>This study provides a preliminary biochemical and <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> assessment of a combined extract of Sidr, Fenugreek, and Black Seed. The extract was evaluated as a single polyherbal formulation, and individual plant contributions were not examined separately. Although this approach reflects the intended functional use of the formulation, future studies should include extract fractionation and component-level analyses to determine compound-specific effects.</p><p>Additionally, the current work focused on antioxidant and cytotoxic activity using <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> assays. These findings do not establish pharmacokinetic behavior, mechanisms of action, or <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> efficacy. Further research incorporating molecular pathway analysis (<italic toggle="yes">e.g.</italic>, gene expression profiling) and animal models is required to determine therapeutic mechanisms, safety, and bioavailability.</p><p>Our results are consistent with recent studies demonstrating antioxidant potential in formulations derived from <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus</italic> species. For example, <xref rid="ref-31" ref-type="bibr">Shnawa et al. (2022)</xref> reported strong DPPH scavenging activity in nickel oxide nanoparticles synthesized using <italic toggle="yes">Z. spina-christi</italic> extracts, suggesting that phenolic components contribute significantly to redox behavior through metal-complexation–assisted radical neutralization. Similarly, <xref rid="ref-30" ref-type="bibr">Shnawa et al. (2024)</xref> observed notable antioxidant capacity in zinc oxide nanoparticles biosynthesized using <italic toggle="yes">Z. spina-christi</italic> leaf extract, further supporting the role of phytochemicals as mediators of electron-transfer activity. Although the current study does not involve nanoparticle synthesis, the observed IC<sub>5</sub>
<sub>0</sub> values for the combined extract fall within the range of antioxidant performance reported for <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus</italic>-based formulations, indicating that integrating multiple herbal constituents may preserve or enhance such properties through complementary phytochemical interactions.</p><p>The antibacterial and anti-inflammatory properties reported for these herbal extracts in previous studies may also contribute to their biological relevance; however, such activities were not evaluated in the present work.</p><p>Finally, while spectroscopic and zeta potential analyses provide initial physicochemical characterization, advanced analytical techniques (<italic toggle="yes">e.g.</italic>, LC-MS/MS, HPLC fractionation, and nanoparticle stability assays) would deepen compound identification and formulation stability assessment.</p></sec></sec><sec sec-type="conclusions"><title>Conclusions</title><p>The combined extract of herbal extract exhibited high phenolic and tannin content and demonstrated notable antioxidant and cytotoxic activity, supporting its potential as a multifunctional natural therapeutic candidate. Future studies will focus on gene-expression profiling and purification of active fractions to elucidate molecular pathways and enhance pharmaceutical applicability.</p></sec><sec
