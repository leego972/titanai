Standardized root extracts of <italic toggle="yes">W. somnifera</italic> alter basal and morphine-induced expression of opioid receptor genes in neuroblastoma cells, affecting signaling pathways involved in analgesia and addiction [<xref rid="B40-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>]. In randomized clinical trials, ashwagandha reduced serum and salivary cortisol levels by 14–28%, correlating with a reduction in symptoms of chronic stress [<xref rid="B26-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>,<xref rid="B39-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">W. somnifera</italic> influences RNA transcription, stability, and processing, thereby modulating molecular changes associated with aging, including alterations in gene expression and signaling networks [<xref rid="B41-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>]. Withanolides regulate genes involved in cell proliferation, apoptosis, immune responses, and energy metabolism through effects on transcription factors such as NF-κB, AP-1, p53, and FOXO [<xref rid="B41-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>]. In aging models, ashwagandha increases the expression of genes related to DNA repair, autophagy, and cellular stress resistance, leading to lifespan extension and improved cognitive function [<xref rid="B41-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>].</p><p>Clinically, ashwagandha demonstrates adaptogenic effects in randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, reducing stress symptoms (28–44%) and anxiety (41–56%) and improving sleep quality (41–72%) at doses of 240–600 mg/day administered for 8–12 weeks [<xref rid="B26-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>,<xref rid="B39-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>]. In studies involving athletes, ashwagandha enhances muscle strength, endurance, and recovery through modulation of testosterone, cortisol, and creatine kinase levels [<xref rid="B28-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>,<xref rid="B39-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>]. Eleutherococcus senticosus (Rupr. &amp; Maxim.) Maxim</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Eleutherococcus senticosus</italic>, commonly known as Siberian ginseng, is a traditional adaptogen that has been used for over two thousand years in Chinese and Russian medicine to reduce fatigue, enhance immune function, and improve physical and mental endurance (<xref rid="nutrients-18-00931-f004" ref-type="fig">Figure 4</xref>) [<xref rid="B43-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>,<xref rid="B44-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>]. The European Medicines Agency (EMA) has approved <italic toggle="yes">E. senticosus</italic> root for the treatment of symptoms of asthenia, such as fatigue and weakness [<xref rid="B43-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>]. Eleutheroside E (syringaresinol diglucoside) and eleutheroside B (syringin) are the principal phenolic bioactive constituents and are used as markers for the standardization of <italic toggle="yes">Eleutherococcus</italic> preparations [<xref rid="B43-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>,<xref rid="B45-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>].</p><p>Eleutheroside E, the main phenolic active compound, exhibits a chemical structure similar to catecholamines—mediators of the sympathoadrenal system (SAS) involved in early activation of the stress response [<xref rid="B1-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B43-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>]. This structural analogy to adrenaline and noradrenaline explains the rapid adaptogenic effects of eleutherosides in modulating neuroendocrine responses to acute stressors [<xref rid="B1-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>]. Eleutheroside E acts through G-protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) pathways, modulating cyclic AMP (cAMP), phospholipase C (PLC), and phosphatidylinositol signaling cascades [<xref rid="B4-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>].</p><p>Clinical studies have demonstrated that <italic toggle="yes">Eleutherococcus</italic> improves mental performance, increases adrenal cortical activity, and enhances metabolic intensity under both normal and non-stress conditions [<xref rid="B1-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B43-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>].
