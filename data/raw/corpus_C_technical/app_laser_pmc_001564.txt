OCTA: optical coherence tomography, PAMM: paracentral acute middle maculopathy.</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="anchor" id="jats-graphic-3" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="CEO-53-682-g008.jpg"/></fig></sec><sec id="ceo14544-sec-0005"><label>2.3</label><title>Non‐Arteritic Anterior Ischaemic Optic Neuropathy (<styled-content style="fixed-case" toggle="no">NAION</styled-content>)</title><p>NAION is the most common form of acquired optic neuropathy in adults over the age of 50 [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0026" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0027" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>]. The pathogenesis is not fully established, and the site of vascular compromise is thought to be at the layer of the Circle of Zinn‐Haller at the optic nerve head. Patients often present with acute painless loss of vision with an altitudinal visual field defect. The sectoral visual field loss on perimetry testing and corresponding RNFL thinning on OCT imaging most commonly occurs in the nasal and inferior quadrants [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0028" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0029" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0030" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0031" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>].</p><p>OCTA has helped to understand the pathogenesis of NAION, showing hypoperfusion of the microcirculation within the anterior 1 mm of the optic disc from the choriocapillaris derived from the short posterior ciliary circulation [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0032" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>]. Imaging of the choriocapillaris is traditionally very difficult with dye‐based angiographic modalities such as fluorescein angiography and indocyanine green angiography due to dye leakage as a result of the fenestrated nature of the capillary network, resulting in dye leakage in the acute phase [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0033" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0034" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0035" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0036" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>]. This is particularly relevant in NAION as it manifests as a sectoral flow void within the RPCs and peripapillary choriocapillaris (PCC) on en face OCTA imaging acutely [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0037" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0038" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0039" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0040" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>]. This measurable capillary dropout in the acute phase of the disease allows for diagnostic delineation of AION from other non‐ischaemic acute optic neuropathies that similarly manifest with acute disc swelling [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0039" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0041" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0042" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0043" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0044" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>]. The observed flow void on OCTA progresses following the acute insult, reaching a plateau chronically; this pattern of capillary dropout correlates strongly with functional and structural changes noted on perimetry and OCT‐RNFL imaging (Figure <xref rid="ceo14544-fig-0003" ref-type="fig">3</xref>) [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0037" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0038" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0039" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0045" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>].
