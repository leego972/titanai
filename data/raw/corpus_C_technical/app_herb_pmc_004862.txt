Neuroserpin controls axon growth and thereby supports the transmission of nerve impulses. It plays an important role in synapse development and regulates synaptic plasticity, suggesting that it may be important for learning and memory (Hastings et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B41">1997</xref>; Davis et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B24">1999</xref>; Kinghorn et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B56">2006</xref>). Moreover, neuroserpin inhibits the activity of tissue plasminogen activator (tPA), which plays a role in cell migration, blood clotting, and inflammation (Yepes and Lawrence, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B137">2004</xref>).</p><p>A characteristic feature common to all tested adaptogens was down-regulation of the <italic toggle="yes">CETP</italic> gene, which encodes cholesteryl ester transfer protein, a lipid plasma protein that facilitates the transport of cholesterol esters and triglycerides between low-density lipoproteins (LDL) and high-density lipoproteins (HDL; Bruce et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B14">1998</xref>). Pharmacological inhibition of CETP alleviated atherosclerosis and other cardiovascular diseases, as well as metabolic syndrome (Barter et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B7">2003</xref>).</p><p>Adaptogens down-regulated the <italic toggle="yes">ESR1</italic> gene (Table <xref ref-type="table" rid="T6">6</xref>). <italic toggle="yes">ESR1</italic> encodes estrogen receptor alpha (ERα), a nuclear receptor belonging to a large family of transcription factors, transducing hormonal signals, and regulating expression of target genes (Evans, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B31">1988</xref>). ERα is primarily localized in the cytoplasm (Welshons et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B127">1984</xref>) in complexes with heat-shock-proteins (hsp90, hsp70, and hsp56) in a transcriptionally inactive form (Pratt, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B99">1992</xref>). ERα are expressed in various tissues such as the breast, ovaries, testis, lung, uterus, bone, liver, and brain. In human forebrain, ERα are predominantly localized in amygdale (suggested to be involved in mood and cognition), hypothalamus (involved in learning and memory), and septum (Österlund and Hurd, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B83">2001</xref>; Yaghmaie et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B133">2005</xref>; Dahlman-Wright et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B22">2006</xref>). Affective mood disorders, such as premenstrual syndrome, postnatal depression, and postmenopausal depression are associated with low serum-levels of estrogens. Estradiol treatment has been shown to down-regulate ERα in the uterus and in several brain areas (Shupnik et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B110">1989</xref>; Lauber et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B60">1990</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B59">1991</xref>; Simerly and Young, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B111">1991</xref>; Österlund et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B82">1998</xref>).</p><p>ESR1 expression is tissue-specific and differentially regulated in a region specific manner in the brain. It has been shown that estrogen signaling via ERα can significantly attenuate an inflammatory neurodegenerative process by acting through astrocytes. ERα expression is necessary in astrocytes, but not neurons for neuroprotection in experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis, the most widely used mouse model of multiple sclerosis – an autoimmune disease characterized by demyelination and axonal degeneration (Spencea et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B112">2011</xref>). Consequently, it can be expected that neuroprotective effect of adaptogens (Kim et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B55">2004</xref>; Bu et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B15">2005</xref>; Chen et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B18">2009a</xref>; Qu et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B101">2009</xref>; Bocharov et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B10">2010</xref>; Zhang et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B139">2007</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B140">2010</xref>; Li et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B66">2011</xref>; Lee et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B63">2012a</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B64">b</xref>; Palumbo et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B84">2012</xref>; Shi et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B109">2012</xref>; Zeng et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B138">2012</xref>) must be associated with up-regulation ESR1 in glia cells.
