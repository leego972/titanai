FA</title><p>Major advantages of OCTA over FA include its quick imaging time, noninvasiveness, and lower cost.<sup><xref rid="bibr14-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>,<xref rid="bibr40-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">40</xref></sup> However, FA is a well-established imaging modality that is still the gold standard for multiple conditions including choroidal neovascularization (CNV) and retinal neovascularization (NV).<sup><xref rid="bibr41-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">41</xref><xref rid="bibr42-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr43-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">43</xref></sup> Benefits of this technology include a wide field of view, visualization of flow from retinal arterioles to venules, and accurate identification of exudates or leakage (which OCTA is unable to detect).</p><p>The field of view in FA (50°, 120°, or 200°) is significantly larger than the 3 × 3 mm<sup>2</sup> area (~7°) traditionally used in OCTA.<sup><xref rid="bibr44-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">44</xref></sup> However, these larger fields of view have lower resolution than 3 × 3 mm<sup>2</sup> OCTA scans, making identification of microvasculature dysfunction in conditions such as diabetes more difficult.<sup><xref rid="bibr45-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>,<xref rid="bibr46-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">46</xref></sup> In addition, with developments in OCTA hardware and software, larger imaging areas up to 12 × 12 mm<sup>2</sup> have become possible with commercial OCTA systems. Techniques like extended field imaging using 20 diopter lenses, as well as image montaging, can increase the field of view even further while preserving detail to become comparable if not superior to that of FA.<sup><xref rid="bibr40-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>,<xref rid="bibr47-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>,<xref rid="bibr48-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">48</xref></sup> Furthermore, OCTA has been found to be superior to FA for visualization of the deeper retinal microvasculature.<sup><xref rid="bibr49-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>,<xref rid="bibr50-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">50</xref></sup></p></sec></sec><sec id="section6-2515841419840249"><title>Study of posterior pole disease</title><sec id="section7-2515841419840249"><title>Diabetic retinopathy</title><p>Numerous OCTA measures including vessel density, perfusion density, and vessel diameter index have been developed and studied for diabetic retinopathy (DR). Changes in these measures of vascular function have almost universally been shown to correlate with increasing severity of DR and worsening visual acuity, thereby suggesting a promising role for OCTA in the monitoring of disease progression.<sup><xref rid="bibr9-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>,<xref rid="bibr33-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>,<xref rid="bibr45-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>,<xref rid="bibr51-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">51</xref><xref rid="bibr52-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr53-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr54-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">54</xref></sup> In addition, OCTA parameters such as foveal avascular zone (FAZ) area have been reported to be enlarged in diabetic eyes prior to the development of clinically apparent DR, thereby indicating value as a potential screening tool.<sup><xref rid="bibr55-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">55</xref></sup> Of note, the derivation of many of these measures requires the use of third-party software such as ImageJ (developed by Wayne Rasband, National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA) or MATLAB (MathWorks, Natick, MA, USA).</p><p>OCTA has been found to be superior to FA in delineating the microvasculature, allowing an improved visualization of capillary dropout and changes in the FAZ.<sup><xref rid="bibr56-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">56</xref></sup> However, while Ishibazawa and colleagues have reported that OCTA can be useful in identifying microaneurysms and retinal perfusion deficits, La Mantia and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr57-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">57</xref></sup> have shown FA to be more effective in visualizing microaneurysms relative to OCTA.
