Mice were first allowed to explore the empty arena freely for 15 min to habituate to the environment. During the sample phase, animals were introduced to two identical objects and permitted to explore for 5 min. After a 30 min retention interval, one of the familiar objects was replaced with a novel object, and exploratory behavior was recorded during a 5 min test session. To eliminate olfactory cues, the arena and all objects were thoroughly cleaned with 70% ethanol between trials. During the acquisition phase, mice completed four daily trials over five consecutive days, starting from different quadrants positions and swimming to locate the fixed platform. Measurement of Lipid Peroxidation Using TBARs Assay</title><p>Lipid peroxidation is a major contributor to oxidative injury, particularly in neural tissues, where reactive oxygen species readily attack membrane phospholipids. Malondialdehyde (MDA), a stable end product of polyunsaturated fatty acid peroxidation, is widely used as an indicator of oxidative stress and can be quantified using the thiobarbituric acid reactive substances (TBARS) assay. In the present study, MDA levels in the hippocampus and frontal cortex were measured following the protocol described by Sultana et al. [<xref rid="B39-pharmaceuticals-19-00339" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>]. Briefly, brain tissues were homogenized and mixed with trichloroacetic acid (TCA; Sigma-Aldrich<sup>®</sup>, St. Louis, MO, USA), followed by centrifugation at 8000× <italic toggle="yes">g</italic> for 10 min at 4 °C to remove precipitated proteins. The resulting supernatant was reacted with 0.8% thiobarbituric acid (TBA; Sigma-Aldrich<sup>®</sup>, St. Louis, MO, USA) and heated at 100 °C for 15 min to facilitate formation of the colored MDA–TBA adduct. Absorbance was subsequently measured at 550 nm using a spectrophotometer. MDA concentrations were quantified using a standard calibration curve and normalized to total protein content, with results expressed as nanomoles per milligram of protein.</p></sec><sec id="sec4dot9-pharmaceuticals-19-00339"><title>4.9. Determination of Antioxidant Enzymatic Activities</title><p>Superoxide dismutase (SOD) plays a pivotal role in cellular antioxidant defense by catalyzing the dismutation of superoxide anion radicals into hydrogen peroxide and molecular oxygen, whereas catalase (CAT) further detoxifies hydrogen peroxide by converting it into water and oxygen. Accumulation of these reactive oxygen species can result in oxidative injury through damage to cellular macromolecules [<xref rid="B40-pharmaceuticals-19-00339" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>]. Thus, the coordinated actions of SOD and CAT constitute essential enzymatic mechanisms for mitigating oxidative stress. In this study, SOD and CAT activities were assessed in the hippocampus and frontal cortex. Brain tissues were homogenized in ice-cold phosphate buffer (5 mM, pH 7.4) to obtain 20% (<italic toggle="yes">w</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">v</italic>) homogenates. Enzyme activities were quantified using commercially available assay kits—the SOD Assay Kit (No. 19160) and Catalase Assay Kit (CAT100) from Sigma-Aldrich<sup>®</sup> (St. Louis, MO, USA)—according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The resulting enzyme activities were normalized to total protein concentrations, which were determined using the Bradford protein assay [<xref rid="B41-pharmaceuticals-19-00339" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec4dot10-pharmaceuticals-19-00339"><title>4.10.
