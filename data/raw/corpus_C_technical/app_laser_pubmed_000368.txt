Choroidal Neovascularization in Malattia Leventinese Diagnosed Using Optical 
Coherence Tomography Angiography.

Serra R(1), Coscas F(2), Messaoudi N(3), Srour M(3), Souied E(3).

Author information:
(1)Department of Ophthalmology, Centre Hospitalier Intercommunal de Creteil 
University Paris Est Creteil, Creteil, France; Department of Surgical Sciences,

(2)Department of Ophthalmology, Centre Hospitalier Intercommunal de Creteil

coscas.f@gmail.com.
(3)Department of Ophthalmology, Centre Hospitalier Intercommunal de Creteil

PURPOSE: To compare optical coherence tomography angiography (OCT-A) with 
traditional multimodal imaging in patients with Malattia Leventinese.
DESIGN: Retrospective case series.
METHODS: Eight eyes of 4 consecutive patients with Malattia Leventinese were 
retrospectively studied. All patients underwent a complete ophthalmologic 
examination including color fundus photography, fluorescein and indocyanine 
green angiographies, spectral-domain optical coherence tomography, and optical 
coherence tomography angiography.
RESULTS: The choriocapillaris segmentation of OCT-A revealed the presence of a 
hyperflow signal corresponding to active choroidal neovascularization in 3 eyes 
and inactive choroidal neovascularization in 1 eye. Traditional multimodal 
imaging did not show each vascular layer and any leakage in these cases.
CONCLUSION: OCT-A, unlike traditional multimodal imaging, helps diagnose 
choroidal neovascularization in patients with Malattia Leventinese suffering 
from metamorphopsia and reduced visual acuity.
