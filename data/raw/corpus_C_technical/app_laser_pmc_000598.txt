Only one study has investigated the potential of C-PDT, conducted every 4–8 weeks for 2 years, to reduce the incidence of SCC in 12 high-risk solid-organ transplant recipients (SOTRs) (<xref rid="cit0030" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>). The number of SCCs developed after the start of the study was compared with the number of SCCs developed during the year before study initiation. The median reduction in SCCs between the pretreatment counts and the 12- and 24-month post-treatment counts was 79% and 95%, respectively. The authors conclude from these results that cyclic C-PDT may reduce the incidence of SCC in SOTRs.</p><p>In conclusion, DL-PDT and cryosurgery did not differ significantly with regard to the cumulative number of newly developed AKs; a too-short observation period and a small sample size may be responsible for this result. Secondary endpoints, such as photoageing and tolerability, indicated superiority of DL-PDT over cryosurgery. A sufficiently powered large-scale trial with a clinically adequate treatment and observation period is needed to further investigate the prophylactic properties of DL-PDT.</p></sec></body><back><ack><title>ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS</title><p>The authors thank Monika Schöll for linguistic revision of the manuscript.</p><p>This study was financially supported by Galderma Laboratorium GmbH, Düsseldorf, Germany.
