Although several phytosterols have been identified, including rare or unusual compounds such as 22-dehydro-24-isopropylcholesterol (<bold>42</bold>), their biosynthetic origins, tissue-specific distribution, and regulatory mechanisms have not been elucidated. Moreover, the lack of in vivo studies hinders a comprehensive understanding of their pharmacological roles, including potential synergistic effects with other lipophilic metabolites. Further investigations should aim to characterize the sterol biosynthetic pathway, assess the bioavailability of individual compounds, and explore their therapeutic relevance through integrative pharmacological models. Such studies would clarify the contribution of sterols to the traditional and modern medicinal use of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> and enhance the rationale for their inclusion in phytopharmaceutical formulations.</p><sec><title>Biological Properties of Sterols and Steroidal Derivatives in <italic toggle="yes">Polyscias fruticosa</italic></title><p><italic toggle="yes">Polyscias fruticosa</italic> contains a diverse array of sterolic constituents with distinct structural features and pharmacological implications. Among them, stigmasterol, detected in both n-hexane and dichloromethane extracts, is the most thoroughly studied [<xref rid="B4-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>]. Stigmasterol (<bold>40</bold>) is known for its anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, antidiabetic, and anticancer effects [<xref rid="B118-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">118</xref>,<xref rid="B119-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">119</xref>,<xref rid="B120-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">120</xref>]. Its anti-inflammatory activity involves suppression of cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) and inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS), inhibition of NF-κB translocation, and downregulation of pro-inflammatory cytokines including TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6.</p><p>The antioxidant effects result from both direct ROS scavenging and induction of endogenous antioxidant enzymes such as catalase, superoxide dismutase (SOD), and glutathione peroxidase (GPx).</p><p>Anticancer effects are mediated through modulation oncogenic signaling via inhibition of Akt/mTOR and JAK/STAT pathways and downregulation of anti-apoptotic proteins such as Bcl-2 and Bcl-xL [<xref rid="B119-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">119</xref>,<xref rid="B120-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">120</xref>].</p><p>(3β,5α)-Stigmasta-7,16-dien-3-ol (<bold>41</bold>), a less commonly reported Δ<sup>7</sup>-sterol with double bonds at C-7 and C-16, was also identified in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> [<xref rid="B10-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>]. Although direct pharmacological data on this compound are limited, structurally similar Δ<sup>7</sup>-sterols have demonstrated membrane-stabilizing, antifungal, and cytostatic properties [<xref rid="B116-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">116</xref>]. Their rigid steroidal structure is thought to support membrane integrity under oxidative and osmotic stress. The co-occurrence of this sterol with stigmasterol in lipophilic extracts may imply cooperative effect in modulating membrane fluidity and lipid raft-associated signaling.</p><p>Of particular interest is 22-dehydro-24-isopropylcholesterol (<bold>42</bold>), an atypical marine-type sterol isolated from the roots of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> [<xref rid="B118-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">118</xref>]. This sterol, characterized by a double bond at C-22 and an isopropyl group at C-24, is rarely found in terrestrial plants.
