Collectively, these findings further support the non-linear and parameter-specific character of the LLLT response.</p><p id="Par41">In our study, LLLT not only enhanced ATP-dependent metabolic activity of fibroblasts, but also influenced their membrane integrity and inflammatory status, as evidenced by a decrease in AK and NO release (Table <xref rid="Tab2" ref-type="table">2</xref>). Excessive NO production promotes nitro-oxidative stress and ECM degradation [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR39">39</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR40">40</xref>]. Therefore, the simultaneous decrease in AK and NO levels may reflect a protective and anti-inflammatory effect of LLLT, potentially associated with inhibition of inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS) activity and reduced NF-κB signalling [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR18">18</xref>]. Together, these findings suggest that mitochondrial activation and chanes in the NO production may represent interconnected mechanisms underlying the observed immunomodulatory effects.</p><p id="Par42">The mechanism of light-mitochondria interaction was described by Karu et al., who demonstrated that NO donors modulated the response of HeLa cells to radiation at a wavelength of 600–860 nm by interacting with cytochrome c oxidase (CCO). At a wavelength of 820 nm, the presence of NO donors abolished the radiation-induced stimulation of the cell adhesion [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR41">41</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR42">42</xref>]. In the context of our results, this suggests that changes in NO secretion affect mitochondrial activity and that NO signalling may participate in the regulation of LLLT-induced cellular responses.</p><p id="Par43">A decrease in NO secretion was observed by Kocherova et al. during the irradiation of HGF fibroblasts with light at a wavelength of 635nm, whereas no changes were observed at 808 nm using the same power and dose (100 mW, 4 J/cm²) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR43">43</xref>]. However, different results were achieved by George et al., who demonstrated an increase in NO secretion following irradiation of primary fibroblasts with a laser beam at 636 and 825 nm wavelengths (power 74 and 104 mW, doses 5–25 J/cm²) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR44">44</xref>]. Taken together, these findings suggest that NO modulation is highly sensitive to irradiation parameters and may vary depending on wavelength and dose configuration.</p><p id="Par44">When assessing the potential immunomodulatory effect of LLLT on parameters promoting fibroblast functions associated with tissue regeneration, a decrease in IL-6 and IL-8 secretion was exhibited (Table <xref rid="Tab3" ref-type="table">3</xref>). IL-6 and IL-8 are among factors playing an important role in the healing process [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR45">45</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR46">46</xref>]. The observed reduction in IL-6 and IL-8 secretion indicates inhibition of fibroblast proinflammatory activity, which may promote the transition from the inflammatory to the proliferative phase of healing. This pattern is consistent with the biphasic dose response characteristic of photobiomodulation. <table-wrap id="Tab3" position="float" orientation="portrait"><label>Table 3</label><caption><p> Effect of LLLT on cytokine, MMP activity and oxidative-antioxidative balance in fibroblasts of the Hs680Tr. cell line</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="anchor" id="MO9" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="10103_2026_4873_Tab3_HTML.jpg"/></table-wrap></p><p id="Par45">Similar results to those reported in the present study were reported by Shaikh-Kader et al., who demonstrated a decrease in IL-6 secretion by WS1 fibroblasts after exposure to light at a wavelength of 660 nm, a power of 100 mW and dose of 5 J/cm² [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR36">36</xref>]. Harorli et al., in contrast, examined the effect of irradiation of fibroblasts with a beam wavelength of 940nm (power of 40–90 mW, doses of 0.84–1.97 J/cm²), and showed that a dose of 1.97 J/cm² inhibited the release of IL-6 and IL-8 cytokines, while lower doses (0.84–1.4 J/cm²) caused an increase in their secretion [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR38">38</xref>]. Some authors additionally propose that photobiomodulation may influence inflammatory signalling through epigenetic mechanisms, including microRNA modulation; however, this aspect requires further dedicated mechanistic investigation [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR38">38</xref>].</p><p id="Par46">Irradiation of fibroblasts with a lower power (100 mW) and a higher dose (10 J/cm²) caused a transient increase (day 3) in pro-MMP-9 and its dimer form, followed by a delayed increase (day five) in MMP-2 activity (Table <xref rid="Tab3" ref-type="table">3</xref>). This indicates controlled, sequential activation of proteolysis, typical of the normal inflammatory and regenerative phases.
