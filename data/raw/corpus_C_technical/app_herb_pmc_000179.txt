BY</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><sec id="s0005"><label>1</label><title>Introduction</title><p id="p0030">Integrating physical and chemical technologies for the characterization and modification of natural plants, like herbs and spices, has been used for several decades to improve their potency and quality <xref rid="b0005" ref-type="bibr">[1]</xref>, <xref rid="b0010" ref-type="bibr">[2]</xref>. Herbs have a long history of medicinal uses that have been documented by their traditional uses and recent scientific evidence, in particular in China. Chinese herbs have been traditionally used for the treatment of different disorders, such as respiratory and heart diseases and mental disorders. During the SARS epidemics, traditional Chinese herbal medicine treatments were reported to have positive therapeutic effects on SARS <xref rid="b0015" ref-type="bibr">[3]</xref>, <xref rid="b0020" ref-type="bibr">[4]</xref>. Scientists have been exploring the scientific basis and mechanism of action of herbal medicine with great interest in their chemical constituents <xref rid="b0025" ref-type="bibr">[5]</xref>. Special attention has been paid to investigate the different methods for the preparation and processing of herbs to maximize the efficacy of their bioactivities and to understand the changes in their chemical compositions <xref rid="b0030" ref-type="bibr">[6]</xref>.</p><p id="p0035">Ultrasound treatment is an acoustic technology that can be used for non-invasive detection and/or modification of herbal bioactive compounds. As a physical treatment, it can modify the chemical and physical properties of biological systems at varying levels depending on the processing conditions (e.g. frequency, intensity, and duration) and the herb's structure and composition <xref rid="b0035" ref-type="bibr">[7]</xref>. The technology is a green technology that offers opportunities to create new functional products or extract more powerful functional bioactive compounds from herbs. Also, it is a low energy and maintenance cost technology that has several economic benefits <xref rid="b0040" ref-type="bibr">[8]</xref>. However, US is not a largescale standardized technology that could be adapted at a commercial scale for modification of herbs and other foods. A better understanding of the complex relationships among the processing conditions (frequency, power, and processing duration) and herbs structures and chemical compounds can support future applications in the herbal industry <xref rid="b0045" ref-type="bibr">[9]</xref>. On the other hand, phytochemical changes can occur by free radicals that are generated by cavitation caused by the US shock wave energy. These changes in phytochemicals are considered a huge challenge for US technology. Collectively, recent research suggested that US can be considered as a viable technology in quality assurance and food safety applications <xref rid="b0050" ref-type="bibr">[10]</xref>, which adds greater benefit for the optimization of the latest extraction technologies <xref rid="b0010" ref-type="bibr">[2]</xref>.</p><p id="p0040">This review aims to provide a comprehensive review of mechanisms of action of ultrasound and their effects on the quality of herbal products.
