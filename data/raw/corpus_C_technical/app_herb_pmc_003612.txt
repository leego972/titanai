Preference for their use may be related to their availability.</p></sec><sec><title>Discussion</title><p>Different parts of medicinal plants were used as medicine by the local traditional healers. Among the different plant parts, the leaves were most frequently used for the treatment of diseases followed by whole plant parts, fruit, stem, root, stem and root bark, seed, flower and latex. The methods of preparation (Figure <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F3">3</xref>) fall into four categories, viz.: plant parts applied as a paste (38%), juice extracted from the fresh plant parts (24%), powder made from fresh or dried plant parts (20%), some fresh plant parts (6%), and decoction (12%). External applications (mostly for skin diseases, snake bites and wounds) and internal consumption of the preparations were involved in the treatment of diseases.</p><fig position="float" id="F3" orientation="portrait"><label>Figure 3</label><caption><p>Method of preparation.</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="float" xlink:href="1746-4269-2-43-3.jpg" orientation="portrait"/></fig><fig position="float" id="F4" orientation="portrait"><label>Figure 4</label><caption><p>Number of remedies used for various ailments.</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="float" xlink:href="1746-4269-2-43-4.jpg" orientation="portrait"/></fig><p>It was observed that, most of the remedies consisted of single plant part and more than one method of preparation. However, many of the remedies consisted of different parts of the same plant species to treat single or more diseases.
