Fatty Acids, Acylglycerols And Derivatives</title><p>Fatty acids are the second most described class of compounds in halophytic grasses, with their presence reported in eight species: <italic toggle="yes">A. donax</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">C. ciliaris</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">C. gayana</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">C. dactylon</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">H. mucronatum</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">L. multiflorum</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">S. spontaneum</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">S. patens</italic> (<xref ref-type="app" rid="app1-ijms-20-01067">Table S1</xref> and <xref ref-type="fig" rid="ijms-20-01067-f002">Figure 2</xref>). <italic toggle="yes">A. donax</italic> has the most detailed description on fatty acid composition with 21 compounds reported on its lipophilic extract [<xref rid="B43-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>]. Furthermore, 10 compounds were retrieved from the methanol, ethyl acetate, hexane root extract [<xref rid="B56-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>] and aerial parts essential oil [<xref rid="B57-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>] of <italic toggle="yes">C. ciliaris</italic>. In the case of <italic toggle="yes">C. dactylon</italic>, 13 compounds were obtained from the surface cuticular wax [<xref rid="B58-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>], hydroalcoholic extract [<xref rid="B64-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>,<xref rid="B65-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>] and ethanol extract (80%) [<xref rid="B66-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>] (<xref ref-type="app" rid="app1-ijms-20-01067">Table S1</xref>). In <italic toggle="yes">H. mucronatum</italic>, from methanol/chloroform (1:2 <italic toggle="yes">v</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">v</italic>) extract [<xref rid="B67-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>], eight compounds were reported while in <italic toggle="yes">L. multiflorum</italic> only five were described (<xref ref-type="app" rid="app1-ijms-20-01067">Table S1</xref>). At last, 10 fatty acids were retrieved from carbon tetrachloride extract (It should be emphasized that the use of carbon tetrachloride is not recommended due to its toxicity.) [<xref rid="B59-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>] of <italic toggle="yes">S. spontaneum</italic>, eight from the hexane extract of <italic toggle="yes">C. gayana</italic> [<xref rid="B68-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>] and five by direct acidic trans-esterification from <italic toggle="yes">S. patens</italic> [<xref rid="B69-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>].</p><p>Fatty acids reported in halophytes from the Poaceae comprised of saturated mid-chain and long chain fatty acids and unsaturated long chain fatty acids. Among these, three unsaturated and five polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) were reported (<xref ref-type="app" rid="app1-ijms-20-01067">Table S1</xref>). These compounds play key roles in human physiology as they are building blocks of phospholipids and glycolipids [<xref rid="B70-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>]. Additionally, they also change proteins by covalent attachment, which targets them to membrane locations and their derivatives serve as hormones and intracellular messengers [<xref rid="B70-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>]. Palmitic acid (<bold>41</bold>), a saturated fatty acid, reported in <italic toggle="yes">A. donax</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">C. ciliaris</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">C. dactylon</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">C. gayana</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">H. mucronatum</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">S. patens</italic>, is known for its ability to increase blood HDL-cholesterol levels without changes in the overall cholesterol/HDL-cholesterol ratio [<xref rid="B71-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>]. Its antioxidant, hemolytic and anticarcinogenic activities were also described [<xref rid="B56-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>].</p><p>Polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) contribute greatly to the resistance to photoinhibition of halophyte species, with their concentration increased in membrane lipids, which enhances the tolerance of photosystem II to salt stress [<xref rid="B72-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>]. This could explain the wide distribution of these compounds among halophytic grasses (<xref ref-type="app" rid="app1-ijms-20-01067">Table S1</xref> and <xref ref-type="fig" rid="ijms-20-01067-f002">Figure 2</xref>).
