The combined anthraquinones are used as indicator compounds to characterize the quality of this plant, which should have a minimum content of 0.10%, as delineated in the Pharmacopoeia of the People׳s Republic of China; this percentage is calculated based on the amounts of emodin and physcion. However, chrysophanol-8-O-β-<sc>d</sc>-glucopyranoside and other combined anthraquinones (<xref rid="bib185" ref-type="bibr">Yang et al., 1998</xref>) are also found in <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic>. Therefore, the development of a method to accurately measure the amount of the combined anthraquinones requires further investigation. Although we summarized 102 ingredients in this plant, this number is quite limited at present, and future research on the chemical composition of the plant should be more precise.</p><p id="p0295">In traditional Chinese medicine, <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> is commonly used with other herbs. However, modern experiments have validated that this plant alone exhibits significant pharmacological effects. Therefore, it is important to investigate the molecular mechanisms of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> combined with other herbs based on traditional uses. Second, the pharmacological effects of some traditional uses of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> have been validated in recent studies, but these studies were primarily conducted <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic>. Thus, the effects of these compounds need to be verified <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic>. Third, to date, approximately 100 chemical compounds have been isolated from this plant; however, the pharmacological effects of only a few of the ingredients, such as 2,3,5,4′-tetrahydroxystilbene-2-O-β-<sc>d</sc>-glucopyranoside, resveratrol, polydatin, emodin, and physcion, have been studied. Some stilbenes and quinones, the major components of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum,</italic> have not been sufficiently researched in terms of their pharmacological effects, and this is also true for emodin glycosides, physcion glycosides, and some stilbenes, among others.</p><p id="p0300">The number of reports of the adverse effects of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic>, such as hepatotoxicity, nephrotoxicity, and embryonic toxicity, is increasing. The primary toxicity among these is hepatotoxicity, which is of wide concern worldwide. However, the identity if the component(s) that causes hepatotoxicity remains unclear. Some studies have shown that free anthraquinones, such as emodin and physcion, were the main hepatotoxicants (<xref rid="bib63" ref-type="bibr">Li et al., 2012a</xref>, <xref rid="bib141" ref-type="bibr">Sun et al., 2010</xref>). Other studies have demonstrated that the hepatotoxicity of the water extract was stronger than that of the ethanol and acetone extracts of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> (<xref rid="bib164" ref-type="bibr">Wu et al., 2012</xref>, <xref rid="bib54" ref-type="bibr">Huang et al., 2011</xref>). Moreover, the hepatotoxicity of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> was also stronger than that of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum Praeparata.</italic> The emodin and physcion contents increased after processing of this herb (<xref rid="bib209" ref-type="bibr">Zhang et al., 2003</xref>, <xref rid="bib15" ref-type="bibr">Chen et al., 2012c</xref>, <xref rid="bib88" ref-type="bibr">Liu et al., 2005a</xref>, <xref rid="bib220" ref-type="bibr">Zhou, 2005</xref>, <xref rid="bib89" ref-type="bibr">Liu et al., 2005b</xref>), and the contents of these components in the ethanol and acetone extracts of this herb were higher than those of the water extract. Thus, the above-described results appear to be contradictory.</p><p id="p0305">Another issue is that <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> exhibits both hepatoprotection and hepatotoxicity, which appears to be contradictory. Based on an examination of the literature, we speculate that the possible reasons are as follows. The first is the size of the administered dose. High doses are more likely to result in liver toxicity, and low doses may show liver protection. The second is the length of the delivery time: long-term drug delivery can easily show liver toxicity, whereas short-term drug delivery may result in liver protection. Third, a study revealed that TSG and physcion likely attenuated the cytotoxic effect of emodin (<xref rid="bib195" ref-type="bibr">Yu et al., 2011</xref>); therefore, the different experimental results may be due to the different TSG, physcion and emodin ratios in the herbs. Fourth, TSG and emodin are also hepatoprotective due to their antioxidant activity. Thus, this issue requires further study.</p><p id="p0310">In conclusion, <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> is one of the most popular traditional Chinese medicines, and it is an ingredient of many patent medicines or prescriptions.
