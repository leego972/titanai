Further research using objective voice measures is warranted to determine PDT's efficacy as a voice sparing procedure.</p></sec></sec><sec id="sec5"><title>Cryotherapy</title><sec id="sec5.1"><title>Mechanism</title><p id="p0240">The medical applications of cold have been in use for thousands of years, dating back to the ancient Egyptians use of cold for its anti-inflammatory properties.<xref rid="bib82" ref-type="bibr"><sup>82</sup></xref> It continues to be a popular method of treatment with high potential due to its healing properties. In recent years, cryotherapy has been applied to laryngeal surgery as an adjuvant therapy to laser surgery. Cryotherapy consists of using a cryogen, often liquid nitrogen, to apply extreme cold to a target area.<xref rid="bib83" ref-type="bibr"><sup>83</sup></xref> Cryotherapy uses a cryoprobe which acts as an applicator through which the cryogen is delivered to the target site. Liquid nitrogen is currently the most common choice of cryogen due to its minimum achievable temperature (−197 °C) being appropriate for benign and malignant lesions.<xref rid="bib82" ref-type="bibr"><sup>82</sup></xref> Cryotherapy achieves its tissue destructive effects by using extreme cold to damage blood vessels and capillaries in the target area leading to ischemia and eventually tissue necrosis.<xref rid="bib83" ref-type="bibr"><sup>83</sup></xref> The formation of ice crystals also occurs, which creates an osmotic gradient that draws water extracellularly and leads to cell rupture. Typically, cryotherapy consists of multiple freeze–thaw cycles. The length and target temperature of these cycles depends on the tissue type and whether cell target cells are benign or cancerous.<xref rid="bib83" ref-type="bibr"><sup>83</sup></xref> Multiple sessions of cryotherapy may be necessary depending on the type of the lesion.</p><p id="p0245">As with any surgical technique, cryotherapy requires careful attention to several factors in order to achieve acceptable surgical outcomes. The first consideration regards isotherms or the spherical area surrounding the site of application.<xref rid="bib83" ref-type="bibr"><sup>83</sup></xref> The effects of extreme cold spreads throughout the tissue from the center of application with an equal radius in all directions. Surgeons must have adequate knowledge regarding the properties of isotherms in the target tissue to accurately achieve the target temperature. Another important factor to consider is that air has low thermal conductivity. This limits the cryoprobe's effectiveness as its distance from the target area increases.<xref rid="bib83" ref-type="bibr"><sup>83</sup></xref> Finally, no excised tissue remains after cryotherapy which limits determination of adequate surgical margins.<xref rid="bib83" ref-type="bibr"><sup>83</sup></xref></p></sec><sec id="sec5.2"><title>Tissue effects</title><p id="p0250">The primary value of cryotherapy stems from its excellent healing properties, resulting in minimal scarring and desirable postoperative voice quality.<xref rid="bib82" ref-type="bibr"><sup>82</sup></xref> Because scarring is the primary cause of poor postoperative voice quality, cryotherapy's potential to produce minimal scarring yields high potential as a treatment for laryngeal lesions. Gong et al<xref rid="bib15" ref-type="bibr"><sup>15</sup></xref> found that cryotherapy in a rabbit model simulates an environment similar to that of the fetal wound-healing environment, which is characterized by minimal inflammation, rapid re-epithelialization, and reduced collagen formation. Knott et al<xref rid="bib84" ref-type="bibr"><sup>84</sup></xref> found both reduced collagen formation with more organized structure after application of cryotherapy, along with decreased keratinization.
