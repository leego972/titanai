As previously discussed, the HPLC-based analysis of the aqueous extract revealed that benzoic acid <bold>24</bold>, caffeine <bold>46</bold>, <italic toggle="yes">o</italic>-coumaric acid <bold>36,</bold> quercetin <bold>3</bold>, naringenin <bold>42</bold>, and kaempferol <bold>22</bold> were the most abundant.</p><p>Although screening of these pure identified compounds was not carried out, these identified compounds were reported to have antifungal activities. For instance, benzoic acid <bold>24</bold> and its hydroxyl derivatives are known to protect tomato plants from early blight disease caused by <italic toggle="yes">Alternaria solani</italic> [<xref rid="B51-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>,<xref rid="B88-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">88</xref>]. Benzoic acid <bold>24</bold> and <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-hydroxybenzoic acid <bold>31</bold> effectively inhibited the mycelial growth of <italic toggle="yes">A. solani</italic> with IC<sub>50</sub> values of 44.69 and 58.80 ppm, respectively. Furthermore, benzoic acid <bold>24</bold> has been used as a preservative against fungi that cause food spoilage. Furthermore, benzoic acid <bold>24</bold> and its hydroxy derivatives have been known to have broad antifungal activity against soil borne fungi, such as <italic toggle="yes">Fusarium</italic> sp. [<xref rid="B89-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">89</xref>], and seed-borne pathogens, such as <italic toggle="yes">Aspergillus flavus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Penicillium citrinum</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Alternaria alternata</italic> [<xref rid="B90-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">90</xref>].</p><p>Therefore, benzoic acid <bold>24</bold> and its derivatives in the extract support the observed antifungal activities. The high concentration of benzoic acid <bold>24</bold> indicated the plant’s defence mechanism against fungal infection. Interestingly, benzoic acid <bold>24</bold> was also reported to be produced by the soil bacteria <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus licheniformis</italic> MH48 as a defensive second metabolite against pathogenic fungal infections, with a minimum inhibitory concentration of 128 mg/mL [<xref rid="B88-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">88</xref>]. The identified flavonoids, such as naringenin <bold>42</bold> [<xref rid="B91-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">91</xref>], quercetin <bold>3,</bold> and kaempferol <bold>22</bold> [<xref rid="B92-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">92</xref>,<xref rid="B93-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">93</xref>], are also known natural antifungal compounds. Therefore, their presence would synergistically support the fugal activities observed in this aqueous flower extract. </p><p>Gumgumjee et al. [<xref rid="B17-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>] investigated the inhibitory activities of their EtOH extract of leaves collected in Saudi Arabia on the growth of fungi known to cause human infections; these were <italic toggle="yes">Aspergillus niger, A. fumigatus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">A. flavus</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">C. albican</italic> (<xref rid="molecules-28-04396-t003" ref-type="table">Table 3</xref>). The study was investigated using the disc diffusion method against the four fungi species at 200 mg/mL concentration compared to the positive controls amphotericin and nystatin (10 mg/disc). In decreasing order, the extract was active against the fungi; <italic toggle="yes">A. fumigatus&gt; C. albicans&gt; A. flavus&gt; A. niger.</italic> The diameters of the inhibition zones of the extract against these fungi strains were 25.67, 23.33, 21.33, and 20.00 mm, respectively, compared to the average inhibition zones of amphotericin and nystatin of 27.00 and 29.75 mm, respectively.
