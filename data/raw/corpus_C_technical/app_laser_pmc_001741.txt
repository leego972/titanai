This shift from a pro-inflammatory to a reparative macrophage phenotype improves wound healing and reduces fibrosis in diabetic tissues [<xref rid="B170-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">170</xref>]. This immunomodulatory effect is particularly important for diabetic patients, as chronic low-grade inflammation can lead to insulin resistance and vascular complications. Furthermore, PBMT has been shown to reduce levels of inflammatory cytokines, such as IL-6, TNF-α, and MCP-1, which are increased in patients with type 1 and type 2 diabetes [<xref rid="B22-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>]. Additionally, laser therapy has been shown to impact mucosal immunity by modulating dendritic cell activity and enhancing regulatory T-cell responses. This promotes immune tolerance and reduces autoimmune activation [<xref rid="B74-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>,<xref rid="B201-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">201</xref>].</p><p>The anti-inflammatory, antioxidant and metabolic regulatory mechanisms of photobiomodulation in diabetes are presented in <xref rid="ijms-27-02078-f006" ref-type="fig">Figure 6</xref>.</p><p>Furthermore, neuroprotective effects have been observed in models of brain inflammation, spinal cord injury, and neurodegeneration [<xref rid="B202-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">202</xref>,<xref rid="B203-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">203</xref>]. These anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective actions provide a mechanistic basis for the use of PBMT in treating both metabolic and neurological complications of diabetes [<xref rid="B204-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">204</xref>]. Emerging evidence also suggests that PBMT may enhance neurogenesis and synaptic plasticity, offering potential benefits for cognitive impairment in diabetes [<xref rid="B205-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">205</xref>,<xref rid="B206-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">206</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec8dot4-ijms-27-02078"><title>8.4. Mast Cells Are Another Important Cellular Target</title><p>As they are reservoirs of histamine, proteases, and cytokines, mast cells play a pivotal role in orchestrating inflammation, angiogenesis, and wound repair. PBMT modulates mast cell degranulation through mitochondrial signalling pathways, thereby influencing histamine release and angiogenic factor expression [<xref rid="B207-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">207</xref>,<xref rid="B208-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">208</xref>]. This regulation improves local vascular responses and maintains tissue homeostasis while limiting excessive inflammation. In diabetic wounds, where mast cell dysregulation contributes to delayed healing, PBMT may restore balanced degranulation and promote effective tissue regeneration [<xref rid="B208-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">208</xref>,<xref rid="B209-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">209</xref>].</p><p>Influence on cellular proliferation and tissue regeneration: LLLT promotes cellular proliferation and tissue regeneration, both of which are essential for effective wound healing in diabetes patients [<xref rid="B69-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>]. PBMT increases the availability of ATP and modulates NO-dependent signalling, thereby stimulating fibroblast proliferation, keratinocyte migration, and angiogenesis [<xref rid="B125-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">125</xref>]. Clinical and experimental studies have documented enhanced growth factor expression, including VEGF and fibroblast growth factor (FGF), as well as stem cell activation, following PBMT [<xref rid="B113-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>,<xref rid="B210-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">210</xref>]. These effects are mediated through redox-sensitive transcription factors and mitochondrial signalling cascades, which coordinate cellular responses to injury and metabolic stress [<xref rid="B157-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">157</xref>].</p><p>These reparative processes are particularly relevant in the management of diabetic foot ulcers, as PBMT accelerates wound closure, reduces the risk of infection, and improves functional tissue recovery [<xref rid="B211-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">211</xref>,<xref rid="B212-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">212</xref>]. As well as promoting re-epithelialisation and collagen synthesis, PBMT has been shown to enhance neovascularisation and restore extracellular matrix integrity, both of which are often compromised in chronic diabetic wounds [<xref rid="B112-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>,<xref rid="B185-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">185</xref>,<xref rid="B213-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">213</xref>]. While LLLT exerts local effects, systemic improvements in inflammatory balance and vascular function have also been observed, highlighting its dual role in localised tissue repair and systemic metabolic regulation [<xref rid="B131-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">131</xref>].</p><p>However, the risk of tissue damage when parameters are not selected appropriately emphasises the necessity of standardising treatment protocols [<xref rid="B214-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">214</xref>].
