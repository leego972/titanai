Use of magnetic nanoparticle heating in the treatment of breast cancer.

Hilger I(1), Hergt R, Kaiser WA.

Author information:
(1)Institute for Diagnostic and Interventional Radiology, 
Friedrich-Schiller-University Hospital Jena, D-07740 Jena, Germany. 
ingrid.hilger@med.uni-jena.de

Magnetic nanoparticles are promising tools for the minimal invasive elimination 
of small tumours in the breast using magnetically-induced heating. The approach 
complies with the increasing demand for breast conserving therapies and has the 
advantage of offering a selective and refined tuning of the degree of energy 
deposition allowing an adequate temperature control at the target. The 
biophysical basis of the approach, the magnetic and structural properties of 
magnetic nanoparticles are reviewed. Results with model targets and in vivo 
experiments in laboratory animals are reported.
