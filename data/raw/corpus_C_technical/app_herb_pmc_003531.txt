# PMC: ethnobotany medicinal plants Africa Asia — Overview

relevance [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR149">149</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR153">153</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR156">156</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR170">170</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR185">185</xref>].</p><p id="Par188">FL analysis complements the Informant Consensus Factor (ICF) by pinpointing species that are specifically and consistently trusted for particular conditions. High-FL species are strong candidates for pharmacological investigation, as repeated independent use across multiple informants often correlates with the presence of bioactive compounds with reliable therapeutic effects [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>]. Moreover, high-FL plants are critical in conservation planning, as overharvesting culturally and therapeutically important species could threaten both biodiversity and local healthcare practices. Studies across East Africa, Asia, Europe, and Australia underscore the importance of combining ethnobotanical knowledge with sustainable management, cultivation, and drug discovery initiatives [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR20">20</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR78">78</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR137">137</xref>].</p><p id="Par189">Hence, while acknowledging the methodological limitations of FL, the analysis in Sheikh District highlights that <italic toggle="yes">Vachellia bussei</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Balanites aegyptiaca</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Melia azedarach</italic> are culturally and therapeutically significant, with strong agreement among local informants regarding their effectiveness. These findings provide an evidence-based framework for prioritizing species for pharmacological research, conservation efforts, and integration into local healthcare initiatives. By emphasizing high-fidelity species, this study demonstrates that, despite its limitations, FL is a useful tool for bridging indigenous knowledge and modern scientific inquiry.</p><p id="Par190">
