Here, the ethyl acetate fraction demonstrated the best anti-feedant activity by 80% against <italic toggle="yes">Helicoverpa armigera</italic> at the highest concentration (0.5 mg/mL), outperforming water and petroleum ether fractions (<xref rid="ref-35" ref-type="bibr">Liu et al., 2019</xref>). In a similar investigation, <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> displayed antifeedant and growth-inhibitory activity against <italic toggle="yes">Henosepilachna vigintioctomaculata</italic> through the isolation of three pairs of novel enantiomeric pyrrole alkaloids its leaves. The results revealed enantioselectivity with all enantiomers demonstrating both antifeedant and growth-inhibitory activities having the R configuration at C-2′ exhibiting the most potent effects (<xref rid="ref-34" ref-type="bibr">Liu et al., 2021</xref>). Last but not the least, the extracts of <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> have been utilized as insecticides to control lepidopterous larvae and aphids on fruit and vegetable crops due to their high toxicity, repellent properties, anti-feeding effects, and stomach poisoning effects, thereby mitigating the impact of these insects on crops and reducing economic losses (<xref rid="ref-76" ref-type="bibr">Zhang et al., 2012</xref>).</p></sec></sec><sec><title>Conclusion and Future Perspectives</title><p>Due to phenotypic plasticity (<xref rid="ref-73" ref-type="bibr">Yu et al., 2021</xref>) and other previously mentioned adaptive mechanisms, <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> can withstand both extreme and mild environmental conditions, consequently resulting in the displacement and reduction of native species as well as changes to the vegetation structure (<xref rid="ref-26" ref-type="bibr">Hejda, Pyšek &amp; Jarošík, 2009</xref>). This study unveils a diverse array of phytochemical constituents in <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic>, showcasing its potential significance in the pharmacological/medical and agricultural sectors. This review highlights the importance of <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> as an antioxidant against free radicals in humans which can aid in preventing cell damage and cardiovascular-related diseases, enhancing its pharmacological/medical relevance. In animals, <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> contains the notable anti-inflammatory compound sitosteryl glucoside, reported to be effective against TPA-induced mouse ear edema and Carrageenan-induced paw edema, suggesting potential applications in veterinary medicine. Moreover, the abundance of phenolic compounds in <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> contributes to its anti-carcinogenic properties against human malignant cell lines, specifically breast adenocarcinoma, cervix squamous cancer, and uterine cancer. The review also highlights antimicrobial activities against both human pathogens and phytopathogens. Additionally, <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> have the potentials to serve as herbicides, insecticides, and pesticides, promoting sustainable agriculture that promotes a healthy environment.</p><p>Despite being considered invasive, this study emphasizes the need for future research to fully comprehend appropriate exploration methods, mitigating the impact of under-exploration and paving the way for maximal exploration. Continuous isolation and identification of different phytochemicals with relevant bioactivities are crucial in harnessing <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> potentials particularly in the field of medicine (ethnobotany) and agriculture. From the perspectives of ethnopharmacology and phytochemistry, <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> remains insufficiently researched in this biome. The study advocates for a focus on maximizing the potential benefits of <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> rather than solely concentrating on its invasiveness.</p></sec></body><back><sec
