2024</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><sec id="Sec1"><title>Introduction</title><p id="Par12">The Haitian diaspora is estimated to measure 1.7 million people globally, with the largest population of Haitians outside of Haiti, over 700,000 people, living in the USA [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR1">1</xref>]. Economic and political instability in Haiti has driven waves of immigration to the US, with peaks following the aftermath of the Haitian revolution (1791–1810), US occupation of Haiti (1915–1934), and during (1957–1986) and after (1986–1994) the Duvalier dictatorships [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR2">2</xref>]. In the past two decades, natural disasters and sociopolitical crises such as the 2010 earthquake and the political instability following the 2021 assassination of President Jovenel Moïse prompted US Homeland Security to grant Haitian migrants Temporary Protection Status [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR3">3</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR4">4</xref>]. The Haitian diaspora in the US is primarily concentrated in Florida and New York City (NYC) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR2">2</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR5">5</xref>]. Haitians comprise one of the largest immigrant communities in NYC with an estimated over 80 thousand people [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR6">6</xref>].</p><p id="Par13">As people migrate to new environments, they bring their ethnomedicinal traditions, including medicinal plants, with them [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR7">7</xref>]. Previous research has shown that the Haitian diaspora continues to use medicinal plants after migration [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR8">8</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR10">10</xref>]. However, until now there has not been an ethnobotanical survey focused on the Haitian community in NYC. The use of medicinal plants is not only an essential aspect of Haitian culture, but also has a deep history as a method to combat lack of access to biomedicine in Haiti [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR11">11</xref>]. As Haitians and other immigrants come to the USA, many experience new barriers to healthcare such as immigration status, lack of medical insurance, English proficiency, or sufficient income [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>].
