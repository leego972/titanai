Zhang et al. reported a NIR rechargeable “optical battery” implant for irradiation-free PDT by loading NaYF<sub>4</sub>:Yb<sup>3+</sup>,Tm<sup>3+</sup>, SrAl<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub>: Eu<sup>2+</sup>,Dy<sup>3+</sup>, rose bengal into biocompatible polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR115">115</xref>]. In such a system, 980 nm NIR laser can be firstly excited NaYF<sub>4</sub>:Yb<sup>3+</sup>,Tm<sup>3+</sup> with UV/blue emission, then the activated SAO, in turn, emits green light to trigger rose Bengal for ROS generation. The implants can be repeatably charged by 5 s NIR light but for 30 min effective PDT time, which effectively generate ROS for tumor inhibition.</p><p id="Par32">Inspired by the superior penetration depth of X-ray for the activation of PLNPs, Solberg et al. first employed PLNPs as the photon transducer to achieve X-ray-induced PDT [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR116">116</xref>]. He used Tetrabromorhodamine-123 (TBrRh123) to conjugate on ZnS:Cu,Co PLNPs, where the emission spectrum of ZnS:Cu,Co was overlapped with the absorption spectrum of TBrRh123. After the X-ray excitation, the nanoplatform continuously generated ROS for human prostate cancer cells killing. Later, Xie et al. reported Merocyanine540 (MC540)-loaded silica-coated-SrAl<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub>:Eu<sup>2+</sup> (SAO) nanoplatform for in vivo PDT, where the SAO could effectively convert X-rays photons to visible photons for activating MC540 to generate <sup>1</sup>O<sub>2</sub> and suppress tumor growth [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR117">117</xref>]. However, this therapeutic effect was achieved with intratumorally injected nanoparticles on subcutaneous tumor models. Then Xie et al. later reported 2,3-naphthalocyanine and LiGa<sub>5</sub>O<sub>8</sub>:Cr<sup>3+</sup> co-loaded mesoporous silica nanoparticles (NC-LGO:Cr@mSiO<sub>2</sub>) for PDT of H1299 orthotopic lung cancer [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR69">69</xref>]. The nanoformulation was passively accumulated to lung tumors. Upon X-ray’s irradiation, the tumors were obtained efficient inhibition. Furthermore, Chen et al. reported another nanoplatform by loading silicon phthalocyanine into mesoporous Zn<sub>3</sub>Ga<sub>2</sub>GeO<sub>8</sub>:Cr<sup>3+</sup>,Yb<sup>3+</sup>,Er<sup>3+</sup> (mZGGOs) for X-ray-induced PersL imaging and effective suppression of orthotopic hepatic tumors [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR118">118</xref>]. Due to the safety concern of high-dose X-ray, Yang et al. developed a low-dose X-ray-activated PLNP-mediated PDT nanoplatform for renewable cancer treatment (Fig. <xref rid="Fig13" ref-type="fig">13</xref>a) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR119">119</xref>]. The synthesized ZGO:Cr/W exhibited stronger PersL and excellent X-ray absorption, allowing for more photons to activate Zn(II) phthalocyanine tetrasulfonic acid (ZnPcS4). Currently, PLNPs with different nanostructure have been explored as nanocarriers for chemodrugs loading and delivery. Several design strategies have been proposed on PLNPs, mainly resting on the physical absorption of porous nanostructure (Table <xref rid="Tab3" ref-type="table">3</xref>). Firstly, PLNPs with their own mesoporous structure have attracted considerable attention for drug delivery because of their high cavity volumes and specific surface area. For example, Zhang et al. designed hollow ZnGa<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub>:Cr<sup>3+</sup> with high doxorubicin (DOX) loading for PersL imaging-guided chemotherapy [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR54">54</xref>]. Lv et al. developed raspberry-like mesoporous Zn<sub>1.07</sub>Ga<sub>2.34</sub>Si<sub>0.98</sub>O<sub>6.56</sub>:Cr<sub>0.01</sub> nanostructures for enhanced PersL imaging and chemotherapy of tumor [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR66">66</xref>].
