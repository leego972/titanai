In line with the reviewer’s recommendation, the comparison has been refined to emphasize ecologically comparable dryland and pastoral systems, while minimizing emphasis on humid forest ecosystems that differ substantially from the semi-arid environment of Sheikh District.</p><p id="Par222">The RSI values ranged from 0.62% (Sekela) to 15.15% (Somali), demonstrating generally low to moderate similarity across regions and highlighting the localized nature of ethnomedicinal knowledge. Relatively higher RSI values were observed in ecologically and geographically proximate dryland areas, including Dire Dawa (12.50%), Jigjiga town (12.86%), Jigjiga district (11.43%), Babile (11.11%), Marsabit (11.84%), Turkana (10.00%), Dirre (8.65%), and Yalo (6.50%) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR39">39</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR56">56</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR57">57</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR75">75</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR103">103</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR106">106</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR120">120</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR128">128</xref>]. These findings suggest that shared semi-arid climatic conditions, similar vegetation types, and historical socio-cultural interactions contribute to comparable medicinal plant use patterns.</p><p id="Par223">Notably, relatively high similarity with Turkana (10.00%) and Marsabit (11.84%) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR39">39</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR106">106</xref>] is consistent with their location within the East African arid and semi-arid lands (ASALs), where pastoralist communities rely heavily on drought-tolerant woody species and shrubs for healthcare. Comparable ecological pressures in these regions may encourage the selection of similar plant taxa and therapeutic applications.</p><p id="Par224">Moderate RSI values (approximately 3–6%) were recorded in Samburu County (6.90%), Raya Kobo (5.36%), Yalo (6.50%), Melit area (4.55%), Tabora region (4.40%), North-eastern Tanzania (4.71%), Jabitehnan (4.76%), Dega Damot (4.26%), Jawi (4.27%), Eastern Uganda (3.85%), and the Maasai pastoral communities (3.65%) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR32">32</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR39">39</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR42">42</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR47">47</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR48">48</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR65">65</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR66">66</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR82">82</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR95">95</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR120">120</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR205">205</xref>]. These values indicate partial overlap in both plant species and medicinal uses. While certain ecological similarities exist, variations in ethnic traditions, knowledge transmission systems, and disease treatment preferences likely limit stronger convergence.</p><p id="Par225">Low RSI values (&lt; 3%) were documented in several ecologically distinct or geographically distant areas, including Andracha (0.67%), Sekela (0.62%), Guraferda (0.91%), Fofa (1.52%), Meketewa (1.77%), Bita (1.92%), Sodo (2.13%), Sheka (2.40%), Quara (2.53%), Mana Angetu (3.24%), and Artuma Fursi (3.28%) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR24">24</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR33">33</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR35">35</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR44">44</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR80">80</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR81">81</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR87">87</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR173">173</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR189">189</xref>]. Many of these areas are characterized by humid highland or forest ecosystems that contrast sharply with the dryland ecology of Sheikh District. The limited similarity likely reflects differences in floristic composition as well as distinct cultural practices and ethnomedical traditions.</p><p id="Par226">The comparatively higher RSI value reported for the Somali region (15.15%) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>] may reflect strong ecological and cultural continuity across Somali-inhabited territories, including shared pastoral livelihoods, mobility patterns, and long-standing exchange of traditional knowledge. Such interactions may facilitate not only the sharing of plant species but also the transmission of similar therapeutic applications.</p><p id="Par227">Hence, the RSI analysis reinforces the ecological specificity of medicinal plant knowledge in Sheikh District.
