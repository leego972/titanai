Moreover, Moosavi et al. have prepared N-doped titania nanoparticles and used them to treat Leukemia K562 cells under visible light<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR19">19</xref></sup>. They demonstrated the direct dependence of treatment efficiency on nanoparticle concentration and visible light doses. Maher et al. has reported that the interaction of laser light with H<sub>2</sub>Ti<sub>3</sub>O<sub>7</sub> NTs (with a mean crystallite size of 16.0 nm) in the vicinity of lung and prostate cancer and the effects of various concentrations of bare H<sub>2</sub>Ti<sub>3</sub>O<sub>7</sub> NTs at different incubation times, laser fluence, and laser wavelength to estimate the effectiveness of the photodynamic therapy by the NTs<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR5">5</xref></sup>. Treating lung and prostate cancers (A549 cells and LNCap cells) was the main goal of the paper and their results indicated that H<sub>2</sub>Ti<sub>3</sub>O<sub>7</sub> NTs can be a promising candidate for treating lung and prostate cancers.</p><p id="Par6">Gold nanoparticles (Au NPs) coated on metal oxide nanoparticles like titania nanotubes (TiO<sub>2</sub> NTs) have emerged as a promising combination to obtain synergic effects of photodynamic (PDT) and photothermal (PTT) therapy in cancer treatment. The integration of Au NPs with TiO<sub>2</sub> nanotubes offers synergistic benefits for enhanced PDT. Au NPs possess unique plasmonic properties, such as localized surface plasmon resonance (LSPR), which can enhance the generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) during PDT<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR20">20</xref></sup>. The coating of Au NPs onto TiO<sub>2</sub> NTs provides dual functionality, TiO<sub>2</sub> acting as a carrier for photosensitizers and Au NPs acting as enhancers for ROS generation and light absorption leading to PTT. Additionally, the surface plasmon resonance of Au NPs enables improved light absorption and energy transfer to the surrounding photosensitizers, resulting in enhanced therapeutic effects. Au NPs can absorb incident light and turn into powerful sources of heat and ablation of tumors<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR21">21</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR22">22</xref></sup>. Therefore, adding Au NPs to H<sub>2</sub>Ti<sub>3</sub>O<sub>7</sub> NTs can be an excellent choice to achieve better results in non-invasive treatments. For instance, Zhu et al. found that gold nanoparticles decorated titania nanotubes exhibit enhanced antibacterial activity and a preferable effect in stopping the growth of Escherichia coli and Staphylococcus aureus even in dark conditions which was attributed to the photocatalytic memory effect<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR23">23</xref></sup>. Yang et al. have synthesized carbon-nanodot coated on TiO<sub>2</sub> nanotubes and used them for photodynamic therapy under a laser beam at 650 nm in vitro and in vivo assays<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR24">24</xref></sup>. They found that the nanocomposite was an excellent therapeutic agent and the reason for its success was explained by them.</p><p id="Par7">According to the mentioned advantages, in the paper, a novel and facile photodynamic and photothermal agent based on gold and H<sub>2</sub>Ti<sub>3</sub>O<sub>7</sub> nanotubes (Au-H<sub>2</sub>Ti<sub>3</sub>O<sub>7</sub> NTs) is proposed to treat prostate and lung cancers through dark and laser irradiation conditions. According to the authors’ best knowledge, no reports can be found in the scientific literature on the investigation of the nanostructure in non-invasive cancer treatment until now. The results declare the success of these nanoparticles in non-invasive cancer treatment, especially prostate cancer (LNCap cells), and introduce it as a worthy candidate for the treatment.</p></sec><sec id="Sec2"><title>Material and method</title><sec id="Sec3"><title>Synthesis and characterization of Au-H2Ti3O7 NTs</title><p id="Par8">In the first step, H<sub>2</sub>Ti<sub>3</sub>O<sub>7</sub> NTs were synthesized using the method reported in<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR2">2</xref></sup> and then the co-precipitation method was employed to add Au nanoparticles and provide Au-H<sub>2</sub>Ti<sub>3</sub>O<sub>7</sub> NTs<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR25">25</xref></sup>. The characterization of Au-H<sub>2</sub>Ti<sub>3</sub>O<sub>7</sub> NTs was conducted through the Takram N1-541 Raman spectrometer and Shimadzu 8400 S Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectrometer to explore the structure and vibration modes of Au- H<sub>2</sub>Ti<sub>3</sub>O<sub>7</sub> NTs. The crystalline structure of Au-H<sub>2</sub>Ti<sub>3</sub>O<sub>7</sub> NTs was investigated by X-ray diffraction (XRD), ADVANCE-D8 model, with Cuka radiation source (λ = 1.5406 Å) using silicon as a standard reference material.
