According to the National Center for Health Statistics database [<xref rid="B328-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">328</xref>], evidence that stroke is a leading cause of death for Americans is as follows:<list list-type="bullet"><list-item><p>Every year, more than 795,000 people in the USA have a stroke.</p></list-item><list-item><p>Every 40 s, someone in the United States has a stroke. Every 3 min and 11 s, someone dies of a stroke in the USA.</p></list-item><list-item><p>About 87% of all strokes are ischemic strokes, in which blood flow to the brain is blocked.</p></list-item><list-item><p>Stroke is a leading cause of severe long-term disability.</p></list-item><list-item><p>Stroke reduces mobility in more than half of stroke survivors aged 65 and older.</p></list-item><list-item><p>In the USA, in 2022, 1 in 6 deaths (17.5%) from cardiovascular disease was due to stroke.</p></list-item></list></p><p>Post-stroke brain fatigue (PSBF) is a ubiquitous and overwhelming symptom for most stroke survivors, with a prevalence ranging from 42 to 53% [<xref rid="B329-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">329</xref>]. PSBF is a common subjective experience characterized by extreme and persistent feelings of fatigue, weakness, or exhaustion after a stroke, occurring mentally, physically, or both, which is not alleviated by general rest [<xref rid="B70-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>,<xref rid="B330-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">330</xref>].</p><p>Post-ischemic stroke neuroinflammation, distinct from systemic inflammation, involves active resolution processes mediated by arachidonic acid derivatives [<xref rid="B331-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">331</xref>] and has crucial impacts on neural stem cells for effective brain repair [<xref rid="B332-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">332</xref>]. Acute inflammation is a protective reaction by the immune system in response to tissue damage or invading pathogens. When the acute inflammatory response is not resolved, it can contribute to organ pathology and amplify many widely occurring chronic inflammatory clinical phenotypes, including neurodegenerative diseases, metabolic syndrome, asthma, allergy, diabetes, inflammatory processes of aging, arthritis, cancers, organ fibrosis, and cardiovascular and periodontal diseases [<xref rid="B331-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">331</xref>].</p><p>Neuroinflammation plays a role in both secondary brain injury and neurorepair post-stroke, affecting neurogenesis [<xref rid="B333-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">333</xref>]. Inflammatory impacts on neurogenesis are complex, with positive and negative effects, influencing CNS injury and repair [<xref rid="B334-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">334</xref>]. Acute neuroinflammation promotes neurogenesis and neuronal survival [<xref rid="B335-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">335</xref>,<xref rid="B336-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">336</xref>].</p><p>The recovery from a stroke is mediated primarily by angiogenesis and neurogenesis at the tissue and cellular levels and can be targeted for therapeutic intervention [<xref rid="B12-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>,<xref rid="B337-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">337</xref>]. Utilizing neurogenesis as a therapeutic target for self-repair and recovery from stroke damage is challenging due to complex interactions with neuroinflammation and angiogenesis, particularly the time-dependent effects of immune cells, cytokines, and chemokines in the post-stroke environment [<xref rid="B338-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">338</xref>].</p><p>Cell death induced by ischemic stroke is characterized by the dysregulation of metabolic processes and activating glutamate receptors, triggering an increase in intracellular calcium concentration, and ultimately leading to cellular death [<xref rid="B339-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">339</xref>]. Neurogenesis, or the birth of new neurons, is a key process in post-stroke recovery and repair in the infarct and surrounding areas of the damaged brain region [<xref rid="B340-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">340</xref>,<xref rid="B341-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">341</xref>]. This involves migrating neural stem cells originating from the sub-ventricular zone (SVZ) and the sub-granular zone (SGZ) of the dentate gyrus [<xref rid="B34-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>,<xref rid="B342-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">342</xref>,<xref rid="B343-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">343</xref>] to the infarct and peri-infarct region, followed by their differentiation into functional neurons [<xref rid="B344-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">344</xref>,<xref rid="B345-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">345</xref>,<xref rid="B346-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">346</xref>], as shown in <xref rid="pharmaceuticals-18-00261-f015" ref-type="fig">Figure 15</xref> [<xref rid="B9-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>,<xref rid="B12-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>].</p><p>Cerebral ischemia-induced neuroinflammation causing brain damage has dual beneficial and harmful effects, depending on the spatial-temporal context [<xref rid="B12-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>].
