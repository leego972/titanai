2024</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><sec id="Sec1"><title>Introduction</title><p id="Par2">The skin is the largest organ of the human body, having multiple roles such as maintaining the hydration levels, regulating the temperature and protecting the body from external factors [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR1">1</xref>]. A disruption of the skin due to a wound can influence its structure and function, negatively affecting the human body [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR2">2</xref>]. Wound healing is a non-linear dynamic process, that takes place during four overlapping phases: coagulation/hemostasis, inflammation, proliferation and remodeling [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR2">2</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR4">4</xref>]. Enhancing the wound healing process has been a challenge for many years, with numerous treatments being developed, such as using stem cells, wound dressings, tape removal, electrical stimulation or exposure to laser [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR5">5</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR7">7</xref>].</p><p id="Par3">Photobiomodulation is a healing approach where low-level light exposure to tissues or cells, leads to cell differentiation, proliferation and accelerated wound healing. Non-ionizing photonic energy stimulates photo-biochemical reactions, after absorption of photons by a cellular chomophore, called photoreceptor. In a recent study, Chen et al. reported the existence of two bands in the light spectrum, blue–green and red–infrared that affected wound healing through targeting and stimulation of different photoreceptors [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR8">8</xref>]. In the red and near-infrared regions of the electromagnetic spectrum (600–1200 nm), laser or LED light devices have been used in photobiomodulation (PBM), phototherapy and photodynamic therapy to induce cell proliferation and the wound healing process [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">9</xref>]. The photoreceptor in this region is within the light-sensitive mitochondrial structures, and more specific it is proposed to be cytochrome-c oxidase (CCO) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">9</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR10">10</xref>]. As CCO presents two absorption bands, one at the red region (~ 660 nm) and the other in the NIR region (800–850 nm), the most used lasers in PBM are 660 nm, 810 nm, and 850 nm [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR11">11</xref>]. This region of the light spectrum from red to NIR, is the well-known optical window where light penetration to tissues is maximized. Light with wavelength in the range 600–700 nm is used to treat superficial tissue while longer wavelengths are used to treat deeper—seated tissues [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>].</p><p id="Par4">The beneficial effects of photobiomodulation in the red -NIR region appear to be primarily facilitated through the generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) that increase energy production (ATP) and stimulate enzymes and transcription factors [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">9</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR10">10</xref>].
