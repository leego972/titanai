Therapeutic Potential of Low-Level Laser Therapy in Diabetes Management</title><p>In modern rehabilitation medicine, low-energy laser radiation is widely used. The key technical parameters of laser therapy devices, such as wavelength, output power, and pulse repetition frequency, are closely linked to the depth of tissue penetration, light energy distribution in the sample, and biological effects. In the rehabilitation of diabetic patients, the average power of LLLT is usually less than 100 mW on the wavelengths in the red and infrared (IR) part of the spectrum. The irradiation time must be carefully adjusted to achieve the desired dose, taking into account the size and location of the target area. The therapeutic efficacy of LLLT is largely attributed to its profound impact on components of the mitochondrial respiratory chain, which initiates a cascade of biological responses with far-reaching implications for diabetes treatment. Several studies [<xref rid="B83-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">83</xref>,<xref rid="B102-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>] have demonstrated that these mechanisms explain how low-intensity laser light activates mitochondrial respiratory components [<xref rid="B154-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">154</xref>]. This interaction is wavelength-dependent, with optimal biological responses observed in the red to near-infrared spectrum (600–950 nm), corresponding to the absorption peaks of cytochrome c oxidase [<xref rid="B125-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">125</xref>].</p><p>Mitochondria play a pivotal role in cellular metabolism, energy production, and signalling pathways, with the electron transport chain (ETC) at the heart of their functionality. The ETC consists of protein complexes embedded in the inner mitochondrial membrane, which coordinate electron transfer and ATP synthesis [<xref rid="B155-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">155</xref>]. The transfer of electrons through these complexes stimulates ATP production, which is the primary energy currency of the cell [<xref rid="B125-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">125</xref>,<xref rid="B156-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">156</xref>,<xref rid="B157-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">157</xref>]. In order to understand how LLLT modulates these processes, it is crucial to identify its primary molecular targets (<xref rid="ijms-27-02078-f004" ref-type="fig">Figure 4</xref>).</p><p>One of the main mechanisms by which low-intensity laser light affects mitochondrial function is the activation of COX, which contains haem and copper centres that absorb light in the near-infrared region [<xref rid="B125-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">125</xref>]. COX is the terminal component of the mitochondrial respiratory chain [<xref rid="B158-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">158</xref>] and is also functionally associated with the endoplasmic reticulum and plasma membrane [<xref rid="B159-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">159</xref>]. As demonstrated by Lebiedzinska et al. (2009), the dynamic structural and functional connections between mitochondria, the plasma membrane, and other organelles are crucial for intracellular phospholipid transport, calcium homeostasis, and cyclic AMP regulation [<xref rid="B160-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">160</xref>]. These processes govern fundamental cellular functions, such as motility, contraction, secretion, growth, proliferation, and apoptosis [<xref rid="B125-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">125</xref>]. Disruption to these pathways is commonly observed in diabetic tissues, further supporting the rationale for mitochondrial-targeted therapies such as LLLT.</p><p>Cytochrome c oxidase, also known as complex IV, is the primary site of electron transfer to molecular oxygen, facilitating ATP production via oxidative phosphorylation [<xref rid="B161-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">161</xref>]. Studies have shown that exposure to low-intensity laser light increases cytochrome c oxidase activity, resulting in enhanced electron flow and ATP synthesis [<xref rid="B162-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">162</xref>,<xref rid="B163-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">163</xref>].
