# PMC: phytochemical bioactive compound anticancer — Overview

obtained from the World Flora Online website (<uri xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" xlink:href="https://www.worldfloraonline.org/">https://www.worldfloraonline.org/</uri> accessed on 24 January 2025) and is outlined in <xref rid="molecules-30-01186-t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref>.</p><p>The perennial <italic toggle="yes">C. angustifolium</italic> Scop. (<xref rid="molecules-30-01186-f002" ref-type="fig">Figure 2</xref>) is characterized by scale-like white-pink leaves along its long rhizomes and stolons [<xref rid="B23-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>]. Its unbranched stems typically grow between 50 and 200 cm, either smooth or sparsely covered with short clinging hairs. The alternating leaves, densely arranged along the stem, measure 5–15 cm in length and 10–15 mm in width, featuring a linear-lanceolate shape that tapers at the base [<xref rid="B31-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>,<xref rid="B32-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>]. Dark green, glossy, and smooth, the leaves have prominent veins and margins that are either smooth or slightly serrated. As the stipules rise towards the top, they gradually become smaller, with the uppermost part often bristly [<xref rid="B36-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>,<xref rid="B37-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>,<xref rid="B38-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>]. The plant produces violet-pink flowers with rectangular petals, rounded at the tips, arranged in long terminal spike-like racemes. The lanceolate sepals, twice as long as the petals, are slightly hairy on the outer surface. The protandrous flowers bloom from June to September, beginning at the base of the raceme and progressing upward, with the style extending beyond the stamens [<xref rid="B39-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">C. latifolium</italic> (<xref rid="molecules-30-01186-f003" ref-type="fig">Figure 3</xref>) is a perennial plant that grows between 10 and 50 cm tall, with a thick rhizome reaching up to 1.5 cm in diameter. Its stems are branched and can be either smooth or sparsely covered with hairs, particularly near the upper part [<xref rid="B30-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>,<xref rid="B31-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>]. The leaves, which can be bare or slightly hairy, have a grayish tint. They are sessile or have very short petioles, with the lower leaves arranged oppositely and the upper leaves alternately. These leaves are thick, broadly lanceolate, and wedge-shaped at the base, blunt at the tip, and have smooth edges, measuring 2–3.5 cm in length and 1–1.5 cm in width. They are lighter on the underside and lack prominent lateral veins [<xref rid="B40-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>,<xref rid="B41-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec2dot2-molecules-30-01186"><title>2.2. Phytochemicals</title><sec id="sec2dot2dot1-molecules-30-01186"><title>2.2.1. Primary Metabolites</title><p>Primary metabolites (PMs) are the fundamental building blocks of life, essential for growth, development, and metabolic functions in all organisms. In plants like <italic toggle="yes">C. latifolium</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">C. angustifolium</italic>, these compounds, including carbohydrates, amino acids, fatty acids, and organic acids, are crucial for vital processes such as photosynthesis, respiration, and protein synthesis. They also serve as precursors to secondary metabolites, which contribute to the plant’s pharmacological properties [<xref rid="B42-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>,<xref rid="B43-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>]. The PMs found in <italic toggle="yes">C. latifolium</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">C. angustifolium</italic> are summarized in <xref rid="molecules-30-01186-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>.</p><p>Carbohydrates are the most abundant PMs in <italic toggle="yes">Chamaenerion</italic> species, serving as the primary energy source and structural component. Glucose and galactose are particularly prominent, with glucose dominating the sugar profile. These sugars are vital for energy storage and transport, especially during flowering and seed development, reflecting the species’ adaptation to diverse environmental conditions. In <italic toggle="yes">C. angustifolium</italic>, glucose concentrations can reach up to 11.23 mg/g dry weight [<xref rid="B44-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>,<xref rid="B45-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>].</p><p>Amino acids are essential for protein biosynthesis, nitrogen transport, and stress response mechanisms. Uminska et al. analyzed the amino acid profile of <italic toggle="yes">C. angustifolium</italic> using GC-MS, identifying L-alanine (2.350–6.090 mg/g) and L-phenylalanine as the dominant amino acids, with moderate amounts of L-leucine, L-isoleucine, and L-valine. Interestingly, sulfur-containing amino acids are absent, potentially impacting the synthesis of specific sulfur-rich secondary metabolites.
