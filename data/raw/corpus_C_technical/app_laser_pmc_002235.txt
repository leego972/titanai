The overall quality of the evidence for each outcome was evaluated independently using the GRADE approach. The GRADE approach improves reliability in comparison to intuitive judgments about the certainty of a body of evidence [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR32">32</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="Sec12"><title>Data synthesis</title><p id="Par21">This review deployed narrative synthesis for pain score as the outcome measures. The diversity of scale used for outcome measures and the absence of standard deviation in study results prevented the input from meta-analysis for pain score.</p><p id="Par22">Studies presenting healing time were assessed using the standard meta-analysis (direct comparisons) performed using Review Manager 5.4 to determine the effectiveness of treatments directly compared to each other. The <italic toggle="yes">I</italic><sup>2</sup> statistic was used to quantify the heterogeneity of the results in individual studies since heterogeneity is a common issue encountered while performing meta-analyses. The random-effects model was applied as the default option for illustrative purposes in all the forest plots presenting effect measure data per treatment. MD or mean difference was used for continuous outcomes such as healing time. Data analysis was performed by SLV.</p><p id="Par23">Subgroup analyses were considered in reporting the continuous effect of healing time by splitting intervention based on the type of laser wavelength. Sensitivity analysis by excluding studies with a high risk of bias could not be performed as the number of studies is limited while mixing all types of lasers into one estimated effect and splitting based on the laser dosage may help detect changes in the findings [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR20">20</xref>].</p></sec></sec><sec id="Sec13"><title>Result</title><sec id="Sec14"><title>Description of study</title><sec id="Sec15"><title>Results of the search</title><p id="Par24">A total of 414 papers were identified. Ninety records were screened after removing duplicates (35) and ineligible reports marked by an advance filter for RCTs (289). Seventy-four articles were excluded after title and abstract evaluation, and sixteen reports were retrieved for eligibility assessment. The full-text assessment resulted in two papers discarded and 14 RCTs to be reviewed.</p></sec></sec><sec id="Sec16"><title>Included studies</title><sec id="Sec17"><title>Study population</title><p id="Par25">Fourteen studies involving 664 participants, ranging from 15 to 180 [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR33">33</xref>] participants [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR33">33</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR34">34</xref>], were included. Only one study enrolled more than 100 patients [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR33">33</xref>]. Participants included in this review ranged from 1 year old to 70 years old with three studies not reporting the age of participants. Gender distribution demonstrated males with a total of 156 participants and females with a total of 320 participants. Three studies did not report the characteristics of the participants based on age [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR35">35</xref>] and four studies did not report the characteristics of the participants based on gender [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR36">36</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR38">38</xref>].</p><p id="Par26">Two studies used the history of aphthous ulcer and clinical manifestation in diagnosing RAS followed by laboratory testing and pathergy test to eliminate the presence of any systemic disease [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR39">39</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR40">40</xref>]. One study reported using history taking, clinical manifestation, and laboratory testing only [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR36">36</xref>]. Ten studies reported the diagnosis of RAS based on the history of disease and clinical manifestation, yet not mentioning which classification or characteristics of ulcer were used [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR34">34</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR35">35</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR37">37</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR38">38</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR41">41</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR44">44</xref>]. Only one study mentioned the use of the 1972 Stanley Classification of RAS, while the other two did not mention the method to diagnose RAS [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR33">33</xref>]. Twelve studies reported the use of intervention and control in minor RAS, while the other two did not mention the type of RAS.</p></sec><sec id="Sec18"><title>Study design and setting</title><p id="Par27">All trials were randomized where five trials were single-blinded [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR35">35</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR37">37</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR39">39</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR41">41</xref>], three trials were double-blinded [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR38">38</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR40">40</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR44">44</xref>] and six trials not reporting the blinding of participants and evaluator [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR33">33</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR34">34</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR42">42</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR43">43</xref>].
