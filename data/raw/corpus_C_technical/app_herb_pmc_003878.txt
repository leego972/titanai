This is important since, if the induction of Hsp72 is a secondary event, upstream events would be of the primary interest. The treatment of human neuroglia cells with ADAPT-232 or salidroside significantly increased the expression of HSF1 as judged by Western blot analysis (Figure <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F3">3</xref>A). To prove that the adaptogens stimulate Hsp72 release by a mechanism dependent HSF1, the expression of HSF1 was significantly silenced using siRNA directed against <italic toggle="yes">hsf-1</italic> gene (HSF1-siRNA) before treatment with the adaptogens, as compared to neuroglia cells pre-treated with control-siRNA (Figure <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F3">3</xref>A). Control-siRNA is a non-targeting 20–25 nucleotide siRNA designed as a negative control, with sequences that do not target any gene product nor has any significant sequence similarity to mouse, rat, or human gene sequences, and has been tested in cell-based screens and proved to have no significant effect on cell proliferation, viability, or morphology, according to the manufacturer (Dharmacon RNA Technologies, Lafayette, CO, USA). We further demonstrated that pre-treatment of human neuroglia cells with HSF1-siRNA strongly suppressed the expression of Hsp72 (Figure <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F3">3</xref>B) and release of Hsp72 and NPY (Figure <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F5">5</xref>) stimulated by ADAPT-232 and salidroside, as compared to neuroglia cells pre-treated with control-siRNA (Figures <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F3">3</xref>B and <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F5">5</xref>A). The possibility that the release of Hsp72 from human neuroglia cells was due to cell death was negated by results showing that adaptogens increased significant release of Hsp72 as judged by ELISA (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.05), but there was no similar increase in cell death, as confirmed by the LDH assay.</p><fig id="F3" position="float" orientation="portrait"><label>Figure 3</label><caption><p><bold>Silencing HSF1 significantly suppresses ADAPT-232- and salidroside-mediated Hsp72 expression</bold>.
