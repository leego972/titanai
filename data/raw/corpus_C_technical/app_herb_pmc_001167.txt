Winery Waste</title><p>Grapes, which are commonly used, originate from approximately 60 different species within the Vitis genus, with <italic toggle="yes">Vitis vinifera</italic> L. being the most cultivated species, with production of approximately 77.7 million tons in 2024 [<xref rid="B138-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">138</xref>]. Leading producers include China, Italy, and the United States, accounting for approximately 39% [<xref rid="B139-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">139</xref>,<xref rid="B140-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">140</xref>]. Given its significance, the market value of this product varies between US$550.5 billion [<xref rid="B138-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">138</xref>]. Besides fresh consumption, a wide range of other forms exists, including wine, vinegar, juice, jam, jelly, dried, and seed oil [<xref rid="B141-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">141</xref>,<xref rid="B142-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">142</xref>].</p><p>The viticulture industry stands out as one of the most significant processing sectors, with more than 50% of all grape production allocated to winemaking [<xref rid="B141-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">141</xref>,<xref rid="B143-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">143</xref>]. According to data from the International Organisation of Vine and Wine (OIV), wine production reached approximately 225.6 million hectoliters in 2024, with major producers including European and American countries such as Italy, France, Spain, and USA [<xref rid="B144-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">144</xref>]. The market value of wine from 2024 varies between US$500 and 520 billion [<xref rid="B138-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">138</xref>,<xref rid="B144-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">144</xref>]. This production spans across all countries worldwide, reflecting its profound cultural and socioeconomic significance [<xref rid="B145-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">145</xref>]. The wine industry, while significant, poses considerable environmental concerns due to its high generation of waste and by-products. Negative impacts stem from improper management practices, low pH levels, the toxic effects of phytotoxic compounds, and the antimicrobial effects of residues [<xref rid="B141-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">141</xref>,<xref rid="B146-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">146</xref>]. Generated residues include vine shoots, grape pomace, wine lees, filtration cakes, vinasse, and winery wastewater, necessitating proper treatment for environmentally sound disposal [<xref rid="B147-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">147</xref>]. Consequently, recent research investigates sustainable reuse options to maximize the utilization of these raw materials and enhance their value, given their rich content of bioactive compounds [<xref rid="B148-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">148</xref>].</p><p>In wine production, approximately 20–30% of grape mass is transformed into pomace, consisting of 25% seeds, 25% stalks, and 50% other residues (broken pulp cells and grape skin), generated during crushing and pressing to obtain grape must [<xref rid="B149-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">149</xref>,<xref rid="B150-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">150</xref>]. This constitutes the primary solid residue of vinification, representing about 75% of all solid residues in the process [<xref rid="B151-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">151</xref>,<xref rid="B152-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">152</xref>]. It is estimated that producing approximately 6 L of wine generates about 1 kg of grape pomace, with annual production averaging 12 million tons of pomace [<xref rid="B153-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">153</xref>]. Grape pomace comprises about 55–75% water, 30% polysaccharides, 6–15% proteins, lipids, sugars, and unsaturated fatty acids, with a high concentration of bioactive compounds, as shown in <xref rid="molecules-31-00996-t008" ref-type="table">Table 8</xref> [<xref rid="B154-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">154</xref>]. Grape skin contains fibers, proteins, sugars, anthocyanins, flavonols, and tannins, with red grape skin potentially containing stilbenes (resveratrol), triterpenes, and derivatives of hydroxybenzoic acids [<xref rid="B150-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">150</xref>,<xref rid="B154-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">154</xref>]. The stalks consist of fibers such as cellulose, hemicellulose, lignin, and phenolic compounds like tannins [<xref rid="B150-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">150</xref>,<xref rid="B155-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">155</xref>].
