All animals were maintained in a room at 22°C (±3°C), and relative humidity was kept between 30 and 70%. Artificial light was set to give a cycle of 12 hours light and 12 hours dark. UV-treated water and conventional laboratory rodent diet were offered ad libitum. Animals were adapted to the environment for at least one week before experiments. All the experimental procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Ethics Committee (IAEC) of Natural Remedies Pvt Ltd, Bangalore, India (Approval no.: IAEC/NR-PCL-02/07/2021 &amp; IAEC/NR-PCL-02/03/2020) and experiments were conducted according to the Committee for the Purpose of Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals (CPCSEA) guidelines and in compliance with the Animal Research: Reporting of In Vivo Experiments (ARRIVE) guidelines.</p></sec><sec id="sec006"><title>2.4 Swim endurance test in mice</title><p>The swim endurance test was carried out using male <italic toggle="yes">Swiss albino</italic> mice aged 6–8 weeks [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref019" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0285012.ref020" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>]. Animals were divided into four groups of 6 animals each and were housed with three animals per cage during the experiment. The body weight of each animal within a group was within ±20% of the group mean. Group 1 was administered orally with 0.5% w/v carboxy methyl cellulose (CMC) at a 10 ml/kg dose. <italic toggle="yes">Ocimum tenuiflorum</italic> extract was suspended at a specific concentration in 0.5% CMC and administered orally to groups 2, 3, and 4 at 12.5, 25, and 50 mg/kg body weight per day for ten days. On the 10<sup>th</sup> day, one hour after administering the test substance, animals were subjected to the swim endurance test. Animals were made to swim in separate porcelain tanks of 30 cm in height and 70 cm in diameter filled with water maintained at 28–30°C. The endurance of each mouse was measured as swimming time recorded from the beginning of the time till exhaustion. A mouse was considered exhausted and rescued when it exhibited a loss of coordinated movements and failed to return to the water surface within a 10-s period to breathe for three consecutive times.</p></sec><sec id="sec007"><title>2.5 Antistress activity in the forced swim test</title><p>Animals were divided into five groups of 6 animals each and were housed with four animals per cage during the experiment. The body weight of each animal within a group was within ±20% of the group mean. Groups 1 and 2 were administered orally with 0.5% w/v carboxy methyl cellulose (CMC) at a dose of 10 ml/kg body weight for eight days. <italic toggle="yes">Ocimum tenuiflorum</italic> extract was suspended at a specific concentration in 0.5% CMC and administered orally to groups 3, 4, and 5 at a dose of 12.5, 25, and 50 mg/kg body weight respectively once a day for eight days. On the 8<sup>th</sup> day, one hour after the administration of the test substance or vehicle, animals of groups 2 to 5 were subjected to the forced swim test [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref021" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>]. Animals were allowed to swim for 5 minutes in a cylindrical tank constructed of transparent Plexiglas of 28 cm diameter and a height of 55 cm filled with water to a height of 40 cm. Animals were observed for immobility time as soon as they were subjected to the forced swim test. Immobility is characterized by the lack of movement except that which is necessary to keep the animal’s nose above the water level. Blood was collected from the retro-orbital plexus and centrifuged at 4000 g for serum separation, and corticosterone levels were determined in the serum using LC-MS/MS technique.</p></sec><sec id="sec008"><title>2.6 Cortisol release assay</title><sec id="sec009"><title>2.6.1 Cell culture and treatment</title><p>The cortisol release study was carried out to assess the effect of <italic toggle="yes">Ocimum tenuiflorum</italic> extract on cortisol secretion [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref022" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>].
