Among these advantages, there is the possibility to drive them with triangular signals, so to obtain distortion-free OCT images<sup><xref rid="bibr32-0954411914543963" ref-type="bibr">32</xref></sup> without any images post-processing.<sup><xref rid="bibr27-0954411914543963" ref-type="bibr">27</xref></sup> This scanning regime is employed in the SD-OCT system used in this study.</p><p>The X–Y mini-translation stage (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig1-0954411914543963">Figure 1</xref>, (3)) allows for the alignment of the optical axis of the components: the X-axis adjustment allows the placement of the axis of the fiber collimator (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig1-0954411914543963">Figure 1</xref>, (1)) on the axis of the exit lens (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig1-0954411914543963">Figure 1</xref>, (12)); the Y-axis stage allows the positioning of the axis of the collimator (and of the incoming laser beam) on the oscillatory axis of the GS (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig1-0954411914543963">Figure 1</xref>, (6)).</p><p>A controlled eccentricity of the beam with regard to the galvo mirror axis included in the handheld probe for TD-OCT setups could be used for producing the modulation function without a translation stage.<sup><xref rid="bibr34-0954411914543963" ref-type="bibr">34</xref></sup>
</p><p>This first prototype had been initially attached to the optical table for preliminary studies by using a TD-OCT setup. With this configuration, it can be used as the scanning head of the sample for the OCT system. The probe had been then provided with an adapted, commercially available, and low-cost handle (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig2-0954411914543963">Figure 2</xref>) to be used together with a mobile, portable SD-OCT system.</p><fig id="fig2-0954411914543963" position="float" orientation="portrait"><label>Figure 2.</label><caption><p>Second configuration of the handheld probe: (a) exploded view of the device (without the handle), with the same annotations as in <xref ref-type="table" rid="table1-0954411914543963">Table 1</xref> and <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig1-0954411914543963">Figure 1</xref>, except for the different mount (5c) of the GS; (b) assembly of the probe; and (c) probe mounted in a plastic handle (GS and cover removed for a better view of the GS mount).</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="10.1177_0954411914543963-fig2.jpg"/></fig><p>One may see from the design shown in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig2-0954411914543963">Figure 2</xref> that such a handheld probe can be easily and rapidly assembled in any lab.
