Prunus armeniaca</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Prunus armeniaca</italic> (Armenian plum) belongs to an important plant family Rosacea. Various parts of the plant are used as the major source of some important antioxidant substances and are commonly used against cancer and some other cardiovascular diseases [<xref rid="B102-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>]. The fruit part of <italic toggle="yes">P. armeniaca</italic> contains various important secondary metabolites like β-carotene, flavonoids, organic acids, thiamine, minerals, and oils [<xref rid="B103-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">103</xref>]. The seeds of <italic toggle="yes">P. armeniaca</italic> contains plenty of cyanogenic glycosides, used against different types of cancers [<xref rid="B104-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">104</xref>]. Amygdalin is one of the important glycosides of <italic toggle="yes">P. armeniaca</italic>, used for the treatment of prostate cancer [<xref rid="B105-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">105</xref>]. Gomaa [<xref rid="B106-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>] reported the antioxidant and anticancer activities of <italic toggle="yes">P. armeniaca</italic> at different combinations of methanolic and ethanolic extracts with water. The study concluded that kernels of <italic toggle="yes">P. armeniaca</italic> have better antioxidant capacity compared to almonds that belong to the same genus. The study showed the highest antioxidant activity (67%), total lycopene content (4.70 mg/mL), and total phenolic content (3.290 mg/g of dry extract) in <italic toggle="yes">P. armeniaca</italic> kernels. Furthermore, <italic toggle="yes">P. armeniaca</italic> kernels were also found active in the inhibition of certain cancers when tested on the colon (HCT-116), human breast (MCF-7), and HepG2 cell lines in different concentrations and combinations. The highest cytotoxicity was observed from methanolic extract (IC<sub>50</sub> = 10.1 μg) against HepG2 cells. According to Madrau et al. [<xref rid="B107-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">107</xref>], the fruit part contains a large amount of phenolic compound that shows strong antioxidant and immune-stimulant properties. Similarly, the study conducted by Liu et al. [<xref rid="B108-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>] found low anti-scavenging activity against DPPH of dry seed extract of apricot. Vardi et al. [<xref rid="B109-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">109</xref>] reported the strong protective activity of apricot against intestinal oxidative damage in the rat model experiment. </p></sec><sec id="sec3dot14-biomolecules-10-00047"><title>3.14. Rabdosiae rubescens</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Rabdosiae rubescens</italic> (Chinese: Dong Ling Cao) is a Chinese medicinal herb that belongs to the family Lamiaceae. It possesses multiple biological activities like antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, anti-parasitic, and anticancer [<xref rid="B110-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">R. rubescens</italic> contain important chemical compounds including monoterpenes, sesquiterpene, diterpene, and terpenoids. Oridonin, a tetracyclic terpenoid, is the main active compound in <italic toggle="yes">R. rubescens</italic> [<xref rid="B111-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">111</xref>]. Oridonin gained its attention because of the remarkable properties of growth inhibition and the induction of apoptosis in cancer cells. In vitro and in vivo studies showed the induction of apoptosis in a variety of cancer cells by oridonin as in hepatocellular carcinoma, breast, gastric, skin, colorectal, gallbladder, and pancreatic cancers [<xref rid="B112-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>].</p><p>Bao et al.
