# PMC: phytochemical bioactive compound anticancer — Overview

Prominent among the glycosides with therapeutic values are flavonoid glycosides, anthraquinone glycosides, coumarin glycosides, cardiac glycosides, cyanogenic glucosides, indole glycosides, etc. Efforts in recent times have elucidated novel cellular targets specific to this class of chemicals. Two novel cellular targets, YYI/p65/p300 complex and VRKI/p53BPI, were recently reported to be the target of hyperoside (a flavonol glycoside) and ginsenoside Rg3, respectively [<xref rid="B43-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>,<xref rid="B44-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>]. The anticancer potency of cardiac glycosides is linked to their inhibition of sodium potassium ATPase (NKA), Ca<sup>2+</sup> apoptosis induction, sequential activation of autophagy and apoptosis, modulations of topoisomerase II, fibroblast growth factors (FGF-2), and nuclear factor kappa B (NF-ƙB) inhibitions [<xref rid="B45-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>]. Nur77 (an orphan nuclear receptor) pathway has also been established as a target of some cardiac glycosides. Nur77 protein is expressed in cardiac glycoside-treated cells, which results in an apoptosis induction. This is due to an alteration in the mitochondria occurring because of the translocation of Nur77 from the nucleus to the cytoplasm [<xref rid="B46-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>].</p><p>The chemical structures presented in <xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f003" ref-type="fig">Figure 3</xref> contain some newly reported glycosides with promising antiproliferative activities, as well as some cardenolide lactates. Some new glucosides (compounds <bold>1</bold> and <bold>2</bold>, <xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f003" ref-type="fig">Figure 3</xref>A) were reported to be cytotoxic against HL-60 and HepG2 (IC<sub>50</sub> values 1.3 and 2.1 µM; 5.1 and 12.1 µM, respectively). No report was found as to the mechanisms involved in their antiproliferative activities [<xref rid="B47-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>]. Another glycoside (compound <bold>3</bold>) reported by Zilla et. al. from <italic toggle="yes">Podophyllum hexandrum</italic> shows cytotoxicity against a panel of cancer cell lines (see <xref rid="app1-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="app">Supplementary Table S1</xref>) with an IC<sub>50</sub> value range of 0.208–0.291 µM [<xref rid="B48-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">48</xref>].</p><p>As described by Raees et al., some novel pregnane glycosides from <italic toggle="yes">Desmidorchis flava</italic> possess antiproliferative effects against breast and ovarian cancer cell lines [<xref rid="B49-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>,<xref rid="B50-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>]. Compounds <bold>4</bold>–<bold>7</bold> (<xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f003" ref-type="fig">Figure 3</xref>A) showed cytotoxicity on MDA-MB 231 and SKOV-3 cancer cells [<xref rid="B51-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>]. Additionally, compound <bold>4</bold> (nizwaside) showed a stronger antiproliferative effect in MDA-MB-231 cell lines than the known antitumor drug doxorubicin [<xref rid="B49-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>]. In contrast, compounds <bold>6</bold> and <bold>7</bold> showed no cytotoxicity on normal breast epithelial cell line MCF-10-2A [<xref rid="B51-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>].</p><p>A group of potent-to-moderate antiproliferative compounds was isolated from <italic toggle="yes">Asclepias curassavica</italic> [<xref rid="B52-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>]. These include four new cardenolide lactates plus one glucoside lactate (compounds <bold>8</bold>–<bold>11</bold>, <xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f003" ref-type="fig">Figure 3</xref>A) and a new double-linked cardenolide glycoside (compound <bold>13</bold>, <xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f003" ref-type="fig">Figure 3</xref>B). While compound <bold>13</bold> showed a strong cytotoxic effect on DU145 cells (IC<sub>50</sub> 0.29 µM), the cardenolide lactates showed moderate cytotoxicity (IC<sub>50</sub> value range 1.66–16.96 µM) [<xref rid="B52-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>]. Other previously known compounds from this plant were identified to be cytotoxic against DU145 prostate cancer cells. These were six normal cardenolides (IC<sub>50</sub> values: 0.33–0.92 µM), four double-linked cardenolide glycosides (IC<sub>50</sub> Values: 0.03–0.28 µM), and some C-21 steroidal glycosides [<xref rid="B53-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>,<xref rid="B54-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">54</xref>,<xref rid="B55-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>]. Compound <bold>14</bold> (asclepiasterol), a C-21 steroidal glycoside from <italic toggle="yes">A. curassavica</italic>, was reported to modulate the MDR phenotype in MCF-7/ADR and HepG-2/ADM cells at low concentrations (2.5–5.0 µM) and enhancing the cytotoxicity of anticancer drugs [<xref rid="B56-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>].</p><p>Two new quinochalcone C-glycosides (carthorquinosides A and B, <xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f003" ref-type="fig">Figure 3</xref>B) were isolated from Carthamus tinctorius.
