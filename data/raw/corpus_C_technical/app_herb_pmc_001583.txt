Novel Terpenoids</title><p>Valepotriates, which are triesters of monoterpene alcohol, are known for their excellent anticancer properties [<xref rid="B91-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">91</xref>]. Novel compounds belonging to this chemical class include compounds <bold>43</bold>–<bold>45</bold> (<xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f006" ref-type="fig">Figure 6</xref>A) isolated from <italic toggle="yes">Valeriana jatamansi</italic>. These compounds obtained as degradation products of valepotriates show selective cytotoxicity against PC-3M and HCT-8 cancer cells with IC<sub>50</sub> values between 2.1 and 6.5 µM [<xref rid="B92-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">92</xref>]. Some new chlorinated valepotriates (compounds <bold>46</bold>–<bold>60</bold>, <xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f006" ref-type="fig">Figure 6</xref>A,B) isolated from <italic toggle="yes">Valeriana jatamansi</italic> were reported to be cytotoxic on several cancer cell lines [<xref rid="B93-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">93</xref>]. Some of these compounds (<bold>56</bold>–<bold>59</bold>) had potent activity on A-549, PC-3, HCT-8, and Bel-7402 cancer cells with IC<sub>50</sub> values ranging between 1.06 and 10 µM.</p><p>Compound <bold>61</bold> (<xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f006" ref-type="fig">Figure 6</xref>B), one of two new iridoids (named Jatamanvaltrate P) isolated from <italic toggle="yes">Valeriana jatamansi</italic>, showed selective anticancer properties in vivo and in vitro [<xref rid="B94-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>]. It inhibited the growth of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) and MCF-7 cancer cell lines in a concentration-dependent manner (IC<sub>50</sub> values 4.05–7.05 µM, respectively). Additionally, 61 induced apoptosis and cell cycle arrest in treated cells. It also triggered autophagosome formation, indicated by the upregulation of LC3-II level in treated cells [<xref rid="B95-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>]. Some new sets of novel iridoids and bisiridoids were also reported from <italic toggle="yes">Patrinia scabiosaefolia</italic>. Three iridoids (compounds <bold>62</bold>–<bold>64</bold>) and one bisiridoid (compound <bold>65</bold>) showed selective cytotoxicity against HL-60, SMMC-7721, MCF-7, and SW-480, with IC<sub>50</sub> values ranging from 1.2 to 23.9 µM [<xref rid="B96-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>].</p><p>Among the major challenges facing cancer chemotherapy is the resistance and cross-resistance of cancer cells to anticancer agents. This menace results from the overexpression of ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transporter proteins such as P-glycoprotein (P-gp) and breast cancer resistance protein (BCRP, ABCG2) “drug efflux transport” [<xref rid="B97-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>]. These membrane proteins are responsible for the excretion of drugs from the cell, resulting in low intracellular accumulation and consequent inefficacy of the drug, even at lethal concentration [<xref rid="B98-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">98</xref>,<xref rid="B99-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>]. Examples of drugs against which cells have developed cross-resistance are taxanes [<xref rid="B100-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">100</xref>], anthracyclines [<xref rid="B101-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">101</xref>], vinca alkaloids [<xref rid="B102-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>], platinum compounds [<xref rid="B103-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">103</xref>], and mitoxantrone [<xref rid="B104-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">104</xref>]. Available MDR modulator drugs such as verapamil [<xref rid="B105-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">105</xref>], cyclosporine [<xref rid="B106-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>], dexverapamil [<xref rid="B107-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">107</xref>], tariquidar [<xref rid="B108-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>], and zosuquidar [<xref rid="B109-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">109</xref>] show high toxicity and pharmacokinetic interactions with anticancer drugs [<xref rid="B110-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>].</p><p>Reports from various experiments have shown promising phytochemicals that are more potent than the currently available MDR modulators.
