Title: Systematic Review of Attribution Modeling Methods Resolving Bias in Multi Touch Journeys

Abstract:
Attribution modeling plays a crucial role in accurately quantifying the contribution of marketing touchpoints across customer journeys. However, traditional single-touch and rule-based attribution models often introduce systemic bias by overvaluing specific channels or interactions, leading to misinformed marketing decisions and inefficient budget allocation. This systematic review critically examines contemporary attribution modeling methods designed to resolve bias in multi-touch journeys, integrating insights from probabilistic, algorithmic, and data-driven frameworks. Emphasis is placed on the evolution from heuristic models, such as linear and time-decay attribution, to advanced machine learning and causal inference–based approaches that account for path dependency and interaction effects among touchpoints. The study also explores the role of deep learning, Shapley value decomposition, and Markov chain–based models in improving interpretability and fairness in channel credit distribution. By synthesizing findings from recent empirical and theoretical literature, this review identifies key methodological challenges, including data sparsity, cross-device tracking, and privacy constraints. Furthermore, it highlights emerging research directions that combine explainable AI and Bayesian inference for unbiased, transparent attribution modeling. The findings provide valuable implications for marketing analysts, data scientists, and decision-makers seeking robust, bias-resilient models that enhance campaign evaluation accuracy and optimize omnichannel marketing performance.
