Genetic Diversity</title><p>The genetic diversity of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> is decreasing due to widespread harvesting; thus, single nucleotide polymorphisms have been used to study the remaining diversity [<xref rid="B21-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>], and microsatellite markers have been studied as tools for DNA profiling and genetic diversity studies [<xref rid="B22-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>]. Razi <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, showed that in an uncontrolled cultivated area, the <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> samples could be characterized based on their cultivar’s origins. They proved that identification of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> from various cultivars can be obtained using PCR-RAPD, with the help of some analytical software. The method yielded high quality and quantity of DNA. Six random primers (OPA-3, OPA-4, OPA-13, OPA-18, OPC-5 and OPC-6) were found to give good amplifications of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> DNA samples [<xref rid="B23-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>]. Some scientists are interested in the <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> production of the <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> plantlets or plant tissues for sustainable production of active ingredients [<xref rid="B24-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>,<xref rid="B25-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>,<xref rid="B26-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>,<xref rid="B27-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>,<xref rid="B28-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>,<xref rid="B29-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>,<xref rid="B30-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>,<xref rid="B31-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>]. Ling <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> developed a protocol to optimize protoplast isolation from callus of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> [<xref rid="B32-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>]. Most recently, Lulu <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, optimized the conditions for the production of adventitious roots from <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic>, in balloon-type bubble bioreactor cultures, suitable for the large-scale commercial production of its roots containing high yield of bioactive compounds [<xref rid="B33-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>].</p></sec></sec><sec id="sec2-molecules-21-00331"><title>2. Historical or Traditional Uses</title><p><italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> is used to cure lumbago and indigestion. It is used as a power tonic after delivery, and use for treatment of fever, jaundice, cachexia, and dropsy [<xref rid="B12-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>,<xref rid="B34-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> is one of the most popular folk medicines for its aphrodisiac effects and treatment of intermittent fever (malaria) [<xref rid="B35-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>]. Decoctions of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> leaves are used for washing itches, while its fruits are used in curing dysentery [<xref rid="B12-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>]. Its bark is mostly used as a vermifuge [<xref rid="B12-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>], while the taproots are used to treat high blood pressure, and the root bark is used for the treatment of diarrhea and fever [<xref rid="B36-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>].
