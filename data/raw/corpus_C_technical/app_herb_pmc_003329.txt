[<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref051" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec016"><title>Plant part use and mode of preparation</title><p>According to Hoffman and Gallaher [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref022" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>], calculating the use of plant parts (Plant Part Use) is useful to determine the dominant plant parts being used as medicinal ingredients. Plant parts are capable of accumulating diverse and interesting natural compounds. They attract attention because of their ability to act as factories, producing and offering important pharmaceutical potential [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref052" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>]. Our results showed that leaves were the most predominantly utilized plant parts at 61.5%, while gum, stem bark and bulb represent parts that are infrequently used by people of Ngadisari (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="pone.0235886.g003">Fig 3</xref>).</p><fig id="pone.0235886.g003" orientation="portrait" position="float"><object-id pub-id-type="doi">10.1371/journal.pone.0235886.g003</object-id><label>Fig 3</label><caption><title>Percentage of medicinal plant part use for herbal preparation in Ngadisari village, Indonesia.</title></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="pone.0235886.g003.jpg"/></fig><p>Leaves are the major plant components commonly reported to be used as herbal medicine materials in Indonesia [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref008" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0235886.ref053" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>] and also in other countries [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref054" ref-type="bibr">54</xref>–<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref056" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>].
