Subsequently, the photosensitizer transfers energy to molecular oxygen (<sup>3</sup>O<sub>2</sub>), exciting it to its highly reactive singlet state (<sup>1</sup>O<sub>2</sub>) [<xref rid="B14-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>,<xref rid="B15-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>]. These singlet oxygen species produced are highly reactive, extremely toxic, and lead to cell damage [<xref rid="B16-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>]. PDT is conducted typically in the wavelength range between 600–800 nm [<xref rid="B17-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>]. This technique enables the light dose and treatment conditions to be moderated to prevent over- and undertreatment and can be repeated at the same site if necessary [<xref rid="B18-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>].</p><p>Meso-tetrahydroxyphenylchlorin (mTHPC) photosensitizer, known also as “Temoporfin” and by the proprietary name ‘Foscan<sup>®</sup>’, is one of the oldest second-generation PS drugs present and is one of the most widely studied drugs in PDT and photodiagnostics [<xref rid="B19-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>,<xref rid="B20-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>,<xref rid="B21-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>,<xref rid="B22-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>]. Clinical trials showed that PDT activated by mTHPC is safe and effective for treating cancer [<xref rid="B23-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>,<xref rid="B24-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>,<xref rid="B25-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>]. The mTHPC has many advantages over different PSs such as stronger phototoxicity, high triplet yield, and high absorption in the deep red (652 nm), which is highly beneficial for light penetration in tumor tissue [<xref rid="B26-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>,<xref rid="B27-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>,<xref rid="B28-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>]. Moreover, mTHPC has an extended lifetime in its triplet state that allows greater singlet oxygen production, improving the efficacy of PDT [<xref rid="B29-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>]. The mTHPC, like other anti-cancer drugs, amasses in malignant tissues to a far greater extent than in normal tissues [<xref rid="B30-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>,<xref rid="B31-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>,<xref rid="B32-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>]. Nevertheless, conjugated drugs to NPs demonstrate long circulation time and greater levels of drugs in tumor tissues compared to freely administered drugs due to the enhanced permeability and retention effect (EPR) [<xref rid="B33-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>].</p><p>On the other hand, photothermal therapy (PTT), also known as optical hyperthermia, relies on photothermal agents (PTAs) with high photothermal conversion for converting light into heat to destroy cancer cells [<xref rid="B34-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>]. Gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) have been considered efficient photothermal agents [<xref rid="B35-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>,<xref rid="B36-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>,<xref rid="B37-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>,<xref rid="B38-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>,<xref rid="B39-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>,<xref rid="B40-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>] due to their surface plasmon resonance (SPR) effect [<xref rid="B41-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>], which has a high efficiency of light-to-heat conversion [<xref rid="B42-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>]. When an electromagnetic field of light interacts with AuNPs, it causes a collective oscillation of electrons at the surface, also known as the localized surface plasmon resonance (LSPR), at a specific frequency [<xref rid="B43-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>]. AuNPs typically show the SPR band around 520 nm in the visible region [<xref rid="B39-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>,<xref rid="B44-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>,<xref rid="B45-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>]. At the biological molecular level, hyperthermia of PTT-mediated AuNPs induces cell membrane destruction, DNA denaturation, and ultimately cell death [<xref rid="B46-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>]. Overall, hypothermia depends on the light dose duration, drug dose, and intensity of the light [<xref rid="B47-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>,<xref rid="B48-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">48</xref>].</p><p>In the present study, we synthesized a biocompatible AuNP–mTHPC complex and investigated its ability for a dual PDT and PTT treatment. Our hypothesis was that the amalgamation of PDT and PTT treatment increases efficacy compared to a mono-phototherapy approach, thus increasing overall cell death and consequently reducing the duration and intensity of laser irradiation needed.
