Antidiabetic and antioxidant properties of Paeonia mascula L.: In vitro and in 
vivo studies, and phytochemical analysis.

The medicinal plant Paeonia mascula L. is commonly used in Anatolian folk 
medicine for its antidiabetic properties. This study aimed to investigate the in 
vitro α-glucosidase enzyme inhibition effect, in vivo antidiabetic, and 
antioxidant activities of extracts obtained from P. mascula. The in vivo studies 
were conducted on diabetic rats induced with streptozotocin. The ethyl acetate 
and n-butanol extracts showed the highest efficacy in both in vitro and in vivo 
experiments, reducing AST, ALT, and MDA levels while increasing GSH and SOD 
activities in rats. In total, seven compounds were isolated from both extracts, 
and their structures were identified using spectroscopic methods such as 1D and 
2D NMR and Mass Spectrometry. The in vitro α-glucosidase inhibition assay on 
purified compounds revealed that 1,2,3,4,6-penta-O-galloyl-β-d-glucose was the 
most effective compound. These findings support the traditional use of P. 
mascula as an antidiabetic agent.
