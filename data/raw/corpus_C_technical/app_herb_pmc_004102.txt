This GABAergic mechanism explains the anxiolytic and calming effects of ginseng without the sedative side effects characteristic of benzodiazepines [<xref rid="B58-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>].</p><p>Ginsenoside Rc enhances mitochondrial biogenesis by increasing the activity of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma coactivator 1α (PGC-1α), resulting in increased ATP synthesis and reduced reactive oxygen species (ROS) production in skeletal muscle cells [<xref rid="B59-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>]. PGC-1α is a master regulator of mitochondrial biogenesis, activating genes encoding mitochondrial proteins, including respiratory chain complexes I–V and ATP synthase [<xref rid="B59-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>]. This mechanism accounts for the anti-fatigue and ergogenic effects of ginseng in oxidative stress-induced muscle atrophy [<xref rid="B59-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>].</p><p>20(S)-Protopanaxadiol (20S-PPD), the major intestinal metabolite of ginsenosides, alleviates DRP1 (dynamin-related protein 1)-dependent mitochondrial dysfunction in models of depression via the SIRT1/PGC-1α signaling pathway [<xref rid="B60-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>]. 20S-PPD inhibits excessive mitochondrial fragmentation by reducing DRP1 translocation to mitochondria, thereby improving mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm), decreasing apoptosis, and increasing serotonin (5-HT) levels in neurons [<xref rid="B60-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>]. Total ginsenosides from ginseng root regulate the AMPK/SIRT1/PGC-1α pathway, as demonstrated by network pharmacology and transcriptomic analyses, identifying this mechanism as central to their antidepressant effects [<xref rid="B61-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>].</p><p>Korean red ginseng, produced by steaming and drying fresh <italic toggle="yes">P. ginseng</italic> roots, prevents post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD)-like depression through activation of the serotonergic system in single prolonged stress (SPS) rat models [<xref rid="B62-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>]. It increases mRNA expression of tryptophan hydroxylase-1 (TPH1) in the intestine and tryptophan hydroxylase-2 (TPH2) in the brain, the rate-limiting enzymes of serotonin biosynthesis [<xref rid="B62-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>]. Additionally, it elevates serotonin levels in serum and brain tissue and normalizes depressive-like behaviors in forced swimming and open-field tests [<xref rid="B62-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>].</p><p>Ginsenosides, particularly Rg1, demonstrate strong antidepressant activity in preclinical studies, reducing immobility time in the forced swimming test by 30–50% compared with controls [<xref rid="B53-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>]. A meta-analysis of 29 preclinical studies showed that ginsenosides increase the expression of brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) and its receptor TrkB in the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex, thereby promoting neurogenesis and synaptogenesis [<xref rid="B53-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>,<xref rid="B62-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>]. Ginsenoside Rd exerts antidepressant effects via activation of the hypoxia-inducible factor 1α (HIF-1α) pathway, increasing the expression of synapsin I (SYN1) and postsynaptic density protein 95 (PSD95), markers of synaptic plasticity [<xref rid="B63-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>].</p><p>Ginsentides, recently identified cyclic peptides from <italic toggle="yes">P. ginseng</italic>, are key bioactive constituents responsible for the so-called “cure-all” effects of ginseng, coordinating multiple physiological systems to alleviate stress and stress-related disorders [<xref rid="B64-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>]. Ginsentides modulate membrane receptors, including opioid, adrenergic, and serotonergic receptors, and exhibit stronger adaptogenic activity than ginsenosides alone [<xref rid="B64-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>]. Clinically, hydroponically grown red <italic toggle="yes">P. ginseng</italic> (600 mg/day for 8 weeks) reduced perceived stress by 23%, improved cognitive function, and modulated emotional processing in moderately stressed individuals in a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial [<xref rid="B65-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>].</p><p>Ginseng normalizes HPA axis function by regulating the expression of ACTH, corticosterone, and BDNF genes in the hippocampus of CUMS-exposed rats, as demonstrated in molecular mechanistic studies [<xref rid="B66-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>]. The standardized ginseng extract G115<sup>®</sup> enhances the antidepressant effects of fluoxetine by reducing immobility time in the forced swimming test and increasing BDNF expression in the left hippocampus and left prefrontal cortex [<xref rid="B64-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>].
