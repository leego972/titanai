Dysregulation of angiogenesis is involved in several pathological conditions, including atherosclerosis, proliferative retinopathies, rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis, tumor growth and metastasis [<xref rid="B155-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">155</xref>]. It is well recognized that angiogenesis is essential for the growth and metastasis of most solid malignancies, an increased body of evidence supports the enhancement of angiogenesis in hematologic malignancies as well [<xref rid="B156-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">156</xref>]. Therefore, angiogenesis is currently becoming an important target for chemotherapeutic approaches in cancer therapy [<xref rid="B157-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">157</xref>].</p><p>Antiangiogenic potential of partially purified quassinoid-rich fraction (TAF273) of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> root extract was evaluated using <italic toggle="yes">ex vivo</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> angiogenesis models and the anti-angiogenic efficacy of TAF273 were investigated in human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVEC). <italic toggle="yes">In vivo</italic>, it causes significant suppression in sprouting of microvessels in the rat aorta (IC<sub>50</sub>, 11.5 μg/mL), and shows a remarkable inhibition (63.13%) of neovascularization in chorioallantoic membrane of the chick embryo (IC<sub>50</sub>, 50 μg/mL). <italic toggle="yes">In vitro</italic>, TAF273 significantly inhibited the major angiogenesis steps such as proliferation, migration and differentiation of HUVECs. Thus, <italic toggle="yes">E</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">longifolia</italic> could be the potential source of promising therapeutic agents to treat angiogenesis-related disorders [<xref rid="B158-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">158</xref>].</p><p>Fractions of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> extract have also been reported to induce apoptosis in breast cancer cells [<xref rid="B152-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">152</xref>]. Further, Tee <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, elucidated the mode of action of F16 (a plant-derived pharmacologically active fraction) and observed that the intrinsic apoptotic pathway was invoked, with the reduction of Bcl-2 protein. It was concluded that the F16 from <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> exerts anti-proliferative action and growth inhibition on MCF-7 cells through apoptosis induction, and that it may have anticancer properties [<xref rid="B151-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">151</xref>].</p><p>The anti-proliferative, apoptotic and differentiating activities of partially purified sub-fractions (F1–F3) of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> root extracts were investigated on HL-60 leukemic cells. F1 showed unremarkable growth inhibition rate while F2 and F3 showed growth inhibitory effects with median inhibitory concentration (IC<sub>50</sub>) values of 15.2 and 28.6 µg/mL, respectively. <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> extract (F2) showed promising anti-leukemic activity and can be a candidate for the development of a drug for the treatment of acute promyelocytic leukemia (APL) [<xref rid="B159-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">159</xref>].</p><p>Nurhanan <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, evaluated the methanol, <italic toggle="yes">n</italic>-butanol, chloroform and water extracts obtained from the root of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> for its possible cytotoxic effect against KB, DU-145, RD, MCF-7, CaOV-3, and MDBK cell lines. Their results indicated that except for the water extract, all the other extracts produced significant cytotoxic effecte on these cell lines with no significant cytotoxic effect on MDBK (kidney) normal cell line.
