Fundoscopic findings in CRAO depend on the duration and degree of retinal ischaemia from the arterial occlusion [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0017" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>]. Fundus changes in the acute phase include a cherry red spot in acute central retinal artery occlusion, box carring, or retinal arterial attenuation. Late findings may include optic atrophy, retinal arterial attenuation, cilioretinal collaterals, and macula pigment epithelial changes. The diagnosis may not be forthcoming especially if a patient presents in the subacute phase. The OCTA has the advantage of seeing multiple layers, including the choroidal and capillary layers simultaneously, and several of the “late” fundus findings, such as arterial attenuation, are noted in the deep layers of OCTA [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0001" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>]. In the acute phase in OCTA, there is relative preservation of the radial peripapillary capillary (RPC) plexus. However, deep retinal capillary plexus dropout has been demonstrated, with clear demarcation of non‐perfusion, corresponding to regions of delayed perfusion on FFA [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0018" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0019" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>]. This vessel attenuation is modulated if early reperfusion occurs. the degree of vessel attenuation positively correlates to retinal nerve fibre layer (RNFL) thickness [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0018" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0019" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>]. The severity of superficial capillary density loss correlates closely to visual acuity at presentation [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0020" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>]. Preservation of deep capillary density is observed in those with cilioretinal artery sparing, in branch retinal artery occlusion (BRAO), and in those with transient CRAO with early restoration of deep capillary density. These findings suggest that deep capillary plexus (DCP) density on OCTA may be a predictor of long‐term visual prognosis [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0018" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>]. PAMM is an OCT diagnosis characterised by a hyper‐reflective thickened band in the inner nuclear layer (INL) on OCT cross‐section, and retinal capillary ischaemia in the intermediate capillary plexus (ICP) and DCP on en face OCTA imaging [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0022" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0024" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0025" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>].</p><p>PAMM can be difficult to diagnose clinically on fundus examination, and when clinically evident, appears as a subtle wedge‐shaped retinal blanching in the cilioretinal artery territory [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0022" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0023" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0025" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>].
