(<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR118">2017</xref>)</td></tr></tbody></table></table-wrap></p></sec><sec id="Sec17"><title>Bartogenic acid</title><p id="Par23">Bartogenic acid (BA) isolated from <italic toggle="yes">Barringtonia racemosa</italic> was evaluated in arthritic rats in which arthritis was induced by Complete Freud’s Adjuvant (CFA). The results indicated that BA exhibited a protective role in rats against the haematological perturbations, body weight changes as well as primary and secondary arthritic lesions induced by CFA (doses 2, 5, and 10 mg kg<sup>−1</sup> day<sup>−1</sup>, p.o.) with remarkable reduction in the rheumatoid factor and C-reactive protein (CRP), which are distinct serum markers of arthritis and inflammation. This result establishes the potency of BA in arthritis. However, further clinical investigations are mandatory to demonstrate the efficiency of BA in the treatment of several immuno-inflammatory ailments (Ali et al. <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR7">2007</xref>; Patil et al. <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR87">2011</xref>; Sun et al. <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR104">2006</xref>).</p></sec><sec id="Sec18"><title>Quercetin</title><p id="Par24">Quercetin (QU), a natural flavonoid, is found in hundreds of herbs, vegetables, and fruits. QU has numerous pharmacological potentials, such as anti-inflammation, anti-platelet aggregation, expanding coronary arteries, antitumor, lowering blood pressure, anti-allergy, etc. For a single oral dose of 4 g or 500 mg twice a day, no side effects were reported after continuous administration for a month (Haleagrahara et al. <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR43">2017</xref>). When analyzed on adjuvant-induced arthritis in mice, it exhibited anti-inflammatory action by lowering the production of macrophage inflammatory mediators by regulating NF-κB activity, and finally resulted in the inhibition of inflammatory cytokine expression and inflammatory reactions. QU can inhibit the activity of MMP-2, thereby inhibiting the degradation of the basement membrane and indirectly inhibiting angiogenesis and cartilage damage (Haleagrahara et al. <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR43">2017</xref>; Ji et al. <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR49">2013</xref>).</p><p id="Par25">Askari and colleagues studied the effect of flavonoid QU (500 mg/day) supplementation in healthy male non-professional athletes for 2 months, with regular exercise. The results showed a remarkable lowering in the levels of CRP (Askari et al. <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR11">2012</xref>). QU having the ability to inhibit xanthine oxidase prevented the uric acid accumulation, which benefited the gout patients (Anand David et al. <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">2016</xref>).</p><p id="Par26">In a pathological condition, QU did not show any significant alteration in CRP levels in women with RA (Javadi et al. <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR48">2017</xref>). The QU dosage continuously for 8 weeks significantly reduced after-activity pain, morning pain, and EMS (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.05). In comparison to the placebo, hs-plasma TNF-α level, Health Assessment Questionnaire (HAQ) scores, DAS-28, and the number of patients were decreased in the QU group with active disease (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.05). Thus, there were notified improvements in clinical symptoms, hs-TNFα, disease activity, and HAQ after 500 mg/day QU dose intake in women suffering from RA for 8 weeks (Javadi et al. <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR48">2017</xref>).</p></sec><sec id="Sec19"><title>Epigallocatechin-3-gallate</title><p id="Par27">Epigallocatechin-3-gallate (EGCG) is abundant in green tea and can be an effective adjunct therapy for the treatment. It has been reported to inhibit inflammatory mediators like PGE2, COX-2, NF-κB, iNOS, interferon-γ, TNF-α in arthritic joints collagen type II-induced arthritis (CIA) in pre-clinical studies (Ahmed <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR2">2010</xref>). Experiments conducted by Ahmed and colleagues have shown that pre-treatment of human chondrocytes with EGCG significantly prohibited the activities and expression of MMP-13 and MMP-1 (IC<sub>50</sub> values 16.5 and 27 mM, respectively) in vitro in a dose-dependent manner.
