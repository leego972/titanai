Patients with ADOA show more generalised peripapillary and macular capillary loss, while LHON has earlier papillomacular bundle loss.</p></sec><sec id="ceo14544-sec-0013"><label>4.2</label><title>Optic Disc Anomalies</title><p>Coloboma is a rare congenital malformation that stems from incomplete embryonic fissure closure, with potential for significant visual impairment. OCTA of colobomatous optic discs (ODC) demonstrates disorganisation and attenuation of the microvascular plexus at the optic disc [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0112" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0113" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0114" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>]. Retinal pigment epithelium abnormalities and disintegrity of Bruch's membrane can rarely lead to the development of choroidal neovascularisation in adulthood in patients with coloboma (Figure <xref rid="ceo14544-fig-0007" ref-type="fig">7</xref>) [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0115" ref-type="bibr">115</xref>]. OCTA may be a potentially useful tool to prognosticate regarding such complications based on microvascular patterns in and around the site of coloboma [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0113" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>]. ODC manifests clinically with a tilted optic disc and can be difficult to differentiate from other optic disc anomalies, such as optic disc pit, morning glory disc, and peripapillary staphyloma (PPS). OCTA can assist with differentiating ODC from these optic disc anomalies [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0114" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>]. Optic disc pit manifests with excavation and a hyperreflective region in the optic disc head with possible associated macular schisis on OCT cross section [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0114" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>]. There is associated absence of a RPC network in the region of the optic disc pit [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0114" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0116" ref-type="bibr">116</xref>]. Morning glory syndrome (MGS) manifests clinically with axial displacement of the optic disc and peripheral optic nerve atrophy. OCT cross section reveals optic disc excavation and preretinal membrane [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0114" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>]. OCTA demonstrates a normal superficial and deep RPC network on OCTA [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0114" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0117" ref-type="bibr">117</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0118" ref-type="bibr">118</xref>]. PSS accounts for 1.5%–65% of posterior staphyloma, a characteristic feature of pathological myopia. While individuals with PPS have optic disc tilt and peripapillary atrophy, which can manifest similarly to posterior segment examination as with individuals with MGS [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0119" ref-type="bibr">119</xref>]. OCTA demonstrates significantly decreased microvascular density in the RPC and choriocapillaris, especially in the inferior and superior quadrants when compared to myopic control eyes. This was correlated with reduced RGCL count and RNFL thinning in these same quadrants [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0119" ref-type="bibr">119</xref>].
