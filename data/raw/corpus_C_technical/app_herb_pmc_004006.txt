These adaptogens contain mainly phenolic compounds and do not contain triterpenes. In contrast, repeated administration of stress-protectors that contained mainly triterpenes but no phenolics, produced little effect on p-SAPK/p-JNK during stress. Under resting conditions, <italic toggle="yes">R. rosea</italic> and rhodioloside induced increases in total JNK after single (but not repeated) dose administration. It appears, therefore, that repeated administration of <italic toggle="yes">R. rosea</italic> and rhodioloside results in adaptation of the organism to these “stressors.”</p><p>The curative effect of an extract of <italic toggle="yes">S. chinensis</italic> on patients with asthenia and depressive syndromes has been established in several studies (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b45-dti-2-2007-039">Staritsina, 1946</xref>; <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b54-dti-2-2007-039">Zakharova, 1948</xref>; <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b24-dti-2-2007-039">Leman, 1952</xref>). Furthermore, it has recently been demonstrated that the standardized extract SHR-5 from <italic toggle="yes">R. rosea</italic> possesses a clear and significant anti-depressive activity in patients suffering from mild to moderate depression (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b7-dti-2-2007-039">Darbinyan et al. 2006</xref>). It can be hypothesized that the antidepressant effects of <italic toggle="yes">R. rosea</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">S. chinensis</italic> are associated with the inhibition of emotional stress induced by the over-activation of p-SAPK/p-JNK. The SAPK/JNK pathway is known to be involved in the pathogenesis of glucocorticoid resistance (GR) found in certain chronic immune/inflammatory diseases and in subgroups of patients with major depression, and activation of SAPK/JNK has been reported to inhibit GR function (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b49-dti-2-2007-039">Wang et al. 2005</xref>). Moreover, physiologic activation of SAPK/JNK appears necessary for the induction of long term depression, and over-activation of these kinases by cytokines at patho-physiological concentrations is detrimental to long term potentiation. It has thus been suggested that SAPK/JNK pathways may represent a therapeutic target for the normalization of GR function in these disorders (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b49-dti-2-2007-039">Wang et al. 2005</xref>).</p><p>Since SAPK/JNK is activated in Alzheimer disease (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b23-dti-2-2007-039">Lagalwar et al. 2006</xref>), the inhibition of such activation might provide some protection from stress-induced apoptotic cell death. In this context, adaptogens could induce a positive effect in neurodegenerative disorders characterized by the loss of neurons in brain regions involved in learning and memory. Thus, it is suggested that the beneficial effects of <italic toggle="yes">R. rosea</italic> and rhodioloside on mental performance in stress, as well as the protection against neurotoxicity offered by <italic toggle="yes">S. chinensis</italic>, might be associated with their inhibitory effect on the formation of p–SAPK. Related data may be considered to add further support to the hypothesis that adaptogens have a therapeutic effect in neurological and neurodegenerative disorders. Thus, both <italic toggle="yes">R. rosea</italic> and rhodioloside inhibit propyl endoperoxidase, which is known to play a role in the degradation of neuropeptides involved in the process of learning and memory (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b8-dti-2-2007-039">Fan et al. 2001</xref>). Moreover, rhodioloside may protect PC12 cells against the excitotoxic action of glutamate by suppressing the excessive entry of Ca<sup>2+</sup> and the release of calcium stores (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b5-dti-2-2007-039">Cao et al. 2006</xref>), whilst an extract of <italic toggle="yes">S. chinensis</italic> fruit, and the active components schizandrins A, B and C, significantly reduce the neurotoxic action of glutamate (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b19-dti-2-2007-039">Kim et al. 2004</xref>).</p></sec></sec></sec><sec><title>Conclusion</title><p>It has been demonstrated that nitric acid and cortisol are appropriate stress markers that can be employed in the evaluation of the anti-stress effects of stress-protectors and adaptogens. It is noteworthy that only p-SAPK/p-JNK appears to be a potential marker in bioassays of adaptogens and presumably of potential antidepressants.</p><sec><title>Notice of conflict of interest</title><p>This study was funded with project grants from the Research and Development Division of the Swedish Herbal Institute (the Sponsor): all materials were supplied by the sponsor.
