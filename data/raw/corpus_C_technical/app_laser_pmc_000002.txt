BY-NC</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><p>Photodynamic therapy (PDT) is a first-line treatment option for actinic field cancerization (<xref rid="CIT0001" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>). The efficacy and cosmetic outcomes of PDT are adequate; however, conventional PDT (cPDT) can cause extensive pain during treatment (<xref rid="CIT0001" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>).</p><p>Daylight-PDT (dPDT) has been introduced as a near-painless treatment alternative to cPDT. It enables treatment of large areas and is as effective as cPDT for thin grade I–II actinic keratoses (AKs) (<xref rid="CIT0002" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>). However, for thicker AKs, the efficacy of dPDT can decrease (<xref rid="CIT0003" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>). According to European guidelines, natural dPDT (ndPDT) can be used to treat grade I–II AKs at temperatures above 10°C from March to October in Finland, but not during rain (<xref rid="CIT0002" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>). In environments with variable and cold weather conditions, in particular, an option is artificial dPDT (adPDT) performed indoors, although this has not yet been extensively studied. There are several benefits of adPDT: the pain is as low as in ndPDT (<xref rid="CIT0004" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>), it can be performed at any time in a controlled and standardized environment, and there is no ultraviolet radiation. There have been a few studies on adPDT, using various light sources (<xref rid="CIT0005" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>–<xref rid="CIT0008" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>). Only 1 of these studies is a randomized controlled trial (RCT), in which adPDT was compared split-side with ndPDT on 22 patients, with comparable results (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> = 0.21) (<xref rid="CIT0006" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>).</p><p>Pre-treatment with ablative fractional laser (AFXL) facilitates absorption of the photosensitizer (<xref rid="CIT0009" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>), and enhances the formation of the active substance, protoporphyrin IX (PpIX), compared with curettage and other pre-treatments (<xref rid="CIT0010" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>). AFXL pre-treatment has proven effective without causing additional pain in cPDT (<xref rid="CIT0009" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>, <xref rid="CIT0011" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>), and is thus a promising alternative to improve the outcome and possibly also extend the use of dPDT in the treatment of thicker AKs. To our knowledge, there are 5 studies on AFXL-mediated dPDT (AFXL-dPDT) (<xref rid="CIT0012" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>–<xref rid="CIT0016" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>). Four studies used natural daylight and 1 used a multi-light lamp as illumination source. All studies included AKs of all grades. There is a need for larger scale studies on the subject, as 4 of the studies included fewer than 20 patients.</p><sec sec-type="materials|methods" id="sec1"><title>MATERIALS AND METHODS</title><p>This study compared AFXL-dPDT with dPDT alone in a randomized controlled split-head and investigator-blinded study on 60 patients during the years 2018 to 2022 at the Dermatology Outpatient Clinic, Skin and Allergy Hospital, Helsinki University Hospital, Helsinki, Finland.
