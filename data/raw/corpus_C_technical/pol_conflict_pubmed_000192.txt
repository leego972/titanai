Artificial oxygen carriers and red blood cell substitutes: A historic overview 
and recent developments toward military and clinical relevance.

Bialas C(1), Moser C, Sims CA.

Author information:
(1)From the Department of Biochemistry and Biophysics (C.B., C.M., C.A.S.), 
Johnson Research Foundation, Penn Acute Research Collaboration (C.A.M.), and 
Division of Trauma, Surgical Critical Care, and Emergency Surgery (C.B., 
C.A.M.), Perelman School of Medicine, University of Pennsylvania, Philadelphia, 
Pennsylvania.

Packed red blood cells are a critical component in the resuscitation of 
hemorrhagic shock. The availability of donor-derived blood products, however, 
suffers from issues of supply, immunogenicity, and pathogenic contamination. 
Deployment in remote or austere environments, such as the battlefield, is 
further hindered by the inherent perishability of blood products. To address the 
significant limitations of allogenic packed red blood cells and the urgent 
medical need for better resuscitative therapies for both combat casualties and 
civilians, there has been significant research invested in developing safe, 
effective, and field deployable artificial oxygen carriers. This article 
provides a comprehensive review of the most important technologies in the field 
of artificial oxygen carriers including cell-free and encapsulated 
hemoglobin-based oxygen carriers, perfluorocarbon emulsions, natural hemoglobin 
alternatives, as well as other novel technologies. Their development status, 
clinical, and military relevance are discussed. LEVEL OF EVIDENCE: Systematic 
review.
