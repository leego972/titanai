Low-level laser therapy (LLLT) has emerged as a potential regenerative treatment to enhance tissue repair. This study aims to investigate the effects of LLLT on oral mucosal wound healing and oxidative stress markers in rats with DM. <italic toggle="yes">Materials and Methods</italic>: Male Wistar rats (n = 108) were divided into six equal groups (healthy and diabetic, with or without mucosal ulcers, with or without LLLT). DM was induced with alloxan, and standardized mucosal ulcers were created. Every other day for 10 days, LLLT (6 J/cm<sup>2</sup>) was applied, and tissue samples were collected after 3, 7, and 10 days. Wound healing was assessed by planimetry, while systemic biochemical analyses included total oxidative status (TOS), total antioxidant capacity (TAC), superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity, and oxidative stress index (OSI). <italic toggle="yes">Results</italic>: LLLT significantly accelerated oral ulcer closure and showed between-group differences in redox markers. In healthy rats, LLLT increased wound closure on day 7 (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> = 0.018). In diabetic rats, LLLT improved closure on day 3 (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> = 0.035) and on day 7 (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> = 0.001). Across groups, oxidative markers differed significantly (e.g., TOS on day 10 overall, <italic toggle="yes">p</italic> = 0.011; OSI on day 10 overall, <italic toggle="yes">p</italic> = 0.047; SOD <italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.001 at all time points). In diabetic rats, on day 10, median TOS was lower with LLLT (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> = 0.004). <italic toggle="yes">Conclusions</italic>: LLLT enhances oral wound healing and restores redox balance in diabetic rats, which supports the potential usage of LLLT as an adjunctive therapy for managing oral lesions in diabetic patients.</p></abstract><kwd-group><kwd>low-level laser
