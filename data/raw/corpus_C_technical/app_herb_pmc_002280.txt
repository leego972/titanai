BY</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><sec sec-type="intro"><title>Introduction</title><p>Natural products have long played a foundational role in drug discovery due to their structural diversity, biological specificity, and pharmacological potential. In recent years, interest in traditional medicinal plants has intensified owing to their demonstrated antioxidant, antimicrobial, and anticancer activities (<xref rid="ref-8" ref-type="bibr">Chaachouay &amp; Zidane, 2024</xref>; <xref rid="ref-10" ref-type="bibr">Chunarkar-Patil et al., 2024</xref>). Marine- and land-derived botanicals continue to serve as a pivotal source of novel therapeutic compounds, supporting ongoing natural-product–based pharmaceutical development (<xref rid="ref-6" ref-type="bibr">Banday et al., 2024</xref>). Foundational literature further highlights the historical contribution of natural compounds to modern drug discovery platforms (<xref rid="ref-11" ref-type="bibr">Cragg &amp; Newman, 2013</xref>; <xref rid="ref-34" ref-type="bibr">Thomford et al., 2018</xref>). Within this context, the combination of herbal extract represents a promising polyherbal approach, as each plant contains bioactive compounds with clinically relevant properties. Sidr, a member of the Rhamnaceae family, has been traditionally used for dermatological, inflammatory, and gastrointestinal illnesses. Its phytochemical profile includes flavonoids, alkaloids, saponins, and tannins, contributing to strong antioxidant and antimicrobial activity (<xref rid="ref-12" ref-type="bibr">Egigu &amp; Mogesse, 2024</xref>; <xref rid="ref-1" ref-type="bibr">Abdel-Sattar et al., 2024</xref>). Several studies have also demonstrated anticancer effects associated with <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus</italic> species, attributed to phenolic and alkaloid-mediated apoptosis pathways (<xref rid="ref-22" ref-type="bibr">Mongalo, Mashele &amp; Makhafola, 2020</xref>; <xref rid="ref-26" ref-type="bibr">Pourahmadi et al., 2023</xref>). Fenugreek (<italic toggle="yes">Trigonella foenum-graecum</italic>), a widely cultivated Fabaceae herb, contains diverse bioactive constituents such as diosgenin and steroidal saponins, which contribute to anticancer, and hypoglycemic effects (<xref rid="ref-18" ref-type="bibr">Khatoon et al., 2024</xref>; <xref rid="ref-16" ref-type="bibr">Kalantari et al., 2024</xref>). Its antioxidant properties are particularly notable, reducing oxidative stress linked to metabolic disorders and tumorigenesis (<xref rid="ref-28" ref-type="bibr">Shadab, Akhtar &amp; Siddiqui, 2024</xref>). Additional studies support its roles in lipid regulation and metabolic stability (<xref rid="ref-32" ref-type="bibr">Singh, Chaurasia &amp; Bharati, 2023</xref>; <xref rid="ref-19" ref-type="bibr">Kumar et al., 2021</xref>).</p><p>Recent <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> studies have demonstrated that fenugreek extracts exert sex-specific pharmacological effects, particularly in hepatic and renal tissues.
