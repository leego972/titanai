Active Compounds</title><p>Chemically, the adaptogens are typically either complex phenolics or tetracyclic triterpenoids/ steroids (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="pharmaceuticals-03-00188-f002">Figure 2</xref>). The phenolic compounds include phenylpropanoids and phenylethane derivatives, such as salidroside (rhodioloside), rosavin, syringin, triandrin, tyrosol, [<xref rid="B28-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>,<xref rid="B35-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>,<xref rid="B38-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>,<xref rid="B39-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>,<xref rid="B40-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>,<xref rid="B41-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>,<xref rid="B42-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>,<xref rid="B43-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>] and lignans, such as eleutherosid E [<xref rid="B44-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>,<xref rid="B45-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>,<xref rid="B46-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>,<xref rid="B47-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>,<xref rid="B48-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">48</xref>] and schisandrin B [<xref rid="B49-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>,<xref rid="B50-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>,<xref rid="B51-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>,<xref rid="B52-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>,<xref rid="B53-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>,<xref rid="B54-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">54</xref>,<xref rid="B55-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>,<xref rid="B56-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>,<xref rid="B57-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>,<xref rid="B58-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>,<xref rid="B59-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>,<xref rid="B60-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>]. They are structurally similar to the catecholamines -the mediators of the sympathoadrenal system (SAS) involved in activation of the stress system in the early stages of stress response. The tetracyclic triterpenoids, such as cucurbitacin R diglucoside [<xref rid="B61-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>,<xref rid="B62-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>], ginsenosides [<xref rid="B63-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>,<xref rid="B64-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>,<xref rid="B65-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>,<xref rid="B66-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>,<xref rid="B67-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>,<xref rid="B68-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>] and phytosterol-glycosides (e.g, SG, eleutheroside A, sitoindosides, daucosterol) [<xref rid="B27-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>,<xref rid="B69-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>,<xref rid="B70-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>,<xref rid="B71-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>] structurally resemble the corticosteroids that act as stress hormones involved in protective inactivation of the stress system [<xref rid="B72-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>,<xref rid="B73-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>,<xref rid="B74-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>].
