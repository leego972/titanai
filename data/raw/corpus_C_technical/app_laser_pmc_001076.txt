<italic toggle="yes">Analysis of defective dental polymer composite restorations</italic>
</p><p>Conventional OCTs are considered useful in detecting the cavitated border, fracture lines, and interfacial gaps in the tooth-restoration interface. <sup><xref rid="ref49" ref-type="bibr">49</xref></sup> This property appears to be due to strong reflection from the material surface in conventional OCT setups for detecting the reflective surface from the defects. <sup><xref rid="ref50" ref-type="bibr">50</xref></sup> Concerning dental polymer composites, the polymer's composition could affect signal attenuation of OCT and depends on the refractive index difference between polymers resin and the filler. <sup><xref rid="ref51" ref-type="bibr">51</xref></sup> OCT imaging can detect the air bubbles or void within the composite restoration
<sup><xref rid="ref52" ref-type="bibr">52</xref></sup> and evaluate bonding interface. <sup><xref rid="ref53" ref-type="bibr">53</xref></sup> This assessment becomes easy as poorly sealed polymer composites demonstrate brightly clustered images (revealing gaps), whereas tightly fit boundaries do not exhibit too much scattering. <sup><xref rid="ref54" ref-type="bibr">54</xref></sup> Ishibashi
<italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> performed a study previously and reported that SS-OCT could detect resin-based polymer composite restorations defects as it provides a much higher resolution than other conventional techniques. <sup><xref rid="ref55" ref-type="bibr">55</xref></sup> The OCT technique is also a useful tool to analyze volumetric polymerization shrinkage (VPS). Sampio
<italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> performed a study to measure the VPS of different polymer composites via micro-computed tomography, and to qualitatively compare the gap formation through OCT. <sup><xref rid="ref56" ref-type="bibr">56</xref></sup> It was concluded from the results of their study that while both techniques were useful, VPS assessment was largely material dependent. <sup><xref rid="ref56" ref-type="bibr">56</xref></sup>
</p><p>
<italic toggle="yes">Imaging of root canals</italic>
</p><p>In an earlier
<italic toggle="yes">in-vitro</italic> study, Lino
<italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> reported that SS-OCT could be used to locate the second mesiobuccal canal in maxillary molars. <sup><xref rid="ref57" ref-type="bibr">57</xref></sup> Rashed
<italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> also reported similar findings in their study and reported that SS-OCT has the potential to accurately measure residual dentin thickness (RDT) and image root canals along with other internal tooth anatomic structures like pulp horns and isthmus. <sup><xref rid="ref58" ref-type="bibr">58</xref></sup> The SS-OCT technique is a useful tool to accurately observe fractures of tooth roots and root canal endoscopy. <sup><xref rid="ref59" ref-type="bibr">59</xref></sup> Krause
<italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> utilized OCT to monitor the pulp chamber’s roof and RDT and reported that this technique could be useful in preserving the pulp’s vitality while preparing deep cavities in dentin. <sup><xref rid="ref60" ref-type="bibr">60</xref></sup>
</p><p>
<italic toggle="yes">Detection of oral cancer</italic>
</p><p>Oral cancer is one of the most fatal and prevalent cancers in the world. In India, it is the third most typical form of cancer,
<sup><xref rid="ref61" ref-type="bibr">61</xref></sup> and among those, 90% are oral squamous cell carcinomas. <sup><xref rid="ref62" ref-type="bibr">62</xref></sup> Lee
<italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> earlier reported that OCT is a reliable technique to diagnose oral precancerous lesions with high sensitivity and specificity. <sup><xref rid="ref63" ref-type="bibr">63</xref></sup> OCT has been successfully applied previously to diagnose oral malignancies in the hamster cheek pouch model. <sup><xref rid="ref64" ref-type="bibr">64</xref></sup> Adegun
<italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> used OCT to diagnose epithelial dysplasia and reported that this technique could offer non-invasive method to locate the appropriate site of biopsy and thus could be useful in the early diagnosis of oral cancer.
