Elongation of lifespan and anti-aging activity</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> markedly increased the lifespan of <italic toggle="yes">Drosophila melanogaster</italic>, possibly by increasing the resistance of organs to stress and alleviating oxidative stress [<xref rid="b33-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>]. Salidroside considerably reversed senescence-like phenotypes in an oxidant-challenged model, altering the morphology, cell cycle, SA-b-gal staining, and DNA damage as well as the expression of related molecules such as p53, p21, and p16 in the 2BS cell line (human fetal lung fibroblasts). Furthermore, the protection occurred in a dose-dependent manner [<xref rid="b34-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>]. In addition, salidroside blocked D-galactose-induced increases in serum advanced glycation endproduct levels in C57BL/6J mice [<xref rid="b35-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>]. It reversed D-galactose-induced aging effects in neural and immune systems, improved motor activity, increased memory latency time, and enhanced lymphocyte mitogenesis and interleukin-2 (IL-2) production [<xref rid="b35-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>]. Furthermore, salidroside reduced the elevated expression of glial fibrillary acidic protein and neurotrophin-3 in a mouse model of aging [<xref rid="b35-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>].</p><p>Accordingly, <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> plant extracts and salidroside are potential agents for retarding aging and attenuating age-related diseases in humans and animals.</p></sec><sec sec-type="other"><title>4.
