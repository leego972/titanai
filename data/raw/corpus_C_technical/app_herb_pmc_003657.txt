The location of the herbal market at the DBF is centered on the Qianwanjia Agriculture Trade Fairs and extends to two streets, Xinjie Road and Gongmao Road in Nayong County. <fig id="Fig1" position="float" orientation="portrait"><label>Fig. 1</label><caption><p>The location of Nayong County, Guizhou Province, China</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" id="MO1" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="13002_2021_447_Fig1_HTML.jpg"/></fig></p></sec><sec id="Sec4"><title>Ethnobotanical data collection</title><p id="Par16">This survey was conducted from June 2018 to June 2019 at the DBF. In this survey, 52 informants were investigated, of whom 33 were males and 19 were females, aged 25 to 80 years, with an average age of 56 years. The medicinal materials sold included one to 25 kinds per informant, 90% of which were wild medicinal materials, obtained mainly through self-collection. Key informant interviews, semi-structured interviews, and free listing were used to obtain information about the ages of the vendors, the names of the medicinal materials, the medicinal parts, the preparation methods, and the functions, indications, sources and collection methods of the medicinal materials (<xref rid="Sec20" ref-type="sec">Appendix</xref>). After collecting the basic information, the medicinal materials were bought from the vendors and used as specimens. Each exsiccata was identified according to the <italic toggle="yes">Flora of China</italic> [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR27">27</xref>] and <italic toggle="yes">Flora of Guizhou</italic> [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR28">28</xref>]. The plant families and species followed the <italic toggle="yes">World Flora Online</italic> (<ext-link xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" ext-link-type="uri" xlink:href="http://www.worldfloraonline.org/">http://www.worldfloraonline.org</ext-link>.), which was used to provide a uniform nomenclature after identification. All the voucher specimens were identified by Hongxiang Yin, an Associate Professor of the Chengdu University of TCM and Yuxiang Shen, an Associate Professor of the Anshun College. The voucher specimens were preserved at the Specimen Center of Chengdu University of TCM (CDCM). At the same time, the collected plant information was compared with the ChP [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR22">22</xref>], QSG [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR23">23</xref>], and the traditional medicines in the countries of Southeast Asia. The protected status of the collected medicinal plants was identified by the Information System of Chinese Rare and Endangered Plants [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>] of the Chinese Academy of Sciences.</p></sec><sec id="Sec5"><title>Data analysis</title><p id="Par17">The quantitative statistical indexes of ethnobotany were calculated by Microsoft Excel 2010, including the use-value (UV) and cultural importance index (CI).
