Consistently, our in vitro studies confirmed that LLLT significantly accelerates Zol-treated HaCaT cell migration. However, the mechanisms of LLLT increasing IL-1RA expression are not clear. The increase of IL-1RA may be due to the direct stimulation of LLLT or the subsequent response caused by LLLT-mediated elevation of other cytokines, like TGF-β1 or IL-4 [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR49">49</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR55">55</xref>]. Studies have also suggested that LLLT can upregulate IL-1RA in the keratinocytes and fibroblast co-culture system [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR56">56</xref>], or increase salivary levels of IL-1RA in the periodontitis model [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR57">57</xref>].</p><p id="Par58">There are still some limitations in the present study. There are many factors affecting the healing process of the MRONJ extraction sockets, like angiogenesis, bone remodeling, and immune regulation [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR58">58</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR60">60</xref>]. In our study, we focus more on how LLLT promoted primary gingival wound healing and prevented MRONJ; however, more research is required to fully understand the comprehensive effects of LLLT.</p></sec><sec id="Sec21"><title>Conclusions</title><p id="Par59">LLLT can improve primary soft tissue healing by IL-1RA-mediated tissue inflammation inhibition and epithelial cell migration, thus promoting underlying osseous tissue repair and preventing the development of MRONJ. This study sheds new light on how LLLT helps to prevent MRONJ and offers new ideas and strategies for treating other disorders caused by an IL-1β-mediated excessive inflammatory response in the wound.</p></sec><sec sec-type="supplementary-material"><title>Supplementary Information</title><sec id="Sec22"><p>
