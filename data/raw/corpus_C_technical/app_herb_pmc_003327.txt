[<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref046" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>], the fidelity level (FL) is useful to determine the level of species importance in relation to a particular disease. FL shows the percentage of respondents who mention the use of a plant species for the same main purpose. Ouedraogo <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>. [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref021" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>] reported that relative frequency of citation could also be counted based on FL. This is designed to measure species importance for specific purposes. Our results showed that FL of the 30 plant species ranged from 1.92 to 80% (<xref rid="pone.0235886.t003" ref-type="table">Table 3</xref>). <italic toggle="yes">A</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">calamus</italic> demonstrated the highest FL for fever (80%), followed by <italic toggle="yes">A</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">graveolens</italic> (76.92%) and <italic toggle="yes">A</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">fistulosum</italic> (71.15) for treating hypertension and cough, respectively. Based on a previous study, plants with a high percentage of FL are more frequently used as bio-pharmacological resources [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref047" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>] and should be
