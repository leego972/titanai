Introduction</title><p>Surgical extraction of third molar is one of the most common oral surgeries in dentistry. After extraction, a traumatic inflammatory response occurs along the mucosal wound with pain and facial swelling [<xref rid="B1-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>]. The mucosal wound remains under high risk of infection in the oral environment [<xref rid="B2-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>]. To reduce the postoperative complications and promote healing of the mucosal wounds, application of additional methods could be considered [<xref rid="B3-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>]. Low-level laser therapy (LLLT) is increasingly being used for reducing postoperative complications after various surgical procedures in intraoral regions because it enhances tissue regeneration and wound healing, as well as decreasing pain and edema through anti-inflammatory procedures [<xref rid="B4-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>]. This anti-inflammatory procedure is achieved by a mechanism of increased adenosine triphosphate (ATP) synthesis and decreased oxidative stress [<xref rid="B3-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B5-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>,<xref rid="B6-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>]. The accelerated healing effect of laser is caused by stimulation of natural biological processes [<xref rid="B7-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>]. Furthermore, the advantageous effects of LLLT were also reported by several in vitro experiments; LLLT induces proliferation of human gingival fibroblast [<xref rid="B8-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>] and reduces inflammatory conditions [<xref rid="B9-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>].</p><p>Previous studies, however, showed debatable results regarding the efficacy of LLLT [<xref rid="B10-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>]. Batinjan et al. [<xref rid="B11-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>] have proven that LLLT significantly reduced postoperative complications after surgical removal of third molars. On the other hand, it has been documented that no statistically significant differences were observed in the levels of pain, postoperative swelling, or trismus between the control and LLLT groups after a third molar extraction [<xref rid="B8-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>]. One of the reasons for this controversy is that the conditions of optimal laser irradiation for anti-inflammatory and accelerated healing effects after oral surgery have not been established due to its diverse forms such as output power, energy, duration, pulse rate, and wavelength of laser.</p><p>Recently, diode laser has been the most popular LLLT technique in dentistry for its good tissue penetration, lower financial costs, small size for portable application, and convenience to use [<xref rid="B12-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>].
