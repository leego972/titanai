Double Resonance
Double Resonance is the phenomena when an additional magnetic field is applied to a two-level atom in addition to the typical electromagnetic field used to drive resonance fluorescence. This lifts the spin degeneracy of the Zeeman energy levels splitting them along the energies associated with the respective available spin levels, allowing for not only resonance to be achieved around the typical excited state, but if a second driving electromagnetic associated with the Larmor frequency is applied, a second resonance can be achieved around the energy state associated with  and the states associated with . Thus resonance is achievable not only about the possible energy-levels of a two-level atom, but also about the sub-levels in the energy created by lifting the degeneracy of the level. If the applied magnetic field is tuned properly, the polarization of resonance fluorescence can be used to describe the composition of the excited state. Thus double resonance can be used to find the Landé factor, which is used to describe the magnetic moment of the electron within the two-level atom. Resonance fluorescence of a single artificial atom
