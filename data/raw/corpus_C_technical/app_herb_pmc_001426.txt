# PMC: phytochemical bioactive compound anticancer — Overview

These species are grown mostly in the northern hemisphere’s Mediterranean, temperate, and cold climates [<xref rid="B7-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">B. oleracea</italic>, the dominant vegetable species, includes a wide range of morphological variants such as broccoli (<italic toggle="yes">B. oleracea</italic> var. <italic toggle="yes">italica</italic>), cabbage (<italic toggle="yes">B. oleracea</italic> var. <italic toggle="yes">capitata</italic>), kohlrabi (<italic toggle="yes">B. oleracea</italic> var. <italic toggle="yes">gongylodes</italic>), cauliflower (<italic toggle="yes">B. oleracea</italic> var. <italic toggle="yes">botrytis</italic>), Brussels sprouts (<italic toggle="yes">B. oleracea</italic> var. <italic toggle="yes">gemmifera</italic>), and kale (<italic toggle="yes">Brassica oleracea</italic> var. <italic toggle="yes">acephala</italic>) [<xref rid="B8-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">B. napus</italic>, including oilseed rape or canola, is an allotetraploid oilseed crop derived from <italic toggle="yes">B. oleracea</italic> L. and <italic toggle="yes">B. rapa</italic> L. [<xref rid="B9-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. The botanical classifications and dietary nomenclature of the most cultivated and consumed <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> species worldwide are listed in <xref rid="molecules-27-06008-t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref>.</p><p>The <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> genus constitutes a potential reservoir for food products with high economic and medicinal value in the world owing to the synergistic action of its bioactive compounds [<xref rid="B6-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="B11-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. The phytochemical screening and the beneficial properties in the vegetative organs of various <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> species have been well documented, such as <italic toggle="yes">B. nigra</italic> [<xref rid="B12-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>], <italic toggle="yes">B. oleracea</italic> [<xref rid="B8-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>,<xref rid="B13-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>], <italic toggle="yes">B. rapa</italic> [<xref rid="B14-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>], <italic toggle="yes">B. carinata</italic> [<xref rid="B15-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>], <italic toggle="yes">B. juncea</italic> [<xref rid="B16-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>,<xref rid="B17-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>], and <italic toggle="yes">B. napus</italic> [<xref rid="B18-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>,<xref rid="B19-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> species are mainly cultivated as vegetable crops, as fodder, and for their seeds as spices and oil sources. In particular, rapeseed oil is the world’s third-largest producer of edible oil, behind only soybeans and palm trees, accounting for 14% of global production [<xref rid="B10-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>,<xref rid="B20-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>]. Over the past few years, Nepal has been the leading producer of mustard seeds, with more than 32% of the worldwide global production in 2019, followed by Russia with 25% and Canada with 21%. The US, Germany, and France were the global highest importers of yellow and brown mustard seeds used as condiment and cooking oil. Notably, the FAOSTAT (Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations, 2021) data report that the UK (221,000 tons), Germany (202,000 tons), and Italy (190,000 tons) consumed the most prepared mustard worldwide in 2019 [<xref rid="B21-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>].</p><p>Recently, special emphasis has been placed on the seeds of <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> vegetables, and several research studies conducted on phytochemical screening have revealed that <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds, like all other organs of the vegetative system, are a very rich source of nutrients (carbohydrates, fats, proteins, vitamins, and minerals), and they contain a broad spectrum of various bioactive secondary metabolites of medicinal value, mainly phenolic compounds, glucosinolates and carotenoids. This richness in nutritional and medicinal compounds offers <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds a strong bioactive potential, mainly antioxidant, antiproliferative, antimicrobial, antidiabetic, anti-inflammatory, and neuroprotective properties.</p><p>Considering the versatility of the <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> genus, this review provides a comprehensive summary of research progress on the <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> species seed traditional and agronomic uses, phytochemical screening, and pharmacological properties.
