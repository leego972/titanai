Aliphatic, indolic, and aromatic glucosinolates are the three principal chemical groups of GLSs, classified according to the amino-acid precursor, forming more than 130 types. Aliphatic GLSs are mainly derived from methionine but also from alanine, leucine, isoleucine, or valine, while the indolic GLSs are tryptophan-derived compounds and aromatic GLSs are phenylalanine- and tyrosine-derived compounds [<xref rid="B91-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">91</xref>]. Detecting and identifying GLS directly or indirectly mainly depend on the existence (intact) or absence (desulfo) of the sulfate group. GLSs are hydrolyzed by myrosinase after cell disruption and by the gut microbiota [<xref rid="B92-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">92</xref>,<xref rid="B93-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">93</xref>]. Isothiocyanates (ITCs), nitriles (CNs), epithionitriles (EPNs), and thiocyanates are the common GLS breakdown products [<xref rid="B69-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>,<xref rid="B94-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>]. Myrosinase is inactivated at 60 °C and 700 MPa [<xref rid="B95-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>]. GLS content and profile vary with <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> species and organ, with a high amount in the reproductive system including florets, flowers, and seeds. Variations in the profile of glucosinolates and their hydrolysis products of different seeds have been detected, as shown in <xref rid="molecules-27-06008-t004" ref-type="table">Table 4</xref>. Thus, for example, it has been reported that the major glucosinolates found in <italic toggle="yes">B. rapa</italic> L. seeds were progoitrin, glucoraphanin, gluconapin, and 4-hydroxyglucobrassicin [<xref rid="B51-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>], whereas <italic toggle="yes">B. oleracea italica</italic>, the ethyl acetate oil of <italic toggle="yes">B. juncea raya</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">B. campestris</italic> were characterized by the presence of glucosinolates hydrolytic products such as allyl isothiocyanate, 2-phethyl isothiocyanate, 3-butenyl isothiocyanate, and 3-(methylthio) propyl isothiocyanate [<xref rid="B96-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>,<xref rid="B97-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>]. Glucoerucin and glucoraphanin were the two major glucosinolates found in broccoli seeds [<xref rid="B85-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">85</xref>]. Moreover, the indole glucosinolate, 4-hydroxy-3-indolylmethyl glucosinolate, was purified and identified as a major constituent of cabbage seed and rapeseed [<xref rid="B98-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">98</xref>]. In addition, it was found that sulforaphane is an abundant isothiocyanate of broccoli, containing 49.77 mg/g [<xref rid="B96-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>]. Given the richness of the seeds in sulforaphane (SFN), research work has focused on its isolation and purification in <italic toggle="yes">Brassica oleracea</italic> L. var. <italic toggle="yes">rubra</italic> seeds [<xref rid="B99-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>] and <italic toggle="yes">B. oleracea</italic> L. var. <italic toggle="yes">italica</italic> (broccoli) seeds, which were purified to 186 mg of sulforaphane from 850 mg of the ethyl acetate seed meal extract by solid-phase extraction, preparative high-performance liquid chromatography, and high-speed countercurrent chromatography (HSCCC) before being characterized by MS and H- or C-NMR [<xref rid="B100-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">100</xref>,<xref rid="B101-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">101</xref>].</p><p>A phytochemical investigation conducted on <italic toggle="yes">B. napus</italic> seeds led to the isolation by NMR spectroscopy of two new nitrogenous compounds, (2S)-2-sinapoyl-4-pentenenitrile and brassicalkaloid A, together with four known alkaloids, coixspirolactam C, 1H-indole-3-acetonitrile, 3-indolealdehyde, and indole-3-acetonitrile-2S β-D-glucopyranoside [<xref rid="B102-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>].</p><p>The phytochemical screening of <italic toggle="yes">B. oleracea</italic> L. var. <italic toggle="yes">acephala</italic> seeds demonstrated the presence of 13 carotenoids, among which all-elutein was the main component [<xref rid="B35-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>].</p><p>Furthermore, an investigation conducted on broccoli seeds (<italic toggle="yes">B. oleracea</italic> L. var. <italic toggle="yes">italica</italic> cv. <italic toggle="yes">Legacy</italic>) revealed a large number of amino acids with a predominance of glutamic acid (72.83 mg/g fresh weight (FW)), asparagine (51.81 mg/g FW), serine + histidine (34.16 mg/g
