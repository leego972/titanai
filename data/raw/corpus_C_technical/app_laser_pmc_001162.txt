# PMC: optical coherence tomography clinical application — Overview

The goal was to study variations of the distribution parameters among groups. A significant statistical difference, (<inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math33" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mml:mrow><mml:mi>p</mml:mi><mml:mtext> value</mml:mtext><mml:mo>&lt;</mml:mo><mml:mn>0.05</mml:mn></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula>, Kruskal–Wallis test) was observed for all three parameters. For an increase in age, the scale (<inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math34" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mml:mrow><mml:mi>a</mml:mi></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula>) and shape (<inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math35" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mml:mrow><mml:mi>p</mml:mi></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula>) parameters decreased, while the parameter relation <inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math36" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mml:mrow><mml:mi>d</mml:mi><mml:mo stretchy="false">/</mml:mo><mml:mi>p</mml:mi></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula> increased, resulting in a narrow distribution. Significant correlation (<inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math37" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mml:mrow><mml:mi>p</mml:mi><mml:mtext> value</mml:mtext><mml:mo>&lt;</mml:mo><mml:mn>0.001</mml:mn></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula>) was found between both the scale parameter (<inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math38" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mml:mrow><mml:mi>a</mml:mi></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula>) and the ratio of the shape parameters (<inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math39" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mml:mrow><mml:mi>d</mml:mi><mml:mo stretchy="false">/</mml:mo><mml:mi>p</mml:mi></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula>) with IOP, where the scale parameter decreases when the IOP increases. A possible physical explanation for this phenomenon is the influence of IOP on the decrease of interlamellar gaps (absence of collagen), as studied by Wu et al.,<xref rid="r27" ref-type="bibr"><sup>27</sup></xref> resulting in a more compacted tissue. This shifts the speckle distribution toward lower values, similarly to the age effect.</p><p>Moreover, Danielewska et al.<xref rid="r28" ref-type="bibr"><sup>28</sup></xref> ascertain the influence of IOP in untreated and crosslinked rabbit eyes. The GG distribution achieved the best goodness of fit against Rayleigh, Weibull, Nakagami, and Gamma distributions, considering the mean squared error between the fitted PDF and the kernel density estimator (KDE). Both the scale and ratio parameters presented significant statistical differences with respect to increasing IOP in anterior and central corneal stroma. In addition, the GG distribution shape parameter (<inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math40" display="inline" overflow="scroll"><mml:mrow><mml:mi>p</mml:mi></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula>) was also statistically significant when comparing untreated and crosslinked for anterior, central, and posterior corneal stroma.</p><p>Iskander et al.,<xref rid="r26" ref-type="bibr"><sup>26</sup></xref> used the GG to model information from the microstructure of the cornea to differentiate glaucoma suspects, glaucoma patients, and healthy controls.
