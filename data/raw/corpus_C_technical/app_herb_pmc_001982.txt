Although preliminary in vitro and in silico studies are promising [<xref rid="B10-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>], we emphasize the need for in vivo validation, pharmacokinetic characterization, and mechanistic analyses to substantiate these bioactivities. In particular, sesquiterpenes with lipophilic properties and receptor-binding potential represent attractive candidates for development into topical or transdermal formulations, aligning with the traditional modes of administration.</p><p>The foundational study by Brophy et al. [<xref rid="B9-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>] provided one of the first comprehensive GC-MS analyses of essential oils distilled from <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> leaves collected in Fiji and Thailand. Hydrodistillation of the leaf material yielded approximately 24 sesquiterpenes, among which β-elemene (<bold>60</bold>), α-bergamotene (<bold>52</bold>), germacrene D (<bold>55</bold>), and (E)-γ-bisabolene (<bold>51</bold>) were predominant. Other constituents included α-humulene (<bold>64</bold>), β-caryophyllene (<bold>57</bold>), and aromadendrene (<bold>63</bold>), as well as trace amounts of monoterpenes (e.g., limonene) (<bold>43</bold>) and low-molecular-weight alcohols such as 1-hexanol (<bold>45</bold>) and (Z)-hex-3-en-1-ol (<bold>46</bold>) (<xref rid="molecules-30-03460-t004" ref-type="table">Table 4</xref>) [<xref rid="B9-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>].</p><p>Importantly, Brophy et al. [<xref rid="B9-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>] observed considerable qualitative and quantitative variation in oil composition depending on geographical origin. For example, oxygenated sesquiterpenes accounted for ~20% in the Bangkok I sample, were nearly absent in the Bangkok II sample, and reached intermediate levels (~7%) in the Fiji sample. These differences may be attributed to environmental and genetic factors, as well as to chemical transformations such as isomerization or oxidation induced by light or heat during distillation. The detection of sesquiterpene isomers, including β-bourbonene (<bold>68</bold>) and δ-cadinene (<bold>65</bold>), may reflect such degradation processes. In light of these concerns, we support the future application of orthogonal techniques such as NMR or GC×GC to confirm compound identity and eliminate possible artifacts.</p><p>Whereas Brophy et al. [<xref rid="B9-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>] focused on hydrodistilled essential oils, Rarison et al. [<xref rid="B10-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>] extended the analysis to include solvent-extracted fractions, thereby broadening the understanding of the chemical variability and potential pharmacological significance of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> terpenoids. The combination of hydrodistillation and solvent extraction techniques allows for a more complete characterization of both volatile and semi-volatile constituents with differing biological potential.</p><p>From the authors’ perspective, the sesquiterpene-rich essential oil profile of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> demonstrates high pharmacological potential, especially for compounds such as β-caryophyllene, caryophyllene oxide, and germacrene D. Due to their lipophilicity and documented bioactivities, these metabolites warrant further investigation as candidates for topical or transdermal therapies. Standardization efforts should also account for chemical variability linked to environmental and genetic factors. To ensure compound authenticity and uncover novel activities, advanced analytical methods such as NMR or GC×GC are recommended.</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot5-molecules-30-03460"><title>5.5. Pharmacologically Relevant Rare Terpenoids and Norisoprenoids Identified in Polyscias fruticosa</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Polyscias fruticosa</italic> is recognized for its complex phytochemical profile, which includes numerous structurally unique compounds with potential pharmacological applications. In addition to the commonly reported sesquiterpenes and monoterpenes, advanced gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC-MS) analyses have revealed the presence of several rare and structurally distinctive terpenoids and norisoprenoids in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>.</p><p>Although many of these constituents occur in low abundance, their structural uniqueness and bioactivities—also documented in other species—suggest potential relevance in pharmacological or ecological contexts.</p><p>However, the possibility that some volatile constituents may result from analytical artifacts or thermal degradation during GC-MS analysis should be taken into account.
