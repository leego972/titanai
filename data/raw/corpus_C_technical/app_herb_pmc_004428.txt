</p><p>The interest in <italic toggle="yes">S. chinensis</italic> (known as <italic toggle="yes">limonnik = лимонник</italic> in Russian) arose from ethnopharmacological investigations by. Komarov (1895) and Arsenyev (1903–1907) in far eastern Siberia and northern Manchuria. The berries and seeds were determined to have been used by Nanai hunters (natives of far eastern Siberia and Chinese Manchuria, also known as Goldis or Samagir) as a tonic to reduce thirst, hunger, and exhaustion and to improve night‐time vision.<xref rid="med21743-bib-0069" ref-type="ref">
<sup>69</sup>
</xref>
</p><p>The first studies on the stimulating and tonic effects on <italic toggle="yes">S. chinensis</italic> were published in World War II‐era military journals.<xref rid="med21743-bib-0064" ref-type="ref">
<sup>64</sup>

<sup>65</sup>

</xref> During the 1960s and 1970s, other Soviet scientists extended the research of adaptogens to “rejuvenating and invigorating” medicinal plants traditionally used in China, Korea, Japan, Siberia, and the far east of the USSR for a variety of pathological conditions including diseases and their symptoms such as hypodynamia, asthenia, shortness of breath, palpitation, insomnia, hemorrhage, impotence, and diabetes.<xref rid="med21743-bib-0070" ref-type="ref">
<sup>70</sup>

<sup>72</sup>
</xref>
</p><p>The authors screened many plants assuming that “adaptogens must be safe and normalize body functions irrespective of the nature of stressors” and in 1967, some were incorporated into official medical practice in the USSR as central nervous system (CNS)‐stimulating medicinal products and as tonics to fight fatigue and general weakness during convalescence for infectious diseases, chemotherapy and psychiatric disorders, after surgery, poisoning, heart attacks, ischemia, chemotherapy, and psychiatric disorders (Table <xref rid="med21743-tbl-0003" ref-type="table">3</xref>). <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> extract (Rhodiolae roseae rhizomatum et radicum extractum liquidum) is an example of an adaptogenic medicinal product used since 1975 in officinal medicine in the USSR/Russia.
