Many phenolic compounds have been isolated from the <italic toggle="yes">R. nervosus</italic> ethyl acetate fraction, including kaempferol, quercetin, gallic acid, hyperoside, quercetin 3-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-(6″-acetyl)-galactoside, hesperidin, quercetin, <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-hydroxy benzoic acid, catechol, and pyrogallol [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR63">63</xref>]. Additional file <xref rid="MOESM1" ref-type="media">1</xref>: Figure S2 shows the structures of the major compounds isolated from <italic toggle="yes">R. nervosus</italic>.</p><p id="Par31"><italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus spina-christi</italic> is commonly called “Jujube” and is locally known as “Sidr” or “Nabuk.” It is a multiuse tree belonging to the family Rhamnaceae. Sidr is a native plant that grows in tropical and subtropical regions, and it is widely distributed throughout the Mediterranean regions, Africa, Asia, and tropical America. In Saudi Arabia, Sidr is wildly distributed throughout the southern and southwestern area and has been used as an ornamental plant and for shade. It is a tall tree that can reach 20 m in height. Its leaves are glabrous on the upper surface, finely pubescent on the lower surface, and ellipsoid or ovate lanceolate in shape with an obtuse or acute apex [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR64">64</xref>].</p><p id="Par32"><italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus spina-christi</italic> is greatly respected by Muslims since it is mentioned in the Sunnah and the Holy Quraan twice [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR65">65</xref>]. From ancient times, in Chinese traditional medicine, suan zao ren (<italic toggle="yes">Z. spinosa</italic>) has been used to increase blood flow to the heart and liver, and it is used to control irritability, insomnia, and palpitations [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR66">66</xref>]. In Saudi folk medicine, the leaves of <italic toggle="yes">Z. spina-christi</italic> (jujube) are used to heal wounds, treat some skin diseases and sores, cure ringworm, antipyretic, gonorrhea, sex diseases, some inflammatory conditions, and ulcers. Furthermore, it has been reported that <italic toggle="yes">Z. spina-christi</italic> leaves are used in folk medicine as antidiabetic remedy [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR67">67</xref>]. In the Bedouin, the decoction of the stem bark and fresh fruits is used as a body rinse, to cure fresh wounds, and is also used for the management of dysentery, bronchitis, coughs, and tuberculosis [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR68">68</xref>].</p><p id="Par33">Previous phytochemical investigation revealed it contains biologically active secondary metabolites including, tannins, flavonoids, terpernoids, saponin glycosides, and alkaloids [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR68">68</xref>].</p><p id="Par34">Pharmacological studies have demonstrated that the total alcoholic extract of the current plant leaves and stem bark has showed significant antioxidant, antimicrobial, and antidiarrheal activities [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR66">66</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR69">69</xref>]. Additionally, the aqueous extract from the root bark has an antinociceptive activity [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR70">70</xref>] and central nervous system depressant effect in mice [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR71">71</xref>]. The butanol extract of <italic toggle="yes">Z. spina-christi</italic> leaves has shown potent hypoglycemic/antidiabetic activities [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR72">72</xref>]. The aqueous and ethanolic extracts of stem bark of <italic toggle="yes">Z. spina-christi</italic> have been studied, and an anticholinergic effect was observed [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR73">73</xref>], which proved the traditional use of the plant as antispasmodic. A cytotoxic effect was observed for the aerial part of <italic toggle="yes">Z. spina-christi</italic> against cervical, breast, and colon cancers [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR66">66</xref>].</p><p id="Par35">A phytochemical study of <italic toggle="yes">Z. spina-christi</italic> indicated the presence of betulic and ceanothic acids [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR74">74</xref>]. Cyclic peptide alkaloids, franaganine, mauritine C, and sativanine A have been isolated and fully characterized from the stem bark of <italic toggle="yes">Z. spina-christi</italic> [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR75">75</xref>]. Triterpenoidal saponins were recently isolated from the leaves of the same plant and screened for their antidiabetic activity [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR76">76</xref>]. Additionally, four saponin glucosides identified as christinin A–D have been isolated from the leaves <italic toggle="yes">n</italic>. butanol extract leaves and their structures were fully characterized by using spectroscopic technique [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR77">77</xref>].
