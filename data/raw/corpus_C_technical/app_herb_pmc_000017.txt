# PMC: herbal medicine pharmacology mechanism review — Overview

Meyer [<xref rid="B39-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>] evaluated the acetone and water extracts using a rapid radiometric method against <italic toggle="yes">Mycobacterium tuberculosis</italic> and obtained a MIC value of 0.1 mg/mL against the strains. In 2001, Lall and Meyer [<xref rid="B64-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>] evaluated the antimycobacterial activities of diospyrin <bold>11</bold> isolated from <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> against the drug-sensitive and drug-resistant strains of <italic toggle="yes">Mycobacterium tuberculosis</italic> using the radiometric respiratory BACTEC assay. The compound diospyrin <bold>11</bold> was active against <italic toggle="yes">Mycobacterium tuberculosis</italic> with MIC value of 100 µg/mL for all strains. Weigenand et al. [<xref rid="B52-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>] evaluated antimycobacterial activities of betulin <bold>1</bold>, lupeol <bold>2</bold>, shinanolone <bold>13</bold>, 20(29)-lupene-3β-isoferulate <bold>14</bold> and octahydroeuclein <bold>15</bold> compounds isolated from root bark of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> against a drug-sensitive strain of <italic toggle="yes">Mycobacterium tuberculosis</italic> H37Rv using a rapid radiometric method with TB drugs, streptomycin and ethambutol as controls. The compound shinanolone <bold>13</bold> showed inhibitory activity against a drug sensitive strain of <italic toggle="yes">Mycobacterium tuberculosis</italic> at a concentration of 0.1 mg/mL [<xref rid="B52-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>]. Lall et al. [<xref rid="B17-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>] evaluated antimycobacterial activities of crude extracts, the compounds betulin <bold>1</bold>, lupeol <bold>2</bold>, diospyrin <bold>11</bold> and 7-methyljuglone <bold>12</bold> isolated from the roots of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> against <italic toggle="yes">Mycobacterium tuberculosis</italic> both in axenic medium and in a macrophage cell line. Crude extract, diospyrin <bold>11</bold> and 7-methyljuglone <bold>12</bold> isolated from the plant exhibited MIC values of 8.0, 8.0 and 0.5 µg/mL, respectively, against <italic toggle="yes">Mycobacterium tuberculosis</italic> while betulin <bold>1</bold> and lupeol <bold>2</bold> were inactive. The MIC value of 7-methyljuglone <bold>12</bold> against a panel of clinical pan-sensitive and drug-resistant strains of <italic toggle="yes">Mycobacterium tuberculosis</italic> ranged from 0.32 to 1.25 µg/mL [<xref rid="B17-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>]. Similarly, Van der Kooy et al. [<xref rid="B52-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>] evaluated antimycobacterial activities of isodiospyrin <bold>3</bold>, mamegakinone <bold>4</bold>, neodiospyrin <bold>10</bold>, diospyrin <bold>11</bold>, 7-methyljuglone <bold>12</bold> and shinanolone <bold>13</bold> isolated from root extracts of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> against <italic toggle="yes">Mycobacterium tuberculosis</italic> using the radiometric respiratory BACTEC assay. The MIC values of isodiospyrin <bold>3</bold> (10.0 µg/mL), neodiospyrin <bold>10</bold> (10.0 µg/mL), diospyrin <bold>11</bold> (8.0 µg/mL) and 7-methyljuglone <bold>12</bold> (0.5 µg/mL) compared well to those of the known antimycobacterial drugs ethambutol, isoniazid and rifampicin [<xref rid="B55-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>]. McGaw et al. [<xref rid="B65-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>] evaluated antimycobacterial activities of root extracts of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> and the compound diospyrin <bold>11</bold> isolated from the species against <italic toggle="yes">Mycobacterium bovis, Mycobacterium fortuitum</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Mycobacterium smegmatis</italic> using a twofold serial dilution assay with anti-TB drug isoniazid as positive control. The root extracts showed some activities with MIC values ranging from 5.7 µg/mL to 16.3 µg/mL against the tested organisms, and the compound diospyrin <bold>11</bold> also showed some activities with MIC values ranging from 15.6 µg/mL to 62.5 µg/mL against the tested organisms [<xref rid="B65-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>]. McGaw et al. [<xref rid="B66-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>] evaluated antimycobacterial activities of acetone, chloroform and methanol root extracts of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> and the compounds lupeol <bold>2</bold>, neodiospyrin <bold>10</bold>, diospyrin <bold>11</bold>, 7-methyljuglone <bold>12</bold> and shinanolone <bold>13</bold> isolated from the species against <italic toggle="yes">Mycobacterium bovis, Mycobacterium fortuitum</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Mycobacterium smegmatis</italic> using a twofold serial dilution assay with anti-TB drug isoniazid as positive control. The plant extracts demonstrated activity with MIC values ranging from 5.7 to 664.1 µg/mL against <italic toggle="yes">Mycobacterium bovis, Mycobacterium fortuitum</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Mycobacterium smegmatis</italic> [<xref rid="B66-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>].
