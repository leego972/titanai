Weak</p></table-wrap-foot></table-wrap></p><p id="Par29">Figure <xref rid="Fig1" ref-type="fig">1</xref>a of the FTIR spectrum reveals that ChlE displays a strong peak at 3456.33 cm<sup>−1</sup> owing to primary N–H group and primary O–H group vibrations. Strong peaks were observed at about 2917 and 2849 cm<sup>−1</sup>, and these peaks corresponded to the –CH<sub>2</sub> antisymmetric stretch of methyl groups and C–H aldehydic groups, respectively. Also, a strong peak was found at 1721 cm<sup>−1</sup> that matched the C=O stretching and a weak peak at 1638 cm<sup>−1</sup> that matched the C=C stretching in aromatic molecules of phenyl and amide compounds, with a weak peak at 1559 cm<sup>−1</sup> owing to aromatic C=C Stretching. Weak to moderate peaks were observed at 1461, 1379 and 1278 cm<sup>−1</sup>, and these peaks corresponded to S=O stretching/O–H bending, CH<sub>3</sub> bend, and NO<sub>3</sub> stretch, respectively. In addition, a moderate peak was found at 1100 cm<sup>−1</sup> that matched the C–O–C group. It is worth noting that all of these peaks were reproduced in the ChlE-NS spectrum as well. This indicates that all key functional groups of ChlE were present in the final formulation of ChlE-NS, confirming the lack of extract–polymer incompatibility and ensuring that there were no chemical interactions between ChlE and any of the excipients utilized. These results are consistent with prior reports [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR53">53</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR55">55</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="Sec22"><title>Preparation and characterization of nanosuspension</title><p id="Par30">The main problem with using chloroform extract to make a good dosage form is that it does not dissolve well in water. The best way to solve this problem and make these extracts more bioavailable is to use nanosuspension formulations. The goal of this study was to increase the bioavailability and solubility of ChlE by using a nano-precipitation technique to make the nanosuspension.</p><p id="Par31">The antisolvent precipitation technique is one of the bottom-up approaches that may be used for the manufacture of nanosuspensions.
