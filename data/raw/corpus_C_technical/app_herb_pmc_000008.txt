This study reviews information on ethnomedicinal uses, botany, phytochemical constituents, pharmacology and toxicity of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic>. Results of this study are based on literature search from several sources including electronic databases, books, book chapters, websites, theses and conference proceedings. This study showed that <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> is used as traditional medicine in 57.1% of the countries where it is indigenous. <italic toggle="yes">Euclea natalensis</italic> has a high degree of consensus on abdominal pains, antidote for snake bites, diabetes, diarrhoea, malaria, roundworms, stomach problems, toothache, venereal diseases and wounds. Several ethnopharmacological studies have shown that crude extracts and chemical compounds from <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> demonstrated many biological activities both in vitro and in vivo, which included antibacterial, antidiabetic, antifungal, antimycobacterial, antiviral, antioxidant, antiplasmodial, larvicidal, antischistosomal, molluscicidal, dentin permeability and hepatoprotective activities. Future studies should focus on the mechanism of biological activities of both crude extracts and chemical compounds from the species, as well as structure–function relationships of bioactive
