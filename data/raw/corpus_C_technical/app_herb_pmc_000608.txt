Anti-Tumor Effect</title><p>Anti-tumor effects are also regarded as primary pharmacological properties of <italic toggle="yes">X. strumarium</italic>, and have been extensively investigated in lung cancer, breast cancer, cervical cancer, colon cancer, liver cancer, meningioma, and leukemia.</p><p>Tao et al. studied the inhibitory effect of xanthatin (1–40 μM), an active agent in <italic toggle="yes">X. strumarium</italic>, against lung cancer cells (Cell lines of A549, H1975, H1299, H1650 and HCC827) and its potential mechanisms [<xref rid="B66-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>,<xref rid="B67-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>]. It found that xanthatin could downregulate the STAT3, GSK3β and β-catenin, moreover, xanthatin could also trigger Chk1-mediated DNA damage and destabilize Cdc25C via lysosomal degradation [<xref rid="B66-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>,<xref rid="B67-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>,<xref rid="B68-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>]. In 1995, Ahn et al. isolated three cytotoxic compounds from the leaves of <italic toggle="yes">X. strumarium</italic>, among them, xanthatin and 8-epi-xanthatin possessed obvious anti-tumor activity on A549 cells with IC<sub>50</sub> (half maximal inhibitory concentration) values of 1.3 and 1.1 μg/mL, respectively [<xref rid="B17-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>]. Later, in 2002, it was reported that 1,8-epi-xanthatin epoxide has notable anti-tumor effect against A549 cells with IC<sub>50</sub> value of 3.0 μM [<xref rid="B69-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>]. Furthermore, Wang et al. and Ferrer et al. reported that 8-epi-xanthatin-1α,5α-epoxide, 1β-hydroxyl-5α-chloro-8-epi-xanthatin and EEXA can inhibit the proliferation of A549 cells (IC<sub>50</sub> = 9.5 μM, 20.7 μM and 52.2 μg/mL, respectively) [<xref rid="B25-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>,<xref rid="B70-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>]. </p><p>In 2007, by using CellTiter 96 assay in vitro, Ramı’rez-Erosa et al. found that xanthatin and xanthinosin, two sesquiterpene lactones isolated from the burs of <italic toggle="yes">X. strumarium</italic>, obviously restrain the proliferation of breast cancer MDA-MB-231 cells with the IC<sub>50</sub> values of 13.9 and 4.8 μg/mL, respectively [<xref rid="B71-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>]. Furthermore, Takeda et al. studied the mechanism of xanthatin against breast cancer MDA-MB-231 cells in 2011, and the results indicated that xanthatin (5–25 μM) inhibits cell growth via inducing caspase independent cell death which were irrelevant with FTase inhibition [<xref rid="B72-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>]. In addition, xanthatin (2.5–10 μM) can also up-regulate GADD45 γ tumor suppressor gene, and induce the prolonged expression of c-Fos via N-acetyl-<sc>l</sc>-cysteine-sensitive mechanism [<xref rid="B73-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>,<xref rid="B74-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>]. In 2016, the anti-tumor activity of EEXA on MFC7 cells was reported as well, with an IC<sub>50</sub> value of 70.6 μg/mL [<xref rid="B70-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>]. </p><p>In 2015, Vaishnav et al. demonstrated that WEX with a concentration of 12.5–50 μg/mL were able to induce death in HeLa cervical cancer cells by altering the antioxidant levels [<xref rid="B75-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>]. Recently, Liu et al. revealed that xanthatin (5–20 µM) targeted the selenocysteine (Sec) residue of thioredoxin reductase (TrxR) and inhibited the enzyme activity irreversibly [<xref rid="B76-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>]. Meanwhile, the inhibition of TrxR by xanthatin promoted oxidative stress-mediated apoptosis of HeLa cells.</p><p>In 1995, Ahn et al. reported that xanthatin and 8-epi-xanthatin were remarkably cytotoxic to colon cancer HCT-15 cells with ED<sub>50</sub> (median effective dose) values of 1.1 and 0.1 μg/mL, respectively [<xref rid="B17-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>]. Later, in 2007, Ramı’rez-Erosa et al. (2007) found that xanthatin (IC<sub>50</sub> = 6.15 μg/mL) and xanthinosin (IC<sub>50</sub> = 6.15 μg/mL) possessed the function of inhibiting WiDr cells growth [<xref rid="B71-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>]. Furthermore, eremophil-1(10),11(13)-dien-12,8β-olide,8-epi-xanthatin-1β,5β-epoxide and tomentosin were isolated from the aerial parts of <italic toggle="yes">X. strumarium</italic>, and their anti-tumor activities on BGC-823 cells and KE-97 cells were aslo determined. The related results showed that the IC<sub>50</sub> values of three compounds on BGC-823 cells are 13.22, 2.43, and 4.54 µM, respectively.
