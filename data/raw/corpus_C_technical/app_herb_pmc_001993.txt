According to the World Health Organization, more than 300 million people are affected worldwide, with projections exceeding 400 million by 2025 [<xref rid="B153-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">153</xref>].</p><p>The pathophysiology of asthma is complex, involving genetic predispositions, environmental exposures, and dysregulated immune responses. A hallmark feature is eosinophilic inflammation leading to hyperresponsiveness, obstruction, and structural airway remodeling. T-helper type 2 (Th2) lymphocytes play a central role by secreting IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13, which promote IgE production, eosinophil recruitment, and mediator release from mast cells [<xref rid="B154-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">154</xref>].</p><p>Chronic inflammation also induces basement membrane thickening, smooth muscle hypertrophy, and mucus hypersecretion.</p><p>Pharmacologic management of asthma typically involves inhaled corticosteroids (ICS), β2-adrenergic agonists, leukotriene receptor antagonists, anticholinergic agents, and targeted biologic therapies such as monoclonal antibodies against IL-5, IL-4Rα, and IgE. However, many patients continue to experience exacerbations, and long-term drug use may cause adverse effects that reduce adherence and efficacy [<xref rid="B153-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">153</xref>].</p><p>These limitations have fueled interest in complementary plant-based therapies with anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and immunomodulatory potential. Among these is <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, a member of the Araliaceae family that is traditionally used in the treatment of inflammatory conditions, respiratory ailments, and as a general tonic [<xref rid="B4-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B155-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">155</xref>]. Recent studies suggest that <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> extracts can modulate airway inflammation, oxidative stress, bronchial tone, and mucus secretion, supporting its potential as a phytotherapeutic agent in asthma [<xref rid="B10-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>,<xref rid="B56-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>].</p><sec id="sec6dot1dot1-molecules-30-03460"><title>6.1.1. Antiasthmatic Potential of <italic toggle="yes">Polyscias fruticosa</italic>: Pharmacological and Mechanistic Insights</title><p>The ovalbumin (OVA)-induced asthma model is a widely accepted preclinical approach for evaluating immunopathological features and therapeutic responses in allergic asthma. It reproduces hallmark features of human asthma, such as eosinophilic airway inflammation, bronchial hyperresponsiveness, increased IgE levels, and a Th2-skewed cytokine profile.</p><p>In a study by Koffuor et al. [<xref rid="B56-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>], guinea pigs were sensitized and challenged with OVA and subsequently treated orally with an ethanolic leaf extract of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> (PFE) at doses of 100, 250, and 500 mg·kg<sup>−1</sup> for 21 consecutive days. Therapeutic efficacy was assessed via BALF eosinophil counts, serum CRP, ESR, and histopathological analysis of lung tissue.</p><p>Administration of PFE at (500 mg·kg<sup>−1</sup>) resulted in a 56.3% reduction in eosinophils, a 43% decrease in CRP, and notable histological improvements. The treated animals showed reduced peribronchial infiltration and preserved bronchial epithelium. These effects were comparable to those of prednisolone (2 mg·kg<sup>−1</sup>), underscoring the extract’s anti-inflammatory potency.</p><p>Although cytokine levels were not directly measured, the results suggest Th2 suppression-possibly via reduced IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13 activity. Furthermore, hematological assessments and histological analysis of major organs revealed no signs of systemic toxicity, indicating the safety of prolonged PFE administration.</p><p>These findings confirm the antiasthmatic potential of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, particularly in reducing airway inflammation and systemic inflammatory markers, without inducing toxicity, supporting its development as a phytotherapeutic for allergic asthma [<xref rid="B56-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>]. These results confirm the antiasthmatic activity of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> in vivo; however, the lack of cytokine data limits mechanistic understanding. Further studies should clarify immunomodulatory pathways and identify active constituents.</p></sec><sec id="sec6dot1dot2-molecules-30-03460"><title>6.1.2.
