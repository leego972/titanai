Further studies are required, and several prospective studies are currently underway to look at using OCTA to investigate the microvascular changes in the optic nerve and macula serially in the affected and fellow eye.</p></sec><sec id="ceo14544-sec-0006"><label>2.4</label><title>Arteritic Acute Ischaemic Optic Neuropathy (<styled-content style="fixed-case" toggle="no">AAION</styled-content>)</title><p>AAION is a large vessel ischaemia from inflammation and thrombotic occlusion of the short posterior ciliary artery. The most common cause of AAION is giant cell arteritis [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0051" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>]. AAION is different from NAION due to the involvement of both the retinal and choroidal circulation, and tends to result in more severe ischaemic changes; hence, the waxy disc pallor clinically corresponding to infarction at the optic nerve head [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0051" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>]. In acute AAION, OCTA demonstrates diffuse dilation of superficial peripapillary capillaries with sectoral capillary dropout in the superficial and deep peripapillary capillaries, closely corresponding to visual field loss, with variable choriocapillaris nonperfusion (Figure <xref rid="ceo14544-fig-0004" ref-type="fig">4</xref>) [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0041" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0052" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0053" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>]. These dilated superficial peripapillary capillaries become attenuated in the chronic phase on follow‐up, correlating to RNFL thinning on ancillary OCT optic disc cross‐section analysis, and thus may represent a feature that can be used for visual prognostication in AAION [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0052" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0053" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>].</p><fig position="float" fig-type="FIGURE" id="ceo14544-fig-0004" orientation="portrait"><label>FIGURE 4</label><caption><p>(A) Colour fundus photo of a patient with acute right AAION from GCA with pallid superior disc swelling.
