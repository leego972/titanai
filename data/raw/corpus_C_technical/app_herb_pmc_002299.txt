However, with the growing interest in sustainability and the development of new analytical and extraction technologies, many of these species are being re-evaluated with promising results, having been shown to be a significant source of metabolites with beneficial properties [<xref rid="B9-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>,<xref rid="B10-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>]. This approach not only allows for the diversification of available natural resources, but also contributes to the conservation of biodiversity and the development or economic improvement of local communities that can benefit from the cultivation and marketing of species widely distributed in their region [<xref rid="B11-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>].</p><p>In this context, <italic toggle="yes">Camellia japonica</italic> (<italic toggle="yes">CJ</italic>), an evergreen shrub native to Asia, is a species belonging to the <italic toggle="yes">Camellia</italic> genus that is widely distributed in different regions of the world, highlighting the region of Galicia (NW Spain), which constitutes one of the main international benchmarks regarding the cultivation and production of this species, presenting almost 8000 different varieties, a continuous production with stable quality of 1.5 million plants per year (40 million euros), which supposes about 9% of the total Galician agricultural production [<xref rid="B12-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>,<xref rid="B13-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>]. Despite its widespread distribution, currently, nearly all production of this species is dedicated to ornamental purposes. However, other species of the <italic toggle="yes">Camellia</italic> genus, widely distributed worldwide, such as <italic toggle="yes">Camellia sinensis</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Camellia oleifera</italic>, have aroused great interest due to their industrial applications, particularly in the production of tea and oil, respectively [<xref rid="B14-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>]. Furthermore, extracts of <italic toggle="yes">C. sinensis</italic> have been used in traditional medicine in multiple countries in Asia and East Africa for the treatment of various pathologies, including chronic diseases (i.e., type 2 diabetes), hypertension, hypercholesterolemia, and cataract disease, suggesting significant pharmacological potential [<xref rid="B15-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>]. From this perspective, it is important to highlight the evidence suggesting a relatively homogeneous chemical composition within the <italic toggle="yes">Camellia</italic> genus. This opens the door to further investigation into the composition of <italic toggle="yes">CJ</italic>, a species whose chemical characterization remains scarce in the current scientific literature, even though certain parts, such as its seeds and oils, have been traditionally used in China [<xref rid="B16-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>,<xref rid="B17-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>].</p><p>In this regard, the most recent advances in <italic toggle="yes">CJ</italic> research have revealed that this plant represents a matrix rich in a wide variety of bioactive molecules, including significant amounts of various phenolic compounds, carotenoids, triterpenes, flavonoids, and fatty acids, among others [<xref rid="B18-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>,<xref rid="B19-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>]. The presence of these compounds confers extracts obtained from <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> (<italic toggle="yes">C. japonica</italic> leaves) with diverse bioactivities, particularly antioxidant, antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and antidiabetic properties [<xref rid="B20-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>,<xref rid="B21-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>]. This diversity of bioactivities suggests that extracts obtained from <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> could have diverse therapeutic applications in the treatment or prevention of various diseases, as well as in the development of new food additives, with special emphasis on functional foods and preservatives. However, edaphoclimatic conditions (such as soil type, climate, and growing conditions), as well as the time of harvest, are crucial factors that can influence the quantity and quality of the compounds present, and therefore the bioactivity of the extracts, which makes homogenization processes necessary to try to avoid these differences as much as possible [<xref rid="B22-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>,<xref rid="B23-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>]. This knowledge highlights the importance of optimizing harvesting and extraction processes, as well as studying the interactions between the plant’s compounds to maximize its therapeutic potential.
