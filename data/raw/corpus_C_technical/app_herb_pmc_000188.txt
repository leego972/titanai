SPME, Solid Phase Microextraction; PMP: Polymethylpentene membrane <xref rid="b0045" ref-type="bibr">[9]</xref> (License Number: 4958661463662).</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="gr4.jpg"/></fig></p></sec><sec id="s0060"><label>4.6</label><title>Ultrasound effect on essential oils and other volatiles</title><p id="p0130">The effects of ultrasound on plant essential oils have been recently studied [<xref rid="b0425" ref-type="bibr">87</xref>]. Essential oils are unstable to heat, thus, the use of US at low intensities could prevent the degradation of these thermally sensitive compounds. Furthermore, excellent diffusion rates were afforded by the use of US, which can facilitate successful extraction with minimum solvent use and subsequently lower solvent residues in the extracted compounds. The composition of the extracted essential oil could be modified and more selective extraction of desired compounds could be obtained [<xref rid="b0140" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>]. For example, by using 20 kHz and 90 °C for 70 min of US, red pepper seeds' alcohol and aldehyde contents were decreased, while other volatile components, such as pyrazine derivatives, esters, and olefin components were increased [<xref rid="b0430" ref-type="bibr">88</xref>]. These changes in the composition of the extract could eventually have subsequent effects on the efficacy of the extracts and potentially on their sensory attributes.</p><p id="p0135">For Lu'an Guapian tea herb (<italic toggle="yes">Camellia Sinensis</italic>), Meng and Zhengquan <xref rid="b0045" ref-type="bibr">[9]</xref> established a method for extracting volatiles by US nebulization extraction (UNE) combined with solid-phase microextraction method (SPME) (<xref rid="f0020" ref-type="fig">Fig. 4</xref>). In that study, the authors reported that the extracted total volatiles percent was increased significantly (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.05) to 42.23% by using 1.7 MHz and 50 °C for 20 min of US. A significant increase in some aldehydic volatiles such as pentanal, heptanal, octanal, and dodecanal was also found in the obtained extracts compared to controls <xref rid="b0045" ref-type="bibr">[9]</xref>.</p><p id="p0140">It is worth mentioning that the extraction conditions play an important role in determining the composition of the volatiles. For example, while no significant differences were observed in alcoholic volatiles such as hexanol at 20 °C, increasing the extraction temperature to 50 °C resulted in a significant increase in alcoholic volatiles, such as α-terpineol. Therefore, US can increase the volatile extraction efficiency of medium-to-low volatiles compounds in herbs <xref rid="b0045" ref-type="bibr">[9]</xref>. The combined use of US and heat treatment using a thermosonication method improved the extracted essential oil efficiency and extracted yield from clove (53 kHz and 52 °C) [<xref rid="b0180" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>]. Also, time savings have been reported for the US processing where Gavahian, Farhoosh, Javidnia, Shahidi, Golmakani and Farahnaky [<xref rid="b0435" ref-type="bibr">89</xref>] reported of reducing essential oil extraction time by 17% for peppermint herb.</p><sec id="s0065"><label>4.6.1</label><title>Ultrasound effect on terpenes</title><p id="p0145">It has been shown that thermosonication can significantly increase the percentage of some extracted terpenes from herbal sources <xref rid="b0045" ref-type="bibr">[9]</xref>. For example, a significant increase in β-ocimene, and D-limonene contents of tea (<italic toggle="yes">Camellia Sinensis</italic>) were reported as a result of using 50 °C of US (1.7 MHz, 20 min) compared to 20 °C. On the other hand, alcoholic terpenes such as α-terpineol were not affected by US treatments <xref rid="b0045" ref-type="bibr">[9]</xref>. As mentioned earlier, CPT extraction from the <italic toggle="yes">Camptotheca acuminata</italic> herb by US transformed the disulfide bonds to thiol bonds of these molecules, which resulted in the disulfide motif to be in the center of the macromolecular framework (e.g. Hydrocarbon) and enabled efficient mechanochemical scission and the release of this monoterpene molecule [<xref rid="b0335" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>].
