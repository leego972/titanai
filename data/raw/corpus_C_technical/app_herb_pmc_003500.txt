# PMC: ethnobotany medicinal plants Africa Asia — Overview

This multi-method approach allowed for a comprehensive documentation of indigenous knowledge related to MPs, including their local identification, preparation techniques, therapeutic applications, routes of administration, dosage practices, availability, perceived threats, and local conservation measures. Data collection followed established ethnobotanical research procedures as outlined by [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR25">25</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR26">26</xref>].</p><p id="Par33">Semi-structured interviews and group discussions were conducted using pretested checklists developed in accordance with the objectives of the study. Information recorded included informant socio-demographic characteristics, common human ailments treated traditionally, local plant names, plant parts used, preparation and application methods, dosage forms, routes of administration, additional non-medicinal uses, plant sources (wild or cultivated), perceived abundance, and conservation status.</p><p id="Par34">Interviews were carried out primarily with individuals who were native to, or long-term residents of, Sheikh District to ensure the depth and reliability of ethnobotanical knowledge. All interviews were conducted in the Somali language with the support of local translators, and responses were later recorded in English for analysis. The semi-structured format provided flexibility, allowing informants to share detailed explanations, personal experiences, and local variations in medicinal plant use (Fig. 2).</p><p id="Par35">Guided field walks were undertaken with key informants and local guides to locate and identify MPs in their natural habitats. These walks enabled direct observation of plant morphology, growth forms, ecological settings, and distribution patterns across the different landscapes of the district.</p><p id="Par36">During field excursions, additional information was documented, including harvesting practices, preparation techniques, alternative uses, and indigenous conservation strategies. Plant collection sites were georeferenced using GPS coordinates, and field notes were recorded on vegetation types, soil characteristics, habitat conditions, and topographic features. To respect confidentiality and cultural sensitivity, most field walks were conducted individually with informants, and any sensitive knowledge shared was kept anonymous.</p><p id="Par37">Eight focus group discussions were held with key informants to capture shared community knowledge and collective practices regarding medicinal plant use. These discussions encouraged open dialogue among participants and provided insights into preparation methods, dosage determination, additive ingredients, possible side effects, and culturally specific healing perceptions.</p><p id="Par38">Participants also reflected on knowledge transmission systems, local management practices, and emerging threats to medicinal plant resources, thereby contributing to a broader understanding of both cultural and ecological dimensions of ethnomedicine in Sheikh District.</p><p id="Par39">A market survey was conducted at Wadhato market in Sheikh District to assess the extent of medicinal plant commercialization. Semi-structured interviews with medicinal plant vendors documented the types of plant materials sold, their quantities, prices, sources, and trade dynamics. This survey helped evaluate the economic value of MPs, the role of markets in traditional healthcare, and potential harvesting pressures linked to commercial demand.</p><p id="Par40">Voucher specimens of all recorded MPs were systematically collected during guided field walks, following prior informed consent from landowners and in coordination with district administrative and agricultural offices. Specimen collection adhered to ethical research standards and conservation guidelines.</p><p id="Par41">Plants were photographed in situ, labeled, numbered, pressed, air-dried or deep-frozen as appropriate, and accompanied by detailed field notes describing habitat, growth form, and reported traditional uses.</p><p id="Par42">Preliminary identification was performed in the field using vernacular names and morphological characteristics. Final taxonomic identification was conducted at the Biology Department Laboratory, College of Natural and Computational Sciences, Jigjiga University, between 14 and 16 June 2023.
