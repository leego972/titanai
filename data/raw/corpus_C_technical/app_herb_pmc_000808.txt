Garcinia oblongifolia </title><p><italic toggle="yes">Garcinia oblongifolia</italic> (Lingnan Garcinia) belongs to the family of Clusiaceae and has a wide range of pharmaceutical activities. The important metabolites of the <italic toggle="yes">G. oblongifolia</italic> species; polyisoprenylated benzophenones and xanthones have anticancer, antioxidant, antifungal, apoptotic, and anti-pathogenic properties [<xref rid="B45-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>,<xref rid="B46-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>]. In vitro study showed that the bark of <italic toggle="yes">G. oblongifolia</italic> contains important secondary metabolites including oblongifolin A–G, oblongixanthones A–C along with other important compounds. These metabolites showed maximum apoptotic activities in HeLa-C3 cell lines and cytotoxic properties in the cervical cancer cells [<xref rid="B47-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>,<xref rid="B48-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">48</xref>]. Li et al. [<xref rid="B49-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>] isolated about 40 different compounds from fruit, leaves, branches, and other parts of <italic toggle="yes">G. oblongifolia</italic>. They noted very high cytotoxic activities of these metabolites in the tested MCF-7 breast cancer cell line. However, they found the higher anti-cytotoxic activity of branch as compared to other plant parts. A small vacuole body formation was found at a low bark concentration of 0.250 g/mL. The vacuole size was increased at high concentrations of 500 g/mL and 1000 g/mL. The leaf part showed mild vacuole formation at a high concentration of 500 g/mL. Similarly, Feng, Huang, Gao, Xu and Luo [<xref rid="B48-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">48</xref>] tested the pro-apoptotic activities of twenty different isolated compounds from <italic toggle="yes">G. oblongifolia</italic> in cervical cancer HeLa cells. Among all tested compounds the oblongifolins F and G, xanthone, nigrolineaxanthone T, and garcicowin B gave high pro-apoptotic properties at 10 μM concentration.</p></sec><sec id="sec3dot6-biomolecules-10-00047"><title>3.6. Garcinia indica</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Garcinia indica</italic>, commonly known as kokum, is also an important medicinal plant that belongs to the Garcinia genus. The garcinol of <italic toggle="yes">G. indica</italic> shows positive activities in the experimental HT-29 and HCT-116 colon cancer cells along with normal immortalized intestinal cells (IEC-6 and INT-407). In another study, the fruit extract of <italic toggle="yes">G. indica</italic> was used for the isolation of garcinol. The garcinol at IC<sub>50</sub> values (3.2–21.4 μM) for 72 h treatment shows strong inhibitory properties in all intestinal cells. The anticancer properties were higher in the cancer cells as compared to normal immortalized cells [<xref rid="B50-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>]. </p><p>Similarly, Liao et al. [<xref rid="B51-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>] also observed a high tumor-inhibiting activity of <italic toggle="yes">G. indica</italic> in a human colorectal cancer cell line (HT-29). The garcinol at 10 μM concentration retarded the cell invasion activities several folds. The fruit extracts of <italic toggle="yes">G. indica</italic> has been shown very effective in the activation of caspase-3/CPP32 and the breakdown poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) protein to inhibit leukemia in humans in the HL-60 cells [<xref rid="B52-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>]. These results indicated that garcinol (IC<sub>50</sub> = 9.42 μM) shows strong growth inhibitory effects against human leukemia HL-60 cells.</p></sec><sec id="sec3dot7-biomolecules-10-00047"><title>3.7.
