These antibacterial compounds, therefore, sufficiently support the antibacterial activities exerted by the crude extracts of <italic toggle="yes">A. saligna</italic>.</p><p>Elansary et al. [<xref rid="B13-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>] were the first to determine which phytochemicals contributed to the antibacterial activities observed in their methanolic leaf extract (<xref rid="molecules-28-04396-t003" ref-type="table">Table 3</xref> and <xref rid="molecules-28-04396-t004" ref-type="table">Table 4</xref>). The extract was shown to inhibit the growth of <italic toggle="yes">B. cereus, P. aeruginosa, Listeria monocytogenes</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">E. coli</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Micrococcus flavus</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">S. aureus</italic> with MIC values of 0.35, 0.37, 0.47, 0.31, 0.41 and 0.30 mg/mL, respectively, compared to the positive control streptomycin (MIC = 0.07–0.15 mg/mL). The extract was also bactericidal against all the tested bacteria, with minimum bactericidal concentration (MBC) values of 0.73, 0.79, 0.99, 0.72, 0.85, and 0.73 mg/mL, respectively (<xref rid="molecules-28-04396-t004" ref-type="table">Table 4</xref>). HPLC-based analysis of their leaf extract revealed a high content of phytochemicals (reported in % <italic toggle="yes">w</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">w</italic> of crude extract, as shown in <xref rid="app1-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="app">Supplementary Table S1</xref>), which revealed rutin <bold>6</bold> as the main flavonoid in the extract. Pure rutin <bold>6</bold>, hyperoside <bold>9</bold>, <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-coumaric acid <bold>37</bold>, quercetin <bold>3</bold>, and miquelianin <bold>7</bold> were screened against the tested bacteria alongside the crude methanolic extract, as shown in <xref rid="molecules-28-04396-t004" ref-type="table">Table 4</xref>. Rutin <bold>6</bold> and <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-coumaric acid <bold>37</bold> showed substantial broad-spectrum antibacterial activities against tested bacteria, which were comparable to the positive control streptomycin. In contrast, hyperoside <bold>9</bold>, quercetin <bold>3</bold>, and miquelianin <bold>7</bold> showed moderate to low antibacterial activities. The antibacterial activities of compounds, especially rutin <bold>6</bold> and <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-coumaric acid <bold>37</bold>, supported the activity observed in the crude extract. In addition, the flavonoids such as rutin <bold>6</bold>, quercetin <bold>3</bold>, miquelianin <bold>7</bold>, and isoquercetin <bold>8</bold> have been reported to have antibacterial properties [<xref rid="B72-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>]. As previously discussed, gallic acid <bold>25</bold> and <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-coumaric acid <bold>37</bold> are known antibacterial compounds.</p><p>El-Toumy et al. [<xref rid="B16-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>] reported the antibacterial activities of their EtOAc leaf extract against Gram-positive bacteria, including <italic toggle="yes">S. aureus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Streptococcus pyogens</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">B. cereus</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">B. subtilius</italic> with MIC values of 0.41, 0.46, 0.41, and 0.14 μg/mL, respectively. Interestingly, the ethyl acetate leaf extract was much more active against <italic toggle="yes">S. aureus</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">B. cereus</italic> than the methanolic leaf extract reported by Elansary et al. (<xref rid="molecules-28-04396-t004" ref-type="table">Table 4</xref>) [<xref rid="B13-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>]. </p><p>The leaf extract from the El-Toumy group was subjected to pure compound isolation by column chromatography to yield sixteen polyphenolic compounds. The compounds were structurally elucidated by NMR spectral data analysis to mainly provide flavonoids [<xref rid="B16-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>]. Among these were (reported in % <italic toggle="yes">w</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">w</italic> of crude extract) quercetin <bold>3</bold> (0.025), quercitrin <bold>4</bold> (0.030), quercetin-3-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-arabinoside <bold>5</bold> (0.030), myricetin <bold>10</bold> (0.025), myricitrin <bold>11</bold> (0.034), myricetin-3-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-arabinoside <bold>12</bold> (0.028), myricetin-3-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-glucopyranoside <bold>13</bold> (0.029), (+)-catechin <bold>14</bold> (0.025), 7-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-Galloyl-cathecin <bold>15</bold> (0.029), apigenin <bold>16</bold> (0.002), apigetrin <bold>17</bold> (0.002), luteolin <bold>19</bold> (0.029), cynaroside <bold>20</bold> (0.027), gallic acid <bold>25</bold> (0.027), and methyl gallate <bold>26</bold> (0.030), as shown in <xref rid="molecules-28-04396-t003" ref-type="table">Table 3</xref> and <xref rid="app1-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="app">Supplementary Table S1</xref>.
