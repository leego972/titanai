Indeed, the GC–MS analysis of <italic toggle="yes">Brassica napus</italic> petroleum ether extract revealed the presence of different chemical compounds such as acetic acid butyl ester, docosane 11-decyl, 2-pentanone 4-hydroxy-4 methyl, bicyclooct-2-ene-4α, 6α-carbolactone, benzene 1,4-dimethyl, 2-methyl-5-phenyl-5-pentanonenitrile, 5,8,11,14-eicosatetraenoic acid methyl ester, 3,4-dihydrothienyl-5-carboxtthiol, 1-butene 4-isothiocyanato, 7,7-dimethyl-tetracycloheptane, 9-methyl-l0-tetradecen-1-ol acetate, and 9-hydroxy-1-methyl-1,2,3,4-tetrahydro-8H-pyrido (1,2α) pyrazin-8-one [<xref rid="B104-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">104</xref>].</p><p>Moreover, the essential oil composition of <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds is represented in <xref rid="molecules-27-06008-t004" ref-type="table">Table 4</xref>. The essential oil of <italic toggle="yes">B. napus</italic> seeds was dominated by 2-phenyl ethyl isothiocyanate (39.2%) followed by bicyclohept-6-en-1-yl-tert-butyl ether (13.7%), 2-(allylthio) 1-nitrobutane (12.8%), 4-bromo-3-phenylbut-2-enoate (9.8%), 1,3,6,10-cyclotetradecatetraene 3,7,11-trimethyl-l4-(1-methylethyl) (5.2%), 1-butene 4-isothiocyanato (4.8%), cyclohexane 1,12-[1-(2, 2-dimethylbutyl)-1,3-propanediyl] bis (1.2%), and 4 trifluroacetoxytetradecane (1.1%) [<xref rid="B104-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">104</xref>]. In research conducted to evaluate the <italic toggle="yes">Sinapis alba</italic> seeds, 14 components in the essential oil from mustard seeds representing 97.94% of the total amount were identified. The predominant component of the essential oil was allyl isothiocyanate (AITC), representing 71.06% [<xref rid="B105-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">105</xref>]. The GC–MS analysis revealed that the main components in the essential oil of <italic toggle="yes">B. nigra</italic> seeds were di-(9-octadecenoyl)-glycerol (42.16%), 9,12-octadecadienoyl chloride, (Z,Z)-(41.40%), and hexadecanoic acid, 1-(hydroxymethyl)-1,2-ethanediyl ester (13.20%), while the main components in the essential oil of <italic toggle="yes">B. hirta</italic> were cyclopropanenonanoic acid (48.70%), 2-[(2-butylcyclopropyl) methyl]-methyl ester, and hexadecanoic acid, 1-(hydroxymethyl)-1,2-ethanediyl ester (42.08%) [<xref rid="B78-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">78</xref>].</p><p>Overall, several phytochemistry studies have investigated the phytochemical compositional diversity in <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> spp. seeds, and a wide range of bioactive compounds (polyphenols, glucosinolates, carotenoids, alkaloids, amino acids, fatty acids, and organic acids) have been identified and quantified using different analytical approaches such as HPLC-UV, HPLC-DAD, LC–MS, LC–MS/ESI, GC, and GC–MS. Polyphenols such as hydroxycinnamic acids and flavonoids were the most common. Flavonoids (flavonols) were mostly found as quercetin, kaempferol, and isorhamnetin derivatives. Glucosinolates were mostly found in aliphatic forms. The hydrolytic glucosinolates products with the highest content were allyl isothiocyanate, 2-phethyl isothiocyanate, 3-butenyl isothiocyanate, and 3-(methylthio) propyl isothiocyanate. Sulforaphane, a powerful anticancer isothiocyanate, was also identified and quantified in <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds (<xref rid="molecules-27-06008-f001" ref-type="fig">Figure 1</xref>).</p><p>However, available data of phytochemical screening of diverse <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> spp. seeds are still limited, and different understudied phytochemicals require more detailed characterization.</p><p>The biosynthesis, variability, and abundance of <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seed phytochemicals depend on genotype, environmental factors, germination, and degree of maturity [<xref rid="B106-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>]. Indeed, germination improved the phenolic compounds content by 49% and 44% in <italic toggle="yes">Sinapis alba</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Brassica nigra</italic> seeds, respectively, as well as in broccoli seeds [<xref rid="B36-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>,<xref rid="B107-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">107</xref>].
