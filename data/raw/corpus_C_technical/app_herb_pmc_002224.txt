In contrast, traditional methods such as percolation, infusion, and decoction were less commonly applied, likely reflecting their stronger association with ethnomedicinal practice than experimental anticancer research. Differences in extraction techniques were associated with variations in phytochemical profiles and anticancer potency, as evidenced by the wide range of IC<sub>50</sub> values reported across studies. Most extracts were rich in secondary metabolites, including polyphenols, flavonoids, alkaloids, and terpenoids, which were consistently linked to anticancer activity (<xref rid="molecules-31-01202-f001" ref-type="fig">Figure 1</xref>). These compounds exert their effects through multiple mechanisms, including apoptosis induction, inhibition of cell proliferation, cell-cycle arrest, and oxidative stress-mediated cytotoxicity. Commonly investigated models included breast, colon, lung, cervical, prostate, and liver cancer cell lines, although the magnitude of cytotoxic effects varied depending on extraction conditions, phytochemical composition, and experimental design. <xref rid="molecules-31-01202-t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref>, <xref rid="molecules-31-01202-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref> and <xref rid="molecules-31-01202-t003" ref-type="table">Table 3</xref> summarize the studies reviewed under selected extraction methods, detailing the plant species investigated, extraction procedures, identified phytochemicals, cancer cell models, reported IC<sub>50</sub> values, and the associated anticancer mechanisms. Influence of Pre-Extraction Processing and Extraction Optimization</title><p>The recovery of BACs from plant matrices is shaped not only by solvent composition, extraction time, and temperature, but also by upstream processing steps that define the chemical and structural state of the material before extraction begins. Variability in pre-extraction treatment and extraction design is therefore a major, yet often underreported, source of inconsistency across phytochemical studies. Since plant matrices contain metabolites that differ in polarity, stability, localization, and association with structural components, extraction outcomes depend as much on matrix preparation and extraction completeness as on solvent choice itself [<xref rid="B1-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B2-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="B3-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B4-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B9-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>,<xref rid="B11-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>,<xref rid="B19-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>,<xref rid="B31-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>,<xref rid="B32-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>,<xref rid="B33-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>,<xref rid="B34-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>,<xref rid="B35-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>,<xref rid="B36-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>,<xref rid="B37-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>,<xref rid="B38-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>,<xref rid="B39-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>,<xref rid="B40-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>,<xref rid="B41-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>,<xref rid="B42-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>,<xref rid="B43-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>,<xref rid="B44-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>]. Among upstream variables, drying and size reduction are especially important as they affect both compound stability and solvent accessibility. Across the reviewed studies, shade drying at ambient temperature was used more frequently than oven drying under controlled thermal conditions [<xref rid="B1-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B2-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="B3-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B4-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B9-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>,<xref rid="B11-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>,<xref rid="B19-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>,<xref rid="B31-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>,<xref rid="B32-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>,<xref rid="B33-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>,<xref rid="B34-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>,<xref rid="B35-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>,<xref rid="B36-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>,<xref rid="B37-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>,<xref rid="B38-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>,<xref rid="B39-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>,<xref rid="B40-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>,<xref rid="B41-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>,<xref rid="B42-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>,<xref rid="B43-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>,<xref rid="B44-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>], whereas oven drying was applied in a smaller group [<xref rid="B18-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>,<xref rid="B45-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>,<xref rid="B46-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>,<xref rid="B47-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>,<xref rid="B48-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">48</xref>].
