Question: <p>I have a function that expects real numbers (either integers or floats) as its input, and I'm trying to validate this input before doing mathematical operations on it.</p>

<p>My first instinct is to cast inputs as floats from within a try-except block.</p>

<pre><code>try:
   myinput = float(input)
except:
   raise ValueError("input is not a well-formed number")
</code></pre>

<p>I could also call <code>isinstance(mydata, (float, int, long) )</code> but the list of "all these could be numbers" seems a bit inelegant to me.</p>

<p>What's the most pythonic way of going about it? Is there another option I overlooked?</p>

Answer: <p>In Python 2.6 and 3.0, a type hierarchy of numeric abstract data types has been <a href="http://www.python.org/dev/peps/pep-3141/" rel="nofollow">added</a>, so you could perform your check as:</p>

<pre><code>&gt;&gt;&gt; import numbers
&gt;&gt;&gt; isValid = isinstance(myinput , numbers.Real)
</code></pre>

<p>numbers.Real will match integral or float type, but not non-numeric types, or complex numbers (use numbers.Complex for that).  It'll also match rational numbers , but presumably you'd want to include those as well. ie:</p>

<pre><code>&gt;&gt;&gt; [isinstance(x, numbers.Real) for x in [4, 4.5, "some string", 3+2j]]
[True, True, False, False]
</code></pre>

<p>Unfortunately, this is all in Python >=2.6, so won't be useful if you're developing for 2.5 or earlier.</p>
