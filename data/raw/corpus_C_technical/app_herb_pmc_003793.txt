Mice were acclimated for 1 week in the animal house at the University of Mons (agreement: LA1500550T) and were sustained in a 12-hour light–dark cycle. The animals were housed in groups (6 mice per cage) and kept in a room with controlled temperature and humidity, with food and water available ad libitum. At the end of the experiment, mice were anesthetized by isoflurane inhalation and euthanized by decapitation for blood collection. Blood samples were collected two hours after the final behavioral test.</p><p id="Par16">To avoid stress-related bias due to fights, which are often observed in cohorts of male mice, only female mice were used for this study.</p></sec><sec id="Sec4"><title>Botanical compound and measurement of salidroside, rosin, rosarin and rosavin (UHPLC)</title><p id="Par17">The <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea L.</italic> roots powder used in this study (batch number RR_2405_001) was produced by Botalys (Ghislenghien, Belgium). A carefully selected cultivar of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> L. is hydroponically cultivated in an innovative vertical farming technology, with a strict control of growing conditions (BOTALYS is FSSC22000 certified), allowing a reproducible chemical composition of the roots from one batch to another, and containing high content of active compounds. At the end of the culture the fresh roots are harvested and dried. The dried <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea L.</italic> roots is then grounded to obtain powder. The powder is sieved on 300 µm. The final product is analysed for salidroside and Rosavins content before release.</p><p id="Par18">The identity of the <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> L. roots was verified by DNA sequencing. The sequence of the DNA fragment obtained from Botalys <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> L. root powder presents 99.66% of similarity with <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea L.</italic> sequence recorded in the Genbank genetic database. Moreover, the active compounds of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea L.</italic>, i.e. salidroside and rosavins are detected in the Botalys <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea L.</italic>.</p><p id="Par19">Compounds were extracted and analyzed as follows: the dry powder of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea L.</italic> (0.1 g) was extracted in 10 mL of 70% methanol during 45 minutes in an ultrasonic bath. After extraction, the solution was filtered through a 0.22-μm Millipore filter and used for UHPLC analysis. The content of salidroside, rosin, rosarin and rosavin was quantified using a SHIMADZU UHPLC LC-20 ADXR modular system, which included an SPD-40 V detector, SIL-40C autosampler, LC-40B XR pump, CTO-40C column oven, and a Shim-pack GIST C18 2 μm column (150 ×2.1 mm). A 2 μL sample injection volume was used, with analysis conducted at 40 °C and detection at 192 nm. Separation was achieved using a linear gradient elution with solvent A (0.1% phosphoric acid solution) and solvent B (acetonitrile). The gradient was as follows: <italic toggle="yes">t</italic> = 0 min, 98% A; <italic toggle="yes">t</italic> = 13.33 min, 88% A; <italic toggle="yes">t</italic> = 22 min, 30% A; and <italic toggle="yes">t</italic> = 22.66 min, 98% A. The flow rate was set to 0.45 mL/min.
