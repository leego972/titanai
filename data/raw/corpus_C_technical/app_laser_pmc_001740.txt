The absorption of light by cytochrome c oxidase promotes the photodissociation of NO bound to the enzyme, thereby increasing the availability of free NO [<xref rid="B4-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B194-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">194</xref>]. NO is a multifunctional signalling molecule playing a pivotal role in vascular homeostasis, neurotransmission, and immune regulation [<xref rid="B78-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">78</xref>]. By enhancing NO release, LLLT supports vasodilation, improves microcirculation, and increases tissue oxygenation—mechanisms that are particularly relevant in the context of diabetic complications, such as peripheral ischaemia and diabetic foot ulcers [<xref rid="B144-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">144</xref>,<xref rid="B195-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">195</xref>,<xref rid="B196-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">196</xref>]. These vascular effects are particularly important for diabetic patients, in whom endothelial dysfunction and impaired perfusion can lead to delayed wound healing and an increased risk of tissue necrosis [<xref rid="B112-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>].</p><p>Experimental evidence highlights the importance of nitric oxide in these processes. Karu et al. (2005) [<xref rid="B194-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">194</xref>] demonstrated that specific wavelengths (619–820 nm) can enhance the attachment of HeLa cells through cytochrome c oxidase-mediated signalling. However, pre-irradiation with NO donors modified this effect, suggesting that NO binding to the enzyme plays a regulatory role [<xref rid="B194-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">194</xref>]. Similarly, Atum et al. (2022) showed that preconditioning with PBMT reduced doxorubicin-induced cardiotoxicity in human cardiomyocytes by lowering oxidative stress levels and restoring endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) activity [<xref rid="B197-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">197</xref>]. Restoration of eNOS function is critical because diabetes is known to suppress eNOS expression and activity, resulting in reduced NO bioavailability and vascular complications [<xref rid="B170-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">170</xref>].</p><p><xref rid="ijms-27-02078-f005" ref-type="fig">Figure 5</xref> presents the effects of photobiomodulation on nitric oxide signalling and microcirculation in diabetes.</p><p>Taken together, these findings suggest that modulation of the NO pathway is crucial for the beneficial vascular and cytoprotective effects of PBMT. In addition to promoting vasodilation, NO modulates leukocyte adhesion and platelet aggregation, potentially reducing chronic inflammation and thrombosis risk in diabetic patients [<xref rid="B80-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>]. Therefore, targeting NO signalling through LLLT is a promising strategy for improving microvascular health and mitigating the progression of diabetes-related tissue damage.</p></sec><sec id="sec8dot3-ijms-27-02078"><title>8.3. Anti-Inflammatory and Neuroprotective Mechanisms</title><p>Chronic inflammation is a hallmark of diabetes, contributing to insulin resistance, beta-cell dysfunction, and vascular complications [<xref rid="B198-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">198</xref>]. Laser therapy has emerged as a promising strategy for modulating inflammatory responses. LLLT can induce ROS generation in physiological conditions; however, in stressed or diseased cells, it reduces ROS accumulation while enhancing antioxidant defences [<xref rid="B80-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>]. Notably, PBMT modulates the activity of nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB): it activates NF-κB in resting cells but inhibits its pro-inflammatory activity in activated cells, thereby reducing cytokine release [<xref rid="B199-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">199</xref>,<xref rid="B200-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">200</xref>]. This dual regulatory effect enables PBMT to maintain immune vigilance while preventing chronic inflammation, which is particularly beneficial in the context of tissue damage associated with diabetes.</p><p>In addition to modulating NF-κB, PBMT influences the polarisation of macrophages by reducing the expression of markers of the M1 phenotype, thereby shifting immune responses towards tissue repair. It also reduces the production of prostaglandins and reactive nitrogen species, as demonstrated in multiple preclinical models [<xref rid="B3-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B84-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">84</xref>]. This shift from a pro-inflammatory to a reparative macrophage phenotype improves wound healing and reduces fibrosis in diabetic tissues [<xref rid="B170-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">170</xref>]. This immunomodulatory effect is particularly important for diabetic patients, as chronic low-grade inflammation can lead to insulin resistance and vascular complications.
