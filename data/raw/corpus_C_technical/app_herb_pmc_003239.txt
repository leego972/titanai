# PMC: ethnobotany medicinal plants Africa Asia — Overview

sec-type="results" id="sec3-molecules-27-00492"><title>3. Results and Discussion</title><sec id="sec3dot1-molecules-27-00492"><title>3.1. Ethnobotanical Aspects of Artemisia herba-alba</title><p>SARS-CoV-2 infection and its comorbidities are particularly dangerous for older people who have comorbidities such as diabetes, hypertension, lung, liver, kidney diseases, and cancer [<xref rid="B6-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>]. In addition, malaria, tuberculosis, and acquired immune deficiency syndrome (AIDS) can add potential complications to COVID-19 patients in many sub-Saharan African countries [<xref rid="B8-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>,<xref rid="B9-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>,<xref rid="B39-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>]. For example, the similar generic symptoms between COVID-19 and malaria also make immediate diagnosis difficult, causing both diseases to progress. From that viewpoint, the choice of <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia herba-alba</italic> for our present study makes good sense because the plant is quite prolific in northern African countries and contains the current antimalarial drug artemisinin in amounts that are possibly greater than the compound’s current plant source <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia annua</italic> (note that the drug has not been possible to be chemically synthesized) [<xref rid="B22-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>,<xref rid="B40-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>]. Although one or two papers on the presence of artemisinin in <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia herba-alba</italic> do not make the plant an automatic choice (artemisinin is also present in <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia annua</italic>), the present selection of this plant was based on consideration of other factors as well, such as ethnic uses and pharmacological activities, which were deemed to be suitable for further studies towards alleviating COVID-19 symptoms and comorbidities.</p><p>This roadmap is not a comprehensive list of ethnic uses of <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia herba-alba</italic>. Only ethnic reports based on their relevancy to COVID-19 symptoms and comorbidities are presented here. The English name for <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia herba-alba</italic> is wormwood or desert wormwood; in Arabic, it is known as Shih, and in French, it is known as Armoise blanche. In the folk medicine of northeastern Morocco (otherwise known as Oriental Morocco), as well as in Errachidia province in southeastern Morocco, the plant is used to treat arterial hypertension and diabetes; in Tunisian folk medicine, the plant is used for the treatment of bronchitis, diabetes, diarrhea, hypertension, and neuralgia [<xref rid="B41-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>,<xref rid="B42-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>]. The Bedouins of the Negev desert in Israel use the plant to alleviate stomach disorders [<xref rid="B43-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>]. People living in the Middle Atlas Mountains in Morocco use a decoction of fresh leaves and stems of this plant to make herbal tea, which is taken orally 2–3 times a day for a week for the treatment of gastrointestinal disorders [<xref rid="B44-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>]. The traditional practitioners, healers, and herbalists of M’sila city in the central part of northern Algeria use the whole plant in decoction, tisane, powder, and in the masticated form for treatment of diabetes, helminthic infections, colonopathy, nausea, abdominal pain, breast pain, and as antispasmodic and carminative [<xref rid="B45-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>].</p><p>The leaves and flowers of the plant are considered hypoglycemic by people in the Souk Ahras District in Algeria [<xref rid="B46-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>]. The leaves of the plant are considered anti-diabetic, antispasmodic, pectoral, and anti-arthritic in the folkloric medicine of Jordan [<xref rid="B47-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>]. The decoction of the leaves and flowers of the plant are used for gargling to treat bad breath, gingivitis, toothache, gingival bleeding, mouth ulcers, herpes labialis, and stomatitis by herbalists in the central Middle Atlas (Morocco) [<xref rid="B48-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">48</xref>]. In two Saharan regions of southwest Algeria (Adrar and Bechar), traditional healers administer a decoction of the aerial parts of the plant orally for stomachache and ulcers [<xref rid="B49-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>]. In Pakistan, a decoction of the plant is used to treat fever and nervous problems [<xref rid="B50-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>]. Taken together, <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia herba-alba</italic>, if the traditionalists are proved correct in clinical trials, by itself can possibly assuage many of the symptoms of COVID-19 as well as serious comorbidities such as malaria, bronchitis, diabetes, diarrhea, hypertension, neuralgia, abdominal pain, and high fever.
