The greater usage of plants as herbal remedies in mountain areas is also due to the prevalence of traditional health systems such as folklore medicinal systems and the Amchi system. The traditional healing systems are both culturally acceptable [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B97">97</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B98">98</xref>] and induce locales to use plant resource most. Because of the high level of cohesion and strong cultural links with nature in mountain areas [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B11">11</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B99">99</xref>], local people have a close affinity with locally available resources and easily internalize the resources for their livelihood and needs because these are also readily available.</p><p>Home herbal treatment is not merely a medical system but a part of culture [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B100">100</xref>], and the extensive use of locally available plant resources was observed in mountain areas. Most of the people of mountain areas, particularly elderly people, farmers, and mid-wives, are well acquainted to medicinal herbs and their usage [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B101">101</xref>], and plants are prescribed to the patients whenever required [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B102">102</xref>]. The availability of traditional healers is also higher in rural and mountain areas. The use of herbal remedies in mountain areas to alleviate suffering is perhaps as old as the origin of man itself [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B103">103</xref>], and there is a general trend of increase in the use of medicinal plants [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B104">104</xref>].</p><p>Besides health care and subsistence uses, medicinal plants have a high potential as alternative income-generating sources of the rural hilly populace of the region [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B105">105</xref>]. Therefore, sustainable use of the resources and conservation of indigenous knowledge of medicinal plants may compliment the income of local people. Medicinal plants are mainly harvested in the wild, traded, and eventually consumed as processed form in lowland cities [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B106">106</xref>]. Up to 50% of the Nepal's rural household's income is derived from commercial collection of medicinal and aromatic plants [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B107">107</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B108">108</xref>]. The collection of medicinal plants in a sustainable manner is an integrated process with potential for development and conservation [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B109">109</xref>].</p><p>The indigenous knowledge and practice of usage of medicinal plants in rural areas of Nepal is passed down through oral tradition and personal experiences [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B110">110</xref>]. The knowledge clearly decreased with age. People of ages between 40–60 possessed greater knowledge on identification and uses of medicinal and aromatic plants in Nepal Himalaya [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B111">111</xref>], which was consistent to the observation of Philips and Gentry [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B112">112</xref>]. The young generations tend to leave ancestral practices behind, refocusing their interests on treatments offered by western medicine [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B113">113</xref>]. Due to changing lifestyles, perception as well as social transformation, the plant resource and indigenous knowledge of utilization are being severely degraded [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B114">114</xref>]. This impact is inevitable to the Nepal Himalayas and plant resources are in great peril. Indigenous knowledge systems are not only of value for the cultures from which they evolve, but also for scientists and planners striving to improve the living conditions in rural societies [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B115">115</xref>]. Lambert et al [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B116">116</xref>] pointed out that preserving and enhancing the indigenous plant knowledge and use was equivalent to 'rescuing a global heritage'. Indigenous plant based traditional knowledge and use has become a recognized tool in search for new sources of drugs and pharmaceuticals [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B117">117</xref>]. In countries like Kenya [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B118">118</xref>] and Nepal [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B119">119</xref>], where the indigenous knowledge is predominantly used for utilization of plant resource for various purposes, and high priority needs to be given to the documentation of indigenous knowledge and use of plant resources to help their conservation.</p></sec></sec><sec><title>Conclusion</title><p>The richness of medicinal plants was decreasing with increasing altitude, but the percentage of plants used as medicine steadily increased with increasing altitude.
