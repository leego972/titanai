Similar sustainability concerns have been noted globally, where balancing commercial demand with conservation remains a critical challenge [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR51">51</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR53">53</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR113">113</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR114">114</xref>].</p><p id="Par209">Hence, the marketability of MPs in the Karamara forest patches is shaped by an interplay of therapeutic efficacy, accessibility, growth form, and sustainable harvest potential. These dynamics mirror global trends in the medicinal plant trade, where high-demand species often occupy both cultural and economic importance. Promoting sustainable harvesting, community-based cultivation, and value-added processing could therefore enhance both the conservation of medicinal plant biodiversity and the livelihoods of local communities.</p></sec><sec id="Sec37"><title>Informant consensus factor (ICF) and fidelity level (FL) of medicinal plants</title><p id="Par210">The Informant Consensus Factor (ICF) is widely used in ethnobotanical research to explore the degree of agreement among informants regarding the use of medicinal plants for particular ailment categories. However, several authors, including Leonti, have highlighted important conceptual and methodological limitations of this index, emphasizing that ICF does not assess pharmacological efficacy and may be strongly influenced by sample size, informant selection, and patterns of cultural knowledge transmission rather than therapeutic effectiveness [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR103">103</xref>]. Consequently, its application requires careful and cautious interpretation. In this study, ICF is therefore used as a heuristic tool to examine patterns of shared cultural knowledge and local agreement rather than as a direct indicator of therapeutic effectiveness.</p><p id="Par211">In the Karamara forest patches, ICF values ranged from 0.60 to 0.85. Dermatological disorders showed the highest consensus, indicating strong agreement among informants on remedies for wounds, ringworm, and other skin conditions. Similar patterns have been reported in other Ethiopian studies, where visible and frequently encountered ailments tend to generate higher levels of shared knowledge and agreement [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR53">53</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR56">56</xref>]. High ICF values in such categories may also reflect the accessibility of the plant species involved and the collective learning processes within the community rather than superior medicinal efficacy.</p><p id="Par212">Gastrointestinal disorders ranked second in terms of consensus, reflecting widespread recognition of herbal treatments for stomachache, diarrhea, constipation, and intestinal parasites. Comparable trends have been reported in ethnobotanical studies from Kenya, Nigeria, and Sudan, where digestive ailments are commonly treated using a relatively small number of well-known plant species [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR92">92</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR107">107</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR110">110</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR111">111</xref>]. Respiratory illnesses and malaria-related conditions exhibited moderate consensus, suggesting a broader range of species and treatment options, a pattern similarly observed in India, Pakistan, and China [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR93">93</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR94">94</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR101">101</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR108">108</xref>].</p><p id="Par213">The lowest ICF values were recorded for urogenital and reproductive disorders, indicating limited agreement among informants. This may reflect fewer reported cases, the involvement of specialized or sensitive knowledge, or restricted modes of cultural transmission. Comparable observations have been made in Africa and Asia, where ethnomedicinal knowledge related to reproductive health is often confined to a smaller group of practitioners [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR70">70</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR106">106</xref>].</p><p id="Par214">By definition, high ICF values (approaching 1) indicate that relatively few species are repeatedly cited by many informants for a given ailment category, whereas low values reflect greater variability or individualized knowledge [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR17">17</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR56">56</xref>].
