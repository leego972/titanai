files.</p></notes></front><body><sec sec-type="intro" id="sec001"><title>Introduction</title><p>The interaction between humans and plants has been long described as one of the factors influencing human civilization, especially in medicinal fields [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref001" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>]. Documentation of the medicinal use of plants through ethnobotanical studies enables the development of contemporary drugs and treatments as well as for plant conservation [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref002" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0235886.ref003" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>]. Many ethnobotanical studies around the world, including in Indonesia, report the use of herbal plants for the healing process, which has been in use for several generations in their respective societies [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref004" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0235886.ref005" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>]. Though the cultural diversity in Indonesia contributes to the extensive this traditional knowledge [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref006" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>], access to this is limited. Traditional knowledge is usually passed on orally and often person-specific [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref007" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>]. Therefore, the knowledge is often owned by tribal leaders, village heads, elders, heads of <italic toggle="yes">kampung</italic> (small village), or traditional healers in the particular community or tribe [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref008" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>].</p><p>Indonesia has around 40,000 different plant species, of which approximately 6,000 are used for traditional healing processes [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref004" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>], especially in certain tribal areas including Bromo Tengger Semeru National Park (BTSNP) [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref009" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. BTSNP is designated as a national park because of its fascinating vegetation (about 600 floral species) and is home to the unique Tengger tribe. Some plants have been cultivated for daily consumption and trading whereas others are naturally found and used for particular purposes such as tribal ceremonies and medicinal uses [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref009" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. People of Tengger are distributed in buffer zone villages around the BTSNP including Ngadisari village [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref010" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>].</p><p>The Tengger people use plants from the BTSNP for traditional ceremonies [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref011" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>], as well as medicinal applications [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref012" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>], industrial materials, food sources, and building materials in some buffer village areas [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref013" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>]. However, there are no reports regarding the ethnobotanical aspect of medicinal plants used in these buffer village areas in the BTSNP by the Tengger people.
