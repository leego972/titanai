Properties of <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic></title><sec id="sec5dot1-molecules-27-04240"><title>5.1. Anti-Drought and Anti-Thermal Characteristics of Ziziphus nummularia</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus</italic> trees and shrubs are able to thrive in extreme habitats, particularly in arid regions (<xref rid="molecules-27-04240-f001" ref-type="fig">Figure 1</xref>) [<xref rid="B47-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>]. In the wake of climate change, drought has become a calamitous abiotic stress, affecting plant growth and hampering agricultural production and yield. The economic impact of this stress is likely to increase further; as a result, there has been growing interest in studying drought stress responses and tolerance mechanisms of natural drought-tolerant plants [<xref rid="B48-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">48</xref>], such as <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic>. The purpose will be to develop crops with increased tolerance and resilience to drought. In a study to explore the physiological basis of drought, <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> plants were subjected to moisture stress and results showed that chlorophyll <italic toggle="yes">a</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">b</italic> content in leaves decreased, and electrolyte leakage, which normally accompanies the plant’s response to stress, was increased [<xref rid="B49-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>]. Furthermore, in another effort to understand the molecular basis of abiotic stress tolerance mechanisms, isolate drought-responsive genes, and eventually develop drought resistant crop varieties, transcriptome profiling and gene expression analysis were carried out on <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> seedlings subjected to drought stress caused by treatment with 30% polyethylene glycol (PEG 6000) [<xref rid="B50-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>,<xref rid="B51-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>]. In line, Padaria et al. [<xref rid="B52-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>] characterized the <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> abscisic acid-stress-ripening gene 1 (<italic toggle="yes">ZnAsr1</italic>), and validated its transcriptional pattern in response to drought stress. This gene was upregulated in response to drought stress, and when recombinantly expressed in <italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic>, it allowed the bacteria to survive better in PEG-containing media, suggesting that the <italic toggle="yes">ZnASR1</italic> may be used to develop drought tolerant transgenic crops. </p><p>Sivalingam et al. [<xref rid="B53-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>] screened the parameters contributing to drought tolerance by comparing three <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> ecotypes from semi-arid, arid and hyper-arid regions. Results showed that the latter was the most drought-tolerant ecotype and was associated with an increase in root length and number, an increase in leaf hair growth, and in elongation of thorns. The leaves were reduced in size and curled, while older leaves fell and shoot growth was sustained. In order to maintain a high relative water content for longer periods of time, membrane permeability was increased, and proline, catalase and sugar content was higher. Transcriptome profiling showed that genes associated with drought response mechanisms were constitutively expressed at higher levels [<xref rid="B53-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>]. </p><p><italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> also show tolerance to heat stress. Here, the chaperone protein ClpB1, which plays an important role in thermotolerance, was investigated [<xref rid="B54-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">54</xref>]. It was found that the transcript levels of the <italic toggle="yes">JClpB1</italic>-C isoform from a heat-tolerant <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> ecotype was highly up-regulated under heat stress conditions and that the overexpression of <italic toggle="yes">ZnJCIpB1</italic>-C in tobacco showed a significant increase in its heat tolerance by exhibiting higher photosynthetic rates, relative water content, chlorophyll content, and membrane stability index, suggesting that <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> could provide an important source for developing heat-stress tolerant germplasms.
