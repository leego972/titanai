Cytotoxic and Anti-Proliferative Effect</title><p>Cytotoxic effects of novel drug entities and traditional medicines are very essential to be investigated before testing their further pharmacological activity. After establishment of positive cytotoxic effects, anti-proliferative effects (rate of cytotoxicity) are also investigated to check and confirm their further anti-cancer effectiveness, using <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> as well as <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> models. Various constituents from <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> have been tested for cytotoxic effects, and some of these also showed positive anti-proliferative effects.</p><p>Cancer, medically known as a malignant neoplasm, is a broad group of diseases involving unregulated cells. In malignant neoplasm (cancer), cells divide and grow uncontrollably, forming malignant tumors, and invading nearby parts of the body. It may also spread to more distant parts of the body through the lymphatic system or bloodstream. Over 200 different known cancers that can affect humans; and there are over sixty different organs in the body where a cancer can develop. A statistical report in 2012 showed that total 338,623 people were diagnosed with cancer in the UK, while 161,823 deaths from cancer ocurred (survival rate was 50%) [<xref rid="B137-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">137</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> has cytotoxicity and antiproliferative effects on various human cancer cell lines, as well as various solid tumors, including lung, breast and cervical cancers. Kuo <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, [<xref rid="B36-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>] isolated and identified nearly 65 compounds from the roots of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> and screened them for the potential cytotoxicity and anti-HIV activities by <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> assays. Among the compounds evaluated, 13β,21-dihydroxyeurycomanol [<xref rid="B60-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>], 6-dehydroxylongilactone [<xref rid="B72-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>], 9-methoxycanthin-6-one [<xref rid="B75-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>], canthin-6-one [<xref rid="B76-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>], eurylene [<xref rid="B53-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>], 9-hydroxycanthin-6-one [<xref rid="B76-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>], longilactone [<xref rid="B75-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>], 9-methoxycanthin-6-one 3<italic toggle="yes">N</italic>-oxide [<xref rid="B76-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>], 14,15β-dihydroxyklaineanone [<xref rid="B75-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>], pasakbumin C [<xref rid="B50-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>], canthin-6-one 9-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-β-glucopyranoside [<xref rid="B76-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>], were screened for <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> cytotoxicity against A-549 and MCF-7 tumor cell lines [<xref rid="B138-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">138</xref>] and no inhibition of HIV replication in H9 lymphocytes except for eurylene and pasakbumin B [<xref rid="B139-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">139</xref>].
