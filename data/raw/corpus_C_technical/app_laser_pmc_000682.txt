Hypoxia induces the stabilization of the HIF-1α subunit, which, in turn, allows formation of the transcriptionally active protein complex. A number of HIF-1–responsive genes have been identified, including those encoding vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), erythropoietin and glucose transporter-1 [<xref rid="b112-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>,<xref rid="b113-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>]. Following PDT, increases in VEGF secretion and angiogenic responses stimulated via HIF-1 pathways have been documented <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> [<xref rid="b114-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>–<xref rid="b117-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">117</xref>]. VEGF induction can contribute to tumor survival and regrowth and, therefore, may represent one of the factors that prevent PDT from achieving its full tumoricidal potential. PDT has been considered for both palliative therapy and as an early treatment option for cancer. Numerous ongoing clinical studies have been designed to optimize PDT conditions. However, no standardized biological markers of cell death and PDT efficacy, other than cell viability itself, have been reported.</p><p>Human cancer is associated with changes in miRNA expression. The pattern of miRNA expression varies dramatically across tumor types, and miRNA profiles reflect the developmental lineage and differentiation state of a tumor [<xref rid="b118-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">118</xref>]. miRNA is also likely to play critical roles in various aspects of hematopoiesis, including the differentiation of hematopoietic stem/progenitor cells, as well as in events that lead to hematological disorders. Nonetheless, very few miRNA expression patterns of specific diseases are available. Moreover, no profiles of miRNA expression after PDT have been reported. Cheng <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> found that inhibition of miR-95, -124, -125, -133, -134, -144, -150, -152, -187, -190, -191, -192, -193, -204, -211, -218, -220, -296 and -299 resulted in a decrease in cell growth, whereas inhibition of miR-21 and miR-24 profoundly increased cell growth in HeLa cells [<xref rid="b119-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">119</xref>]. In addition, they identified miRNAs, whose expression increased levels of apoptosis (miR-7, -148, -204, -210, -216 and -296). Those data suggest that specific miRNAs are involved in the cell-death response. We have shown that a miRNA specific to apoptosis is expressed at increased levels in HeLa cells in response to PDT using talaporfin sodium as a photosensitizer [<xref rid="b120-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">120</xref>]. Our study was the first to characterize miRNA expression levels following PDT. In our experiments, miR-210 and miR-296 expression levels increased significantly 1 h after PDT in cells treated with 50 μg/mL talaporfin sodium, relative to the control group (<italic toggle="yes">i.e</italic>., 0 μg/mL talaporfin sodium), as shown in <xref rid="f2-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="fig">Figure 2</xref>. However, the expression levels of other miRNAs, e.g., miR-7, -148a, -204 and -216, were indistinguishable from those of the control group after PDT.</p><p>miR-210 is the miRNA most consistently stimulated under hypoxic conditions [<xref rid="b121-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">121</xref>]. Because hypoxia and stabilization of intracellular HIF are inherent consequences of PDT [<xref rid="b92-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">92</xref>], Giannakakis <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>. investigated miR-210 expression in the context of its hypoxic effect, and they reported evidence for the involvement of the HIF signaling pathway in miR-210 regulation. To study the biological impacts of a partial or complete loss of miR-210 functions, they also identified the putative mRNA targets of miR-210. According to their report, miR-210 targets important regulators of transcription, cell metabolism, differentiation and development, <italic toggle="yes">i.e</italic>., processes that are critically affected by hypoxia [<xref rid="b121-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">121</xref>]. The identification of key regulators of important cellular processes among miR-210 target mRNAs, as well as the high frequency of gene copy-number aberrations in tumors, underscore the involvement of miR-210 in oncogenesis and highlight miR-210 as a potential link between hypoxia and cell-cycle control in cancer cells.</p><p>Würdinger <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>. reported a role for miR-296 in promoting angiogenesis in tumors [<xref rid="b122-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">122</xref>], and in particular, they showed that VEGF alone is capable of increasing miR-296 expression levels. Their results revealed a feedback loop, wherein VEGF induces miR-296 expression, which targets the hepatocyte growth factor-regulated tyrosine kinase substrate (HGS), which, in turn, results in increased levels of VEGF receptor 2 and platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF) receptor β protein and, ultimately, in an increased response to VEGF.
