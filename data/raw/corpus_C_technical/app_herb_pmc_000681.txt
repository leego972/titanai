Introduction</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Hypericum japonicum</italic> Thunb. ex Murray (Hypericaceae) is an annual herb mainly distributed throughout Asia, Oceania and North America [<xref rid="B1-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B2-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>]. In China, it is widely distributed in Liaoning Province, Shandong Province and other provinces in southern China where it grows in rice fields, ditches, marshes, grasslands and waste places where the altitude is below 2800 m [<xref rid="B2-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="B3-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> called “Tian-Ji-Huang” or “Di-Er-Cao” in Chinese was first listed as a herbal medicine in “Sheng Cao Yao Xing Bei Yao” published during the Qi Dynasty. It has been used in Traditional Chinese Medicine for a long time for relieving internal heat or fever, hemostasis and detumescence [<xref rid="B4-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>]. In addition, it has also been used as a medicinal herb in Asian countries, such as Japan, South Korea, Thailand, Nepal, India, Vietnam and Philippines [<xref rid="B2-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> has been studied due to its high value in traditional medicine. The results of modern pharmacological studies have shown that <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> could be used for the treatment of bacterial diseases, infectious hepatitis, acute and chronic hepatitis, gastrointestinal disorders, internal hemorrhages and tumors, which generally matches its traditional uses [<xref rid="B4-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B5-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>]. So far, no studies on the toxicity of <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> have been reported.</p><p>Recently, different classes of chemicals such as flavonoids, xanthonoids, chromone glycosides, phloroglucinol derivatives and lactones have been found in this plant. Among these isolated components, some single flavonoid compounds such as quercitrin, isoquercitrin and quercetin-7-O-α-L-rhamnose are shown to have a variety of bioactivities <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> or <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic>, and thereby are thought as the bioactive components of <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic>. Hence, quality control based on these bioactive components to ensure the effects of <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> materials and its related products is urgent and necessary.
