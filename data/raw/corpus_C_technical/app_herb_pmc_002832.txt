The commonly reported bacteria include different strains of <italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Shigella</italic> spp., <italic toggle="yes">Vibrio cholerae</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Campylobacter</italic> spp., <italic toggle="yes">Salmonella</italic> spp. and <italic toggle="yes">Yersinia</italic> spp. [<xref rid="B12-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>]. The major implicated viruses are rotavirus, adenovirus, and hepatitis A virus [<xref rid="B12-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>,<xref rid="B13-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>], while parasites include <italic toggle="yes">Entamoeba histolytica</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Giardia</italic> spp. [<xref rid="B13-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>,<xref rid="B14-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>].</p><p>Undiagnosed cases of diarrhea are treated with different antimicrobials, contributing to the accelerated rates of bacteria that are resistant to a few existing antimicrobials [<xref rid="B15-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>,<xref rid="B16-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>]. For instance, increased resistance to ciprofloxacin has been partly attributed to its overuse in treating diarrhea of unknown etiologies [<xref rid="B16-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>]. Other contributors to antimicrobial resistance include natural mutations in pathogens, misuse of antimicrobials in animal husbandry, and the use of counterfeit drugs [<xref rid="B15-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>,<xref rid="B17-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>]. Drug discovery is very slow compared to the exponential increase in the number of antimicrobial-resistant (AMR) pathogens [<xref rid="B15-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>]. Therefore, with the ending era of conventional antimicrobials [<xref rid="B18-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>], there is a pressing demand to develop new antimicrobial agents if we are to cope with the threat of multidrug-resistant (MDR) pathogens [<xref rid="B14-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>,<xref rid="B19-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>].</p><p>Since ancient times, medicinal plants have been known as raw materials for the manufacturing of over one-third of currently used antimicrobial agents, and modern allopathic medicine has its roots in traditional medicine [<xref rid="B15-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>,<xref rid="B20-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>,<xref rid="B21-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>]. The literature shows that over 80% of currently used drugs are from natural products (herbal medicines, microbes, or their bioactive compounds) [<xref rid="B9-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. Despite a rich plant diversity (45,000 species) in Africa, data on their medicinal use is only available for 5000 species [<xref rid="B22-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>]. As of recently, a few anti-diarrheal drugs from plants include aesculetin from <italic toggle="yes">Frazinus rhychophylla</italic>, emetine from <italic toggle="yes">Cephaelis ipecacuanha</italic>, agrimophol from <italic toggle="yes">Agrimonia supatoria</italic>, berberine from <italic toggle="yes">Berberis vulgaris</italic>, hemsleyadin from <italic toggle="yes">Hemsleya amabilis</italic>, and neoandrographolide from <italic toggle="yes">Andrographis paniculata</italic> [<xref rid="B23-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>].</p><p>In SSA, rural households depend on plant sources for food, animal feeds, and medicines [<xref rid="B24-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>]. Complementary and alternative medicine (CAM) is defined as “a group of diverse medical and healthcare systems, practices, and products that are not generally considered part of conventional medicine” [<xref rid="B25-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>]. The estimate of people relying on CAM for basic health services worldwide and in Africa is 80% and 88%, respectively [<xref rid="B26-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>,<xref rid="B27-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>,<xref rid="B28-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>]. In East Africa, the population using traditional medicine, especially in rural areas, is over 80% [<xref rid="B29-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>]. The preference for folk medicine over conventional health services has been motivated by its easy accessibility, low cost, social-cultural influence, and the belief that herbal medicine is regarded as safe [<xref rid="B2-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="B29-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>].
