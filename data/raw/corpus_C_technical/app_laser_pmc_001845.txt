# PMC: low level laser therapy healing review — Overview

In line with our hypothesis, the obtained results from MTT along with cell cycle assays demonstrated that dendrosomal nano curcumin (DNC) at antioxidant concentrations (0.5, 0.75μM) as well as low doses of LLLT 450 nm (0.63, 0.95 j/cm<sup>2</sup>) were able to promote growth and proliferation in MEFs; however, combination treatment exerted more obvious proliferative effect on mouse embryonic fibroblasts. Therefore, it can be said that increasing the rate of growth and proliferation of fibroblasts in the wound microenvironment, may contribute to fill the denuded area and replace the lost cells at the site of the lesion as well as releasing cytokines and growth factors required for proper healing [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref053" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>].</p><p>Cell proliferation is a fundamental process by which the cell cycle is executed from G1 phase, entering the subsequent S phase and G2/M phase thereafter [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref054" ref-type="bibr">54</xref>]. In 2015, a study reported an increase in the population of keratinocyte cells at the G2/M transition point, which represents an increase in cell proliferation when treated with low concentrations of curcumin along with red and blue LED light [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref013" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>]. To verify the influence of DNC (0.5, 0.75 μM) and LLLT 450 nm (0.95 j/cm<sup>2</sup>) alone and in combination on cell cycle regulation of MEFs during wound healing, DNA content of cells were evaluated in three major cell phases (G1, S, G2/M). In accordance with the results obtained from MTT assay, the cell population under treatment with DNC and LLLT 450 nm each alone and concurrently was enriched in the S phase representing progression of cells towards growth and proliferation. Likewise, a significant diminish in the Sub-G0/G1 phase was observed.</p><p>One of the most important events contributing to wound healing is the migration of fibroblasts to the site of the wound mediated by chemotaxis agents required for further proliferation and differentiation into myofibroblasts [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref031" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0247098.ref055" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>]. Using the scratch-wound assay, we showed that cell migration into the cell-free area was evoked by culturing with DNC (0.5, 0.75 μM) and LLLT 450 nm (0.95 j/cm<sup>2</sup>) exposure. This aspect of our results was consistent with previous reports revealed the increasing effect of curcumin [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref018" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0247098.ref019" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0247098.ref050" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>] and low-level lasers [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref037" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0247098.ref051" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>] on cell migration, through activating cellular signaling pathway involved in this process. However, the migration and progression of MEFs towards each other in the denuded area was counted more intense in simultaneous DNC+LLLT 450 nm treatment group so that the created gap was partially filled after 24h.</p><p>Among the large number of growth factors required for wound healing, transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β), produced by immune and non-immune cells such as fibroblasts, has the most extensive spectrum of effects including reducing inflammation, stimulating proliferation and migration, initiating angiogenesis, conversion of fibroblasts to myofibroblasts and the production of structural proteins and wound strength [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref056" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>]. Previous studies have also designated that low doses of curcumin [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref057" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0247098.ref058" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>] and LLLT (wavelengths ranging from 660 nm to 1064 nm) [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref034" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0247098.ref059" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>] increased the expression of TGF-β acting as both growth factor and anti-inflammatory cytokine. This study also points out that although the expression of TGF-β was significantly increased in MEFs treated with anti-oxidant dose of dendrosome-coated curcumin (0.75μM) and low doses of LLLT 450 nm (0.95 j/cm<sup>2</sup>), this enhancement was more intense and noticeable in MEFs receiving the concurrent combination treatment suggesting a synergistic effect in TGF-β transcripts quantity as well as its secretion.
