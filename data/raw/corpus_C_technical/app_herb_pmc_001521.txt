# PMC: phytochemical bioactive compound anticancer — Overview

Many Onagraceae species, including <italic toggle="yes">Oenothera biennis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Epilobium angustifolium</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Ludwigia octovalvis</italic>, exhibit strong antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties due to their rich flavonoid and polyphenol content. Additionally, compounds from <italic toggle="yes">Oenothera biennis</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Oenothera paradoxa</italic> have demonstrated cytotoxic effects against prostate and breast cancer cells [<xref rid="B7-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>,<xref rid="B8-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>,<xref rid="B9-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. Among the genera of <italic toggle="yes">Onagraceae</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Chamaenerion</italic> (including <italic toggle="yes">C. angustifolium</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">C. latifolium</italic>) stands out for its notable medicinal properties, which are closely linked to its natural habitat and distribution. The geographical range of these species is illustrated in <xref rid="molecules-30-01186-f001" ref-type="fig">Figure 1</xref>.</p><p>In the last decade, the chemistry and biological activity of <italic toggle="yes">Chamaenerion</italic> species have been studied intensively, highlighting the significance of fireweed as an important medicinal plant widely utilized in the pharmaceutical, food, and cosmetic industries [<xref rid="B10-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>,<xref rid="B11-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">C. angustifolium</italic>, commonly known as willowherb or rosebay willowherb, <italic toggle="yes">Chamaenerion angustifolium</italic> (L.) <italic toggle="yes">Holub</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Chamaenerion angustifolium</italic> (L.) <italic toggle="yes">Scop.</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Epilobium angustifolium</italic> L., is a perennial herbaceous plant widely distributed across various habitats in the Northern Hemisphere [<xref rid="B3-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B11-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>,<xref rid="B12-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>]. Traditional medicine has used fireweed plants to treat a variety of ailments, such as wound healing, infections, skin diseases, colds, urinary problems like prostatitis, gastric disorders, migraine headaches, and sleep disturbances [<xref rid="B13-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>,<xref rid="B14-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>]. In northern and eastern Europe, it is utilized as a food plant, particularly in the form of tea or as a traditional herbal remedy. The widespread appeal of this plant primarily stems from its anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, antibacterial, and anticancer properties [<xref rid="B15-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>]. It was also reported that fireweed extract demonstrates analgesic, anticholinesterase, and skin photoprotective properties, while recent studies have emphasized its wound-healing and cosmetic benefits [<xref rid="B16-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>,<xref rid="B17-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>,<xref rid="B18-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>].</p><p>The therapeutic potential of <italic toggle="yes">C. angustifolium</italic> lies in its rich polyphenolic profile, particularly tannins (ellagitannins), flavonoids, and phenolic acids [<xref rid="B19-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>,<xref rid="B20-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>,<xref rid="B21-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>]. The principal phenolic acids include gallic acid, caffeic acid, chlorogenic acid, rosmarinic acid, ellagic acid, p-coumaric acid, and cinnamic acid, while the predominant flavonoids consist of quercetin, myricetin, kaempferol, rutin, quercetin-3-O-glucoside, and hyperoside. Oenothein B, the most abundant ellagitannin, plays a central role in the plant’s medicinal properties [<xref rid="B22-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>,<xref rid="B23-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>], exhibiting antiandrogenic, antiproliferative, anticancer, antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and immunomodulatory activities [<xref rid="B24-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>,<xref rid="B25-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>,<xref rid="B26-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>]. This synergistic interaction of polyphenols and ellagitannins underscores the plant’s extensive use in traditional medicine [<xref rid="B27-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>,<xref rid="B28-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>,<xref rid="B29-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">C. angustifolium</italic> also contains a smallquantity of essential oil, primarily composed of terpenes, such as limonene, bisabolene, and caryophyllene, as well as eugenol, linalool, pelargol, and terpineol [<xref rid="B30-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">C.
