# PMC: laser photodynamic therapy cancer mechanism — Overview

<sup>15</sup>
</xref> an overall response rate of 86.2% was achieved, including 55.2% CR and 31.0% PR, following 4.08 ± 1.73 PDT sessions. Other studies also reported a positive response of 72.3%–83.3% in patients who underwent 6–10 sessions of PDT.<xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0016" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>16</sup>
</xref>, <xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0017" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>17</sup>
</xref> The results of this study showed that 87.5% of the lesions responded to only one session of AFL‐PDT, which indicates that AFL can greatly improve the effectiveness of PDT treatment for OL, and AFL‐PDT shows excellent efficacy in OL treatment. The recurrence rates reported by two retrospective studies<xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0018" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>18</sup>
</xref>, <xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0019" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>19</sup>
</xref> that evaluated AFL for OL were 33.8% during an average 15‐month follow‐up and 32% in a &gt;6‐month follow‐up. Studies<xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0020" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>20</sup>
</xref>, <xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0021" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>21</sup>
</xref> evaluating the effectiveness of PDT for OL have reported recurrence rates that ranged from 18.1% to 30.8% during 3–19 months follow‐up. Both recurrence rates in AFL and PDT for OL were higher than that in this study (37.5% recurrence during a 3‐year follow‐up). Compared with AFL and PDT, AFL‐PDT may be a more effective management strategy for OL treatment and reducing the risk of OL recurrence.<xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0022" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>22</sup>
</xref> In this study, each treatment area was limited to 4 cm<sup>2</sup> or a diameter of 3 cm. When the lesion area exceeded the size limit, it was divided into multiple smaller areas for treatment. Each treatment area underwent one session of AFL‐PDT. This method may confirm the efficacy of AFL‐PDT.</p><p>Intriguingly, lesions in the gingiva/palate (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.001; OR: 1.64, 95% CI: 1.13–2.37) exhibited a higher risk of recurrence than that of other lesions. Alfonso et al.<xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0018" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>18</sup>
</xref> and Yuri et al.<xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0023" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>23</sup>
</xref> reported a consistent result, that the gingiva was the most common recurrence site. The gingiva/palate is subjected to friction and pressure when chewing. Furthermore, the mucosa of both sites was too thin to undergo operation with a sufficient safety margin,<xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0023" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>23</sup>
</xref> and consequently, gingiva and palate lesions were risk factors for recurrence. Interestingly, among the 18 enrolled lesions on the cheek mucosa in this study, 17 lesions responded to AFL‐PDT, and only three lesions recurred. None of the patients developed OSCC. These results suggest that compared to other sites, AFL‐PDT is particularly suitable for lesions on the cheek mucosa.</p><p>A systematic review and meta‐analysis<xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0024" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>24</sup>
</xref> suggested that malignant transformation of OL is independent of laser treatment, including AFL, Nd: YAG laser, KTP laser, or diode laser. Based on the possibility of selective destruction of pathological tissues with accumulated photosensitizers, PDT has been successfully used in oncology.<xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0025" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>25</sup>
</xref> The malignant transformation rate of this study was 8.3%, which was comparable with the rates reported by other studies.<xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0001" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>1</sup>
</xref>, <xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0002" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>2</sup>
</xref> This study cannot yet evaluate the influence of AFL‐PDT on malignant transformation of OL because of the relatively small sample size and short follow‐up period.</p><p>The severity of dysplasia was a common risk factor for malignant transformation,<xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0026" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>26</sup>
</xref>, <xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0027" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>27</sup>
</xref> which was also verified in this study (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> = 0.02; OR: 2.93, 95% CI: 1.96–4.42).
