Novel Polyphenolic Compounds</title><p>Polyphenolic compounds are generally characterized by the presence of an aromatic ring with hydroxyl groups attached. This phytochemical class is of medical importance to humans as antioxidants, antivirals, and anticarcinogens [<xref rid="B59-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>,<xref rid="B60-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>]. Polyphenols are subdivided into phenols and flavonoids and can occur as flavonoid glycosides [<xref rid="B61-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>]. Several studies have established phenolic compounds as modulators of multiple inflammatory components [<xref rid="B62-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>]. Polyphenolic anticancer activity is linked to the modulation of proteins that are involved in procancer signaling pathways, e.g., survival kinases, transcription factors, and growth factors [<xref rid="B63-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>]. Reports have demonstrated the role of polyphenols in targeting the PI3K/Akt/mTOR and Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathways and the downregulation of cell cycle regulatory proteins such as cyclins and its kinases. Polyphenols are also known as modulators of epigenetic regulators such as HDAC1 and HDAC2; tumor suppressor proteins p53, PTEN, p21, and NF-kβ, NRF2 and STATs p27 [<xref rid="B64-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>,<xref rid="B65-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>,<xref rid="B66-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>]. Flavonoids share some targets such as MAPK, PI3K/AKT, NF-κB, and NRF2 signaling pathways with phenols [<xref rid="B67-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>]. Reports from studies established some flavonoids as antimitotic and microtubule-targeting agents (destabilizers and stabilizers of microtubules) and inhibitors of polo-like kinase 1 (PLK1) [<xref rid="B68-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>,<xref rid="B69-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>,<xref rid="B70-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>,<xref rid="B71-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>].</p><p>As shown in <xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f004" ref-type="fig">Figure 4</xref>, novel polyphenols from <italic toggle="yes">Calophyllum soulattri</italic> (compound <bold>18</bold>) showed cytotoxicity against the MDA-MB-231 breast cancer cell line. Compound <bold>18</bold> was almost as active as cisplatin (IC<sub>50</sub>: 19.3 µM) on MDA-MB 231cells. It was found not to be cytotoxic against normal HEK293 cells [<xref rid="B72-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>].</p><p>A novel proanthocyanidin (compound <bold>19</bold>, <xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f004" ref-type="fig">Figure 4</xref>) purified from <italic toggle="yes">Camellia ptilophylla</italic> proved to be antiangiogenic [<xref rid="B73-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>]. This compound suppressed microtubule formation in endothelial cells and cell migration in HMEC-1 cells. It inhibited intersegmental vessels formation (ISV) in Zebrafish and inhibited the phosphorylation of ERK, p38, and Akt in HMEC cells. MAPK and ERK signaling pathways play an important role in angiogenesis [<xref rid="B73-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>].</p><p>Compounds <bold>20</bold> and <bold>21</bold> (<xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f004" ref-type="fig">Figure 4</xref>) isolated from <italic toggle="yes">Leucaena leucocephala</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Celosia argentea</italic> were reported to have potent antioxidant scavenging and reducing power, and to be cytotoxic against some cancer cells [<xref rid="B74-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>,<xref rid="B75-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>].
