# PMC: optical coherence tomography clinical application — Overview

The difference in the average measured attenuation coefficient of normal skin from that estimated by Gong et al.<xref rid="r35" ref-type="bibr"><sup>35</sup></xref> could be due to many factors, including intersubject variation in skin type, variation in skin locations, the use of different AFRs and different instruments, and the use of PS-OCT. The longitudinal measurement/imaging of OCT attenuation provides an important approach for monitoring the tissue response to treatment. Assuming careful calibration is performed at each time point of measurement/imaging, longitudinal imaging potentially provides a useful relative measure of alterations over time, but more research is required to understand the meaning and reliability of absolute attenuation coefficients of skin.</p><p>There is also preliminary use of OCT attenuation to analyze cancer tissue in skin, which will be summarized in Sec. <xref ref-type="sec" rid="sec4.3">4.3</xref> on oncology.<xref rid="r15" ref-type="bibr"><sup>15</sup></xref><sup>,</sup><xref rid="r64" ref-type="bibr"><sup>64</sup></xref> Additionally, Olsen et al.<xref rid="r75" ref-type="bibr"><sup>75</sup></xref> used OCT attenuation as a surrogate measure of skin edema, reporting an increase of the attenuation coefficient in edema of 10 subjects (median <inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math224"><mml:mrow><mml:msub><mml:mi>μ</mml:mi><mml:mi>OCT</mml:mi></mml:msub></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula> increased from <inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math225"><mml:mrow><mml:mn>1.8</mml:mn><mml:mtext>  </mml:mtext><mml:msup><mml:mrow><mml:mi>mm</mml:mi></mml:mrow><mml:mrow><mml:mo>−</mml:mo><mml:mn>1</mml:mn></mml:mrow></mml:msup></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula> at baseline to <inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math226"><mml:mrow><mml:mn>2.3</mml:mn><mml:mtext>  </mml:mtext><mml:msup><mml:mrow><mml:mi>mm</mml:mi></mml:mrow><mml:mrow><mml:mo>−</mml:mo><mml:mn>1</mml:mn></mml:mrow></mml:msup></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula>).</p><p>Overall, the characteristic attenuation coefficients of normal skin and skin conditions (<xref rid="t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref>), and their changes during treatment, indicate the great potential of OCT attenuation for clinical monitoring of skin conditions. The reported values in <xref rid="t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref> show variations among different body locations, possibly caused by different tissue microstructures, and also for the same body locations, such as the normal forearm skin. This might be due to the variation between subjects and, more importantly, the inconsistency in data acquisition and processing methods.
