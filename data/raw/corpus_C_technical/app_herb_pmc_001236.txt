Antimicrobial Activity</title><p>Antimicrobial resistance is nowadays a major global challenge. It occurs when infectious agents such as bacteria, viruses, fungi, and parasites become resistant to standard treatment, instigating a public health threat by spreading infectious diseases and causing huge global economic losses through food spoilage and damages to crops [<xref rid="B55-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>,<xref rid="B56-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>]. In order to overcome antimicrobial resistance, researchers, in their search for new drugs, are showing increased interest in medicinal plants due to their rich diversity in secondary metabolites and phytochemicals [<xref rid="B57-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>]. The antimicrobial properties of <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> from several studies have been analysed and listed in <xref rid="molecules-27-04240-t003" ref-type="table">Table 3</xref>. Using the agar well diffusion method, Beg et al. compared the antimicrobial activity of <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> extracts from fruits, leaves and bark against the gram-positive strain <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic> and the gram-negative strain <italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic> using different extraction solvents [<xref rid="B58-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>]. Results showed that the methanolic extract, followed by hexane and chloroform extracts, of the fruits had the strongest activity against both of the tested strain; the aqueous extract of all plant parts did not show any antimicrobial activity [<xref rid="B58-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>]. Interestingly, it was found that the fruit part of the plant displayed substantial antibacterial activity against various gram-positive strains tested [<xref rid="B59-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>]. Another study demonstrated that the chloroform and ethyl acetate fractions of <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> were effective against both gram-negative and gram-positive bacteria, but the methanol and aqueous fractions did not exhibit any activity towards any of the tested microorganisms [<xref rid="B41-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>]. Gautam et al. demonstrated that the ethanolic and aqueous <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> extracts exhibited significant activity against <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Pseudomonas aeruginosa</italic>, although it had no activity against <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus subtilis,</italic> noting that the ethanolic extract was more active than the aqueous one [<xref rid="B60-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>]. Sharma et al. attributed the antimicrobial activity of <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> to the presence of alkaloids, flavonoids, glycosides, and saponins in the leaf extracts of the plant, proposing that leaf extracts may be an effective agent for the treatment of several diseases [<xref rid="B61-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>]. </p><p>Besides, Pandey and Devi showed that the difference in antimicrobial activity was associated with variations in the cyclopeptide alkaloid composition [<xref rid="B62-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>]. For instance, the 13-membered cyclopeptide alkaloids, nummularine-R, nummularine-S, and nummularine-B, were effective antibacterial agents only against gram-negative bacteria such as <italic toggle="yes">Klebsiella pneumonia</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic>, while the 14-membered cyclopeptide alkaloid, Frangufoline, showed significant antibacterial activity against the gram-positive bacterium <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic> as well as the gram-negative bacteria <italic toggle="yes">Klebsiella pneumonia</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic> [<xref rid="B62-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>].
