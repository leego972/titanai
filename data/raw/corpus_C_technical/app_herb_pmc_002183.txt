The phytochemical study found that the root tissues of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> contained the highest amount of bibenzyl, such as erianin and gigantol. And the transcriptomic analysis revealed that cytochrome P450 genes and other enzymatic genes were functionally associated with the biosynthesis and accumulation of bibenzyl, which might help increase the content of bibenzyl for drug production and industrialization of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> [<xref rid="B20" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>]. Several bibenzyl compounds have also been found in the leaves of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic>, such as the new derivate denofficin, dendrocandin B, 4,4′-dihydroxy-3,5-dimethoxy bibenzyl, gigantol, and densiflorol [<xref rid="B21" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>].</p><p>Alkaloids were found abundant in protocorm-like bodies of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic>, which might be more available for producing alkaloids than other organs. The study also found that the enzymes involved in the alkaloid biosynthesis were strictosidine <italic toggle="yes">β</italic>-<italic toggle="yes">D</italic>-glucosidase, geissoschizine synthase, and vinorine synthase in <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> [<xref rid="B10" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>]. Additionally, the key enzyme-encoding genes associated with the alkaloid biosynthesis had higher activities in the leaves than that in the stems of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> [<xref rid="B22" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>].</p><p>Due to the increasing demand and rare resources of wild type, there are more and more adulterations of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic>, and it negatively affects the sustainable utilization of this medicinal plant and food resource and increases the potential health risk of using cheaper and poorer products. The composition of ingredients contributes to the quantitative chemotypic variation and characteristics within difference [<xref rid="B23" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>]. Hence, some methods targeting the specific compounds of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> have been developed for distinguishment. For example, the quantifications of naringenin, bibenzyls, and the ratios of mannose to glucose of polysaccharides could be used as key elements to distinguish from other similar spices [<xref rid="B24" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>]. Additionally, the combined analysis of HPLC fingerprints, HPLC-ESI-MS, and HPTLC found that violanthin and isoviolanthin were specific components for <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic>, which could distinguish it from <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium devonianum</italic> [<xref rid="B25" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>].</p><p>Overall, the phytochemical studies found abundant bioactive components in <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic>, and they are closely associated with various bioactivities and health benefits. The identification of chemical structures of some major compounds as well as biosynthesis-related gene encoding enzymes is important for the exploration and protection of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale.</italic> On the other hand, the chemical composition of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> could be used to distinguish the plant from different sources. These findings facilitate a better understanding of the phytochemical variation of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic>, contributing to better quality control.</p></sec><sec id="sec2.2"><title>2.2. Influential Factors</title><p>The varied growth environment and origins with different natural resources lead to significant differences in the yield, quality, and even medicinal values of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic>. Samples from Zhejiang, Fujian, Yunnan, and Jiangxi Provinces resulted in different compositions of the active compounds, of which only the sample from Yunnan Province had three unique medicinal components, and only the sample from Jiangxi Province had no toxic component [<xref rid="B26" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>]. Hence, <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> from different regions need more investigations for better collection, protection, and utilization.</p><p>The quality and biological activities of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> could be influenced by the processing methods and storage conditions [<xref rid="B27" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>–<xref rid="B29" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>].
