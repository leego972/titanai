Question: <p>How would you define your parser and lexer rules to parse a language that uses indentation for defining scope.</p>

<p>I have already googled and found a clever approach for parsing it by generating INDENT and DEDENT tokens in the lexer.</p>

<p>I will go deeper on this problem and post an answer if I come to something interesting, but I would like to see other approaches to the problem.</p>

<p>EDIT:
As Charlie pointed out, <a href="http://stackoverflow.com/questions/232682/how-would-you-go-about-implementing-off-side-rule">there is already another thread very similar if not the same.</a> Should my post be deleted?</p>

Answer: <p>This is kind of hypothetical, as it would depend on what technology you have for your lexer and parser, but the easiest way would seem to be to have BEGINBLOCK and ENDBLOCK tokens analogous to braces in C.  Using the <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-side_rule">"offsides rule"</a> your lexer needs to keep track of a stack of indendtation levels.  When the indent level increases, emit a BEGINBLOCK for the parser; when the indentation level decreases, emit ENDBLOCK and pop levels off the stack.</p>
