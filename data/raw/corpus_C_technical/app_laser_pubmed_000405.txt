Plasmonic photothermal therapy in the near-IR region using gold nanostars.

Etemadi M, Golmohammadi S, Akbarzadeh A, Rasta SH.

Photothermal therapy using nanoparticles is a prominent technique for cancer 
treatment. The principle is to maximize the heat conversion efficiency using 
plasmonic nanoparticle-light interaction. Due to their unique optical 
characteristics derived from their anisotropic structure, gold nanostars (GNSs) 
have gotten significant attention in photothermal therapy. To design a proper 
cancer treatment, it is vital to study the thermal effect induced close to the 
gold nanoparticles, in the vicinity, and the cancerous tissue. A 
temperature-dependent 2D model based on finite element method models is commonly 
used to simulate near-IR tumor ablation. The bioheat equation describes the 
photothermal effect within the GNSs and the environment. Surface cooling and 
heating strategies, such as the periodical heating method and a reduced laser 
irradiation area, were investigated to address surface overheating problems. We 
also determined that the optimal laser radius depends on tumor aspect ratio and 
laser intensity. Our results provide guidelines to evaluate a safe and feasible 
temperature range, treatment time, optimal laser intensity, and laser radius to 
annihilate a tumor volume.
