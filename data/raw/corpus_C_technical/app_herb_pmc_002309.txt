Currently, the available studies on this subject are based on the analysis of other parts of the plant, primarily the flowers, in which fumaric acid is suggested to be the compound responsible for the antimicrobial activity [<xref rid="B99-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>].</p><p>On the other hand, recent studies conducted with <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> have applied encapsulation technologies to improve its properties and efficacy. In one of these studies, zinc oxide nanoparticles were developed, which demonstrated a significant increase in the antimicrobial activity of <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> extracts, specifically against extended spectrum <italic toggle="yes">β</italic>-lactamase producing bacteria [<xref rid="B100-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">100</xref>]. Another study carried out with gold nanoparticles also showed promising results, with increased antimicrobial activity against <italic toggle="yes">B. subtilis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">S. aureus</italic>, Streptococcus <italic toggle="yes">faecalis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">K. pneumoniae</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">P. aeruginosa</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">E. coli</italic>, and the fungus <italic toggle="yes">Candida albicans</italic> [<xref rid="B101-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">101</xref>]. These findings reveal that <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> extracts have broad-spectrum antimicrobial potential against Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria through both conventional extracts and nanoparticle-based systems. However, a comprehensive investigation of these extracts is required to identify the secondary metabolites responsible for this activity, thereby determining the potential of <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> as a source of natural antibiotics.</p></sec><sec id="sec4dot3-nutrients-17-03382"><title>4.3. Anticancer Activity</title><p><italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> have also been tested against different cancer cell lines, including MCF-7 (human breast adenocarcinoma pleural effusion), A549 (human lung carcinoma cell line), HCT-116 (human colorectal carcinoma cell line), DU145 (human prostate cancer cell line), and NCI-H226 (human lung squamous cell carcinoma line) cells (<xref rid="nutrients-17-03382-t003" ref-type="table">Table 3</xref>). Other cancer cell lines sensitive to <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> include Calu-6 (human lung carcinoma) and SNU-601 (human gastric carcinoma) cells, with a strong growth inhibitory concentration below 100 µg/mL [<xref rid="B102-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>]. The majority of studies were conducted using unpurified extracts, which consistently exhibited significant antitumor activity at concentrations in the range of 0.85 to 1.25 mM across various lung and colon cancer cell lines [<xref rid="B42-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>]. According to chromatographic studies, this intense antitumor activity can be attributed to the presence of triterpenoids and flavonol glucosides, such as camellioside A and B [<xref rid="B20-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>]. Another study reported that triterpenoid saponins isolated from <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> effectively suppressed cell proliferation in the MCF-7 breast cancer cell line at a concentration of 100 µg/mL [<xref rid="B20-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>]. It has also been reported that lupeol, a biologically active compound isolated from <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic>, exhibits potent anticancer effects [<xref rid="B50-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>]. These findings suggest that <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> and its natural compounds hold potential as anticancer agents for the treatment of various cancer types. Moreover, the antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties reported for these extracts may contribute to the development of anticancer activity, as both processes are closely linked to the onset of carcinogenesis [<xref rid="B103-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">103</xref>]. Thus, the development of extracts from <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> could offer new therapeutic targets for cancer treatment. However, there is an urgent need for in vivo and interventional studies to adequately evaluate the effectiveness of <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> extracts in human patients.</p></sec><sec id="sec4dot4-nutrients-17-03382"><title>4.4.
