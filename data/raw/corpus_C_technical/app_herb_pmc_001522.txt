Its distribution includes North America, Greenland, Iceland, and northern Russia, while it is largely absent from northern Europe [<xref rid="B31-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>,<xref rid="B32-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>]. In southern Asia, it occurs in the Himalayas, ranging from Afghanistan to western China [<xref rid="B33-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>,<xref rid="B34-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>], and is also found in Central Asian regions such as Altai, Tabagatai, Dzungarian Alatau, Zailiysky Alatau, Kyrgyz Alatau, Kungei Alatau, and the Western Tien Shan (<xref rid="molecules-30-01186-f001" ref-type="fig">Figure 1</xref>) [<xref rid="B20-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>]. This species can be distinguished from its regional counterpart, <italic toggle="yes">C</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">angustifolium</italic> Holub, by its shorter, decumbent to ascending, often branched stems (up to 40 cm) and compact, few-flowered racemes [<xref rid="B33-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">C. latifolium</italic> is noted for its complex chemical composition, which includes bioactive compounds such as terpenes, steroids, triterpenoids, phenolic acids, and flavonoids. The key constituents are phenolic compounds, like quercetin 3-glucoside, rutin, gallic acid, caffeic acid, and chlorogenic acid, which vary based on extraction methods [<xref rid="B35-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>]. Ethanol extracts (ChL-EtOH) have been shown to be very rich in phenolic compounds, with strong antibacterial activity against bacterial and fungal strains, and outstanding antioxidant qualities, according to studies using HPLC-UV-ESI/MS [<xref rid="B20-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>]. In particular, ChL-EtOH performed best in antioxidant assessments that showed significant DPPH scavenging and FRAP capabilities. Additionally, ChL-EtOH demonstrated remarkable antibacterial activity against the fungal strain <italic toggle="yes">Candida albicans</italic> as well as Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria [<xref rid="B9-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. These results, together with its chemical complexity, emphasise its pharmacological potential and suggest that it shares characteristics with <italic toggle="yes">C. angustifolium</italic>, which is a rich source of bioactive compounds with interesting pharmacological significance [<xref rid="B20-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>].</p><p>This review aims to provide a comprehensive analysis of the botanical characteristics, chemical compositions, and biological effects of <italic toggle="yes">C. angustifolium</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">C. latifolium</italic>, exploring potential benefits for human health.</p></sec><sec sec-type="results" id="sec2-molecules-30-01186"><title>2.
