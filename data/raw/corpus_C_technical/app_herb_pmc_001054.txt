Amino acids are important compounds from a nutritional point of view since they are crucial to a balanced diet (maintaining optimal levels of essential amino acids) [<xref rid="B112-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>]. Some of the essential ones were reported in <italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic>, explicitly histidine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, and valine [<xref rid="B110-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>]. The remaining amino acids play an important role in plant physiology by maintaining homeostasis during osmotic stress due to the high concentration of NaCl in the external medium [<xref rid="B5-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>].</p><p>These two classes of compounds are indispensable from a nutritional point of view. The high diversity of amino acids in <italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic> could lead to its use as an alternative to other vegetal sources. Carbohydrates were reported to be well distributed among the studied halophytic grasses, which can also be a sign to their possible use as edible plants. Furthermore, due to its high diversity of carbohydrates (allied with their rich chemical composition), the introduction of <italic toggle="yes">A. donax</italic> in the diet should be considered after toxicological and safety assessments.</p></sec><sec id="sec2dot5-ijms-20-01067"><title>2.5. Terpenoids</title><p>Terpenoids are the class with more structural diversity among halophytic grasses summing 48 compounds distributed in several subclasses: triterpenoids (<bold>167</bold>–<bold>180</bold>), sesquiterpenoids (<bold>181</bold>–<bold>189</bold>), steroids and derivatives (<bold>190</bold>–<bold>209</bold>), diterpenoids (<bold>216</bold>), monoterpenoids (<bold>211</bold>–<bold>215</bold>) and tetraterpenoids (<bold>210</bold>) (<xref ref-type="app" rid="app1-ijms-20-01067">Table S1</xref>). </p><p>Triterpenoids were described in four halophytic grasses namely in <italic toggle="yes">A. donax</italic> (from lipophilic extracts), <italic toggle="yes">C. ciliaris</italic> (methanol, ethyl acetate, and hexane extracts), <italic toggle="yes">C. dactylon</italic> (80% aqueous ethanol extract) and <italic toggle="yes">I. cylindrica</italic> (chloroform/methanol extract) (<xref ref-type="app" rid="app1-ijms-20-01067">Table S1</xref>). This subclass is well known for its pharmacological activities [<xref rid="B113-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>], for instance, α-amyrenone (<bold>168</bold>) and β-amyrenone (<bold>167</bold>), only reported in <italic toggle="yes">A. donax</italic>, have the ability to interfere in acute and chronic inflammatory processes (oral administration, 23.5 μmol/kg) [<xref rid="B114-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>]. Ursolic acid (<bold>180</bold>), found only in <italic toggle="yes">A. donax</italic>, possesses the ability to increase muscle mass and brown fat, leading to increased energy expenditure and therefore reduced obesity, improved glucose tolerance and decreased hepatic steatosis (high-fat diet supplemented with 0.14% ursolic acid, in rats) [<xref rid="B115-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">115</xref>]. Lupeol (<bold>177</bold>), found only in <italic toggle="yes">C. ciliaris</italic>, revealed topic anti-inflammatory activity in mouse’s ears (topical administration, 0.5 and 1 mg/ear dose) [<xref rid="B116-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">116</xref>] and anticarcinogenic activity against T-lymphoblastic leukaemia CEM (IC<sub>50</sub>: 50 µM), breast carcinoma MCF-7 (IC<sub>50</sub>: 50 µM), lung carcinoma A-549 (IC<sub>50</sub>: 50 µM), multiple myeloma RPMI 8226 (IC<sub>50</sub>: 50 µM), cervical carcinoma HeLa (IC<sub>50</sub>: 37 µM) and malignant melanoma G361 (IC<sub>50</sub>: 50 µM) when treated for 72 h [<xref rid="B117-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">117</xref>]. The mentioned biological capacities of lupeol were performed in vivo and in vitro [<xref rid="B118-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">118</xref>] fact that, in our opinion, increases its significance.</p><p>Among terpenoids found in halophytes from the Poaceae, steroids and their derivatives (<bold>190</bold>–<bold>209</bold>) (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="ijms-20-01067-f005">Figure 5</xref>) are the most reported ones. These were described in <italic toggle="yes">A. donax</italic>’s lipophilic extract (15 compounds), <italic toggle="yes">C. ciliaris</italic>’s methanol, ethyl acetate and hexane extracts (7 compounds), <italic toggle="yes">C. dactylon</italic>’s 80% aqueous ethanol extract (1 compounds), <italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic> (4 compounds) and <italic toggle="yes">I. cylindrica</italic>’s chloroform/methanol extracts (2 compounds). A diet rich in phytosterols is associated with lower risks of osteoporosis, heart disease, breast cancer, among others [<xref rid="B119-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">119</xref>].
