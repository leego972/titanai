Geography
At its largest, Sabrina Island was a circular volcanic cone with a perimeter of  and an altitude between  above sea level. The cone was similar in form to the existing Islet of Vila Franca do Campo, with an open ring to the northwest. From the central ring debris escaped into the sea.

Because of water erosion, islands formed by submarine volcanic activity often have short, ephemeral existences, especially if they are formed of uncompacted tephra. This was the case with Sabrina Island: despite reaching a height of about , it was quickly destroyed by erosion.
