</p></sec><sec id="Sec22"><title>Local community preferences for medicinal plants used to treat diarrhea</title><p id="Par159">In Sheikh District, Sahil Region, Somaliland, eight medicinal plant species were documented for the management of diarrhea. Among these, five species were highlighted through preference ranking based on perceived effectiveness, availability, and overall importance, as assessed by seven key informants. This approach reflects the nuanced experiential knowledge of local practitioners and households, illustrating how communities differentiate between plant efficacy, accessibility, and multifunctionality when addressing prevalent health challenges.</p><p id="Par160">
<italic toggle="yes">Citrus × limon</italic> emerged as the most preferred species, receiving the highest number of citations (78; 73.6%) and consistently ranking first in preference. Its prominence in local healthcare aligns with reports from Kenya and India, where <italic toggle="yes">Citrus × limon</italic> is widely used for gastrointestinal disorders due to its antimicrobial, digestive, and anti-inflammatory properties [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR40">40</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR48">48</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR192">192</xref>]. The species’ local dominance likely reflects a combination of therapeutic efficacy and widespread availability, underscoring the importance of accessible plants in sustaining community health practices.</p><p id="Par161">The second most preferred species, <italic toggle="yes">Vachellia bussei</italic>, cited by 66 informants (62.3%), similarly demonstrates high cultural and medicinal significance.
