Phytochemical Significance of Polyscias fruticosa in Green Synthesis of Anticancer Nanoparticles</title><p>The use of plant-derived bioactive compounds in nanomedicine is gaining traction as a strategy to enhance the efficacy, selectivity, and safety of anticancer agents. <italic toggle="yes">Polyscias fruticosa</italic>, traditionally recognized for its anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory properties, has recently gained attention as a valuable resource for the green synthesis of metal-based nanoparticles due to its content of triterpenoid saponins, polyphenols, and flavonoids [<xref rid="B3-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B4-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B8-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>,<xref rid="B10-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>,<xref rid="B11-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>,<xref rid="B13-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>].</p><p>These phytoconstituents play a dual role: (i) acting as natural reducing agents facilitating the conversion of metal ions into nanoparticles, and (ii) stabilizing the resulting nanostructures by improving colloidal stability and bioavailability [<xref rid="B169-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">169</xref>,<xref rid="B170-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">170</xref>].</p><p>Such dual functionality supports its use in environmentally sustainable nanotechnological approaches, particularly in biomedical applications such as cancer therapy.</p><sec id="sec6dot5dot1-molecules-30-03460"><title>6.5.1. Biosynthesis and Cytotoxicity of Nanoparticles</title><p>Recent studies have confirmed the feasibility of green synthesis of silver (AgNPs) and copper (CuNPs) nanoparticles using aqueous or ethanolic extracts from <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> leaves and roots [<xref rid="B169-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">169</xref>,<xref rid="B170-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">170</xref>]. These biosynthesized nanoparticles exhibit spherical morphology (50–120 nm), characteristic UV-Vis plasmon resonance peaks, and negative zeta potentials (−25 to −30 mV), indicating stability and uniformity.</p><p>Cytotoxicity assays demonstrated that Pfr-AgNPs reduced the viability of A549 human lung carcinoma cells by 47.3% at 100 µg·mL<sup>−1</sup>, while Pfr-CuNPs inhibited neuroblastoma cell proliferation by 55.5% at 125 µg·mL<sup>−1</sup> [<xref rid="B169-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">169</xref>,<xref rid="B170-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">170</xref>]. Mechanistically, cytotoxic effects are associated with ROS generation, mitochondrial dysfunction, membrane depolarization, and caspase-mediated apoptosis.</p><p>Notably, brine shrimp lethality assays confirmed the low toxicity of the crude extract (LC<sub>50</sub> &gt; 1000 µg·mL<sup>−1</sup>), whereas the nanoparticles exhibited moderate toxicity (LC<sub>50</sub> = 524 µg·mL<sup>−1</sup>), suggesting selective cytotoxicity toward cancer cells.</p></sec><sec id="sec6dot5dot2-molecules-30-03460"><title>6.5.2. Mechanistic Insights and Therapeutic Prospects</title><p>Current evidence indicates that the cytotoxic effects of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>-derived nanoparticles are mainly due to the release of Ag<sup>+</sup> or Cu<sup>+</sup> ions, which induce intracellular oxidative stress, apoptosis pathways, and inhibition of cellular proliferation [<xref rid="B171-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">171</xref>]. Additionally, phytochemical surface coatings composed of saponins and flavonoids may facilitate cellular uptake and improve intracellular delivery.</p><p>Triterpenoid saponins present in the extract are hypothesized to influence multiple molecular targets, including MAPK signaling, topoisomerase inhibition, and regulation of cell cycle genes and metastasis-related pathways, offering a multimodal anticancer mechanism.</p><p>However, further studies are required to evaluate the in vivo efficacy, biodistribution, and long-term safety of these nanoparticles, as the current evidence is limited to in vitro models.</p></sec></sec><sec id="sec6dot6-molecules-30-03460"><title>6.6.
