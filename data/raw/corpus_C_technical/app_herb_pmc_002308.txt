Once separated, the antioxidant capacity of each compound was analyzed, obtaining the best values with rutin, camellianoside, and isoquercitrin (IC<sub>50</sub> values of 20 µM, 25.8 µM, and 27.9 µM, respectively) [<xref rid="B34-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>,<xref rid="B35-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>,<xref rid="B42-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>]. Furthermore, it should be noted that the composition of the extracts will depend not only on the extraction technique used, but also on the cultivar and soil and climate conditions. Antimicrobial Activity</title><p><italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> are also a relevant source of extracts with antimicrobial properties. As with antioxidant activity, an increase in the effectiveness of this bioactivity was observed when polar solvents such as ethanol were used for extraction, allowing for good results in terms of antibacterial, antifungal, and antiviral activity [<xref rid="B31-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>]. For example, the methanolic extracts of young <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> have been assessed for their antimicrobial properties, demonstrating significant effectiveness against <italic toggle="yes">S. aureus</italic> [<xref rid="B96-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>]. Regarding the influence of cultivar, it has been observed that different <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> cultivars exhibit varying antimicrobial capacities [<xref rid="B83-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">83</xref>,<xref rid="B97-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>,<xref rid="B98-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">98</xref>]. This was demonstrated in a study involving seven varieties (‘Kramer’s Supreme’, ‘C.M. Wilson’, ‘La Pace’, ‘Mrs. Lyman Clarke’, ‘Benikarako’, ‘Fanny Bolis’), where it was found that the ‘Mrs. Lyman Clarke’ cultivar showed the most significant antimicrobial activity against <italic toggle="yes">Enterobacter cloacae</italic> (inhibition zone of 12.5 mm). In contrast, the ‘La Pace’ cultivar exhibited the lowest bioactivity [<xref rid="B90-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">90</xref>]. In another study, <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> demonstrates significant potential in inhibiting the microbial growth of various strains, including <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus epidermidis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus subtilis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Klebsiella pneumonia</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic>, with inhibition zones ranging from 7.0 to 8.7 mm [<xref rid="B31-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>]. In another study, <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> extracts were tested against <italic toggle="yes">B. subtilis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Streptomyces fradiae</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">E. coli</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Pseudomonas aeruginosa</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Enterobacter</italic> spp. and <italic toggle="yes">Salmonella enterica</italic>, which resulted in inhibition zones ranging from 1 to 15 mm [<xref rid="B41-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>]. Based on these previous findings, it can be inferred that <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> extracts exhibit greater efficacy against Gram-positive bacteria, with minimal activity observed against Gram-negative bacteria. However, it should be noted that all these studies were conducted with extracts that did not undergo optimization or purification processes. Therefore, future research would be interesting to analyze the compounds that may be responsible for this bioactivity in order to increase the bioactivity of the extracts.
