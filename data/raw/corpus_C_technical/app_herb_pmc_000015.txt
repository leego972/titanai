The compounds were active against most of the bacteria except <italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Pseudomonas aureginosa</italic>, with inhibition zone demonstrated by the pathogens ranging from 8 mm to 24 mm [<xref rid="B40-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>]. A preliminary antibacterial assay showed that the petroleum ether and chloroform extracts of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> root bark gave an inhibitory zone of 15 mm, at an extract concentration of 0.3 mg/mL against <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic> [<xref rid="B50-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>]. Lall and Meyer [<xref rid="B61-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>] evaluated antibacterial activities of water and acetone root extracts of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> against <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus cereus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus pumilus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus subtilis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Enterobacter aerogenes</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Enterobacter cloacae</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Klebsiella pneumoniae</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Micrococcus kristinae</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Serratia marcescens</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus.</italic> The water and acetone extracts inhibited the growth of <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus cereus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus pumilus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus subtilis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Micrococcus kristinae</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic> at concentrations ranging between 0.1 mg/mL and 6.0 mg/mL. The water extract did not exert any inhibitory action on Gram-negative bacteria, while the acetone extract showed inhibitory activity at a concentration of 5.0 mg/mL against all the Gram-negative bacteria investigated [<xref rid="B61-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>]. Weigenand et al. [<xref rid="B52-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>] evaluated antibacterial activities of betulin <bold>1</bold>, lupeol <bold>2</bold>, shinanolone <bold>13</bold>, 20(29)-lupene-3β-isoferulate <bold>14</bold> and octahydroeuclein <bold>15</bold> compounds isolated from root bark of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> against <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus cereus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus pumilus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus subtilis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Enterobacter cloacae</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Klebsiella pneumoniae, Pantoea agglomerans</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Pseudomonas aeruginosa</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Serratia marcescens</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Streptococcus faecalis</italic> using the agar plate method with streptomycin sulphate as control. The compound shinanolone <bold>13</bold> showed inhibitory activity against Gram-positive bacterial strains at a concentration of 0.1 mg/mL and 20(29)-lupene-3β-isoferulate <bold>14</bold> showed inhibitory activity against <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus pumilus</italic> at a concentration of 0.1 mg/mL [<xref rid="B52-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>]. More et al. [<xref rid="B35-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>] evaluated antimicrobial activities of ethanol leaf extracts of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> against oral pathogens, namely <italic toggle="yes">Actinobacillus actinomycetemcomitans</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Actinomyces naeslundii</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Actinomyces israelii</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Porphyromonas gingivalis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Prevotella intermedia</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Streptococcus mutans</italic>, using the disk diffusion method. The extracts were active against the tested bacteria with both minimal inhibitory concentration (MIC) and minimum bactericidal concentration (MBC) values ranging from 1.6 mg/mL to 25 mg/mL [<xref rid="B35-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>]. Van Vuuren and Naidoo [<xref rid="B62-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>] evaluated antibacterial activities of aqueous and a mixture of methanol and dichloromethane (1:1) leaf extracts of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> against bacterial pathogens associated with urogenital or sexually transmitted infections which included <italic toggle="yes">Gardnerella vaginalis, Neisseria gonorrhoeae, Oligella ureolytica</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Ureaplasma urealyticum</italic>. All methanol and dichloromethane extracts exhibited noteworthy activities with MIC values ranging from 1.5 mg/mL to 2.0 mg/mL against all tested pathogens.
