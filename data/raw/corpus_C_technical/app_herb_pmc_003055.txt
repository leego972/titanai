Seven commonly cited antimalarial species were evaluated, with total scores ranging from 12 to 54, reflecting differences in perceived efficacy and local preference.</p><p id="Par125"><italic toggle="yes">Cleome kelleriana</italic> emerged as the top-ranked species, reflecting strong recognition and frequent use by the Somali community. <italic toggle="yes">Aloe megalacantha</italic> followed closely, ranking second, consistent with its widespread reputation as an effective antimalarial. <italic toggle="yes">Lawsonia inermis</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Eucalyptus camaldulensis</italic> were ranked third and fourth, showing moderate but notable preference.</p><p id="Par126">Lower-ranked species included <italic toggle="yes">Jatropha aceroides</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Tamarindus indica</italic>, while <italic toggle="yes">Cyphostemma adenocaule</italic> was the least preferred, suggesting limited local use or perceived efficacy for malaria treatment.</p><p id="Par127">
