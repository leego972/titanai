Since OCT can distinguish between gray and white matter, fine placement of electrodes for deep brain stimulation could be guided in a rodent model [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR28">28</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR30">30</xref>]. A combination with laser ablation systems has the ability of direct real-time feedback to guide the ablation process in a porcine brain tumor model [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR7">7</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR11">11</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR21">21</xref>]. These yet experimental theranostic instruments could be promising in the advancing field of minimal invasive tumor, radiant necrosis, and epileptic surgery.</p></sec><sec id="Sec8"><title>Extra-axial lesions</title><p id="Par16">OCT-guided dissection of cranial dura mater showed the ability to discriminate dural layers. Thin dura mater in combination with low optical density enabled transdural OCT scanning. These scans showed a sufficient image quality to delineate concealed microanatomical structures like the subarachnoid space, inlying blood vessels, or the brain cortex [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR15">15</xref>] (see Fig. <xref rid="Fig1" ref-type="fig">1</xref>). No literature exists on the use of OCT during meningioma surgery. The above mentioned study suggests that OCT would only be suitable to delineate crucial venous structures like the sinus in certain meningiomas with a low optical density.</p></sec><sec id="Sec9"><title>Vascular neurosurgery</title><p id="Par17">Microscope-integrated OCT could well delineate the microstructural composition of cerebral vessel walls [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR8">8</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR38">38</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR42">42</xref>] (Fig. <xref rid="Fig2" ref-type="fig">2</xref>). Clinical relevant characteristics like wall thickness, different layers, calcifications, and arteriosclerosis could be clearly defined in cerebral arteries, veins, and aneurysms [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR18">18</xref>].
