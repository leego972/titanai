Antiviral Activity</title><p>Using a serum pharmacology method, the anti-HBV effect for different extracts (ethanol extract, ethyl acetate extract, <italic toggle="yes">n</italic>-butanol extract and aqueous extract) of <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> were tested <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic>. The aqueous extract showed better anti-HBV activity than the other three extracts with inhibitory rates on HBeAg and HBsAg of 70% and 30%, respectively [<xref rid="B39-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>]. In an <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> study, <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> extract showed strong activity against duck hepatitis B virus at dosage of 6.5, 13.0 and 26.0 mg/kg, once a day, for 28 days [<xref rid="B41-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>]. In addition, the 75% ethanol extract of <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> exhibited anti-influenza virus H3N2 effect <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> after 10 g/kg oral administration in mice infected with the H3N2 virus [<xref rid="B42-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>].</p></sec><sec><title>3.6. Effects on Cardiovascular System</title><p>The bioactive compound quercetin-7-O-α-L-rhamnoside isolated from <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> was tested for its coagulant activity via an <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> experiment. This flavonol glycoside (10<sup>−5</sup> g/L) exhibited excellent effects on promoting the coagulation of activated partial thromboplastin time and prothrombin time in dose-dependent manner [<xref rid="B7-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>]. Oral administration of the aqueous extract of <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> (11.25 g raw material/kg/day) in hyperlipidemic rats for 8 weeks, significantly reduced the serum levels of triglycerides, total cholesterol, low density lipoprotein cholesterol and atherosclerotic index, but increased the level of high density lipoprotein cholesterol. Meanwhile, the hematocrit, blood and plasma viscosity were markedly decreased in this study, indicating the aqueous extract of <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> had an adjustment effect on hemorheology [<xref rid="B43-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>]. Furthermore, the contents of monocyte chemoattractant protein-1, lipoprotein associated phospholipase A2 and macrophage migration inhibitory factor in serum were also obviously decreased, indicating the inhibitory effect on the atherosclerosis process [<xref rid="B44-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>].</p></sec><sec><title>3.7. Effects on Immunity</title><p>The effect of <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> extract on systemic immune functions was investigated by subcutaneous injection in rats with a dosage of 2 g raw material/kg, and the results showed that <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> extract obviously increased the phagocytic rate of neutrophils (83.5% ± 5.1%) and enhanced the T lymphocyte ratio (68.8% ± 6.5%) in the peripheral blood of male rats [<xref rid="B45-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>]. In addition, the extract showed strong effects on improving the immune function, reducing the toxic effect of 5-fluorouracil and prolonging the survival time in the tumor-bearing mice. Therefore, it was considered to have immunoregulatory activity [<xref rid="B38-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>].</p></sec></sec><sec><title>4. Quality Control</title><p>So far, the quality evaluation of <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> materials has been not listed in the Chinese Pharmacopoeia (2010 edition), even though it has been used as a traditional medicine for a long time. Much effort thus is made to establish a comprehensive method for ensuring the quality of <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic>. For the safety and efficacy of herbal medicines, the first step in assuring quality is correct identification [<xref rid="B46-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>]. Traditionally morphological and microscopic approaches have been used for <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> identification [<xref rid="B3-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>]. However, these traditional methods are very limited for <italic toggle="yes">Hypericum</italic> herbs or processed products with similar macroscopic and anatomical characteristics.
