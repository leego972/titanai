In this method, slightly moist <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> is enclosed in boiler and steamed until the drugs both inside and outside are brown, at which point it is removed and desiccated (<xref rid="bib225" ref-type="bibr">Zhou et al., 2010</xref>, <xref rid="bib144" ref-type="bibr">Tian et al., 2007</xref>, <xref rid="bib76" ref-type="bibr">Li et al., 2012g</xref>). In addition, there are other processing methods, including steaming with wine, rehmannia juice and boiling with a black bean-ginger mixture. There are also novel and improved processing methods using modern science and technology, such as fermentation and autoclave steaming. The pressure processing parameters are as follows: the pressure ranges from 0.08 to 0.25 MPa, the heating time ranges from 4 to 10 h, and the temperature is 120 °C (<xref rid="bib75" ref-type="bibr">Li et al., 2012f</xref>, <xref rid="bib76" ref-type="bibr">Li et al., 2012g</xref>, <xref rid="bib33" ref-type="bibr">Du et al., 2012</xref>, <xref rid="bib140" ref-type="bibr">Sun, 1996c</xref>, <xref rid="bib94" ref-type="bibr">Liu et al., 2013</xref>, <xref rid="bib173" ref-type="bibr">Xu et al., 2011</xref>, <xref rid="bib127" ref-type="bibr">Qiu and Zeng, 2006b</xref>).</p><p id="p0030">Currently in China, <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> is a well-known traditional herb that is used as the main component of powders, decoctions or infusions for the treatments of leptotrichia (<xref rid="bib150" ref-type="bibr">Wang et al., 2001</xref>), hyperlipidaemia (<xref rid="bib183" ref-type="bibr">Yang et al., 2005</xref>), inflammation (<xref rid="bib108" ref-type="bibr">Lv et al., 2001</xref>), learning and memory obstructions (<xref rid="bib96" ref-type="bibr">Liu et al., 2004</xref>) and hypoimmunity (<xref rid="bib112" ref-type="bibr">Ma and Du, 2001</xref>) and as an antioxidant and anti-aging compound (<xref rid="bib171" ref-type="bibr">Xiao et al., 1993</xref>). It has been widely used in clinical and traditional practice. An analysis of dozens of traditional Chinese medicine prescription books, such as <italic toggle="yes">“Beijing traditional Chinese medicine prescription anthology”, “Ji Shan Tang Fang”, “Bi Hua Yi Jing”, “Bianque Xin Shu-Shen Fang”, “Bu Ju Ji”, “Chuai Mo You De Ji”, “Dan Xi Xin Fa”</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">“Gu Fang Hui Jing”</italic> revealed 242 prescriptions containing <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic>, and the compatible herbs that are most frequently found include <italic toggle="yes">Angelica sinensis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Radix rehmanniae</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Glycyrrhiza</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Rhizoma Chuanxiong</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Radix sileris</italic>. The 2010 edition of the Chinese Pharmacopoeia lists 46 Chinese patent medicines containing <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic>, and the compatible herbs that are more frequently described include <italic toggle="yes">Radix rehmanniae</italic>, astragalus, <italic toggle="yes">Angelica sinensis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Salviae miltiorrhizae</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Radix ophiopogonis</italic>. <xref rid="t0005" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref> lists subsets of the Chinese patent drugs and decoctions containing <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum
