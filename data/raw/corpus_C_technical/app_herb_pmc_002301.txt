Phenolic Compounds</title><p>Phenolic compounds encompass a large group of molecules present in a wide variety of plants and algae, in which they are essential compounds for their growth and survival [<xref rid="B28-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>]. This type of compounds are characterized by having a basic structure formed by at least one aromatic phenolic ring with one or more hydroxyl substituents that can be highly polymerized [<xref rid="B29-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>,<xref rid="B30-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>]. This level of polymerization, along with its level of substitution, allows phenolic compounds to be classified into different classes and subclasses. In the case of <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic>, the identified phenolic compounds belong to the groups of phenolic acids, phenylpropanoids, flavonoids, phenolic glycosides, tannins, and other minor compounds such as dihydrochalcone glycosides and phenolic glycosides (<xref rid="nutrients-17-03382-f001" ref-type="fig">Figure 1</xref>). Phenolic acids are the simplest phenolic compounds in nature. Examples of compounds of this class identified in <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> include gallic acid and 4-hydroxyphenol derivatives. The gallic acid content can be increased by fermenting the leaves, a process used to produce some types of <italic toggle="yes">C. sinensis</italic> tea, which results in a lack of bitterness and dark coloration in these teas [<xref rid="B31-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>].</p><p>Another class of phenolic compounds present in this matrix are phenylpropanoids. Phenylpropanoids are a class of phenolic compounds derived from the amino acid phenylalanine and characterized by a basic C6-C3 structure consisting of a benzene ring with a three-carbon chain. This class of compounds includes subclasses such as hydroxycinnamic acids, lignans, flavonoids, and stilbenes [<xref rid="B32-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>]. Among lignans, eugenol stands out as one of the most abundant compounds in <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> [<xref rid="B33-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>]. The main flavonoids are quercetin, rutin, epicatechin, and their derivates [<xref rid="B19-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>,<xref rid="B34-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>,<xref rid="B35-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>,<xref rid="B36-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>]. Within this subclass of phenolic compounds, a group of molecules exclusive to the <italic toggle="yes">Camellia</italic> genus, called camellianosides, has also been identified in significant concentrations. Camellianosides are flavonol glycosides, specifically quercetin-3-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>-d-xylopyranosyl-(1→3)-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-<italic toggle="yes">α</italic>-l-rhamnopyranosyl-(1→6)-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>-d-glucopyranoside. The interest in these molecules lies in their high antioxidant capacity, which is superior to that of reference antioxidants such as L-cysteine and L-ascorbic acid [<xref rid="B35-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>].</p><p>Phenolic glycosides (<xref rid="nutrients-17-03382-f001" ref-type="fig">Figure 1</xref>) are a class of phenolic compounds composed of a phenolic nucleus linked to one or more sugars via glycosidic bonds. Prominent examples include 1-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>-D-(6-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-<italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-hydroxybenzoyl) glucopyranoside, heterophylliin A, and casuariin [<xref rid="B34-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>,<xref rid="B37-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>].
