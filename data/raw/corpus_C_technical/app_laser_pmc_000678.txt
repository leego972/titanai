Low-Level Laser Therapy (LLLT) and Its Effects on miRNA Expression</title><p>A laser (light amplification by stimulated emission of radiation) is a device that generates electromagnetic radiation that is relatively uniform in wavelength, phase and polarization. This technology was originally described by Maiman in 1960 in the form of a ruby laser [<xref rid="b1-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>]. The properties of lasers have allowed for numerous medical applications, including their use in surgery, activation of photodynamic agents and various ablative therapies in cosmetics, all of which are based on heat generated by the laser beam, in some cases, leading to tissue destruction [<xref rid="b2-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>–<xref rid="b9-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. These applications of lasers are considered “high-energy”, because of their intensities, which range from about 1–100 watt (W)/cm<sup>2</sup>.</p><p>This paper will address another type of laser application, low-level laser therapy (LLLT), which elicits its effects through non-thermal means. This field was initiated by the work of Mester <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>., who in 1967 reported non-thermal effects of lasers on mouse hair growth [<xref rid="b10-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>]. In a subsequent study, the same group reported acceleration of wound healing and improvement in the post-wounding regeneration ability of muscle fibers using a 1 J/cm<sup>2</sup> ruby laser [<xref rid="b11-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. Since those early days, numerous <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> studies of LLLT in the context of regenerative medicine have demonstrated a wide variety of therapeutic effects, including reduction of pain, anti-inflammatory effects and wound healing. According to da Silva <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>. [<xref rid="b12-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>], the types of laser most frequently used for wound healing and tissue repair are helium neon (He-Ne) lasers and diode lasers, including gallium-aluminum-arsenic (Ga-Al-As), arsenic-gallium (As-Ga) and indium-gallium-aluminum-phosphide (In-Ga-Al-P) lasers.</p><p>One of the most distinctive features of LLLT relative to other modalities is that the effects are mediated not through induction of thermal effects, but rather, through a process, still not clearly defined, called “photobiostimulation”. Because this effect of LLLT apparently does not depend on coherence, it is therefore possible to achieve photobiostimulation using non-laser light-generating devices, such as inexpensive light-emitting diode (LED) technology [<xref rid="b13-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>–<xref rid="b17-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>].
