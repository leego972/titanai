</p></sec><sec id="sec4"><title>Dental applications</title><p>In the past, the use of OCT was only limited to the assessment of dental hard and soft tissue morphology; however, as knowledge related to the mechanics and use of OCT increased, it led to more widespread use of OCT for different objectives. Nowadays, OCT is being used in various clinical and research applications related to dentistry. The uses of OCT in the field of dentistry are discussed in detail below.</p><p>
<italic toggle="yes">Tissue imaging</italic>
</p><p>Many dental tissues such as enamel and dentin can be easily visualized utilizing OCT. <sup><xref rid="ref35" ref-type="bibr">35</xref></sup> The optical characteristics of enamel and dentin are different, so these structures can be distinguished from each other. Hariri
<italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> reported that OCT signals in dentin vary due to the presence of dentinal tubules and are also different from enamel. <sup><xref rid="ref36" ref-type="bibr">36</xref></sup> The direction of the OCT beam to the examined surface can affect the signal intensity. The sharp slope of the enamel surface may appear bright compared to the less steep surface. <sup><xref rid="ref37" ref-type="bibr">37</xref></sup> This technique has also been applied previously in various studies to detect the presence of enamel cracks. <sup><xref rid="ref37" ref-type="bibr">37</xref></sup>
<sup>,</sup>
<sup><xref rid="ref38" ref-type="bibr">38</xref></sup> Imai
<italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> visualized enamel cracks utilizing OCT and demonstrated their extension beyond dentin-enamel junction. <sup><xref rid="ref38" ref-type="bibr">38</xref></sup> To detect tooth fracture in the coronal region, swept-source OCT (SS-OCT) is a useful technique. <sup><xref rid="ref39" ref-type="bibr">39</xref></sup>
</p><p>
<italic toggle="yes">Detection of dental caries and demineralization of teeth</italic>
</p><p>There has been a paradigm shift in caries management recently, and this change indicates that caries should be managed conservatively. <sup><xref rid="ref40" ref-type="bibr">40</xref></sup> However, it requires detection of early, un-noticeable caries activity at the earliest for it to be truly conservative. Radiography is an unreliable method of detection of carious activity. Moreover, radiography cannot distinguish properly between active and arrested lesions. <sup><xref rid="ref41" ref-type="bibr">41</xref></sup> In an earlier study, it was reported that when physicians used OCT, they could detect tooth volumetric and thickness changes earlier compared with other conventional methods. <sup><xref rid="ref42" ref-type="bibr">42</xref></sup> Detecting initial changes in the tooth structure could be useful in hindering the progress of dental caries. In another study, the OCT technique effectively observed the depths of carious demineralization of dentin. <sup><xref rid="ref43" ref-type="bibr">43</xref></sup> Tooth demineralization can be differentiated from healthy tooth tissue by increasing light scattering in porous demineralized tooth tissue while performing OCT. <sup><xref rid="ref44" ref-type="bibr">44</xref></sup> SS-OCT possesses an advantage in cases where higher resolution and penetration depth are required for cavitated caries or deeper lesion detection. <sup><xref rid="ref45" ref-type="bibr">45</xref></sup> In enamel caries, the images appear brighter on grayscale OCT, and this could be the result of increased brightness due to the light reflection occurring between two homogenous structures with different refractive indices. <sup><xref rid="ref46" ref-type="bibr">46</xref></sup> The demineralized mineral crystals and water in the pores cause increased reflectivity resulting in characteristic brightness in the OCT image. <sup><xref rid="ref46" ref-type="bibr">46</xref></sup> SS-OCT can image dentin caries as a continuous bright area that extends from enamel into the dentin.
