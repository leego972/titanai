Using the disk diffusion, the antibacterial activity of EOXF on <italic toggle="yes">Rathayibacter toxicus</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Pyricularia oryzae</italic> was evaluated, and the MIC values were 25 and 12.5 µg/mL, respectively [<xref rid="B108-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>]. </p><p>Similar to the antibacterial potentials, the antifungal activities of <italic toggle="yes">X. strumarium</italic> were also deeply investigated. In the year of 2002, Kim et al. found an antifungal constituent from <italic toggle="yes">X. strumarium,</italic> which was named deacetylxanthumin. It can inhibit mycelial growth and zoospore germination of <italic toggle="yes">Phytophthora drechsleri</italic> with a MIC value of 12.5 µg/mL [<xref rid="B117-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">117</xref>]. In 2011, Yanar et al. used radial growth technique to test the antifungal activities of MEX against <italic toggle="yes">Phytophthora infestans</italic>, and the MEX showed the lowest MIC value of 2.0% <italic toggle="yes">w/v</italic> which was lower than the standard fungicide (Metalaxyl 4% + Mancuzeb 64%, MIC value was 2.5%, w/v) [<xref rid="B118-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">118</xref>]. Later, in 2015, Sharifi-Rad et al. investigated the antifungal ability of EOXL on <italic toggle="yes">Candida albicans</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Aspergillus niger</italic>, and the MIC values were 55.2 and 34.3 µg/mL, respectively [<xref rid="B114-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>]. In vitro, using the disk diffusion method, the EOXL exhibited strong inhibition against <italic toggle="yes">Pyricularia oryzae</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Fusarium oxysporum</italic> with MIC values of 12.5 and 50 µg/mL, respectively [<xref rid="B108-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>]. Furthermore, the EOXL showed remarkable growth inhibition of a wide spectrum of fungal strains, such as <italic toggle="yes">A. niger</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Aspergillus flavus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">F. oxysporum</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Fusarium solani</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Alternaria alternata</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Penicillium digitatum</italic> with both MIC and MBC (minimum bactericidal concentration) values of 8 µg/mL [<xref rid="B119-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">119</xref>]. </p></sec><sec id="sec5dot7-molecules-24-00359"><title>5.7. Antidiabetic Effect</title><p>In 1974, Kupiecki et al. found that the WEX (15 and 30 mg/kg, i.p.) exhibited potent hypoglycemic activity in normal rats in a dose-dependent manner [<xref rid="B120-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">120</xref>]. In 2000, the antidiabetic effect of caffeic acid isolated from <italic toggle="yes">X. strumarium</italic> was investigated on both streptozotocin-induced and insulin-resistant rat models. The results showed that caffeic acid (0.5–3.0 mg/kg, i.v.) can decrease the plasma glucose level via increasing the glucose utilization [<xref rid="B121-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">121</xref>]. In 2011, Narendiran et al. found that MEXS at the doses of 100 and 200 mg/kg (p.o., for 30 days) had remarkable diabetic activity in normal-glycemic and streptazocin induced hyperglycemic rats [<xref rid="B105-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">105</xref>]. A report in 2013 demonstrated that the methyl-3,5-di-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-caffeoylquinate showed strong ability to counteract diabetic complications via competitive inhibition of aldose reductase (AR) and galactitol formation in rat lenses [<xref rid="B47-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>]. In addition, it is reported that the CFMEXL exhibited notable inhibitory activity on α-glucosidase enzyme with the IC<sub>50</sub> value of 72 µg/mL [<xref rid="B122-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">122</xref>]. Similarly, another study found that MEX also had a strong α-glucosidase inhibitory effect with IC<sub>50</sub> value of 15.25 µg/mL [<xref rid="B28-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot8-molecules-24-00359"><title>5.8. Antilipidemic Effect</title><p>Recently, investigations into the antilipidemic effects of <italic toggle="yes">X. strumarium</italic> have been conducted. In 2011, the CEXR and EEXR were evaluated for anti-lipidemic activity in Triton WR-1339 induced hyperlipidemia in Swiss albino rats. The results showed that CEXR and EEXR (200 and 400 mg/kg p.o.) can significantly decrease the contents of plasma cholesterol, TG, LDL, and VLDL and increase plasma HDL levels, which was possiblely related to their significant antioxidant activity [<xref rid="B106-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>].
