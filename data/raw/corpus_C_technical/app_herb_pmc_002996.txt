The hepatoprotective activity has been referred to the presence of terpenes in good yield: 4-<italic toggle="yes">epi</italic>-abietol, ferruginol, hinokiol, sugiol, <italic toggle="yes">Z</italic>-communic acid and hinokiol-1-one, and 3-<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>-12-dihydroxyabieta-8,11,13-triene-1-one, in addition to 8-<italic toggle="yes">α</italic>-acetoxyelemol sesquiterpene [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR54">54</xref>]. A toxicity study of <italic toggle="yes">J. procera</italic> extract revealed the safety of the extract even at high doses with no acute or chronic toxicity appearance [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR48">48</xref>]. However, if eaten in large quantities, it can produce toxic effects because of its high calcium oxalate content [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR55">55</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Rumex nervosus</italic> belongs to the family Polygonaceae and is a large annual herb that can grow up to 1.5 m tall, its leaves are usually sagittate, the inflorescence is considerably branched, and it has a leafless panicle. It has a light brown nut, and its fruits are cordate-orbicular. It is widely distributed in Yemen, Saudi Arabia, Ethiopia, Somalia, Kenya, and Tanzania. Ithrib is a very common plant that is used by native people as a diuretic, antipyretic, and antirheumatic and to treat gonorrhea, leprosy, lung tuberculosis, and liver illness; as antihypertensive, antihemorrhoids, antiscabies, antiemetic, aphrodisiac, antitussive, and antirabies; and for dermatitis, antiacne, hypoglycemic, antiinfective, and headache. Decoction of the leaf or root powder produces a substance that is used as a vermifuge. Moreover, the leaves of <italic toggle="yes">R. nervosus</italic> are utilized to treat skin rashes and young leaves are roasted to decrease the acid content before being eaten. The material formed after the burning of the stem is mixed with egg yolk or butter and applied to burns [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR55">55</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR59">59</xref>].</p><p id="Par28">There are some reports on the anthelmintic [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR55">55</xref>], anti-inflammatory and antiviral [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR57">57</xref>], analgesic [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR58">58</xref>], diuretic and laxative [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR59">59</xref>], and antioxidant [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR60">60</xref>] effects of <italic toggle="yes">R. nervosus</italic>.</p><p id="Par29">It has been confirmed that <italic toggle="yes">R. nervosus</italic> extracts contain alkaloids, flavonoids, phenols, amino acids, furanocoumarins, and saponins by preliminary phytochemical screening [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR61">61</xref>].</p><p id="Par30">Moreover, the genus <italic toggle="yes">Rumex</italic> is characterized by the presence of anthraquinones, naphthalene-1,8-diols, flavonoids, and stilbenoids [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR62">62</xref>]. Many phenolic compounds have been isolated from the <italic toggle="yes">R. nervosus</italic> ethyl acetate fraction, including kaempferol, quercetin, gallic acid, hyperoside, quercetin 3-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-(6″-acetyl)-galactoside, hesperidin, quercetin, <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-hydroxy benzoic acid, catechol, and pyrogallol [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR63">63</xref>].
