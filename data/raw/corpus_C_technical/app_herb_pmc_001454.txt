Pharmacological Properties of <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> Genus Seeds</title><p>According to the existing literature, <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> genus seeds are full of many biologically active compounds which have been associated with a host of potential health benefits. Indeed, the mustard extract seeds have been linked to several biological properties such as antioxidant, antiproliferative, antimicrobial, antibacterial, antidiabetic, anti-inflammatory, and neuroprotective activities.</p><sec id="sec4dot1-molecules-27-06008"><title>4.1. Antioxidant Activity</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> species constitute a source of antioxidant compounds such as α-tocopherol, ascorbic acid, canolol, carotenoids (lutein and β-carotene), phenolic acids (gallic acid, caffeic acid, sinapic acid, ferulic acid, and 3,4-di-hydroxybenzoic acid), and flavonoids (rutin, quercetin, and kaempferol), which can protect the immune system by neutralizing free radicals [<xref rid="B6-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="B10-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>,<xref rid="B106-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>,<xref rid="B127-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">127</xref>]. Antioxidants, such as phenolic compounds, considered as protective agents, are involved in the adsorption and neutralization of reactive oxygen species (ROS), quenching singlet and triplet oxygen or decomposing peroxides. Many long-term illnesses, including cancer, atherosclerosis, aging, inflammation, and neurological illnesses such as Parkinson’s and Alzheimer’s disease, are directly linked to ROS [<xref rid="B128-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">128</xref>,<xref rid="B129-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">129</xref>]. Additionally, a positive correlation between the antioxidant capacity of <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> species and their profile and content of polyphenols, especially flavonoids, was established because phenolic compounds show higher antioxidant activity than vitamins and carotenoids [<xref rid="B6-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="B130-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">130</xref>]. Sinapic acid, 3,4-di-hydroxybenzoic acid, ferulic acid, and rutin identified from <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds were strongly associated with the antioxidant capability [<xref rid="B108-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>]. Quercetin, the major representative of the flavonol subclass, shows antioxidant potential by scavenging free radicals and chelating transition metal ions [<xref rid="B131-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">131</xref>].
