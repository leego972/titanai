The presence of α-gurjunene significantly enhances both the pharmacological and aromatic profiles of the essential oil. Studies in other species, such as <italic toggle="yes">Araucaria columnaris</italic> [<xref rid="B134-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">134</xref>] and <italic toggle="yes">Globba schomburgkii</italic> [<xref rid="B135-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">135</xref>], have shown that α-gurjunene exhibits strong antibacterial and antifungal properties, supporting its relevance in plant defense mechanisms.</p><p>It is noteworthy that α-gurjunene, together with other sesquiterpenes such as β-caryophyllene (<bold>57</bold>) and caryophyllene oxide (<bold>73</bold>), may act synergistically to enhance the antioxidant and antimicrobial properties of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> extracts. Their abundance in lipophilic fractions (n-hexane and dichloromethane) suggests their potential applicability in phytotherapeutic formulations and nutraceutical development [<xref rid="B134-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">134</xref>,<xref rid="B135-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">135</xref>].</p><p>Of particular interest is 6,10,14-trimethylpentadecan-2-one (<bold>79</bold>), a C18 isoprenoid ketone derived from the oxidative degradation of phytol (<bold>87</bold>). This compound may result from abiotic breakdown of (E)-phytenal or from the hydrolysis of photochemically generated phytyl-chain derivatives of chlorophyll [<xref rid="B136-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">136</xref>]. Its detection in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> indicates possible environmental influences on the plant’s metabolome and highlights how endogenous metabolism can interact with ecological conditions. However, as this compound can be a photodegradation product, its presence should be interpreted with caution in the context of GC-MS-based metabolite profiling.</p><p>In summary, these rare terpenoids and norisoprenoids highlight the importance of comprehensive chemical profiling in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticose</italic>. Among them, loliolide (<bold>76</bold>) stands out for its multifunctional activities, including antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects [<xref rid="B123-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">123</xref>,<xref rid="B137-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">137</xref>], induction of herbivore resistance [<xref rid="B124-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">124</xref>], stimulation of dermal papilla cell activity [<xref rid="B125-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">125</xref>], and neuroprotective effects [<xref rid="B126-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">126</xref>]. Similarly, alismol (<bold>71</bold>), α-bergamotene (<bold>48</bold>), α-gurjunene (<bold>66</bold>), and 6,10,14-trimethylpentadecan-2-one (<bold>79</bold>) contribute to the plant’s potential therapeutic spectrum, particularly its anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, antimicrobial, and ecological defense properties.
