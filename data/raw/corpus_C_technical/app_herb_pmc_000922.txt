# PMC: herbal medicine pharmacology mechanism review — Overview

ref-type="bibr">97</xref>,<xref rid="B98-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">98</xref>,<xref rid="B99-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>,<xref rid="B100-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">100</xref>,<xref rid="B101-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">101</xref>,<xref rid="B102-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>,<xref rid="B103-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">103</xref>,<xref rid="B104-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">104</xref>,<xref rid="B105-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">105</xref>,<xref rid="B106-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>]. A 2% gel based on <italic toggle="yes">Calendula officinalis</italic> flowers is also used as an individual treatment for inflammation of the oral mucosa and oropharyngeal mucositis caused by radiotherapy and chemotherapy [<xref rid="B62-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>,<xref rid="B77-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>,<xref rid="B107-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">107</xref>,<xref rid="B108-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>,<xref rid="B109-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">109</xref>,<xref rid="B110-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>,<xref rid="B111-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">111</xref>,<xref rid="B112-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>]. The combined extract of <italic toggle="yes">propolis</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Propolis</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Mangosteen Extract Complex</italic> (<italic toggle="yes">PMEC</italic>) was used to treat patients with advanced gingivitis and PD [<xref rid="B113-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>,<xref rid="B114-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>,<xref rid="B115-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">115</xref>,<xref rid="B116-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">116</xref>,<xref rid="B117-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">117</xref>,<xref rid="B118-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">118</xref>,<xref rid="B119-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">119</xref>,<xref rid="B120-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">120</xref>,<xref rid="B121-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">121</xref>,<xref rid="B122-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">122</xref>]. The results showed a strong therapeutic response with reduction of all biomarkers present in GCF (IL-1β: 57.9%, PGE2: 51.1%, MMP-8: 46.3%, and MMP-9: 54.4%), including macrophages, gingival fibroblasts, and neutrophils, resulting in improved osteogenesis, decreased osteoclastic activity, and reduced alveolar bone resorption. However, the use of PMEC found little short-term clinical improvement due to its inability to prevent direct invasion of the gingival tissue by periodontal pathogens. It reduced GCF biomarkers and improved osteogenesis, but <italic toggle="yes">PMEC</italic> had limited short-term clinical benefits due to persistent pathogen invasion [<xref rid="B123-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">123</xref>,<xref rid="B124-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">124</xref>,<xref rid="B125-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">125</xref>]. Mouthwashes with natural herbs (<italic toggle="yes">neem</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">tulsi, pudina, clove</italic> oil, <italic toggle="yes">camellia sinensis</italic> green tea, <italic toggle="yes">aloe vera</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">ajwain, triphala</italic> and many others) have been formulated to treat bleeding gums, bad breath, and mouth ulcers, and to prevent tooth decay. Herbal and essential oil mouthwashes have been used [<xref rid="B49-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>,<xref rid="B126-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">126</xref>,<xref rid="B127-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">127</xref>,<xref rid="B128-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">128</xref>,<xref rid="B129-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">129</xref>]. This study provides a novel perspective by systematically evaluating not only the anti-inflammatory properties of herbal formulations in periodontal therapy but also their antimicrobial and antioxidant effects, highlighting their potential as comprehensive adjuncts to conventional treatments.</p></sec><sec id="sec2-nutrients-17-00762"><title>2. Search Processing</title><p>The current systematic review followed the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses) guidelines and International Prospective Register of Systematic Review Registry procedures (full ID: 641944). The databases PubMed, Web of Science, and Scopus were examined from 18 December 2024 to 24 December 2024 to search articles of the last 10 years (<xref rid="app1-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="app">Table S1</xref>). The search strategy was created by combining terms relevant to the study’s purpose. The following Boolean keyword search was applied: (“herbal medicine” OR “plant extracts” OR “essential oils” OR “phytochemicals”) AND (“anti-inflammatory” OR “anti-inflammatory activity”) AND (“dentistry” OR “oral health” OR “periodontal therapy” OR “gingivitis” OR “periodontitis”)).</p></sec><sec id="sec2dot2-nutrients-17-00762"><title>2.2.
