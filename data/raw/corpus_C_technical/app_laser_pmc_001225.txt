Reprinted with permission.<xref rid="r205" ref-type="bibr"><sup>205</sup></xref></p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="JBO-022-121715-g029.jpg"/></fig><p>Multiple reference OCT (MROCT) systems are another low-cost TD-OCT technology, based on the optical system within a CD/DVD computer drive. This platform has the potential to enable a wide range of point-of-care and personal care applications.<xref rid="r207" ref-type="bibr"><sup>207</sup></xref> In MROCT, a depth scan is achieved by utilizing a partial mirror and actuator to generate a recirculating optical delay line, which extends the axial scan range. The overall cost and size of a typical MROCT system is anticipated to be similar to a CD/DVD unit, although these units are still in the early stages of development.<xref rid="r208" ref-type="bibr"><sup>208</sup></xref></p><p>Given the advantages of Fourier-based systems over TD-OCT, miniaturizing an FD-OCT system has great potential to be useful in clinical applications, especially in low-resource settings. Instead of utilizing standard spectrometers and CCD cameras, systems developed on a miniaturized silicon-based photonics platform can ideally utilize the cost-savings from mass production capabilities in the semiconductor industry. Recently, a commercial entity created an OCT system that retails for less than $10,000 USD.<xref rid="r209" ref-type="bibr"><sup>209</sup></xref> Although limited information is currently available on its design, this achievement was possible given the use of components manufactured at scale for other industries, paired with cost-reduction in other key OCT components such as a reflective parabolic mirror to collimate the light, a liquid lens to scan the beam over a sample, a “loop design” spectrometer, and a low-cost line-scan array detector used for scanning and reading bar-codes.</p><p>One research group created an integrated silicon-based arrayed-waveguide spectrometer,<xref rid="r210" ref-type="bibr"><sup>210</sup></xref> although it still required an external line-scan camera. Later, a “TriPleX” interferometer platform for SS-OCT was fabricated on a chip platform, which integrates a Michelson interferometer, sample and reference arms, and ports for external balanced photodiode detectors.<xref rid="r211" ref-type="bibr"><sup>211</sup></xref> Soon after, these two systems were combined to provide a combined high-quality silicon-based FD-OCT interferometer and spectrometer unit,<xref rid="r212" ref-type="bibr"><sup>212</sup></xref> which provided high-quality images of <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> human skin with an SNR of 74 dB. Another system for SS-OCT integrated the complete dual-balanced quadrature detection system on a chip,<xref rid="r213" ref-type="bibr"><sup>213</sup></xref> with a detection sensitivity of 94 dB.</p><p>Often, these systems are unexpectedly limited by their compact size, as useful working distances for biomedical applications frequently require an external reference arm to properly match optical delay.
