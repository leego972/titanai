CI = confidence interval.</p><p><sup>b </sup>PUSH = Pressure Ulcer Scale for Healing (ranged from 0 to 17, where 0 indicated a healed wound and 17 indicated an ulcer greater than 24 cm<sup>2 </sup>in surface area, with a heavy amount of exudate and the presence of necrotic tissue).</p><p><sup>c</sup>As determined by a dependent sample <italic toggle="yes">t </italic>test.</p><p><sup>d</sup>As determined by an independent 2-sample <italic toggle="yes">t </italic>test.</p><p><sup>e</sup>As determined by the Mann-Whitney <italic toggle="yes">U </italic>test.</p></table-wrap-foot></table-wrap></sec></sec><sec><title>Discussion</title><p>Ulcers in patients with leprosy can remain for several years after the initial infection is resolved and can result in large economic and social losses. Such losses were observed in this study, which was primarily composed of former patients that have lived with their ulcers for many years. The most important causal factor for neuropathic foot ulcers is the presence of a dynamic or static deformity leading to local areas of peak pressure on insensitive skin, which has been well illustrated by pressure studies [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B11">11</xref>]. This repetitive overload on specific areas of the sole could partially explain why plantar ulcers are deeper and smaller than leg and ankle ulcers. Almost half of the evaluated subjects in this study did not use any kind of adapted footwear, suggesting some negligence by the patients in the prevention of disabilities and self-care procedures. The free distribution of special footwear doesn't ensure its adequate utilization. Health care workers need to be constantly pushed to establish a patient continuum education process about self-care routines and to improve the techniques currently employed to encourage the use of preventive tools. Low adherence to such programs and self-care procedures is a concern of countries that still bear a significant leprosy burden.</p><p>In the present study, patient ulcers were predominantly chronic wounds, which could have interfered with LLLT success. Fibroblasts in chronic wounds have impaired responsiveness to growth hormone, which may be due to an increased number of senescent cells [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B12">12</xref>]. It was observed in neuropathic diabetic foot ulcers that wound duration negatively affected the chance of healing after 12 weeks of proper wound care [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B13">13</xref>]. A high prevalence of vasomotor reflex impairment attributed to autonomic nerve lesions has been observed in newly diagnosed patients with leprosy [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B14">14</xref>]. Lepromatous patients exhibit a tendency to develop chronic leg ulcers, which are partially caused by a single vascular disturbance during <italic toggle="yes">M. leprae </italic>infection and/or by peripheral neuropathy [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B15">15</xref>]. These alterations may explain the notable occurrence of chronic leg and ankle ulcers in this sample, especially in subjects who were hypertensive or over 40 years old. One study of 124 patients with leg wounds of different etiologies identified that 54% of them were hypertensive [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B16">16</xref>]. It is necessary to give special attention to the control of high blood pressure, as this comorbidity was common in our study and has the potential to negatively affect the wound healing process in patients with leprosy. The follow-up period of 12 weeks is longer than some previous studies [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B8">8</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B17">17</xref>-<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B19">19</xref>], but was chosen because it is enough time for the wound healing process to complete [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B20">20</xref>-<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B22">22</xref>].</p><p>Rest is a common recommendation as a strategy of self-care, but it was not adopted by the majority of patients.
