While these approaches may underperform classification models that take into account the complete image information or several features, many of these proposed features are very easy to interpret, and to link to the physical changes in the tissue, making them interesting for clinical practice.</p><p>Although a growing interest in the analysis of signal-carrying speckle has been observed over the last years, it is still a research line in an early stage. A considerable number of different methods have already been proposed, but there are only a few applications published in the literature. At the current stage, the analysis of OCT speckle is lacking on validation and information on its reproducibility <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic>. None of the reviewed studies validated the proposed methods on large (the largest dataset in the studies included had 65 subjects<xref rid="r4" ref-type="bibr"><sup>4</sup></xref><sup>,</sup><xref rid="r25" ref-type="bibr"><sup>25</sup></xref>) or multiple datasets. The speckle information is intrinsically related to the spatial arrangement and biomechanical properties of the scatterers in the sample. Scatterers can either be collagen fibers and fibroblasts when imaging the cornea, blood cells flowing through vessels, or just silica particles in a phantom image. What is considered a scatterer in a sample will depend on the relation between the characteristics of the imaging system, namely beam spot size, coherence gate<xref rid="r38" ref-type="bibr"><sup>38</sup></xref> and light source wavelength, and the particles size and concentration.<xref rid="r62" ref-type="bibr"><sup>62</sup></xref> This is particularly important for OCT imaging, given the variability existing between devices (790 to 1330 nm), as it can be observed in <xref rid="t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref>, <xref rid="sec6" ref-type="sec">Appendix A</xref>. Consequently, for the same sample and method, different quantitative values for speckle may be obtained depending on the OCT system used.</p><p>Another important aspect that hampers the development of speckle-based techniques is the limited access to raw data. Images collected from commercial OCT devices are often filtered to reduce the speckle or transformed to increase visualization contrast. It is of utmost importance that raw OCT images are used in speckle studies, otherwise the obtained results are tainted by the used device and preprocessing algorithm.<xref rid="r63" ref-type="bibr"><sup>63</sup></xref> If raw data are not available, the information of the applied image processing algorithms should be provided to understand how the speckle has been processed and hence, comprehend its physical meaning in a biomedical application.</p><p>Despite its early stage, research on methods to study the signal-carrying speckle has been a step forward on the comprehension of the physical meaning of the information retrieved from OCT imaging. Speckle analysis provides information on the size and distribution of the scatters that has not been considered in a clinical practice yet. Such advancements are also particularly interesting for other research lines, such as OCT elastography, adaptive optics imaging, or machine learning applications. For example, recent developments on machine learning, namely on convolutional neural networks, have reported outperforming results in OCT image analysis in comparison to conventional image processing.<xref rid="r64" ref-type="bibr"><sup>64</sup></xref><named-content content-type="online"><xref rid="r65" ref-type="bibr"/></named-content><named-content content-type="print"><sup>–</sup></named-content><xref rid="r66" ref-type="bibr"><sup>66</sup></xref> However, deep learning approaches still lack on interpretability and roughly remain a black box, despite the recent efforts to address this limitation.<xref rid="r67" ref-type="bibr"><sup>67</sup></xref> Therefore, future research may focus on integrating physics and learning based approaches, to combine their strengths.</p></sec><sec id="sec5"><label>5</label><title>Conclusion</title><p>This paper presents an overview of the current state of the art in OCT signal-carrying speckle analysis in biomedical applications.
