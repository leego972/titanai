Wedelia chinensis </title><p><italic toggle="yes">Wedelia chinensis</italic> (Chinese: Peng qi ju), indigenous to India, South-East Asia, and China, is one of the important anticancer plants belonging to family Asteraceae which is rich in many important secondary metabolites like phenol, flavonoids, and tannin [<xref rid="B152-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">152</xref>]. </p><p>The essential oils of <italic toggle="yes">W. chinensis</italic> give a positive effect on lung cancer during the in vitro study. The GC-MS analysis recorded the presence of two important compounds carvacrol and trans-caryophyllene. High anti-scavenging activities were found at different levels of dose. The study of B16F-10 melanoma metastatic cell line showed that the concentrations of some important antioxidant enzymes (including catalase, superoxide dismutase, and glutathione peroxidase) increased many folds in the treatment groups. Similarly, the amount of glutathione also increased while the concentrations of other compounds such as lipid peroxidation and nitric oxide were decreased. The histopathology studies further verified that these essential oils show negative effects on cancer development [<xref rid="B153-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">153</xref>]. </p></sec></sec><sec id="sec4-biomolecules-10-00047"><title>4. In Vivo Studies of Anticancer Herbal Medicine: An Overview</title><p>The herbal medicines are tested both in vitro and in vivo. The anticancer activities of the various medicinal plants have been tested in vivo using different animal models (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="biomolecules-10-00047-f003">Figure 3</xref>). There are many studies available on in vivo experiments of the many different anticancer plants in mice models. For instance, dihydroartemisinin was reported to inhibit tumor tissue, increase the level of interferon-gamma (IFN-γ), and decrease interleukin 4 (IL-4) in tumor-bearing mice [<xref rid="B154-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">154</xref>]. Similarly, artesunate, a derivative of artemisinin is also reported to be a promising drug against angiogenic Kaposi′s sarcoma [<xref rid="B155-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">155</xref>], growth inhibition of A549 and H1299 lung tumors by 100 mg/kg dose [<xref rid="B156-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">156</xref>], the suppression of human prostate cancer xenograft [<xref rid="B157-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">157</xref>] and the inhibition of leukemia growth in mice [<xref rid="B158-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">158</xref>]. </p><p>Irradiation of C57BL/6 mice combined with a dose of 2 mg/kg twice a week was proved effective against lung carcinoma [<xref rid="B159-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">159</xref>]. The effectiveness of berberine was enhanced when it was used in combination with other agents. Coptisine, another alkaloid of <italic toggle="yes">Coptidis rhizoma</italic> is proved to have anticancer effects when used in concentrations of 150 mg/kg against BALB/c nude mice by suppressing tumor growth and reducing cancer metastasis.
