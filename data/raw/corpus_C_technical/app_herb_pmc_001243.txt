These showed enhanced antioxidant potential and anti-microbial activities, similar to the results obtained with silver nanoparticles [<xref rid="B95-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>]. Zinc oxide (ZnO) nanoparticles are widely used in various medical applications such as drug delivery and bio-imaging [<xref rid="B101-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">101</xref>] and are known to exhibit anti-microbial activities [<xref rid="B102-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>]. </p><p>The anti-microbial properties of ZnO nanoparticles synthesized with the aid of <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> leaf extracts were tested against clinical isolates of <italic toggle="yes">Candida</italic> species (<italic toggle="yes">Candida albicans</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Candida glabrata</italic>) and <italic toggle="yes">Cryptococcus neoformans</italic> [<xref rid="B97-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>]. Results showed strong fungicidal activity, better than the leaf extract and the standard antibiotic amphotericin B. The prepared ZnO nanoparticles also exhibited a dose-dependent cytotoxic effect against HeLa cancer cells, higher than the <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> leaf extract [<xref rid="B97-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>]. </p></sec></sec></sec><sec id="sec6-molecules-27-04240"><title>6. Toxicology Studies</title><p>Although natural compounds from plants sources have successfully led to the discovery of important drugs that are still currently being used to treat various diseases, many herbal products are poorly tested or monitored, if tested at all, for their efficacy and safety. In fact, herbs as medicinal drugs are not as safe as claimed and certain medicinal herbs could exhibit adverse effects. Consequently, it is important that toxicology studies are carried out and adequate information is provided to consumers on the risks associated with the application of these products [<xref rid="B103-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">103</xref>,<xref rid="B104-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">104</xref>,<xref rid="B105-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">105</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> was not found to exhibit any behavioral changes or mortality in mice administered with aqueous-methanol <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> leaf extract at oral doses of 3 and 5 g/kg, and up to 10 g/kg in a 24-h study, suggesting that <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> is safe to use, and making it an attractive plant for natural drug discovery approaches [<xref rid="B10-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>]. In addition, toxicity analysis of other <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus</italic> species concluded that they were safe [<xref rid="B106-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>,<xref rid="B107-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">107</xref>]. Nonetheless, although toxicity studies indicate that <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> extracts are nontoxic, there is still a need for further toxicology screening and particularly for long-term chronic toxicity studies to validate their safe usage.</p></sec><sec sec-type="conclusions" id="sec7-molecules-27-04240"><title>7. Conclusions</title><p>Today more than ever, the use of natural compounds from plant sources as potential drug leads is gaining more interest, both in research and the pharmaceutical industry. Phytochemical and pharmacological studies on <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> have shown so far that it can be a rich source of new compounds with wide applications in the pharmaceutical and food industries (<xref rid="molecules-27-04240-f003" ref-type="fig">Figure 3</xref>).
