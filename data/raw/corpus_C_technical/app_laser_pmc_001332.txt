The AUC of 0.95 confirms excellent discriminative ability, indicating that in most threshold settings, the fusion model achieves higher true positive rates for the same false positive rates when compared to single-modality models.</p><p>The ROC curve for the fusion approach lies above those of the fundus-only and OCT-only models across nearly the entire range, reflecting superior sensitivity–specificity trade-offs. The high AUC and the steep initial rise of the curve (indicating that high sensitivity can be obtained with only a small increase in false positive rate) demonstrate the benefit of combining complementary visual information from fundus photographs and OCT scans. In practical terms, this means the fusion model can more reliably distinguish glaucoma from normal eyes.</p><p>To assess convergence and over-fitting control, we monitor training and validation loss and accuracy over 50 epochs for each model. <xref rid="sensors-25-04337-f012" ref-type="fig">Figure 12</xref>, <xref rid="sensors-25-04337-f013" ref-type="fig">Figure 13</xref> and <xref rid="sensors-25-04337-f014" ref-type="fig">Figure 14</xref> illustrate these curves. The fusion model exhibits the fastest convergence and highest validation accuracy, reflecting its superior capacity to learn joint representations from both fundus photographs and OCT B-scans.</p><p>These results demonstrate that integrating fundus and OCT data yields a superior classifier, which is consistent with the hypothesis that the two modalities provide complementary information (structural appearance vs. cross-sectional measurements). <xref rid="sensors-25-04337-t004" ref-type="table">Table 4</xref> shows the evaluation metrics across each model.
