Other Polyphenols</title><p>Other polyphenols such as cylindol A (<bold>250</bold>) and B (<bold>251</bold>), imperanene (<bold>254</bold>) and graminone A (<bold>252</bold>) and B (<bold>253</bold>) (<xref ref-type="app" rid="app1-ijms-20-01067">Table S1</xref> and <xref ref-type="fig" rid="ijms-20-01067-f008">Figure 8</xref>) were also reported. In addition, coumarins (socopoletin, umbelliferone (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="ijms-20-01067-f008">Figure 8</xref>) and 4-methoxy-5-methylcoumarin-7-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-β-<sc>d</sc>-glucopyranoside) were isolated from <italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">I. cylindrica</italic> (<xref ref-type="app" rid="app1-ijms-20-01067">Table S1</xref>). Socopoletin (<bold>255</bold>) has the ability to rescue impaired cholinergic functions (2 mg/kg sc, in rats) [<xref rid="B146-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">146</xref>] while, umbelliferone (<bold>256</bold>), shows anticarcinogenic activities in colorectal cancer (intragastric injection at a daily dose of 30 mg/kg body weight) [<xref rid="B147-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">147</xref>]. Additionally, it also shows great antinociceptive and anti-inflammatory activities due to the inhibition of peripherical and central acting pain mediators (5 and 10 mg/kg) [<xref rid="B148-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">148</xref>].</p><p>Concluding, it is clear that these classes of compounds were not very explored in halophytic grasses with their presence only reported in 2% of the total diversity of the <italic toggle="yes">taxa</italic>. As the role of these compounds in the prevention of degenerative and cardiovascular diseases is evident, more studies on this aspect is recommendable [<xref rid="B149-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">149</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec2dot8-ijms-20-01067"><title>2.8. Stilbenoids and Derivatives</title><p>According to the literature, 13 stilbenoids and derivatives (<bold>272</bold>–<bold>284</bold>) were described solely in the alcohol extract of C. dactylon [<xref rid="B150-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">150</xref>]. These compounds are known for a wide spectrum of biological activities such as neuroprotection, cancer prevention, anti-obesity, anti-platelet aggregation, depigmentation, anti-diabetes and anti-atherosclerosis [<xref rid="B151-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">151</xref>]. For instance, pallidol (<bold>278</bold>) has the ability to inhibit both cell growth in human cancer cells (HCT-116, HT29 and Caco-2 cell lines) [<xref rid="B152-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">152</xref>] and the activity of protein kinase C [<xref rid="B153-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">153</xref>], while parthenostilbenin A (<bold>279</bold>) and B (<bold>280</bold>) are able to inhibit lipid peroxidation (IC<sub>50</sub> = 20.35 and 18.68, respectively) in rat liver homogenate [<xref rid="B154-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">154</xref>]. Stilbenoids are normally found in tea, berries and wine at high concentrations [<xref rid="B150-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">150</xref>] and their beneficial effects have been associated with antioxidant activity and thus, they have been considered of high nutraceutical value [<xref rid="B155-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">155</xref>]. Therefore, the presence of these compounds in the extracts of C. dactylon is considerably interesting. </p></sec><sec id="sec2dot9-ijms-20-01067"><title>2.9. Miscellaneous Compounds</title><p>In addition to the classes discussed above, other compounds belonging to miscellaneous chemical families were reported in halophytes from the Poaceae. Among these, two cyanogenic glucosides were described (<xref ref-type="app" rid="app1-ijms-20-01067">Table S1</xref>). These compounds are toxic due to hydrogen cyanide release upon enzymatic breakdown and are present in more than 2650 plant species, including edible plants. Hydrogen cyanide derived from cyanogenic glucosides can lead to cell death through cytochrome oxidase blocking and ATP production arrestment. Symptoms of cyanogen poisoning include vomiting, nausea, dizziness, weakness, and occasionally death [<xref rid="B156-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">156</xref>]. Two of these compounds were reported in <italic toggle="yes">C. ciliaris</italic> [<xref rid="B58-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>] (<xref ref-type="app" rid="app1-ijms-20-01067">Table S1</xref>); nonetheless, no information is available concerning the quantities present in the plant and therefore, no assumption on edible safety can be made.</p></sec></sec><sec id="sec3-ijms-20-01067"><title>3.
