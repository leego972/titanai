Phytomedicines are derived from centuries-old healing traditions from the natural resources surrounding them. Moreover, these medicines often emphasise curing disease beyond the physical symptoms alone; they maintain a patient’s overall psychological and ethical balance as integral determinants of health, making phytomedicines beneficial amongst community members who value holistic recovery [<xref rid="B6-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>]. Many such preparations are extracted directly from plants; some can be modified by structural transformation. Given the natural diversity of plants globally, there are many unidentified phytochemicals whose biological actions are yet to be discovered [<xref rid="B7-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>]</p><p>In recent years, traditional Chinese and African medicines have been used against diseases, including COVID-19. For instance, the National Administration of Traditional Chinese Medicine organised a study to identify potential treatments against COVID-19, and the Lung Cleansing and Detoxifying Decoction (LCDD) was widely used and studied through clinical trials. LCDD contains 21 ingredients, including <italic toggle="yes">Ephedra sinica</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Atractylodes macrocephala</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Scutellaria baicalensis</italic>, which likely counteract COVID-19 through synergistic activities [<xref rid="B8-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>]. The first trial showed that LCDD was effective on 90% of the 214 COVID-19 patients. Further trials were carried out on a more extensive trial group with 1262 patients, including 57 with severe symptoms. The results showed that 99.28% of the patients recovered, and none developed severe symptoms during the treatment [<xref rid="B8-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>]. In Africa, an elixir based on <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia annua</italic> extract, known as “covid-organics”, was used as a potential cure for COVID-19, and studies are still ongoing [<xref rid="B9-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. Plant-based antimalarials like artemisinin from <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia</italic> spp. have also been tested against the SARS-CoV-2 virus [<xref rid="B10-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">Panax ginseng</italic> has been widely used as a healing plant in Asian traditional medicine. This species contains many natural products, including ginsenosides, that exert qualities such as improving immune health, reducing inflammation, and having anticancer effects [<xref rid="B11-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. Similarly, turmeric, or <italic toggle="yes">Curcuma longa</italic>, a plant in the ginger family <italic toggle="yes">Zingiberaceae</italic>, which is prominent in Ayurvedic culture, contains curcumin and has wound-healing abilities and antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activities [<xref rid="B12-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>]. Moreover, <italic toggle="yes">Echinacea</italic> from North America is effective against respiratory disorders [<xref rid="B13-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Echinacea</italic> possesses phytochemicals such as phenolics, including caftaric acid, chicoric acid, cynarin, chlorogenic acid, and echinacoside. Volatile terpenes, such as germacrene D and polyacetylene, are also present and possess antimicrobial and antioxidant activities. Ascorbic acid is also present, and it aids in immune augmentation. The polysaccharides and glycoproteins in the plant, including arabinogalactans, inulin, and heteroxylans, possess immunostimulatory and anti-inflammatory activities that aid in immune modulation, thus reducing inflammation often experienced during illness episodes [<xref rid="B14-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>,<xref rid="B15-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>,<xref rid="B16-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>].</p><p>Plant-derived compounds have also been used to treat diarrhoea, a major global health issue. Several scientific studies have found that herbal extracts act as antisecretory agents, have antiperistaltic effects, and antimicrobial and antispasmodic properties. Apigenin and friedelin have been identified as antidiarrhoeal agents because of their antisecretory and antimotility activity [<xref rid="B17-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">Arctostaphylos uva-ursi</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Vaccinium macrocarpon</italic> have been used to treat urinary tract infections, and the essential oils from <italic toggle="yes">Allium sativum</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Melaleuca alternifolia</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Melissa officinalis</italic> have been extensively used to treat respiratory, GI, urinary, and skin infections [<xref rid="B18-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>].</p><p>The examples above show that plant-derived phytochemicals may play a role in traditional medicine, offering potential remedies for various health conditions.
