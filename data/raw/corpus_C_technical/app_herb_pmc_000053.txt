Analytical Methods</title><p>Besides the major constituents, secondary metabolites are usually present in a small amount. That’s why, high sensitivity and high mass accuracy is required to produce reliable data. Mostly, data from IR, UV, MS and X-ray analysis was evaluated further for <sup>1</sup>H- and <sup>13</sup>C-NMR spectral analysis. These procedures for identification of unknown entity, require high purity as well as high concentration of extracted compounds.</p><p>Today, mass spectrometry is the most specific and versatile method of detection in liquid chromatography, especially perfect for the analysis of some multiple components pharmaceutical and herbal products [<xref rid="B93-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">93</xref>,<xref rid="B94-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>]. Liquid chromatography with mass spectrometry (LC-MS) is recognized as a most suitable and powerful tool for identification as well as quantification of various herbal product and their constituents [<xref rid="B95-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>,<xref rid="B96-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>,<xref rid="B97-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>]. From the plant kingdom, quassinoids are bitter constituents found exclusively in various species of the Simarouboidaea (a subfamily of the Simaroubaceae) and are biogenetically degraded triterpenes displaying a wide range of physiological properties <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> and/or <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> [<xref rid="B98-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">98</xref>,<xref rid="B99-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>]. Numerous research reports are available on liquid chromatography methods for the analysis of quassinoid <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> bio-constituents, using photodiode array or fluorescence and UV detection. However, none of these methods are sensitive enough to detect nonchromophoric bioactive constituents, such as eurycomanol present in <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> [<xref rid="B57-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>,<xref rid="B58-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>,<xref rid="B100-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">100</xref>], so mass spectrometry is the best option for analysis of all constituents and secondary metabolites from <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic>. Chua <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, used a number of three liquid chromatography mass spectrometry hybrid systems (QTof, QTrap and TripleTof), to scan for small metabolites and also to detect the targeted metabolites, such as alkaloids, quassinoids, triterpene and biphenylneolignans from <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> extracts [<xref rid="B47-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>]. Teh <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, developed and optimized a LC-MS method using ESI in a positive ion mode for bioactive compounds simultaneous determination, from <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> [<xref rid="B101-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">101</xref>].
