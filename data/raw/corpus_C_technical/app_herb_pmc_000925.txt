Camellia sinensis-Based Interventions</title><p>T. S. Hrishi et al. (2016) explored the adjunctive role of <italic toggle="yes">Camellia sinensis</italic>-based dentifrice in treating mild-to-moderate PD. Their randomized clinical trial with 30 patients compared outcomes between a test group using <italic toggle="yes">Camellia sinensis</italic> dentifrice and a control group using commercial toothpaste [<xref rid="B147-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">147</xref>]. The clinical parameters GI, PI, probing PoD, and CAL—along with antioxidant markers in GCF, were evaluated over four weeks. While both groups improved due to SRP, the <italic toggle="yes">Camellia sinensis</italic> group showed significantly greater reductions in gingival inflammation and enhanced antioxidant activity. This suggests the potential of <italic toggle="yes">Camellia sinensis</italic> as a beneficial adjunct, though larger-scale studies are warranted [<xref rid="B147-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">147</xref>,<xref rid="B148-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">148</xref>,<xref rid="B149-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">149</xref>,<xref rid="B150-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">150</xref>,<xref rid="B151-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">151</xref>,<xref rid="B152-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">152</xref>]. Similarly, Kanyawat Rattanasuwan et al. (2016) investigated a locally delivered <italic toggle="yes">Camellia sinensis</italic> gel in chronic PD. Forty-two participants underwent SRP, followed by application of either <italic toggle="yes">Camellia sinensis</italic> gel or placebo. While both groups showed significant clinical improvements, the <italic toggle="yes">Camellia sinensis</italic> gel demonstrated superior anti-inflammatory effects at three months, though its impact on long-term periodontal outcomes was inconclusive. The study emphasized the need for larger trials to determine optimal protocols and long-term efficacy [<xref rid="B153-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">153</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec4dot2-nutrients-17-00762"><title>4.2. Polyherbal Mouthwashes</title><p>Saman Mahyari et al. (2016) evaluated a polyherbal mouthwash containing extracts of <italic toggle="yes">Zingiber</italic> officinale, <italic toggle="yes">Rosmarinus</italic> officinalis, and <italic toggle="yes">Calendula officinalis</italic> in gingivitis management [<xref rid="B68-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>]. Sixty participants were divided into polyherbal, CHX, and placebo groups, with assessments over 14 days. Both the polyherbal and CHX mouthwashes significantly improved clinical indices, demonstrating comparable efficacy. Polyherbal formulation had no adverse effects, suggesting it as a viable alternative to synthetic products [<xref rid="B51-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>,<xref rid="B68-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>,<xref rid="B154-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">154</xref>,<xref rid="B155-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">155</xref>,<xref rid="B156-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">156</xref>]. In 2019, Scilla Sparabombe et al. examined another polyherbal mouthwash containing <italic toggle="yes">propolis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">plantago lanceolata, salvia officinalis</italic> and essential oils. Over three months, the mouthwash significantly reduced inflammation (FMBS) and plaque accumulation (FMPS), particularly in moderate-to-severe PD cases, without adverse effects. The findings underscore its potential as a safe and effective alternative to conventional CHX mouthwash [<xref rid="B157-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">157</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec4dot3-nutrients-17-00762"><title>4.3. Herbal Toothpastes</title><p>He (2019) conducted a double-blind trial assessing toothpaste containing <italic toggle="yes">Rhizoma Chuanxiong</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Rhizoma Imperatae</italic> extracts. Over 12 weeks, 120 participants showed significant reductions in GI, BI, and BOP in the test group compared to controls. The herbal toothpaste demonstrated no adverse effects, highlighting its efficacy and safety for managing gingivitis [<xref rid="B158-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">158</xref>]. Li Cheng et al. (2019) tested <italic toggle="yes">Pudilan</italic> extract-containing toothpaste, composed of flavonoids, alkaloids, and organic acids, over a 12-week period.
