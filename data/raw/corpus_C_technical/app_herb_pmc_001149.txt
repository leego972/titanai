After labelling cancer cells treated separately with ChlE and ChlE-NS at IC<sub>50</sub> for 48 h with AO/EBr, visible alterations were discovered. These changes included cell shrinkage, changed cell shape, and membrane blebbing, all of which are hallmarks of apoptotic cell death and are depicted in Fig. <xref rid="Fig3" ref-type="fig">3</xref>. AO stained all dead and living cells, whereas cells stained orange to red with EB when they lost their membrane integrity due to chromatin condensation and nuclear fragmentation [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>], untreated cells (control) fluoresced green. According to the photos (Fig. <xref rid="Fig3" ref-type="fig">3</xref>), the cytotoxic effects of ChlE-NS were stronger than those of ChlE. These morphological alterations were detected in cancer cells when various nanosuspension formulations were used [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR72">72</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR75">75</xref>]. A DNA fragmentation assay was utilized to verify that cancer cells treated with <italic toggle="yes">D. Simplex</italic> ChlE and its nanosuspension suffered apoptosis as a direct consequence of DNA damage. Treatment with ChlE and ChlE-NS resulted in chromosomal DNA fragmentation in the MCF-7, HEPG-2, and HCT-116 cell lines. The second lane, which was treated with control cells, showed little fragmentation, whereas the third lane, which was treated with <italic toggle="yes">D. Simplex</italic> ChlE, showed considerable DNA fragmentation (Fig. <xref rid="Fig4" ref-type="fig">4</xref>). In this work, we followed the level of caspase-3 to gain insight into the molecular pathways of apoptosis, and the levels of caspase-3 were measured in MCF-7, HEPG2, and HCT-116 cells treated with <italic toggle="yes">D. simplex</italic> ChlE and ChlE-NS. Figure <xref rid="Fig5" ref-type="fig">5</xref> demonstrates a substantial rise in the caspase-3 level in MCF-7 and HEPG-2 cells and a lower level in the case of HCT-116 cells relative to the control group (untreated cancer cells).
