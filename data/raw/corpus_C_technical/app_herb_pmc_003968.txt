RRE Eliminates ROS and Rescues Cell Metabolic Activity Caused by Dexamethasone</title><p>Glucocorticoid-related stress has been shown to induce oxidative damages in cells [<xref rid="B27" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>]. Therefore, in a next step, we aimed to assess whether RRE exerted protective effects in stress condition. Dexamethasone (Dexa), a synthetic agonist of the glucocorticoid receptor which mimics the effect of cortisol, was used to induce stress. The capacity of RRE to counteract the detrimental effects of glucocorticoid stress was examined in two treatment strategies: a preventive treatment in which SH-SY5Y cells where first treated with RRE for 24 h before to be exposed to stress (Dexa 100 <italic toggle="yes">μ</italic>M for 24 h), and a curative treatment in which SH-SY5Y cells were treated with Dexa 3 h before treating with RRE for an additional 24 h.</p><p>Dexa 100 <italic toggle="yes">μ</italic>M caused a significant elevation in cytosolic ROS, mitochondrial ROS, and mitochondrial superoxide anion levels, respectively, in SH-SY5Y cells (Figures <xref rid="fig5" ref-type="fig">5(a)</xref>–<xref rid="fig5" ref-type="fig">5(C)</xref> and <xref rid="fig5" ref-type="fig">5(e)</xref>–<xref rid="fig5" ref-type="fig">5(g)</xref>). A pretreatment with RRE, especially at 5 ng/ml concentration, was able to reduce the Dexa-induced cytosolic, mitochondrial, and mitochondrial superoxide ROS levels by up to 16.9%, 22.0%, and 22.1%, respectively (Figures <xref rid="fig5" ref-type="fig">5(a)</xref>–<xref rid="fig5" ref-type="fig">5(c)</xref>). The most effective concentrations of the extract that significantly decreased ROS levels to those of the control condition were 5 and 10 ng/ml for cytosolic ROS (<xref rid="fig5" ref-type="fig">Figure 5(a)</xref>), 5 ng/ml for mitochondrial ROS (<xref rid="fig5" ref-type="fig">Figure 5(b)</xref>), and 10 ng/ml for mitochondrial superoxide anion (<xref rid="fig5" ref-type="fig">Figure 5(c)</xref>). Pretreatment with the same RRE concentrations was able to rescue the decrease in cellular metabolic activity (an indicator of cell viability) caused by 400 <italic toggle="yes">μ</italic>M Dexa (<xref rid="fig5" ref-type="fig">Figure 5(d)</xref>). Namely, a pretreatment with 1, 5, and 10 ng/ml of the RRE rescued the cell viability by up to 12.2%, almost a complete rescue. Of note, these effects are not unique to the SH-SY5Y cells. Indeed, a similar effect was observed in the HT22 cells in which Dexa 5 <italic toggle="yes">μ</italic>M treatment caused an elevation of the same type of ROS (Suppl. Figures <xref rid="supplementary-material-1" ref-type="sec">1</xref>(a)–<xref rid="supplementary-material-1" ref-type="sec">1</xref>(c)). A pretreatment with RRE, especially the 5 ng/ml and 10 ng/ml concentrations, significantly reduced cytosolic ROS, mitochondrial ROS, and mitochondrial superoxide anion. In line with data obtained in SH-SY5Y cells, Dexa 100 <italic toggle="yes">μ</italic>M caused a decrease in cellular metabolic activity compared to the control in HT22 cells (Suppl. Figure <xref rid="supplementary-material-1" ref-type="sec">1</xref>(d)). Treatment with 5 and 10 ng/ml of the RRE rescued cell metabolic activity up to 16.1%.</p><p>Strikingly, the protective effects of RRE against Dexa-induced oxidative damages were also observed with a curative treatment (Figures <xref rid="fig4" ref-type="fig">4(e)</xref>–<xref rid="fig4" ref-type="fig">4(h)</xref>). Indeed, in the presence of 100 <italic toggle="yes">μ</italic>M Dexa, RRE 5 ng/ml decreased the cytosolic ROS to the level of the untreated SH-SY5Y cells (<xref rid="fig5" ref-type="fig">Figure 5(e)</xref>). Moreover, RRE normalized mitochondrial ROS and mitochondrial superoxide anion levels in the presence of Dexa, with 5 ng/ml being the most effective concentrations decreasing mitochondrial ROS by 20.5% and mitochondrial superoxide anions by 20.3% compared to cells treated with Dexa alone (Figures <xref rid="fig5" ref-type="fig">5(f)</xref> and <xref rid="fig5" ref-type="fig">5(g)</xref>). In line, RRE 5 ng/ml normalized the cellular metabolic activity of Dexa-treated cells to the level of the control cells (<xref rid="fig5" ref-type="fig">Figure 5(h)</xref>).</p><p>Taken together, these data showed that RRE rescued cell metabolic activity and decreased ROS levels under corticosteroid stress. Again, the effects of RRE presented a U-shape (for ROS) and inverted U-shape (for cell metabolic activity), with 5 and 10 ng/ml being the two most efficient extract concentrations.</p></sec><sec id="sec3.6"><title>3.6.
