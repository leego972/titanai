The results suggested that the neuroprotective effect of the 75% <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> ethanol extract is attributable to a substance(s) in the ethanol-soluble fraction (<xref rid="bib77" ref-type="bibr">Li et al., 2005</xref>).</p></sec><sec id="s0075"><label>5.1.3</label><title>Antioxidant effect</title><p id="p0125">Antioxidant and free radical scavenging can also be used to treat AD and PD. A recent study evaluated the protective effect of TSG against <sc>d</sc>-galactose-induced aging in mice (42, 84 and 168 mg/kg, p.o., for 8 weeks). The results suggested that TSG is able to improve the memory ability and regulated the body weight of <sc>d</sc>-galactose induced aging mice through reducing the levels of ROS, NO and IGF-1 and increasing the levels of superoxide dismutase (SOD), Ca2+ and Klotho protein in the serum (<xref rid="bib224" ref-type="bibr">Zhou et al., 2013</xref>). Another study also proved that TSG (20 and 40 mg/kg/day, p.o., for 42 days) can increase SOD and glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px) activities in the serum and organs and decrease the content of 2-thiobarbituric acid-reactive substances (TBARS) in a <sc>d</sc>-galactose-induced mouse model of dementia (<xref rid="bib110" ref-type="bibr">Lv et al., 2007</xref>). However, TSG also has anti-melanogenic activity at doses ranging from 60 μM to 240 μM through a mechanism that is likely mediated through the non-competitive inhibition of tyrosinase, the down-regulation of the expression of melanogenic proteins, and a reduction of tyrosinase/tyrosinase-related protein 1 complex formation (<xref rid="bib27" ref-type="bibr">Cheung et al., 2014</xref>).</p><p id="p0130">In addition, extracts of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> also have antioxidant capacities. <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> extract (70% ethanol extract, 0.32 g crude drugs/kg/day, p.o., 5 days/week, for 4 weeks) has anti-aging effects in a mouse model of <sc>d</sc>-galactose-induced subacute aging. The mechanism for this effect may be mediated by decreases in the levels of LPF in the brain and kidney and enhancements in the activities of Na<sup>+</sup>/K<sup>+</sup>-ATPase in the heart and SOD in the liver (<xref rid="bib131" ref-type="bibr">Song et al., 2003</xref>). Another study showed that mice exhibit a better active shuttle avoidance response, fewer vacuoles, less lipofuscin in the hippocampus, and lower MDA concentrations in the brain after being fed <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> extracts (2.5 g/kg/day, p.o., for 18 weeks; 50% ethanol, 95% ethanol, or aqueous). Consequently, these effects promote improved learning and memory abilities (<xref rid="bib6" ref-type="bibr">Chan et al., 2002</xref>), which may be due to the antioxidant phytochemicals of the extracts (<xref rid="bib7" ref-type="bibr">Chan et al., 2003</xref>).</p></sec><sec id="s0080"><label>5.1.4</label><title>Enhanced cognition</title><p id="p0135">TSG can treat AD by enhancing learning and memory abilities. A previous study has shown that TSG (1 and 5 μM) promotes an ERK-dependent differentiation of PC12 cells, directly enhances ERK1/2 activation and intracellular calcium level in hippocampal neurons. TSG-mediated enhancement of ERK1/2 phosphorylation is involved in the facilitation of hippocampal long-term potentiation (LTP) by TSG, the processes may be involved in the effect of TSG on hippocampal LTP including the ERK–CREB pathway and modulation of the response to the NMDA receptor by calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II (CaMKII) activity. This effect may contribute to the enhancement of learning and memory observed in animal models (<xref rid="bib156" ref-type="bibr">Wang et al., 2011a</xref>). This effect was verified in AD-like APP transgenic mice of different ages treated with TSG (120 and 240 μmol/kg/day, p.o., for 4, 10 and 16 months). The results showed that TSG can significantly improve the learning-memory abilities (<xref rid="bib206" ref-type="bibr">Zhang et al., 2006c</xref>).</p></sec></sec><sec id="s0085"><label>5.2</label><title>Immunomodulating effect</title><p id="p0140">The immunomodulating effect of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> is mainly due to its polysaccharides and anthraquinone glycosides. A polysaccharide fraction was purified from <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum Praeparata</italic>, which is composed of rhamnose, arabinose, xylose and glucose at a molar ratio of 1.64:1.00:1.34:6.06. This fraction increased the levels of serum IL-2 and hematological parameters, enhanced the antioxidant profiles, and promoted the haematopoiesis of splenocytes by up-regulating the expression of EPOR and GATA-1 proteins in a cyclophosphamide (Cy)-induced mouse anemia model (20, 40 and 80 mg/kg/day, for 7 days, intraperitoneal).
