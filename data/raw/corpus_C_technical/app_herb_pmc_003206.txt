The most used plant parts are leaves (35%), fruits (21%) and seeds (18%). Underground parts (9%), flowers (8%), resin (5%), oil (2%), the whole plant (2%) and bark (1%) are also used. Infusion is the most used mode of preparation (26%), followed by decoction (22%), grinding (22%), mixing with food (7%) and maceration (6%). Plants are sometimes used as they were sourced without any (further) preparation process (5%), juiced (4%) or mixed with dates (2%), milk (1%), honey (1%) or fruits (1%). The most popular modes of administration are oral ingestion as a drink (63%) and eaten (21%). Plants are also administered as poultices (7%) or applied directly on the teeth (2%). They are rarely (1%) used in mouthwash, fumigation and lotions, inhaled, chewed, rubbed or used as washes.</p><p id="Par23">A total of 67 mixtures were documented including combinations of two to four plant ingredients (Table <xref rid="Tab3" ref-type="table">3</xref>). Forty-four medicinal plants (40%) were sometimes used in the mixtures. The medicinal plants most commonly used in mixtures are <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus spina-christi</italic> (<italic toggle="yes">zanajabil</italic>) cited in 17 mixtures and <italic toggle="yes">Pimpinella anisum</italic> (<italic toggle="yes">yansun</italic>), <italic toggle="yes">Mentha</italic> sp. (<italic toggle="yes">nena</italic>) and <italic toggle="yes">Citrus</italic> sp. (<italic toggle="yes">limon</italic>) each cited in 10 mixtures.
