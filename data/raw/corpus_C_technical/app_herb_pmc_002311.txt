Studies show that <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> exhibits anti-photoaging effects, reducing the impact of UV-induced premature aging in concentrations ranging from 0.125 to 10.0 mg/mL [<xref rid="B86-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>]. Research has also highlighted the ability of <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> to reduce the production of matrix metalloproteinase-1, an enzyme responsible for collagen degradation induced by UV exposure, particularly in young leaves [<xref rid="B86-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>,<xref rid="B107-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">107</xref>]. Furthermore, oleanane-type triterpene saponins, which have been identified in <italic toggle="yes">CJ</italic> flower buds, are known to suppress melanin production [<xref rid="B16-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>]. Given that these saponins may also be present in the leaves, further research is warranted to explore the potential of <italic toggle="yes">CJ</italic> leaves as a source of bioactive compounds with skin-protective properties. These studies offer a scientific perspective on the potential of <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> as a biological source of skin-protection compounds, highlighting the need for further research to characterize the specific active constituents.</p><p>In addition to its known skin-protective properties, <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> have been tested for a range of other bioactivities, although the number of studies on these effects is still relatively limited. Among the identified bioactivities, <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> has demonstrated anti-pancreatic properties by inhibiting the pancreatic lipase enzyme, with an IC<sub>50</sub> value of 0.308 mg/mL, suggesting a role in lipid metabolism regulation [<xref rid="B31-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>]. Additionally, <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> has shown significant anti-hyperuricemic effects by reducing serum uric acid levels by up to 60%, highlighting its potential in managing hyperuricemia. This effect is attributed to the inhibition of xanthine oxidase activity, a key enzyme involved in uric acid production [<xref rid="B19-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>]. The extract has also demonstrated anti-allergic properties, as evidenced by its ability to suppress Syk kinase activation and the production of inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-<italic toggle="yes">α</italic> and IL-4 at a dose of 50 µg/mL [<xref rid="B33-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>]. This suggests that <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> may offer potential for the management of allergic diseases, autoimmune disorders, and chronic inflammation. Furthermore, <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> has been shown to possess antinociceptive effects. In combination with compounds like epicatechin and rutin, the extract reduced neuropathic pain and inhibited the MAPK3 pathway by suppressing microglial activation in the dorsal root ganglion and spinal cord [<xref rid="B34-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>,<xref rid="B91-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">91</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> also displays neuroprotective effects, with studies indicating an increase in neutral red uptake in neuronal cells, demonstrating its anti-apoptotic properties and mitochondrial protection [<xref rid="B87-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">87</xref>]. These neuroprotective effects were further supported by antioxidant activities in PC12 cells, where phenolic compounds such as quercetin and kaempferol contributed to the observed protection against oxidative stress [<xref rid="B87-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">87</xref>].</p><p>As seen for neurodegenerative diseases, other current prevalent diseases such as diabetes have been assessed from a preventive point of view using <italic toggle="yes">CJL.</italic> In one study, consumption of 0.5 mL/day of tea brewed with <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> for one month was shown to induce antihyperglycemic and hypolipidemic effects in diabetic rats. Furthermore, this <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> intake was associated with a reduction in markers associated with poor diabetes control, such as altered hematobiokinetic parameters (e.g., creatinine, urea, uric acid, aspartate aminotransferase, and alanine aminotransferase) [<xref rid="B108-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>]. In another study, a methanolic <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> extract was administered to diabetic rats, which resulted in a reduction in blood glucose levels after only one oral administration [<xref rid="B109-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">109</xref>].
