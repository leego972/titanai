Our previous studies suggested that TGF-β1-mediate early wound closure could promote the underlying bone repair process and prevent the occurrence of MRONJ [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR6">6</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR31">31</xref>]. Moreover, other studies reported that LLLT could effectively promote wound healing, particularly in resistant wounds such as diabetic wounds [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>], and inflammation-related wounds [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR32">32</xref>]. In diabetic wound healing, LLLT increases the serum anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10 level and reduces the pro-inflammatory cytokines like IL-1β, and TNF-α levels in diabetic animals, restricting sustained inflammation and regulating the immune response [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR15">15</xref>]. Meanwhile, few studies suggested that LLLT promotes MRONJ wound healing [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR33">33</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR35">35</xref>]. In this study, we further found that LLLT promotes primary gingival wound healing and prevents MRONJ by reducing soft tissue inflammation, enhancing epithelial cell migration, and collagen formation.</p><p id="Par56">IL-1RA might exert an important function in LLLT-meditated primary gingival wound healing. The inflammatory response has an important role during the wound-healing process. The excessive inflammatory response may disrupt normal tissue architecture and function and lead to impaired wound healing [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR36">36</xref>]. IL-1β is one of the typical inflammation factors and a crucial mediator of the inflammatory response, such as mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling and activating nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) pathways, which controls the transcription of inflammatory genes, like IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR37">37</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR39">39</xref>]. Many studies have reported that pro-inflammation cytokines like IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-6 are over-expressed in the MRONJ lesions [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR40">40</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR42">42</xref>]. IL-1β overexpression prolongs the inflammatory stage and delays the soft wound healing, while IL-1RA inhibits the IL-1β-driven downstream signaling by combining the IL-1 receptor competitively and effectively blocking the IL-1β–driven inflammatory signals [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR43">43</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR44">44</xref>]. Previous studies also suggested IL-1RA deficiency leads to delayed wound healing, while IL-1RA-upregulation promotes wound healing by inhibiting pro-inflammation effects [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR45">45</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR46">46</xref>]. Our results indicated that LLLT might effectively suppress tissue inflammation by up-regulating IL-1RA expression, down-regulating IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-6 in the gingival tissue, which increases collagen formation, promotes early gingival wound healing, and prevents MRONJ (Fig. <xref rid="Fig5" ref-type="fig">5</xref>).<fig id="Fig5" position="float" orientation="portrait"><label>Fig. 5</label><caption><p>The schematic hypothesis of the function of LLLT to promote wound healing. The IL-1 signaling pathway is initiated when IL-1α or IL-1β binds to the members of the IL-1R1 family, which recruits MyD88, IRAK, and TRAF6 to activate NF-κB and MAPK and then controls the transcription of several inflammatory genes. IL-1RA can suppress this interaction competitively</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" id="MO5" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="12903_2022_2678_Fig5_HTML.jpg"/></fig></p><p id="Par57">Interestingly, we also found that LLLT improves bone regeneration and bone remodeling process. The histology results showed that LLLT might rescue the activated osteoclasts and increase the expression of OCN in the bone tissue. Moreover, the IL-1RA neutralizing antibody dramatically eliminated the effects of LLLT in promoting primary gingival wound healing. The rescue assay suggested that IL-1RA has a vital role in the LLLT-mediated prevention of MRONJ. Several studies found IL-1RA could enhance epithelial cell/ MSCs proliferation and migration capacities [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR47">47</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR48">48</xref>]. Consistently, our in vitro studies confirmed that LLLT significantly accelerates Zol-treated HaCaT cell migration. However, the mechanisms of LLLT increasing IL-1RA expression are not clear. The increase of IL-1RA may be due to the direct stimulation of LLLT or the subsequent response caused by LLLT-mediated elevation of other cytokines, like TGF-β1 or IL-4 [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR49">49</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR55">55</xref>].
