Pharmacological Properties of <italic toggle="yes">Polyscias fruticosa</italic>: An Overview of Current Evidence</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Polyscias fruticosa</italic> has been extensively used in traditional medicine across Southeast Asia for the management of inflammatory disorders, respiratory diseases, metabolic dysfunctions, and stress-related conditions. The pharmacological interest in this species stems not only from its diverse ethnomedicinal applications and the growing body of experimental evidence supporting its therapeutic potential. Notably, in vitro and in vivo studies have demonstrated anti-inflammatory, antiasthmatic, antidiabetic, antioxidant, antimicrobial, and neuroprotective activities [<xref rid="B4-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B10-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>,<xref rid="B15-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>,<xref rid="B16-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>,<xref rid="B17-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>,<xref rid="B20-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>,<xref rid="B21-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>].</p><p>A comprehensive characterization of the phytochemical constituents of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>—including triterpenoid saponins, flavonoids, sterols, polyacetylenes, phenolic acids, and various volatile and lipophilic compounds—has been provided in <xref rid="sec5-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="sec">Section 5</xref>: Chemical Composition and Structural Diversity of Bioactive Compounds in <italic toggle="yes">Polyscias fruticosa</italic>. Accordingly, the present section focuses exclusively on summarizing the current pharmacological findings and elucidating the underlying mechanisms of action where available.</p><p>This section aims to synthesize current experimental findings related to the biological activities of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, with particular emphasis on pharmacodynamic mechanisms and disease-relevant applications.</p><p>Although the current body of pharmacological evidence is promising, it remains fragmented and largely limited to preclinical studies. In our opinion, a critical gap persists in the form of well-designed in vivo studies and clinical trials that would validate the therapeutic relevance of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>. Furthermore, few studies have directly linked specific compounds to distinct bioactivities using bioassay-guided isolation or mechanistic validation. Addressing these limitations will require interdisciplinary approaches combining phytochemistry, pharmacology, and systems biology to elucidate compound–target interactions and therapeutic pathways.</p><sec id="sec6dot1-molecules-30-03460"><title>6.1. Bronchial Asthma—Clinical Background and Treatment Challenges</title><p>Bronchial asthma is a chronic inflammatory disorder of the airways characterized by variable clinical symptoms and reversible airflow limitation. It affects both children and adults, with global prevalence steadily rising. According to the World Health Organization, more than 300 million people are affected worldwide, with projections exceeding 400 million by 2025 [<xref rid="B153-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">153</xref>].</p><p>The pathophysiology of asthma is complex, involving genetic predispositions, environmental exposures, and dysregulated immune responses. A hallmark feature is eosinophilic inflammation leading to hyperresponsiveness, obstruction, and structural airway remodeling.
