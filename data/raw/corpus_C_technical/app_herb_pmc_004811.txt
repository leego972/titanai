Furthermore, salidroside inhibited MPP<sup>+</sup>-induced NO increases, the overexpression of nNOS and iNOS, and the accumulation of ROS and intracellular free Ca<sup>2+</sup>[<xref rid="b82-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">82</xref>]. Salidroside inhibited ROS and NO production, protecting PC12 cells from oxidative stress. The protective effects of salidroside on PC12 cells are mediated by the inhibition of ROS generation and the NO pathway [<xref rid="b82-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">82</xref>]. In addition, salidroside pretreatment protected dopaminergic neurons against MPTP/MPP<sup>+</sup>-induced toxicity in a dose-dependent manner [<xref rid="b83-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">83</xref>]. The mechanisms of this protection included salidroside reducing the production of ROS and NO, regulating the ratio of Bcl-2/ Bax, reducing cytochrome-c and Smac release, inhibiting caspase-3, caspas-6, and caspas-9 activation, and reducing α-synuclein aggregation [<xref rid="b83-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">83</xref>].</p><p>Pretreatment with salidroside markedly attenuated H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub>-induced cell viability loss and apoptotic cell death in a dose-dependent manner in human neuroblastoma SH-SY5Y cells [<xref rid="b84-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">84</xref>]. Salidroside protected neuron cells from oxidative stress by inducing several antioxidant enzymes such as thioredoxin, heme oxygenase-1, and peroxiredoxin-I and downregulating the proapoptotic gene Bax and upregulating the antiapoptotic genes Bcl-2 and Bcl-X<sub>L</sub> [<xref rid="b84-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">84</xref>]. Furthermore, salidroside dose-dependently restored H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub>-induced loss of mitochondrial membrane potential and elevated intracellular calcium levels [<xref rid="b84-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">84</xref>]. Salidroside alleviated hydroxyl-peroxide-induced cell viability loss and apoptotic cell death in a dose-dependent manner in cultured nerve-growth-factor-differentiated PC12 cells [<xref rid="b85-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">85</xref>]. The neuroprotective effects of salidroside might be modulated by the ERK signaling pathway, particularly at the level of or upstream from caspase-3 [<xref rid="b85-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">85</xref>]. In addition, salidroside (100 μM) significantly reduced hydroxyl-peroxide-induced apoptosis and necrosis and markedly attenuated oxidative insult caused by hydroxyl peroxide exposure in cultured rat cortical neurons [<xref rid="b86-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>]. Salidroside prevented cerebral ischemic injury induced by middle cerebral artery occlusion and reperfusion in SD rats. Furthermore, there were more normal neurons and cells in the hippocampus after salidroside treatment [<xref rid="b86-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>]. These results suggest that salidroside has protective effects against oxidative-stress-induced cell apoptosis and is thus a potential therapeutic agent for treating or preventing neurodegenerative diseases involving oxidative stress.</p><p>Salidroside reduced neuronal death and behavioral dysfunction mediated by polyglutamine expressed in ASH neurons in transgenic <italic toggle="yes">Caenorhabditis elegans</italic> [<xref rid="b87-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">87</xref>]. Salidroside’s antioxidative capability, but not its direct inhibition of polyglutamine aggregation, may contribute to neuron protection [<xref rid="b87-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">87</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> plant extracts and salidroside prevented neural disorders such as Parkinson's disease and other neurodegenerative disorders. <xref rid="t2-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref> lists studies of the effects of these agents on neurons.</p></sec><sec sec-type="other"><title>11. Liver protection</title><p>The methanolic extract from the roots of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola sachalinensis</italic> exhibited a protective effect on D-galactosamine-induced cytotoxicity in primary cultured mouse hepatocytes [<xref rid="b88-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">88</xref>]. In addition, the principal constituents, sachalosides III and IV, rhodiosin, and transcaffeic acid, exhibited hepatoprotective effects [<xref rid="b88-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">88</xref>].</p><p>Salidroside attenuated D-galactosamine- and LPS-induced acute increases in serum aspartate aminotransferase and alanine aminotransferase activities and in levels of TNF-α and serum NO in a dose-dependent manner [<xref rid="b52-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>]. It restored depleted hepatic glutathione, SOD, catalase, and glutathione peroxidase activities, reduced MDA levels, and considerably reduced histopathological changes in liver tissue [<xref rid="b52-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>]. In addition, salidroside reduced the size of necrotic regions, caspase-3 expression, and hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF)-1α in liver tissue in mice [<xref rid="b52-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>].
