FGS can provide real-time imaging during surgery, which is much cheaper and much easier to operate compared to conventional imaging technologies. Due to the excellent advantages of PLNPs (NIR emission, long PersL, and high signal-to-background ratio), Tian et al. employed ZnGa<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub>:Cr<sup>3+</sup> for the long-term image-guided surgery of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR102">102</xref>]. These ZnGa<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub>:Cr<sup>3+</sup> were prepared following a reported method [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR56">56</xref>]. Interestingly, there was no uptake of ZnGa<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub>:Cr<sup>3+</sup> by HCC tumor tissue but a significant uptake by normal liver tissue, allowing for a precise mapping of the HCC tumor area with more radical excision <bold>(</bold>Fig. <xref rid="Fig10" ref-type="fig">10</xref>).<fig id="Fig10" position="float" orientation="portrait"><label>Fig. 10</label><caption><p><bold>a</bold> Confocal laser endomicroscopic images of liver, tumor, and peri-tumoral area at 6 h post-injection of ZnGa<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub>:Cr<sup>3+</sup>. <bold>b</bold> PersL images of the exposed tumor lesion following the PLNPs’ injection and LED’s irradiation. <bold>c</bold> GFP fluorescence images of the 3 exposed tumor lesions were merged with the white-light images. <bold>d</bold> H&amp;E staining of the resected tissue from <bold>c</bold>. <bold>e</bold> The survival rates of mice from different treatments.</p><p>(Reproduced with permission [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR102">102</xref>]. Because of the superiority of minimal-invasiveness and spatial specificity, photo-absorbers based PTT has been used in numerous pre-clinical studies [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR103">103</xref>]. Although PLNPs themselves cannot be used directly in photothermal therapy due to their low extinction coefficient, coupling with NIR absorbing materials (such as ICG, CuS) can be achieved for PersL imaging-guided PTT of tumors. Chang et al. designed the PLNPs and ICG co-loaded mesoporous silica for PersL imaging-guided PTT [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR104">104</xref>]. The constructed nanoplatform had strong NIR absorption with an excellent photothermal response, which showed efficient tumor elimination in vitro and in vivo. Yan et al. developed an activatable PLNPs/CuS-based nanoplatform for PersL imaging-guided PTT [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR105">105</xref>]. CuS nanoparticles were regarded as both PTT agents and quencher to afford the high photothermal conversion efficiency to the whole nanoformulation. Thus the synthesized nanoplatform exhibited highly sensitive PersL imaging of tumors and excellent tumor treatment.
