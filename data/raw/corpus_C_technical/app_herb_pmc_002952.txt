# PMC: ethnobotany medicinal plants Africa Asia — Overview

Maria Sibylla Merian’s <italic toggle="yes">Metamorphosis Insectorum Surinamensium</italic> [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR7">7</xref>], published in 1705</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" id="MO2" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="13002_2023_589_Fig2_HTML.jpg"/></fig></p><p id="Par22">The aim of the present report is to reunite the name of many women naturalists who registered knowledge about native flora in scientific expeditions around the globe, mainly South America, focusing on the work of Maria Sibylla Merian and discussing the patterns of the suppression of women scientists. Furthermore, we aimed to inventory useful plants described in Maria Sibylla’s <italic toggle="yes">Metamorphosis Insectorum Surinamensium</italic> and find pharmacological evidence about the traditional uses described for those plants cited as medicinal and toxic.</p></sec></sec><sec id="Sec6"><title>Methods</title><p id="Par23">The study consists of two parts: first, we conducted a survey of women naturalists with a discussion of patterns in their suppression in the history of science, taking the trajectory of Maria Sibylla Merian as an example. In the second part, we analyzed Merian's contributions regarding the useful plants described in <italic toggle="yes">Metamorphosis Insectorum Surinamensium</italic> and correlated them with current scientific evidence.</p><p id="Par24">Part I: The survey of women naturalists was carried out by searching Pubmed (<ext-link xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" ext-link-type="uri" xlink:href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/">https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/</ext-link>), Scielo (<ext-link xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" ext-link-type="uri" xlink:href="https://www.scielo.br/">https://www.scielo.br/</ext-link>), Biblioteca Virtual em Saúde (<ext-link xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" ext-link-type="uri" xlink:href="https://bvsms.saude.gov.br/">https://bvsms.saude.gov.br/</ext-link>) and Google Scholar (<ext-link xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" ext-link-type="uri" xlink:href="https://scholar.google.com.br/">https://scholar.google.com.br/</ext-link>), using the following keywords: “women” and “naturalist” or “women travelers”. This survey was carried out in English, Portuguese and French. In addition to the articles and periodicals deposited in the listed databases, books from BiblioMaison (at the French Consulate in Rio de Janeiro) (<ext-link xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" ext-link-type="uri" xlink:href="https://bibliomaison.net.br/">https://bibliomaison.net.br/</ext-link>) with the same descriptors were consulted. Each woman naturalist’s name found became a descriptor, which was searched separately in Google Scholar. Women who participated in any scientific expedition or trip, or in a curiosity cabinet, or who were collectors of Natural History between the seventeenth and nineteenth centuries, were included. Another criterion for inclusion was having illustrated a botanical species and/or recorded some use of useful plants or reported observations about plants in the form of a published work, letters or diaries.</p><p id="Par25">Part II: The original book <italic toggle="yes">Metamorphosis Insectorum Surinamensium</italic> [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR7">7</xref>] was consulted in the online catalog of the Biodiversity Heritage Library<xref ref-type="fn" rid="Fn1">1</xref> using the English transcription of the <italic toggle="yes">Dissertatio de Generatione et Metamorphosibus Insectorum Surinamensium</italic> [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR47">47</xref>], published by Joannes Oosterwyk in 1719 in Latin, hand-colored, belonging to the John Carter Brown Library. A recent facsimile edition of this book edited by Marieke van Delft and Hans Mulder (2016) was also consulted to compare information [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR48">48</xref>]. All information was systematized in a table organized by botanical description, illustration number, vernacular name, plant part, origin, traditional uses and observations. Information on toxic and medicinal plants was checked in an official translation into French, <italic toggle="yes">Histoire Générale des Insectes de Surinam et de toute l'Europe</italic> [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR49">49</xref>], third edition, published in 1711 by M. Buch'oz, with comments and additions from other works published by Maria Sibylla Merian, available in the online catalog of the Bibliothèque Nationale de France. After reading the book, an inventory of the plants was carried out, with notes on descriptions, uses and popular names. These plants were divided into food, medicinal, toxic or aromatic plants (which have some description about good aroma) and other uses (which have some described use that does not fit the other categories, such as wood for construction, painting, and utensils, among others). Subsequently, the identification of the plants was based on the list of species published by the botanists Tinde van Andel, Hajo Gernaat, Auke Hielkema and Paul Maas [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR48">48</xref>].
