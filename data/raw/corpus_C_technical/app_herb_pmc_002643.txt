The letters a to d represented the power significance.</p></fn></table-wrap-foot></table-wrap><p>The genus Anabasis, a group of halophytic (salt-tolerant) plants native to arid regions of Central Asia and the Middle East, has demonstrated promising AMA. Maatalah et al. [<xref rid="pone.0310298.ref023" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>] reported that, alkaloids and saponin extracts of <italic toggle="yes">Anabasis articulate</italic> showed AMA against <italic toggle="yes">E</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">coli</italic> ATCC 25922, <italic toggle="yes">S</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">aureus</italic> ATCC 6538, <italic toggle="yes">K</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">pneumonia</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">B</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">subtilis</italic> ATCC 6633, <italic toggle="yes">P</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">aeruginosa</italic> ATCC 14028, <italic toggle="yes">C</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">albicans</italic> with MIC from 0.5 to 1 mg/ml. Likewise, alkaloids which extracted from <italic toggle="yes">A</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">articulata</italic> stems exhibited AMA toward bacterial and unicellular fungal strains [<xref rid="pone.0310298.ref024" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>]. The AMA of plant leaves crude extracts can be attributed to several mechanisms of action, including membrane disruption, enzyme inhibition, inhibition of virulence factors, induction of oxidative stress, and disruption of biofilm formation [<xref rid="pone.0310298.ref025" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>,<xref rid="pone.0310298.ref026" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>].
