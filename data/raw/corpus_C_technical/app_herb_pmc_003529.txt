Low consensus for wound management has been noted in Europe, America, and Asia, where diverse plant remedies are employed for complex or culturally variable injuries, resulting in lower informant agreement [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR85">85</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR86">86</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR97">97</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR137">137</xref>].</p><p id="Par179">Sexual and delivery-related conditions, such as gonorrhea, placenta retention, and sexual dysfunction, showed a moderate ICF of 0.65. The lower consensus compared to gastrointestinal disorders likely reflects the sensitive nature of reproductive health and the restricted use of trusted plants for safety and efficacy. Similar patterns have been reported in Ethiopia, Kenya, and Pakistan, where reproductive remedies are culturally guarded and selectively applied [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR34">34</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR168">168</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR178">178</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR187">187</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR195">195</xref>].</p><p id="Par180">Thus, while acknowledging the methodological limitations of ICF, the analysis still provides useful insights into the relationship between disease prevalence, cultural importance, and medicinal plant selection. High-consensus categories such as gastrointestinal, dermatological, and acute conditions highlight plants that are culturally significant and likely to contain reproducible bioactive compounds. Conversely, lower-consensus categories indicate treatments that are either less frequent or highly individualized, underscoring the diversity of local medicinal knowledge. These findings are consistent with global ethnobotanical patterns, confirming that ICF, when interpreted critically, remains a valuable tool for identifying culturally and pharmacologically important species across Africa, Asia, Europe, and the Americas [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR19">19</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR20">20</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR85">85</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR86">86</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR97">97</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR137">137</xref>].</p><p id="Par181">In Sheikh District, the ICF findings emphasize that indigenous knowledge integrates ecological adaptation, empirical validation, and sustainable use. High-consensus plants provide an evidence-based basis for prioritizing species in pharmacological research, conservation efforts, and community health programs, demonstrating the broader relevance of local ethnomedicinal knowledge in both regional and global contexts.</p><p id="Par182">
