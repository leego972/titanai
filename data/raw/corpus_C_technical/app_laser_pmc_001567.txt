The OCTA may also help delineate the distinct phenotypic features of these inflammatory optic neuropathies [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0064" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>].</p><p>Neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD), the majority with AQP4‐IgG positive biomarker, is a distinct entity from demyelinating or MS‐related optic neuritis [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0065" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0066" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>]. This inflammatory optic neuropathy is often more severe at the onset, with a poor chance of spontaneous recovery [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0054" ref-type="bibr">54</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0055" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>]. The severe and prolonged inflammation can cause more extensive damage to the microvascular network and retinal ganglion cells. In acute NMOSD optic neuritis, no loss of vascular density in the RPC is noted (Figure <xref rid="ceo14544-fig-0005" ref-type="fig">5</xref>) [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0042" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>]. However, following the acute inflammatory insult, there is enlargement of the foveal avascular zone and reduced RPC and multi‐layer macular capillary vascular density in individuals with AQP4‐antibody seropositive NMOSD compared to healthy controls, but is not paralleled by RNFL thinning, which remains static on longitudinal study; this difference exists even in those who are AQP4‐antibody seropositive without clinical manifestation of optic neuritis [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0042" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0067" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0068" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>]. The peripapillary and macular vascular density positively correlates with visual acuity and visual field measures but not with RGCL count or RNFL thickness [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0068" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0069" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0070" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0071" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>]. This is distinct from the findings in demyelinating optic neuritis and refutes the leading theory that vascular dropout occurs secondary to loss of neuroaxonal energy demand signals with neurodegeneration [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0063" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0072" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>]. AQP4‐ON is associated with more profound deep macular capillary plexus density loss and outer nuclear layer degeneration; this is proposed to account for the more profound visual morbidity in this subset of NMOSD [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0057" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0067" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0068" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0073" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0074" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>].</p><fig position="float" fig-type="FIGURE" id="ceo14544-fig-0005" orientation="portrait"><label>FIGURE 5</label><caption><p>Colour fundus photo, and OCTA images of a representative patient with acute optic neuritis. (A) Colour fundus photo of left eye demonstrating optic disc swelling. (B) 4.5 mm × 4.5 mm OCTA of the left optic disc, demonstrates intact peripapillary capillary plexus. (C) 6 mm × 6 mm OCTA of the superficial capillary plexus of the macula in the left eye, demonstrating an intact superficial capillary bed. (D) 6 mm × 6 mm OCTA of the deep capillary plexus of the macula in the left eye, demonstrating vascular drop‐out in the inferior‐nasal capillary bed (red arrow). OCTA: optical coherence tomography angiography.</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="anchor" id="jats-graphic-9" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="CEO-53-682-g003.jpg"/></fig><p>MOG‐IgG optic neuritis is distinct from MS‐associated optic neuritis and NMOSD [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0066" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>]. MOG is a component of the myelin sheath; thus, auto‐inflammation affects the perineural layers rather than the nerve axons [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0075" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0076" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>]. This results in distinct long segment MRI changes [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0077" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>]. In MOG‐associated optic neuritis the peripapillary and macular vascular density positively correlates with visual acuity measures [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0068" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0072" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>].
