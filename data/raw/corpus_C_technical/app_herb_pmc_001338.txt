# PMC: phytochemical bioactive compound anticancer — Overview

<italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus</italic> genus exhibit significant antimicrobial activity, attributed to their rich phytochemical composition, including flavonoids, phenolic acids, and other bioactive compounds. Various studies have evaluated their potential to inhibit bacterial and fungal pathogens, demonstrating promising results that support their potential as natural antimicrobial agents.</p><p>A recent study highlighted the strong antimicrobial activity of methanolic extracts from <italic toggle="yes">A. spinosus</italic> leaves, collected from uncultivated farmland in Awo-Omamma, Nigeria. The minimum bactericidal concentration (MBC) was 1.00 mg/mL against <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus</italic> spp., <italic toggle="yes">E. coli</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Pseudomonas aeruginosa</italic>, while the minimum fungicidal concentration (MFC) was 0.125 mg/mL against <italic toggle="yes">Candida albicans</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Aspergillus niger</italic>. These results, comparable to standard antibiotics such as ciprofloxacin and the antifungal ketoconazole, suggest that <italic toggle="yes">A. spinosus</italic> extracts could serve as effective antimicrobial agents [<xref rid="B109-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">109</xref>].</p><p>Similarly, methanolic extracts from the aerial parts of <italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus viridis</italic>, collected from six different districts of the Fayoum Depression in Egypt exhibited significant antimicrobial activity in 96-well plate assays. Specific fractions demonstrated notable effects against <italic toggle="yes">S. aureus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Salmonella typhi</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">E. coli</italic>, with strong biofilm inhibition activity observed for AV4 and AV5 against <italic toggle="yes">E. coli</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus subtilis</italic>, while AV6 was particularly effective against <italic toggle="yes">P. aeruginosa</italic>. The antifungal assays indicated that AV6 was highly active against <italic toggle="yes">Aspergillus flavus</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">A. niger</italic>, whereas AV2 and AV5 displayed minimal inhibition of <italic toggle="yes">C. albicans</italic>. These findings highlight <italic toggle="yes">A. viridis</italic>’ potential in treating biofilm-associated infections [<xref rid="B110-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>]. These antimicrobial effects, alongside species-specific differences in bactericidal and fungicidal activity, are summarized in <xref rid="nutrients-17-01749-f003" ref-type="fig">Figure 3</xref>, which highlights the main bioactivities of various <italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus</italic> spp.</p><p>Another study examined the antifungal activity of mature leaves from various <italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus</italic> spp. against <italic toggle="yes">Fusarium equiseti</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Rhizoctonia solani</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">and Alternaria alternata.</italic> The species tested included <italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus</italic> spp., <italic toggle="yes">A. cruentus</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">A. hypochondriacus × hybridus</italic> cultivated in Bodaczów near Zamość (southeastern Poland), as well as <italic toggle="yes">A. retroflexus</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">A. hybridus</italic> from plantations in the Düzce province (northwestern Turkey). <italic toggle="yes">A. hybridus</italic> exhibited the strongest effect due to its high polyphenol content. However, no inhibitory effect was observed against <italic toggle="yes">Fusarium oxysporum</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Colletotrichum coccodes</italic>. The extracts primarily displayed fungistatic activity, particularly in the initial days of exposure, suggesting a selective and short-term antifungal effect [<xref rid="B111-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">111</xref>].</p><p>Additionally, ethanolic extracts from the whole plant of <italic toggle="yes">A. tricolor</italic>, cultivated in the botanical garden of Hezhou University in China, exhibited notable antimicrobial properties, particularly in the ethyl acetate (EtOAc) fraction. Three key bioactive compounds were identified, with gallic acid exhibiting the highest antimicrobial activity against bacteria and fungi, with MIC values ranging from 63 to 68 μg/mL. In particular, <italic toggle="yes">A. tricolor</italic> extracts were highly effective against <italic toggle="yes">E. coli</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">S. aureus</italic>, suggesting their potential for applications in food preservation and health [<xref rid="B91-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">91</xref>].</p><p>The antimicrobial effects of <italic toggle="yes">A. caudatus</italic> were also evaluated using ethanol and aqueous extracts at different growth stages (including shoots, flowering, and pre-flowering stages). The extracts exhibited significant inhibition against <italic toggle="yes">C. albicans</italic>, suggesting potential antifungal applications.
