Cost of War: Exploring Trauma in the North African Countries Affected by Armed 
Conflict.

Raheel MFS(1), Snoubar Y(2), Yarboua EA(3).

Author information:
(1)Mohamed Faraj Saleh Raheel, PhD, is assistant professor, Qatar University, 
Doha, Qatar.
(2)Yaser Snoubar, PhD, is associate professor of social work, Qatar University, 
Doha, Qatar.
(3)Ekram Abdelhakim Yarboua, PhD, is researcher in trauma and crisis, Doha, 
Qatar.

This study investigates the mental health impacts of war in Libya, with a focus 
on the prevalence and severity of PTSD symptoms. It aims to understand how PTSD 
manifests across different sociodemographic groups, examining symptoms such as 
re-experiencing, avoidance, and hyperarousal. The research sampled 652 
individuals aged 18 to 65 from various Libyan cities, using electronic 
questionnaires over 18 months. Results indicate significant differences in PTSD 
symptoms by gender, with women reporting more avoidance symptoms than men. 
Additionally, individuals in major conflict zones exhibited more severe 
re-experiencing and avoidance symptoms compared with those in less affected 
areas. The study also found that highly educated individuals showed higher 
levels of hyperarousal, which suggests a complex relationship between education 
level and stress responses. The findings highlight the need for culturally 
sensitive social work interventions in mental health tailored to the diverse 
needs of different demographic groups. Such interventions should leverage local 
resources and traditional family institutions to enhance resilience and 
recovery. The study emphasizes the importance of targeted social work 
interventions in postconflict scenarios to facilitate long-term societal 
integration and peace building in regions dealing with the psychological 
aftermath of conflict.
