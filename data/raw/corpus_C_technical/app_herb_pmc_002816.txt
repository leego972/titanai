In fact, many Malasars earn their living treating people's ailments using the flora of the Velliangiri hills.</p></sec><sec><title>Consensus of TAK among Malasars informants</title><p>Our research indicates a high level of consensus within the Malasars community. This is the first consensus analysis research published from an aboriginal group in India and supports many reports of the rich botanical knowledge of aboriginals within India [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B64">64</xref>]. Sajem and Gosai [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B90">90</xref>] reported consensus of medicinal use of plants in northeast India. They did not use the quantitative method proposed by Trotter and Logan's [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B43">43</xref>], but rather defined consensus as the percentage of informants who listed a particular utility for a specific plant. In our study, the informant consensus of medicinal plant usage with the Malasars resulted in F<sub>IC </sub>factors ranging from 0.5 to 0.92 per illness category (Table <xref ref-type="table" rid="T2">2</xref>). The average F<sub>IC </sub>value for all illness categories was 0.71, indicating a significant level of informant consensus compared to similar studies from other countries [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B5">5</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B51">51</xref>]. In the literature, high informant consensus (F<sub>IC </sub>0.875) was also recorded among the snakebite healers of Kamba in Africa [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B52">52</xref>], treating 'mich' or febrile diseases (F<sub>IC </sub>0.80) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B55">55</xref>], and respiratory disorders (F<sub>IC </sub>1.00) among Inuit in Nunavut [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B58">58</xref>]. A high consensus factor may indicate that there is some key phytochemical ingredient(s) in these plants which requires phytopharmacological analyses. Our research revealed that the category jaundice included only 2 species (N<sub>t</sub>), resulting in a high F<sub>IC </sub>factor of 0.92, indicating greater homogeneity among informants.
