Qualitative methods were selected because the exploration of shared herbal medicine practitioner experience is necessary to remedy the gap between practitioner use and understanding of adaptogens, and traditional and modern evidence of adaptogens. The key strength of phenomenology lies in exploring this ‘shared experience’ or knowledge of a particular phenomenon.<xref rid="bib0085" ref-type="bibr"><sup>17</sup></xref> Qualitative methods allow the researcher to seek a deep understanding of the problem by focusing on components that cannot be quantified.<xref rid="bib0090" ref-type="bibr"><sup>18</sup></xref></p><sec id="sec0015"><label>2.1</label><title>Sampling and recruitment</title><p id="par0055">Participants were drawn from purposive sampling to ensure efficient and effective saturation of categories<xref rid="bib0095" ref-type="bibr"><sup>19</sup></xref> and to select participants who fulfilled the following inclusion criteria:<list list-type="simple" id="lis0010"><list-item id="lsti0020"><label>•</label><p id="par0060">Held an advanced diploma of Naturopathy and/or Western Herbal Medicine (WHM) or a Bachelor of Health Science in Naturopathy and/or WHM</p></list-item><list-item id="lsti0025"><label>•</label><p id="par0065">Had a minimum of 5 years clinical experience practicing in Naturopathy or WHM</p></list-item><list-item id="lsti0030"><label>•</label><p id="par0070">Spoke fluent English</p></list-item><list-item id="lsti0035"><label>•</label><p id="par0075">Were able and willing to give their written consent</p></list-item></list></p><p id="par0080">Anyone who did not fulfil every aspect of the inclusion criteria was excluded.</p><p id="par0085">Participants were targeted through accredited education institutes nationally (in Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane) including Endeavour College of Natural Health (ECNH), Australasian College of Natural Therapies (ACNT) and Southern School of Natural Therapies (SSNT). This purposeful sampling strategy limits the impact of individual training organisations on views and generated a sample that is broadly representative of the views of experienced practitioners, whilst also facilitating access to a highly experienced practitioner population that are more likely to be familiar with both traditional and scientific concepts related to herbal medicine. Participants were also targeted through complementary medicine (CM) educators discussion forums and through snowball sampling<xref rid="bib0100" ref-type="bibr"><sup>20</sup></xref> of stakeholders through these avenues identifying other relevant participants. This sampling strategy enabled a rich exploration of the experiences of the people who use adaptogenic herbal medicines without discrimination, improving trustworthiness through sampling adequacy that was evidenced by data saturation.<xref rid="bib0095" ref-type="bibr"><sup>19</sup></xref></p></sec><sec id="sec0020"><label>2.2</label><title>Participants</title><p id="par0090">We conducted three focus groups (FGs) with a total of 17 naturopaths and herbalists. Three participants were male and 14 were female. The participants all worked in private clinical practice as naturopaths with experience ranging from 5 to 20+ years. The majority were lecturers in naturopathy and/or WHM at Bachelor’s degree level. Of the participants, all held a naturopathic qualification at Bachelor’s degree level or higher.</p></sec><sec id="sec0025"><label>2.3</label><title>Data collection</title><p id="par0095">FGs were conducted after informed consent was gained from each participant.</p><p id="par0100">FGs were chosen as the data collection method, as they provide the researcher the opportunity to identify existing perspectives within a subgroup, and are an effective means to gather a broad range of views to identify group norms.<xref rid="bib0105" ref-type="bibr"><sup>21</sup></xref> The key reason for selection of FGs was to benefit from the interpersonal communication style and group interaction that occur in group discussions.<xref rid="bib0110" ref-type="bibr"><sup>22</sup></xref> The act of participants both querying each other and explaining themselves to one another in FGs provides data on consensus and diversity amongst participants.<xref rid="bib0115" ref-type="bibr"><sup>23</sup></xref></p><p id="par0105">The FGs aimed to draw upon clinical experiences, perceptions and attitudes towards adaptogens, and identify where the knowledge and understanding of adaptogens has come from and how it is being taught in the WHM education system.
