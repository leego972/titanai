In <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, triterpenoid saponins constitute the dominant class of bioactive constituents, and their structural diversity provides a valuable basis for both pharmacological evaluation and biotechnological exploitation. Importantly, selected saponins have demonstrated immunostimulatory or antimicrobial properties, reinforcing their therapeutic value.</p><sec id="sec5dot1dot1-molecules-30-03460"><title>5.1.1. Contextual Account of Triterpenoid Saponin Discovery in <italic toggle="yes">Polyscias fruticosa</italic></title><p>The presence of triterpenoid saponins in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> was first reported in the mid-1990s, marking a key milestone in the phytochemical exploration of this species (<xref rid="molecules-30-03460-t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref>). In 1995, Chaboud and colleagues isolated a saponin from the leaves of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, identified as 3-O-[β-D-galactopyranosyl-(1→2)-β-D-glucuronopyranosyl] oleanolic acid [<xref rid="B69-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>]. This compound was characterized as a monodesmosidic saponin, featuring a disaccharide chain at the C-3 position of the oleanolic acid aglycone and lacking substitution at the C-28 position. Its structure confirmed the occurrence of oleanane-type saponins, which are typical for members of the Araliaceae family. The predominance of oleanane-type structures among the saponins isolated from <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> confirms its chemotaxonomic affiliation with other medicinally relevant Araliaceae species such as <italic toggle="yes">Panax</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Aralia</italic>. The presence of shared compounds (e.g., chikusetsusaponin IVa) further supports this classification.</p><p>In 1996, Proliac et al. [<xref rid="B5-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>] reported the isolation of another oleanane-type saponin from the yellow-leaved variant of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, structurally identified as 3-O-[α-L-rhamnopyranosyl-(1→4)-β-D-glucuronopyranosyl]-28-O-β-D-glucopyranosyl oleanolic acid. Unlike the compound described by Chaboud et al. [<xref rid="B69-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>], this saponin exhibited a bisdesmosidic structure, with glycosidic substitutions at both the C-3 and C-28 positions of the aglycone, a feature commonly observed in Araliaceae saponins.</p><p>A major advancement occurred in 1998, when Vo Duy Huan and colleagues conducted a comprehensive phytochemical analysis of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> leaf and root extracts, resulting in the isolation of eleven triterpenoid saponins [<xref rid="B3-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>]. Eight of these were novel compounds, designated polysciosides A–H. All were based on oleanolic acid aglycones and exhibited bisdesmosidic structures with varying sugar compositions and glycosidic linkages. Several of these novel saponins were subsequently reported to exhibit cytotoxicity against tumor cell lines or antioxidant potential. The structural variation among polysciosides A–H, particularly the nature and position of sugar moieties, is likely to influence membrane interaction and bioactivity, although systematic structure–activity relationship (SAR) studies are still lacking.</p><p>The remaining three compounds had previously been reported: ladyginoside A from <italic toggle="yes">Ladyginia bucharica</italic> [<xref rid="B70-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>], zingibroside R1 from <italic toggle="yes">Panax zingiberensis</italic> [<xref rid="B3-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>], and a compound later referred to as PFS (<italic toggle="yes">Polyscias fruticosa</italic> saponin), originally isolated from <italic toggle="yes">Swartzia simplex</italic> and identified as 3-O-[β-D-glucopyranosyl-(1→4)-β-D-glucuronopyranosyl] oleanolic acid 28-O-β-D-glucopyranosyl ester [<xref rid="B71-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>].</p><p>In 2003, Divakar and co-workers [<xref rid="B6-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>] isolated two additional novel saponins: oleanolic acid-28-O-β-D-glucopyranosyl-D-glucopyranosyl-rhamnopyranoside and 3-O-α-L-arabinopyranosyl-oleanolic acid-28-O-β-D-glucopyranosyl-D-glucopyranosyl-rhamnopyranoside.
