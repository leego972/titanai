The optical fiber (not shown in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig3-0954411914543963">Figure 3</xref>) is connected to the collimator (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig3-0954411914543963">Figure 3</xref>, (1)) which transmits the light beam to the objective lens (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig3-0954411914543963">Figure 3</xref>, (12)) which focuses the beam on the sample. The laser beam is then backscattered and recollected by the collimator and the fiber.</p><p>We have already demonstrated the use of a type of this handheld probe in an OCT system in clinical applications,<sup><xref rid="bibr30-0954411914543963" ref-type="bibr">30</xref></sup> with a similar design; however, this is the first presentation of its design.</p></sec><sec id="section5-0954411914543963"><title>Evaluation of the fulfillment of the instrument design requirements</title><p>The several aspects presented regarding the requirements of the instrument design were taken into account for all the three handheld probes.</p><p>The cage is made of an Al alloy (light and non-toxic), while the handles are made of plastic—totally (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig2-0954411914543963">Figure 2</xref>) or in part (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig3-0954411914543963">Figure 3</xref>). The probes are thus relatively light, as requested by users: 0.348 kg for the first probe (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig1-0954411914543963">Figure 1</xref>), 0.276 g for the second (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig2-0954411914543963">Figure 2</xref>), and 0.336 kg for the third probe (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig3-0954411914543963">Figure 3</xref>). In comparison, commercially available probes are, for example, 1.5 kg (Envisu; Bioptigen, Morrisville, NC, USA), while a recent report pointed out MEMS-based probes with a minimum mass of 0.42 kg.<sup><xref rid="bibr27-0954411914543963" ref-type="bibr">27</xref></sup> However, the latter costs higher and also require post-acquisition image correction due to the sinusoidal regime of the MEMS scanners.
