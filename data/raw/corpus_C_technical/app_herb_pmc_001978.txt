Afzelin exhibits antioxidant, cytoprotective, anti-inflammatory, and anticancer properties [<xref rid="B95-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>,<xref rid="B96-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>,<xref rid="B97-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>,<xref rid="B98-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">98</xref>,<xref rid="B99-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>]. In lung cancer models, afzelin induced immunogenic cell death (ICD) by downregulating NAD(P)H: quinone oxidoreductase 2 (NQO2), triggering ER stress, and promoting DAMP release (ATP, HMGB1, calreticulin), thereby stimulating antitumor immune responses [<xref rid="B96-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>]. In murine models, afzelin suppressed Ehrlich ascites carcinoma (EAC) proliferation by ~71% at 50 mg·kg<sup>−1</sup> with minimal toxicity [<xref rid="B97-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>]. It also enhanced SPF values in sunscreen formulations and exhibited mild anti-adipogenic activity in 3T3-L1 cells, broadening its potential applications in cosmetics and metabolic health [<xref rid="B95-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>,<xref rid="B99-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>].</p><p>Quercitrin (quercetin-3-O-α-L-rhamnoside) (<bold>21</bold>), another flavonol glycoside identified in the same fraction [<xref rid="B8-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>], is known for its antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and cytoprotective properties. In osteoarthritis models, quercitrin inhibited NF-κB signaling and suppressed matrix metalloproteinases and pro-inflammatory cytokines [<xref rid="B100-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">100</xref>]. In myocardial infarction models, it improved cardiac function by promoting M2 macrophage polarization and enhancing mitochondrial respiration [<xref rid="B101-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">101</xref>]. Quercitrin also improved gut microbiota composition and reduced serum cholesterol in zebrafish models, confirming its multifaceted health benefits [<xref rid="B102-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>,<xref rid="B103-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">103</xref>].</p><p>Esculetin (6,7-dihydroxycoumarin) (<bold>22</bold>), a coumarin derivative identified for the first time in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> [<xref rid="B8-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>], exhibits anticancer, lipid-lowering, and metabolic regulatory activities [<xref rid="B104-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">104</xref>,<xref rid="B105-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">105</xref>,<xref rid="B106-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>,<xref rid="B107-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">107</xref>]. In hepatocellular carcinoma models, it induced ferroptosis by inhibiting the Nrf2–xCT/GPx4 axis and impairing glycolysis through GPI inhibition [<xref rid="B104-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">104</xref>,<xref rid="B105-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">105</xref>,<xref rid="B106-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>,<xref rid="B107-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">107</xref>]. Additionally, it promoted HDL formation via CD36 upregulation in adipose macrophages and reduced fat accumulation in <italic toggle="yes">C. elegans</italic> via insulin/IGF-1 and AMPK signaling pathways [<xref rid="B105-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">105</xref>,<xref rid="B106-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>].</p><p>Caffeic acid (<bold>24</bold>), a hydroxycinnamic acid detected in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> for the first time [<xref rid="B8-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>], exhibits robust antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. It scavenges reactive radicals, chelates metal ions, and inhibits pro-inflammatory cytokines via NF-κB and MAPK suppression [<xref rid="B108-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>,<xref rid="B109-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">109</xref>,<xref rid="B110-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>]. It also exerts anticancer effects, particularly against HCC, by suppressing STAT3 signaling and promoting apoptosis [<xref rid="B108-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>].</p><p>The identification of such well-known bioactives in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> contributes to the chemotaxonomic characterization of the species and its alignment with other pharmacologically rich Araliaceae taxa.</p><p>4-hydroxycinnamaldehyde (<bold>25</bold>), identified by Do et al.
