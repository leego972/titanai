Carotenoids</title><p>Carotenoids are lipophilic compounds derived from isoprene, characterized by a linear or cyclic chain structure of isoprene units with conjugated double bonds. This configuration gives them optical properties that determine their color, which varies from yellow to red, depending on the chain length and the presence of functional groups. Furthermore, the absence or presence of oxygen in their structure allows carotenoids to be classified into carotenes and xanthophylls. Carotenes are hydrocarbon carotenoids without oxygen atoms. The main carotenoids identified in <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> are <italic toggle="yes">β</italic>-carotene and <italic toggle="yes">α</italic>-carotene (<xref rid="nutrients-17-03382-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>). Xanthophylls, on the other hand, are oxygenated carotenoids that include compounds such as lutein, zeaxanthin, neoxanthin, and violaxanthin [<xref rid="B3-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B13-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>,<xref rid="B21-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>]. The major xanthophylls in <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> are lutein, flavoxanthin, luteoxanthin, neoxanthin, fucoxanthol, violaxanthin, and pheophorbide b and a [<xref rid="B47-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>].</p><p>These structural differences determine the color of the compound and, therefore, of the raw material. Furthermore, these structural differences mean that the biological functions and distribution in plant tissues of the various carotenoids reported in <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> are significantly different. For example, xanthophylls are involved in protection against damage from excessive light, while carotenes are precursors of vitamin A and have antioxidant properties [<xref rid="B55-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>]. These properties determine the distribution of these compounds within the leaf, concentrating them in regions where their functional role is most significant, mainly in the cell membrane and cell wall, and exhibiting hydrophilic characteristics [<xref rid="B47-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>].</p><p>The concentration of carotenoids in <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> increases with the maturation of the leaves due to the biosynthesis of these pigments from isoprenoid precursors, such as geranylgeranyl pyrophosphate (GGPP). This allows carotenoids such as <italic toggle="yes">β</italic>-carotene to be formed through various enzymatic reactions, such as the action of phytoene synthase, which contributes to greater protection against oxidative stress and improved photosynthesis under intense light conditions [<xref rid="B56-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>,<xref rid="B57-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>]. This developmental pattern suggests that determining the optimal harvest time of <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> is crucial for maximizing carotenoid yield. Evaluating leaves at the onset of senescence, when they display more intense orange coloration, may be of interest. Leaf senescence involves a series of complex metabolic processes that affect the carotenoid composition of leaves. During this process, chlorophyll degradation can release previously masked carotenoids, such as <italic toggle="yes">β</italic>-carotene, lutein, and zeaxanthin, compounds of great importance in the food industry due to their antioxidant properties and the benefits associated with their consumption, such as eye protection [<xref rid="B58-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>,<xref rid="B59-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>]. However, it is crucial to distinguish between functional intact carotenoids and chlorophyll degradation products, such as fluorescent red chlorophyll catabolites, which lack significant antioxidant activity [<xref rid="B60-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>,<xref rid="B61-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>,<xref rid="B62-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>]. Accurate identification of these compounds is essential for assessing the nutritional value of senescent leaves.
