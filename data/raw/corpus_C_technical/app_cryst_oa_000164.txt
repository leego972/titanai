Title: Synthesis, characterization and applications of perovskite oxide nanomaterials

Abstract:
What makes perovskite oxide nanomaterials one of the most intensively studied classes of functional ceramics in modern materials science? This review examines the principal synthesis routes, characterization techniques, and applications of ABO?-type perovskite oxide nanomaterials reported in the literature up to 2018. Perovskite oxides, first identified as the mineral CaTiO? in 1839, have evolved into a remarkably versatile family of compounds whose properties can be tuned through compositional substitution at the A- and B-site cation positions. Synthesis methods surveyed include solid-state reaction, sol-gel, co-precipitation, hydrothermal, microwave-assisted, and combustion techniques, each offering distinct advantages in controlling particle size, morphology, phase purity, and surface area. Characterization data drawn from X-ray diffraction, scanning and transmission electron microscopy, Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy, thermogravimetric analysis, and BET surface area measurements are discussed to illustrate how synthesis parameters govern the structural and morphological features of the resulting nanomaterials. Crystallite sizes in the range of 15 to 100 nm have been achieved through wet-chemical routes, with sol-gel and combustion methods consistently yielding single-phase perovskite powders at calcination temperatures between 600 and 900 degrees Celsius. Applications spanning heterogeneous catalysis, solid oxide fuel cells, electrochemical sensing, environmental remediation, and energy storage are reviewed, with emphasis on how nanoscale dimensions enhance catalytic activity, ionic conductivity, and surface reactivity compared to bulk counterparts. The findings indicate that partial substitution of A- and B-site cations generates oxygen vacancies and mixed oxidation states that are central to the functional performance of these materials. This review aims to provide a consolidated reference for researchers working on perovskite oxide nanomaterials and to identify directions for future investigation.
