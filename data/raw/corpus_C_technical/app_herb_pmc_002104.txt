Antioxidant Activity</title><p>The radical scavenging (RSC) of DPPH (at 518 nm) and ABTS (at 734 nm) radicals were performed according to Brand-Williams et al. [<xref rid="B19-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>] and Re et al. [<xref rid="B20-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>], respectively. The ORAC assay was performed according to Ou et al. [<xref rid="B21-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>] using fluorescein [10 nM, excitation (485 nm)/emission (520 nm)], AAPH (240 mM) and Trolox (0.006–0.2 μmol/mL, R2 ≥ 0.95) as standard. All values were expressed in terms of micromole of Trolox equivalents (TE) per gram of dry weight (µmol TE/g dw). For lipophilic fractions, the oils were diluted in 2-propanol (5% <italic toggle="yes">v</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">v</italic>) and 1-butanol (1:25 <italic toggle="yes">v</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">v</italic>) for DPPH and ABTS assays, as suggested by Christodouleas et al. [<xref rid="B22-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>]. Foodinformatics</title><p>Major PMG phytochemicals [<italic toggle="yes">n</italic> = 16; ellagic acid (1), ellagic acid glucoside (2), ellagic acid-4-O-xylopiranoside (3), gallic acid (4), galloyl-6-O-glucoside (5), 1,3,6-Tri-O-Galloyl-D-Glucose (6), vanillic acid (7), D-(+)-catechin (8), phlorozin (9), phellatin (10), quercitrin (11), citric acid (12), cyanidin-3,5-O-diglucoside (13), Del-phnidin-3-O-glucoside (14), Delphinidin-3,5-O- diglucoside (15), and pelargonidin 3,5-O-diglucoside (16)] with the highest relative abundance (UPLC-DAD-MS<sup>2</sup>) were evaluated. Cheminformatics and bioactivity prediction data (including ADMET properties) were calculated using canonical SMILES (Simplified Molecular Input Line Entry Specification) sequences retrieved from the PubChem server (<uri xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" xlink:href="https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/">https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/</uri>; accessed date: 6 October 2022) and using well-established, validated, and public servers: admetSAR 2.0 (<uri xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" xlink:href="http://lmmd.ecust.edu.cn/admetsar2">http://lmmd.ecust.edu.cn/admetsar2</uri>; accessed date: 15 August 2022), SwissADME (<uri xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" xlink:href="http://www.swissadme.ch/index.php">http://www.swissadme.ch/index.php</uri>; accessed date: 15 August 2022) [<xref rid="B23-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>], and Molinspiration (<uri xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" xlink:href="https://www.molinspiration.com/cgi-bin/properties">https://www.molinspiration.com/cgi-bin/properties</uri>; accessed date: 15 August 2022).</p><p>Bioavailability radars and boiled-egg diagram [WLOGP (atomistic interpretation of the fragmental system of Wildman and Crippen: lipophilicity) vs. TPSA (topological polar surface area: apparent polarity) plot] were used to infer the gastrointestinal (GI) absorption and blood–brain barrier (BBB) permeation of all sixteen compounds. Two-dimensional chemical features were used to characterize the chemical space. The chemical space analysis focused on four physicochemical properties (PCP) of pharmaceutical relevance [octanol/H<sub>2</sub>O partition coefficient (cLog P), molecular weight (MW), TPSA, and solubility (Ali/ESOL)]. PCP-based clustering depicted as 3D-dynamic plots (PC1-PC3) was generated with DataWarrior version 5.2.1 [<xref rid="B24-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>]. Potential protein targets (cancer-related, other diseases) for each compound were predicted with SwissTargetPrediction 2019 (<uri xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" xlink:href="http://www.swisstargetprediction.ch/">http://www.swisstargetprediction.ch/</uri>; accessed date: 15 August 2022), and their linkage with cancer and other diseases was confirmed with UniProt [<xref rid="B25-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>] and Pharos [<xref rid="B26-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>] databases.</p></sec><sec id="sec2dot9-biomolecules-12-01649"><title>2.9.
