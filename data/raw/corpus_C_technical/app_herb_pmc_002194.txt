The alkali-soluble polysaccharide from <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> showed better effects on the proliferation of lactic acid bacteria and Bifidobacteria during the fermentation than the water-soluble polysaccharide, which is mainly attributed to its higher level of total sugar, uronic acid, glucose, and mannose as well as the lower level of sugar [<xref rid="B112" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>]. Hence, more attention should be paid to investigations of structure-activity relationships of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> polysaccharides [<xref rid="B113" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>]. On the other hand, more efforts are now being made on the identification and characterization of the structural features and compositions of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> polysaccharide fractions, but few of them have been standardized and developed as individual ingredients or drugs for extensive pharmacological research, which might hinder the definition of mechanism and clinical application. Additionally, the quality of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> is susceptible to multiple factors like the cultivation origin, processing, and storage procedures. In particular, the processing methods are used to extract and purify <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic>, and it might result in the modification of the chemical structure or degradation of active compounds, negatively affecting their bioavailability.</p><p>Moreover, like most herbal medicines, the pharmacokinetic, absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion studies of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> are rarely documented, and the present pharmacokinetic studies mainly concentrated on several herbal medicines, like curcumin, ginseng, and ginger [<xref rid="B114" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>, <xref rid="B115" ref-type="bibr">115</xref>]. However, these studies are essential for modern drug development and clinical application. The effective dose levels, tissue distribution, and metabolites of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> are important elements for its bioactivities and action targets, which should be further analyzed by pharmacokinetic, absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion studies [<xref rid="B116" ref-type="bibr">116</xref>].</p><p>Although <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> has a long history of being used as formulations in folk, clinical study about its individual effects on human health is still scarce and limited. More detailed and large-scale clinical trials are warranted to assess its bioactivities and therapeutical effects on different diseases.</p></sec><sec id="sec7"><title>7. Conclusion</title><p>
<italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> has been widely used as a functional food and herbal medicine for preventing and managing many disorders. The phytochemical studies showed that <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> contains abundant bioactive compounds, such as bibenzyls, polysaccharides, flavonoids, and alkaloids. The experimental investigations revealed that <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> exerted antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and immune-regulatory properties. It had a diversity of pharmaceutical effects like anticancer, antidiabetes, gastrointestinal modulatory, cardiovascular protective, hepatoprotective, lung protective, and neuroprotective activities. Hence, <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> could be considered the potential agent of adjuvant supplements for disease treatment. However, most studies focused on crude polysaccharides as the major medicinal compound, and few new components were purified for investigations. Although <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> has been used for a long time in folk, detailed and large-scale clinical studies are still warranted to demonstrate the pharmacological effects and mechanisms in humans. In addition, more investigations combining different modern technologies are needed for better control of the quality and safety of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic>.</p></sec></body><back><ack><title>Acknowledgments</title><p>This work was supported by the Gala Family Trust (200007008), Contract Research (260007830 and 260007482), Health and
