In conclusion, processing has a marked influence on the chemical constituents of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic>, and the fact that the toxicity of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum preparata</italic> is lower than that of the crude drugs may be associated with the decreased levels of some of the components after processing.</p></sec></sec><sec id="s0055"><label>5</label><title>Pharmacodynamics and potential applications</title><sec id="s0060"><label>5.1</label><title>Anti-aging effect</title><p id="p0100">Age is the leading risk factor for many of the most prevalent and devastating diseases, including Alzheimer׳s disease (AD) and Parkinson׳s disease (PD). AD is a neurodegenerative disease characterized by progressive memory loss and cognitive impairment and is the most common type of dementia in the aging population (<xref rid="bib34" ref-type="bibr">Long and Dougherty., 2003</xref>). PD is a common progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by the loss of specific populations of neurons and the accumulation of protein aggregates in the brain. The disease affects more than 1% of the population over the age of 60, and as the population ages, this frequency is likely to increase (<xref rid="bib36" ref-type="bibr">Fahad et al., 2014</xref>, <xref rid="bib60" ref-type="bibr">Lang and Lozano, 1998</xref>, <xref rid="bib120" ref-type="bibr">Obeso et al., 2010</xref>). The main effect of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> is anti-aging given that it can be used to treat AD and PD. Specifically, 2,3,5,4′-tetrahydroxystilbene-2-O-β-<sc>d</sc>-glucoside (TSG), one of the effective components of this plant, may be able to treat AD and PD (<xref rid="bib69" ref-type="bibr">Li et al., 2010b</xref>, <xref rid="bib204" ref-type="bibr">Zhang et al., 2010</xref>, <xref rid="bib133" ref-type="bibr">Su et al., 2014</xref>). <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> and TSG may potentially treat AD and PD through the following mechanisms: inhibition of acetylcholinesterase (AChE), neuroprotection, antioxidant activity and cognitive enhancement.</p><sec id="s0065"><label>5.1.1</label><title>AChE inhibition</title><p id="p0105">AChE inhibitors are commonly used to treat AD. TSG can decrease AChE activity and increase the expression of protein phosphatase-2A (PP-2A) and microtubule associated protein-2 (MAP-2) in the hippocampus of model rats following the i.g. administration of 30, 60 and 120 mg/kg/day for 11 weeks (<xref rid="bib92" ref-type="bibr">Liu et al., 2008a</xref>). Emodin-8-O-β-<sc>d</sc>-glucopyranoside (EG), another effective component of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic>, was tested <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> against AChE I zymogen (extracted from the mice cerebral cortex) at doses ranging from 23.1 to 92.6 μmol/mL, and the results indicate that EG can inhibit AChE I activity. In addition, <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> AChE I activity could also be inhibited by EG (0.5–80 mg/kg/day, p.o., once or every day for a period of 15 days), and this inhibition was reversible (<xref rid="bib19" ref-type="bibr">Chen et al., 2001a</xref>). Previous studies have also demonstrated that <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> extracts can treat insomnia and brain function disorders by inhibiting the activity of AChE. These experiments were conducted on a 96-well plate assay using Ellman׳s method. The AChE activity inhibition percentages of aqueous and ethanol <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> extracts at doses of 20 μg/mL were 59.88 and 47.24, respectively (<xref rid="bib85" ref-type="bibr">Lin et al., 2008</xref>). <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> aqueous extract, which was administered p.o. to mice for 28 days, obviously improve the cognitive deficits induced by Aβ25-35 on mice. And, the acetylcholinesterase and GPx activity were increased, the TBARS level was decreased in mice brain after administration for 4 weeks. These effects might be related to the antioxidant activity of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> (<xref rid="bib146" ref-type="bibr">Um et al., 2006</xref>).</p></sec><sec id="s0070"><label>5.1.2</label><title>Neuroprotective effect</title><p id="p0110">Many studies suggested that JNK played an important role in the mediation of MPP<sup>+</sup>-induced neurotoxicity, and a research demonstrated the TSG could protect against MPP<sup>+</sup>-induced apoptosis in a dose-dependent manner (1, 5 and 10 μM) in PC12 cells.
