Similarly, leaves of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> are sold as herbal medicines in herbal medicine informal markets in Tanzania [<xref rid="B7-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">Euclea natalensis</italic> is a widely used as herbal medicine in South Africa and the species is an ingredient of a commercial herbal concoction or formula called <italic toggle="yes">imbiza</italic> [<xref rid="B8-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>]. The <italic toggle="yes">imbiza</italic> formula or prescription is in clinical use, sold in informal markets, medicinal herbal markets and pharmacies in South Africa. <italic toggle="yes">Imbiza</italic> is a general term for a class of purgative medicines which affect internal cleansing system, often administered as a vaginal douche, a drink or an emetic [<xref rid="B9-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Imbiza</italic> has also gained popularity in South Africa as an immune booster and as a tonic used to treat and manage various minor and chronic illnesses [<xref rid="B10-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Imbiza</italic> is used as herbal concoction for ailments such as colds, chest infections, skin infections, diabetes, tuberculosis, cancer and symptoms of human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) and acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS) [<xref rid="B11-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. Traditional healers in South Africa prescribe <italic toggle="yes">imbiza</italic> for women’s fertility problems, as a blood purifier, scrofula and for chest complaints [<xref rid="B8-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>,<xref rid="B9-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Imbiza</italic> is also traditionally used to facilitate pregnancy by preparing the uterus to accept a fetus [<xref rid="B9-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. Apart from <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">imbiza</italic> also contain roots of <italic toggle="yes">Polygala fruticosa</italic> P. J. Bergius, <italic toggle="yes">Raphionacme</italic> spp., bulbous roots of <italic toggle="yes">Crinum</italic> spp. and <italic toggle="yes">Cyrtanthus obliquus</italic> (L. f.) Aiton and the root barks of <italic toggle="yes">Zanthoxylum capense</italic> (Thunb.) Harv., <italic toggle="yes">Capparis tomentosa</italic> Lam. and <italic toggle="yes">Rauvolfia caffra</italic> Sond. [<xref rid="B8-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>]. While the cultural, medicinal and ethnopharmacological values of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> have received considerable attention in the last 50 years [<xref rid="B1-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B2-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="B3-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B4-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B8-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>,<xref rid="B12-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>,<xref rid="B13-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>,<xref rid="B14-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>,<xref rid="B15-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>,<xref rid="B16-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>,<xref rid="B17-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>,<xref rid="B18-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>,<xref rid="B19-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>,<xref rid="B20-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>,<xref rid="B21-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>], no attempt has been made to review literature on the medicinal potential, phytochemistry and ethnopharmacological properties. Therefore, the aim of the current review is to comprehensively document information on the botany, medicinal uses, phytochemistry and biological activities of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> to understand its ethnopharmacological value as traditional herbal medicine.</p></sec><sec id="sec2-molecules-22-02128"><title>2. Research Methodology</title><p>To identify relevant information on the botany, medicinal uses, phytochemistry and biological activities of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic>, a review was compiled based on scientific literature from various sources including Google Scholar, Web of Science, SciFinder, Scopus, Science Direct, PubMed, Scielo, Springerlink, Google Patents, Espacenet, BioMed Central (BMC) and Medline. The keywords used for identification of relevant data included different scientific name and synonyms, common English names, and the terms: biological activities, medicinal uses, ethnobotany, ethnopharmacology, medicinal, pharmacology, phytochemistry and therapeutic value, <italic toggle="yes">Euclea natalensis, Euclea multiflora, Royena macrophylla</italic>, Natal guarri, Natal ebony and large-leaved guarri.
