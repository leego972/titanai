Furthermore, eremophil-1(10),11(13)-dien-12,8β-olide,8-epi-xanthatin-1β,5β-epoxide and tomentosin were isolated from the aerial parts of <italic toggle="yes">X. strumarium</italic>, and their anti-tumor activities on BGC-823 cells and KE-97 cells were aslo determined. The related results showed that the IC<sub>50</sub> values of three compounds on BGC-823 cells are 13.22, 2.43, and 4.54 µM, respectively. Similarly, IC<sub>50</sub> values of three compounds on BGC-823 cells are 4.41, 1.44, and 3.47 µM, respectively [<xref rid="B77-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>]. Moreover, Zhang et al. reported that xanthatin (3.9–18.6 µM) inhibited the proliferation of MKN-45 cells by inducing G2/M cell cycle arrest and apoptosis [<xref rid="B78-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">78</xref>]. Later, in 2015, Karmakar et al. found that xanthinosin (8 µM) and lasidiol p-methoxybenzoate (16 µM) potentiate both extrinsic and intrinsic TRAIL-mediated apoptosis pathways and also decreased the level of cell survival protein Bcl-2 in AGS cells [<xref rid="B20-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>]. Simultaneously, fructusnoid C (IC<sub>50</sub> = 7.6 µM) also reported to exhibit cytotoxic effects on AGS cells [<xref rid="B79-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">79</xref>]. EEXA and CFEEXA have been identified as the active ingredients against the growth of CT26 cells with IC<sub>50</sub> values of 58.9 and 25.3 μg/mL, respectively [<xref rid="B70-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>].</p><p>Furthermore, the anti-tumor effects of <italic toggle="yes">X. strumarium</italic> on liver cancers have also been reported in recent years. In 2013, Wang et al. found that the 1β-hydroxyl-5α-chloro-8-epi-xanthatin possessed significant in vitro cytotoxicity with an IC<sub>50</sub> value of 5.1 µM against SNU387 cells [<xref rid="B25-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>]. Later, in 2017, the cytotoxic effects of MEX and EAFMEX on HepG2 cells were verified as LC<sub>50</sub> (Lethal Concentration 50) values of 112.9 and 68.739 μg/mL [<xref rid="B80-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>]. Furthermore, Liu et al. demonstrated that xanthatin (5–40 μM) can induce HepG2 cells apoptosis by inhibiting thioredoxin reductase and eliciting oxidative stress [<xref rid="B76-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>].</p><p>Additionally, an investigation in 1995 indicated that Xanthatin and 8-epi-xanthatin both have cytotoxic effects on SK-MEL-2 cells with ED<sub>50</sub> values 0.5 and 0.2 μg/mL, respectively [<xref rid="B17-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>]. In 2012, the EEXS showed notable inhibitory activity on Mel-Ab cells through downregulation of tyrosinase via GSK3β phosphorylation at concentrations of 1–50 μg/mL [<xref rid="B81-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>]. Later, in 2013, Li et al. reported the anti-tumor effects of xanthatin both in vitro and in vivo. Previous results showed that xanthatin (2.5–40 μM) possess a remarkable anti-proliferative effect against B16-F10 cells, and the related mechanism probably associated with activation of Wnt/β-catenin pathway as well as inhibition of angiogenesis. Meanwhile, the in vivo evidence in mice (xanthatin, 0.1–0.4 mg/10 g, i.p.) also verified the results mentioned above [<xref rid="B82-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">82</xref>]. </p><p>In 1994, DFEEXA was reported to be toxic to leukemia P-388 cells with an IC<sub>50</sub> value of 1.64 μg/mL [<xref rid="B83-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">83</xref>]. In addition, results of Nibret et al. showed that xanthatin has significant cytotoxic on HL-60 cells in 2011 [<xref rid="B84-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">84</xref>]. Another report in 2017 reported that both MEX and EAFMEX have inhibitory effects on Jurkat cells, and EAFMEX showed higher toxicity to Jurkat cells when compared to MEX [<xref rid="B80-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>]. </p><p>Besides, in 1995, Ahn et al. found that xanthatin and 8-epi-xanthatin have cytotoxic effects on CNS carcinoma XF-498 cells, and the ED<sub>50</sub> values were 1.7 and 1.3 μg/mL, respectively [<xref rid="B17-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>]. In 2013, Pan et al. reported that WEX can cause significant cytotoxic effects on arcoma S180 cells in vivo (S180 cells bearing mice, 5–20 g/kg) [<xref rid="B85-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">85</xref>]. The in vitro anti-proliferative activity of CEXR and MEXR on laryngeal cancer HEP-2 cells were implemented at doses of 12.5–100 µg/mL, and the two extracts of <italic toggle="yes">X. strumarium</italic> showed potent cytotoxic activities against the HEP-2 cells [<xref rid="B86-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot3-molecules-24-00359"><title>5.3. Anti-Inflammatory and Analgesic Effects</title><p>In 2004, it was reported that WEX (10, 100 and 1000 µg/mL) inhibited inflammatory responses in Lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-stimulated mouse peritoneal macrophages via decreasing IFN-γ, LPS-induced NO production and TNF-α production in a dose dependent manner [<xref rid="B87-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">87</xref>].
