Quality Assessment and Risk of Bias of Included Articles</title><p>The quality assessment and risk of bias evaluation using the “Risk of Bias in Non-Randomized Studies of Interventions” (ROBINS-I) tool included 17 studies, as reported in <xref rid="app1-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="app">Table S3</xref>. This evaluation assessed six domains (D1–D6) across multiple studies. Bias due to confounding (D1): all studies effectively controlled for potential confounding factors by implementing strategies such as randomization, and clearly defined inclusion and exclusion criteria. This ensured that the results were reliable and attributable to the interventions being tested. Bias arising from measurement of the exposure (D2): the measurement process was consistently standardized across the studies, resulting in a low risk of bias in this domain. Bias in the selection of participants (D3): most studies maintained a low risk by employing proper selection processes. However, a few studies showed some concerns, suggesting minor issues in participant recruitment or allocation. Bias due to post-exposure interventions (D4): all studies effectively standardized post-exposure interventions, resulting in a consistently low risk of bias in this domain. Bias due to missing data (D5): some studies were flagged for missing data, leading to some concerns. This highlights areas where dropouts or incomplete data handling could potentially influence the outcomes. Bias arising from the measurement of the outcome (D6): most studies demonstrated low risk in this domain, utilizing validated and objective outcome measures. However, a few instances of some concerns were noted.</p><p>The majority of the studies demonstrated a low overall risk of bias, reflecting methodological rigor and reliable findings. Nonetheless, a few areas with some concerns, particularly regarding missing data and participant selection, highlight opportunities for improved reporting and data handling in future research.</p></sec></sec><sec sec-type="discussion" id="sec4-nutrients-17-00762"><title>4. Discussion</title><p>PD and gingivitis represent chronic inflammatory conditions primarily caused by microbial dysbiosis and exacerbated by oxidative stress [<xref rid="B130-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">130</xref>,<xref rid="B131-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">131</xref>,<xref rid="B132-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">132</xref>,<xref rid="B133-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">133</xref>,<xref rid="B134-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">134</xref>]. Conventional treatments, including SRP, are often augmented with chemotherapeutics or antioxidants to enhance outcomes. The integration of natural and plant-based products as adjunctive therapies has gained substantial attention due to their antimicrobial, antioxidant, and anti-inflammatory properties. This review synthesizes findings from 17 studies investigating the efficacy of various natural compounds and formulations in managing periodontal conditions [<xref rid="B135-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">135</xref>,<xref rid="B136-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">136</xref>,<xref rid="B137-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">137</xref>,<xref rid="B138-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">138</xref>,<xref rid="B139-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">139</xref>,<xref rid="B140-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">140</xref>,<xref rid="B141-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">141</xref>,<xref rid="B142-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">142</xref>,<xref rid="B143-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">143</xref>,<xref rid="B144-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">144</xref>,<xref rid="B145-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">145</xref>,<xref rid="B146-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">146</xref>].
