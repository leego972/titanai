Introduction</title><p> <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> is a perennial flowering plant belonging to the Crassulaceae family. It grows naturally in the wild at high altitude and can be found in the Arctic and in mountainous areas of Central Asia and Europe [<xref rid="B1" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>]. Extracts from <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> are considered as adaptogens (EMEA/HMPC/102655/2007). Adaptogens are nontoxic agents that increase resistance against physical, chemical, biological, and psychological stressors by normalizing the harmful effect of these stressors independently of the nature of the pathologic state. In this way, they help the organism to achieve a physiological equilibrium and homeostasis during stressful situations [<xref rid="B2" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>, <xref rid="B3" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>]. Historically, <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> had been used by Vikings to increase their endurance and physical strength [<xref rid="B4" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>]. Clinical studies have indicated beneficial effects of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> extracts (including the WS®1375 extract) in stress and fatigue management as well as in building resilience [<xref rid="B5" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>–<xref rid="B13" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>]. RRE has also shown antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and neuroprotective effects in animal [<xref rid="B14" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>–<xref rid="B17" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>] and cellular models [<xref rid="B18" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>]. Phytochemically, extracts of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> roots and rhizomes contain mainly six classes of compounds: flavonoids, phenolic acids, phenylethanoloids, phenylpropanoids, monoterpenes, and triterpenes. Salidroside, a phenylethanol glycoside, is considered as the main pharmacologically active compound of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic>, although it is also found in many other <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> species. On the contrary, some cinnamic alcohol glycosides such as rosavin, rosarin, and rosin have been solely found in <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic>. These compounds are referred to as “rosavins” and are represented by the phenylpropanoid rosavin [<xref rid="B1" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>]. Both salidroside and rosavin represent the lead marker compounds of the RRE. Extracts of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> rhizomes and roots have been characterized as “traditional herbal medicinal product for temporary relief of symptoms of stress, such as fatigue and sensation of weakness” by the European Medicines Agency (EMA) (EMEA/HMPC/102655/2007). Although there is already published research on the individual RRE compound markers [<xref rid="B19" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>–<xref rid="B22" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>], limited studies have examined cellular and molecular mechanisms underlying the action of the whole RRE using in depth in vitro methods.</p><p>Stress is defined as a state of threatened or recognized as threatened homeostasis.
