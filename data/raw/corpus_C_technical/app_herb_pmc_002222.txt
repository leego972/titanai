Plant-derived BACs have gained considerable attention as potential anticancer agents, given their longstanding role in drug discovery. Approximately 68% of small-molecule anticancer drugs developed between 1940 and 2014 were derived from or influenced by natural products [<xref rid="B8-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>]. These compounds can induce apoptosis, inhibit proliferation, modulate oxidative stress and inflammatory pathways, and suppress angiogenesis and metastasis, while often displaying lower toxicity toward normal cells [<xref rid="B1-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B2-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="B3-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B4-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B5-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>,<xref rid="B6-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="B9-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>,<xref rid="B10-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>,<xref rid="B11-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>,<xref rid="B12-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>,<xref rid="B13-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>,<xref rid="B14-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>]. Compounds such as curcumin, resveratrol, and epigallocatechin gallate exemplify this therapeutic potential. However, their effectiveness depends not only on bioactivity but also on efficient extraction methods that enable their recovery and stability. Plant matrices contain structurally diverse metabolites, often present at low concentrations, making extraction a critical determinant of phytochemical yield and functionality [<xref rid="B13-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>,<xref rid="B15-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>]. Solvent-based techniques including maceration, percolation, decoction, infusion, Soxhlet extraction, and hydro-distillation remain widely used due to their simplicity and scalability. However, these methods are often associated with long extraction times, high solvent consumption, and degradation of thermolabile compounds [<xref rid="B1-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B2-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="B11-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>,<xref rid="B16-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>,<xref rid="B17-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>,<xref rid="B18-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>,<xref rid="B19-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>,<xref rid="B20-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>,<xref rid="B21-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>,<xref rid="B22-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>].</p><p>Advanced extraction technologies such as ultrasound-assisted extraction (UAE), microwave-assisted extraction), accelerated solvent extraction, enzyme-assisted extraction, supercritical fluid extraction, pressurized liquid extraction, high hydrostatic pressure extraction, pulsed electric field extraction, and green solvent systems offer significant advantages such as higher extraction efficiencies and yields, shorter extraction durations, reduced use of organic solvents, and better preservation of efficacy of thermolabile metabolites under milder operating conditions [<xref rid="B15-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>,<xref rid="B23-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>]. By improving the recovery of key phytochemicals, these advanced methods can enhance the potency of plant extracts and ensure that the resulting compounds retain their desired anticancer properties. Despite these advantages, their adoption remains limited due to cost, operational complexity, and technical requirements [<xref rid="B15-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>,<xref rid="B23-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>]. Consequently, solvent-based extraction methods continue to dominate, even though extraction conditions strongly influence phytochemical composition and bioactivity. The current literature remains fragmented, with substantial variability in reported anticancer activity even for the same plant species. Such inconsistencies arise from differences in extraction parameters, solvent properties, and plant matrix characteristics, which govern the selective recovery of BACs [<xref rid="B24-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>,<xref rid="B25-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>,<xref rid="B26-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>,<xref rid="B27-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>].
