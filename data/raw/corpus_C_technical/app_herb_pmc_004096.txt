This combination suppresses excessive cortisol and nitric oxide production under stress conditions, thereby restoring neuroendocrine homeostasis [<xref rid="B1-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>]. Rosavins also increase ATP levels and modulate proteasome activity, promoting the clearance of pathological proteins such as α-synuclein [<xref rid="B12-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>].</p><p>Randomized clinical trials have demonstrated that <italic toggle="yes">R. rosea</italic> (200–680 mg/day of extract) significantly reduces fatigue symptoms in patients with occupational burnout and improves cognitive function in individuals exposed to psychological stress [<xref rid="B1-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>]. In double-blind, placebo-controlled studies, <italic toggle="yes">R. rosea</italic> reduced salivary cortisol levels by 14–30% after four weeks of supplementation while simultaneously improving attention, working memory, and reaction speed [<xref rid="B1-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>]. Meta-analyses confirm a moderate adaptogenic effect of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> in reducing fatigue (SMD = −0.69) and improving stress-related parameters without significant adverse effects [<xref rid="B1-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>].</p><p>Additionally, <italic toggle="yes">R. rosea</italic> modulates immune function by increasing the activity of natural killer (NK) cells and T lymphocytes, supporting its adaptogenic effects under conditions of stress-induced immunosuppression [<xref rid="B6-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>]. Animal studies have demonstrated protection against cerebral ischemia via regulation of the PI3K/PKB/Nrf2/NF-κB pathway, independent of complement component C3 activity [<xref rid="B13-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>]. These multitarget mechanisms provide a mechanistic basis for the clinical efficacy of <italic toggle="yes">R. rosea</italic> in the treatment of anxiety disorders, depression, and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) [<xref rid="B1-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec3dot2-nutrients-18-00931"><title>3.2. Schisandra chinensis (Turcz.) Baill.</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Schisandra chinensis</italic> (Turcz.) Baill., known as <italic toggle="yes">wu wei zi</italic> in Traditional Chinese Medicine, is an adaptogen containing more than 50 dibenzocyclooctadiene lignans, among which schisandrin B (Sch B) is the principal active constituent (<xref rid="nutrients-18-00931-f002" ref-type="fig">Figure 2</xref>) [<xref rid="B14-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>,<xref rid="B15-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>]. Schisandrin B exhibits potent neuroprotective effects associated with the stimulation of heat shock protein 70 (Hsp70) expression in healthy neuronal cells [<xref rid="B1-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B14-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>]. This hormetic effect of schisandrin B is based on an adaptive enhancement of cellular resistance to oxidative stress via Hsp70 induction, thereby preventing apoptosis and neuronal degeneration in models of aging and neurodegenerative diseases [<xref rid="B1-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B14-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>].</p><p>The stimulation of Hsp70 by schisandrin B is linked to improvement of mitochondrial glutathione status (mtGSH), increased activity of antioxidant enzymes, and enhanced ATP production [<xref rid="B1-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B14-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>]. Schisandrin B alleviates age-related impairment of mitochondrial oxidative capacity by protecting complexes I and II of the electron transport chain from oxidative damage [<xref rid="B1-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>]. In Alzheimer’s disease models, schisandrin B inhibits β-amyloid accumulation through suppression of the RAGE/NF-κB/MAPK signaling pathway and by increasing the expression of Hsp proteins and beclin-1, key markers of autophagy [<xref rid="B16-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">S. chinensis</italic>, including schisandrin B, influences the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway, modulating autophagy and conferring protection against atherosclerosis through the reduction of oxidative stress and inflammation [<xref rid="B17-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>,<xref rid="B18-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>].
