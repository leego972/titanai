The main mechanisms underlying such interactions are via pharmacokinetic modulations such as inhibition or induction of drug-metabolizing enzymes and transporters. Among them, the inhibition of cytochrome P450 (CYP), a representative drug-metabolizing enzyme, is considered as one of the most frequent causes for herb–drug interactions [<xref rid="B11-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>,<xref rid="B12-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>]. Therefore, evaluating the inhibition of CYP enzyme activity by herbal and herb-derived medicine is vital to predict any possible pharmacokinetic interactions with conventional drugs and to characterize their safety profile.</p><p>Due to its properties as a respiratory remedy and a traditional herbal medicine, <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extracts are very likely to be used as an adjuvant to conventional drugs in treating various diseases accompanied by respiratory disorders. In this context, it is necessary to investigate and characterize the drug interactions with <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extracts to ensure safe use. It has been reported that liver enzymes are the major metabolizing enzymes to convert the principal bioactive constituents of <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> to the secondary metabolites [<xref rid="B13-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>,<xref rid="B14-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>]. In two in vivo interaction studies [<xref rid="B15-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>,<xref rid="B16-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>], subcutaneously administered α-hederin influenced P450 enzymes in a dose-dependent manner, but no clinical relevance was expected from the results, as the IC<sub>50</sub> values are high in comparison with its bioavailability [<xref rid="B14-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>]. However, to our knowledge, no previous studies have investigated how <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> whole extracts affect CYP enzyme activity. Here, we examined the inhibitory effects of <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extract (as a whole) and its major bioactive constituent HDC on CYP450-mediated drug metabolism using human liver microsomes and individual recombinant CYP isozymes.</p></sec><sec id="sec2-molecules-22-01241"><title>2. Results</title><sec id="sec2dot1-molecules-22-01241"><title>2.1. CYP Inhibition Assay in Pooled Human Liver Microsomes</title><p>We investigated the inhibitory effect of <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extract on CYP enzymes in pooled human liver microsomes. The CYP inhibition assay system was confirmed with the following well-known CYP-selective inhibitors: furafylline for CYP1A2, methoxsalen for CYP2A6, quercetin for CYP2C8, sulfaphenazole for CYP2C9, ticlopidine for CYP2C19, quinidine for CYP2D6, and ketoconazole for CYP3A4. Each of these inhibitors reduced the formation of each corresponding CYP-specific metabolite by &gt;95%, indicating that the assay system was functioning well. The activities of seven CYP isozymes were tested with various concentrations of <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extracts, and the amount of metabolite produced at each concentration was measured. <xref ref-type="fig" rid="molecules-22-01241-f001">Figure 1</xref> presents the representative multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) chromatograms of the control and <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extract/HDC-treated human liver microsome samples. Notably, <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extracts showed significant inhibitory activity against CYP2C8, CYP2C19, and CYP2D6 enzyme activity in a concentration-dependent manner (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="molecules-22-01241-f002">Figure 2</xref>A,C). The IC<sub>50</sub> values of the extract against CYP2C8, CYP2C19 and CYP2D6 were 0.13 ± 0.01, 1.04 ± 0.06 and 7.41 ± 0.09 mg/mL, respectively. The inhibitory effects of the extracts on the other CYP isozymes were negligible at all the concentrations tested. As HDC is a known principal bioactive component of the <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extract, its effects on CYPs were also investigated. The resulting data showed slight inhibition of the CYP2C8 isozyme (18%) by HDC (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="molecules-22-01241-f002">Figure 2</xref>B,D), indicating that HDC is not primarily responsible for the CYP inhibition of <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extract.</p></sec><sec id="sec2dot2-molecules-22-01241"><title>2.2. CYP Inhibition Assay in cDNA-Expressed Recombinant CYP Isozymes</title><p>The results of the initial assessment with human liver microsomes revealed that <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extract significantly inhibited the enzyme activities of all three enzymes. Accordingly, the CYP inhibitory effect of <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extract was further investigated using the cDNA-expressed recombinant CYP isozymes for CYP2C8, CYP2C19, and CYP2D6.
