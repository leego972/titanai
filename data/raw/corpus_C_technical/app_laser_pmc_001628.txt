In comparison, commercially available probes are, for example, 1.5 kg (Envisu; Bioptigen, Morrisville, NC, USA), while a recent report pointed out MEMS-based probes with a minimum mass of 0.42 kg.<sup><xref rid="bibr27-0954411914543963" ref-type="bibr">27</xref></sup> However, the latter costs higher and also require post-acquisition image correction due to the sinusoidal regime of the MEMS scanners. In contrast, the GSs are less costly, their frequency can be adjusted, and they can employ triangular scanning (linear on the active portions of the scan), which provides distortion-free images.<sup><xref rid="bibr32-0954411914543963" ref-type="bibr">32</xref></sup> They are also employed anyway in most OCT setups to achieve lateral scanning; therefore, the effort to construct these configurations is minimal.</p><p>The testing of the probes has been done in the ENT Department of the Northwick Park Hospital in London (UK), for the OCT investigations of the ear, nose, and throat—as it has been presented in a former study,<sup><xref rid="bibr30-0954411914543963" ref-type="bibr">30</xref></sup> and in the Imaging Group of the <italic toggle="yes">Victor Babes</italic> University of Medicine and Pharmacy of Timisoara (Timisoara/Timis, Romania), in dentistry applications—as it will be presented in an example in the next section of this study. It has thus been demonstrated that the probes are light enough and easy to handle for a significant duration (a few hours) in a medical environment throughout the day.</p><p>The material chosen was easy to process using conventional mechanical technologies: turning, milling, cutting, drilling, and wire cutting, the latter for the more complex components (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig3-0954411914543963">Figure 3</xref>, (13)) and (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig3-0954411914543963">Figure 3</xref>, (0)). Manufacturing with high precisions was possible on the active surfaces of the probe (e.g. on the inner cylindrical surface of the mount in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig4-0954411914543963">Figure 4(d)</xref>). The robust design has allowed for the heat resistance, the structural integrity, and the maintaining of the imposed tolerances over more than 1 year of functioning of the product. A similar conclusion is expected for the lifetime of the probes, of at least 3–4 years. One also has to remark that because of the concept and of the simplicity of the handheld probes, they can be employed with any type of TD-, SD-, or SS-OCT systems.</p><fig id="fig4-0954411914543963" position="float" orientation="portrait"><label>Figure 4.</label><caption><p>Mounts designed and manufactured to accommodate different types and sizes of GSs in the different handheld probes developed (a) for the first variant of handheld probe (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig1-0954411914543963">Figure 1</xref>) and for a Cambridge Technology (Bedford, MA, USA) GS with a 4 mm aperture of the galvo mirror and (b) for a GS of the same type, but with a 5 mm aperture. This part is also a heatsinker for the scanning device. The design was made to avoid the heat dissipation to propagate through the entire probe to affect the patient or the person who handles the probe.</p><p>
<italic toggle="yes">Design and tolerances</italic>: In the <italic toggle="yes">concept</italic> of the assembly, the handle of the probe is distinct from its main part where not only 1D but also 2D GSs, as well as different lenses (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig1-0954411914543963">Figures 1</xref><xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig2-0954411914543963"/>–<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig3-0954411914543963">3</xref>, (12)), may be used in the tube (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig1-0954411914543963">Figures 1</xref><xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig2-0954411914543963"/>–<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig3-0954411914543963">3</xref>, (11)) to focus the laser beam at different distances, for example, for different parts of the body. This issue appears when switching from imaging the skin to imaging the ear<sup><xref rid="bibr29-0954411914543963" ref-type="bibr">29</xref></sup> or the oral cavity (as it will be presented in the next section). The flexibility of the design thus allows for different functions and complexities of the probe: with a 1D GS B-scans are obtained (as shown in the following section); with a system of dual-axis 2D GSs mounted on the handle (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig3-0954411914543963">Figure 3</xref>, (0)), both 3D reconstructions of the samples and C-scans (<italic toggle="yes">en face</italic> images) can be obtained.<sup><xref rid="bibr3-0954411914543963" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>, <xref rid="bibr35-0954411914543963" ref-type="bibr">35</xref></sup>
