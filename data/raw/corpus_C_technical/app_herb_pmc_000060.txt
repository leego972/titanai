The anti-tumorigenic activity of SQ40 was successfully demonstrated in the mouse xenograft model [<xref rid="B142-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">142</xref>].</p><p>Recently, Hajjouli <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> concluded that <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> constituents, eurycomanone and eurycomanol are the regulators of signaling pathways involved in proliferation, cell death and inflammation. Both eurycomanone and eurycomanol inhibited Jurkat and K562 cell viability and proliferation without affecting healthy cells. Furthermore, eurycomanone inhibited NF-κB signaling pathway through inhibition of IκBα phosphorylation and upstream MAPK (mitogen activated protein kinase) signaling. Eurycomanone and eurycomanol present differential toxicity towards leukemia cells, and eurycomanone having the α,β-unsaturated ketone could be prerequisite for the NF-κB inhibition [<xref rid="B143-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">143</xref>].</p><p>Wnt signaling regulates various processes such as cell proliferation, differentiation, and embryo development. 9-hydroxycanthin-6-one, decreased the expression of Wnt signal target genes, mitf and zic2a, through the activation of GSK3β independent of CK1α [<xref rid="B144-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">144</xref>].</p><p>The quassinoids isolated from <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> have been studied for thir <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> cytotoxicities against KB cells derived from human epidermoid carcinoma of the nasopharynx [<xref rid="B140-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">140</xref>]. Itokawa <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, isolated a new squalene-type triterpene, named eurylene, from <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> which were found to be cytotoxic [<xref rid="B53-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>]. Chan <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, isolated a new C19 quassinoid 6α-hydroxyeurycomalactone from the roots of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> and have reported that the cytotoxic activity of these quassinoids was not mediated through DNA cleaving properties [<xref rid="B49-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>].</p><p>Chronic myelocytic leukemia (CML) is a malignant disease of the human hematopoietic stem cell which is characterized by a marked increase in granulocytes bone marrow hyperplasia and splenomegaly [<xref rid="B145-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">145</xref>]. CML accounts for 15–20 percent of all leukemias [<xref rid="B145-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">145</xref>,<xref rid="B146-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">146</xref>] with a worldwide incidence of 1–2/100,000 [<xref rid="B147-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">147</xref>,<xref rid="B148-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">148</xref>,<xref rid="B149-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">149</xref>]. The various isolates and purified eurycomane, an active compound from the roots of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic>, were examined for their cytotoxic effect in K-562 cells isolated from patients with chronic myelocytic leukaemia (CML).</p><p>Al-Salahi <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, assessed the <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> anti-proliferative and apoptotic potentials of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> on K-562 leukemic cell line. Intraperitoneal administration of TAF273 (<italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> fraction, 50 mg/kg) resulted in a significant growth inhibition of subcutaneous tumor. TAF273 showed potent anti-proliferative activity <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> models of Chronic Myelogenous Leukemia (CML) and therefore, justifies further efforts to define more clearly the potential benefits of using TAF273 as a novel therapeutic strategy for CML management [<xref rid="B150-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">150</xref>]. The cytotoxic activity of quassinoids was not found to be mediated through DNA cleaving properties [<xref rid="B49-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">In vitro</italic>, the anticancer effects of a fraction of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> were due to apoptosis via a caspase-9 and p53-independent manner [<xref rid="B151-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">151</xref>] that perhaps involved Bcl-2 protein [<xref rid="B152-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">152</xref>].</p><p>Angiogenesis, a process of formation of new branches of blood vessels, is strongly implicated in several important physiological situations [<xref rid="B153-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">153</xref>,<xref rid="B154-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">154</xref>].
