It can generate high-resolution cross-sectional images that minimize patient discomfort, which is particularly useful in trauma cases.<sup>
<xref rid="bibr11-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>
</sup> Other diagnostic methods such as slit-lamp examination, ultrasound biomicroscopy (UBM), gonioscopy, and specular microscopy have significant limitations. Compared with AS-OCT, UBM offers lower resolution images of the anterior segment due to its shorter wavelength, resulting in increased scatter and signal loss.<sup>
<xref rid="bibr12-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>
</sup> Moreover, it requires direct contact with the eye, which is particularly uncomfortable for trauma patients.<sup>
<xref rid="bibr13-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>
</sup> Gonioscopy and slit-lamp examinations are operator dependent and limited by corneal clarity and patient cooperation.<sup>
<xref rid="bibr14-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>
</sup> Finally, the most significant limitation of specular microscopy is its inability to fully visualize the anterior segment.<sup>
<xref rid="bibr15-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>
</sup> In contrast, AS-OCT excels in detecting subtle structural changes.<sup>
<xref rid="bibr16-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>
</sup> It is particularly useful when corneal edema, opacities, or hyphema obstruct the view, allowing clear evaluation of the underlying pathology.<sup>
<xref rid="bibr16-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>
</sup> AS-OCT has become an important instrument in the evaluation of anterior segment anomalies, overcoming the aforementioned limitations and improving diagnosis and treatment outcomes.<sup><xref rid="bibr11-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">11</xref><xref rid="bibr12-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr13-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr14-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr15-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr16-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr17-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr18-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">18</xref></sup></p><p>There have been significant technological advancements in the field of AS-OCT. The earliest form of AS-OCT was time-domain OCT (TD-OCT), which used a moving reference mirror to create images.<sup>
<xref rid="bibr19-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>
</sup> Although it is currently uncommon in clinical practice due to its lower resolution and acquisition speeds, TD-OCT was the predecessor to the newer devices being used today.<sup>
<xref rid="bibr19-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>
</sup> Spectral-domain OCT (SD-OCT) replaced this moving mirror with a stationary spectrometer. This change allowed visibility of the anterior and posterior segments, with improved axial resolution and faster image acquisition.<sup>
<xref rid="bibr19-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>
</sup> The most advanced form of AS-OCT is swept-source OCT (SS-OCT). SS-OCT uses a tunable laser source that enables deeper tissue penetration due to its longer wavelength and faster scanning speeds than SD-OCT.<sup><xref rid="bibr19-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>,<xref rid="bibr20-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">20</xref></sup> It is particularly useful for clearly visualizing the entire anterior segment and deeper anatomic structures.<sup>
<xref rid="bibr21-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>
</sup></p><p>As a consequence of these technological advancements, AS-OCT has demonstrated diagnostic utility in various ocular conditions. However, its application in ocular emergencies, particularly ocular trauma and infectious keratitis, has not been comprehensively reviewed. This narrative review aimed to provide an overview of the diagnostic and clinical utility of AS-OCT in these cases. A literature search was conducted using the PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science databases, with a focus on studies related to AS-OCT use in acute anterior segment pathologies. No specific quality assessment of the studies was performed.</p></sec><sec sec-type="methods" id="sec2-03000605251382298"><title>Methods</title><p>A narrative literature review was conducted to evaluate the clinical applications of AS-OCT in ocular trauma and emergencies. No formalized reporting guideline or protocol was followed for this review. A comprehensive search was performed via the PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science databases using a combination of keywords, including “Anterior Segment Optical Coherence Tomography,” “AS-OCT,” “ocular trauma,” “corneal laceration,” “foreign body,” “traumatic cataract,” “angle recession,” “hyphema,” “infectious keratitis,” “limbal ischemia,” “chemical burn and injury,” “posterior capsular rupture,” “extraocular muscle injury,” and “keratitis.” Studies published in English were considered, with no restrictions on publication year.
