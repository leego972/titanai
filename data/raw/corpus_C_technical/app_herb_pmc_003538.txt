# PMC: ethnobotany medicinal plants Africa Asia — Overview

Menz Keya (11.54%), Andracha (11.48%), Ensaro (12.59–15.79%), Bita (12.60%), Mana Angetu (14.72%), Sodo (14.29%), and Yeki (14.02%) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR24">24</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR33">33</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR35">35</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR44">44</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR81">81</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR87">87</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR101">101</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR146">146</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR172">172</xref>]. Many of these areas are situated in humid highland or forested agro-ecological zones that differ substantially from the dryland ecology of Sheikh District. The limited overlap therefore reflects both ecological dissimilarity and differences in local ethnomedical traditions.</p><p id="Par217">The strong similarity between Sheikh District and nearby dryland areas such as Dire Dawa, Jigjiga town, Jigjiga district, Babile, and Dirre [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR56">56</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR57">57</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR75">75</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR103">103</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR128">128</xref>] likely results from shared floristic zones, comparable rainfall regimes, and long-standing socio-cultural interactions that facilitate knowledge exchange. Likewise, the overlap observed with Turkana and Marsabit [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR39">39</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR106">106</xref>] supports the broader pattern that arid and semi-arid environments promote reliance on drought-tolerant woody species and shrubs with multipurpose medicinal value.</p><p id="Par218">The anomalously high JSI value reported for the Somali region (187.50%) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>] exceeds the theoretical maximum range of the Jaccard index (0–100%), indicating probable computational or reporting inconsistencies in the cited study. In the revised manuscript, this value is interpreted cautiously and not used as a principal benchmark for comparison.</p><p id="Par219">Thus, the Jaccard similarity analysis demonstrates that Sheikh District shares the strongest medicinal plant affinities with geographically proximate and ecologically comparable dryland and savannah regions, while exhibiting limited overlap with humid forest ecosystems. These findings underscore the ecological specificity of ethnobotanical knowledge and highlight the importance of site-specific documentation and locally adapted conservation strategies for sustaining medicinal plant diversity and associated cultural heritage.</p><p id="Par220">
