With PD being a major global health issue, exacerbated by microbial dysbiosis and oxidative stress, the integration of phytochemicals and herbal formulations into periodontal therapy offers a promising avenue for adjunctive treatments. Methods: A systematic review was conducted following PRISMA guidelines and registered under the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (ID: 641944). Databases, including PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science, were searched between 18–24 December 2024, using Boolean keywords combining terms such as “herbal medicine”, “plant extracts”, “anti-inflammatory”, and “periodontal therapy”. Studies involving animal models, in vitro data, or non-peer-reviewed articles were excluded. Results: Seventeen studies met inclusion criteria. Polyherbal formulations and single-component extracts (e.g., <italic toggle="yes">Camellia sinensis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Punica granatum</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Zingiber officinale</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Rosmarinus officinalis</italic>) demonstrated comparable efficacy to conventional agents like chlorhexidine (CHX). Polyherbal rinses, <italic toggle="yes">camellia sinensis</italic> gels, and extracts like <italic toggle="yes">Punica granatum</italic> reduced inflammation, improved gingival health, and showed antimicrobial properties, offering effective natural alternatives. Conclusions: Natural products, including single extracts and polyherbal formulations, provide effective and safe alternatives for managing gingivitis and PD. Their anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and antioxidant properties support their adjunctive role alongside with scaling and root planning therapy (SRP) in periodontal therapy. However, further large-scale, long-term studies are needed to standardize formulations and establish optimal protocols.</p></abstract><kwd-group><kwd>herbal medicine</kwd><kwd>plant
