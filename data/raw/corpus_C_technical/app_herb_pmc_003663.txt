Moreover, people who lived in humid climates and engaged in agriculture, typically had a variety of musculoskeletal system diseases, such as rheumatism [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR52">52</xref>], traumatic injuries, and other diseases, as do the Chuanqing people.</p></sec><sec id="Sec17"><title>Comparison with ChP, the QSG, and Southeast Asian medicines</title><p id="Par34">The existing studies on the Chuanqing people showed that they were closely related to the Han and local ethnic groups in Guizhou such as the Miao in their social culture [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR20">20</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR21">21</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR53">53</xref>] or medical research [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR54">54</xref>]. Eleven medicinal plants were also recorded in the ChP, the QSG, and the Chuanqing people’s medicine simultaneously, suggesting that the diseases treated with medicinal plants by the Chuanqing people were similar to those found among the ChP and the QSG. For example, <italic toggle="yes">Aconitum carmichaelii</italic> Debeaux was used to treat noxious sores and had the function of restoring yang for resuscitation; <ext-link xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" ext-link-type="uri" xlink:href="http://www.theplantlist.org/tpl1.1/record/kew-2516864"><italic toggle="yes">Tinospora sagittata</italic> Gagnep</ext-link>. was used to treat neck pain, laryngitis, dysentery, and abdominal pain; and <italic toggle="yes">Ligusticum striatum</italic> DC. was used to relieve pain by people of ChP, QSG, and the Chuanqing people. However, the diseases treated with some medicinal plants of the Chuanqing people were different from those of both ChP and QSG. For example, <italic toggle="yes">Cynanchum paniculatum</italic> (Bunge) Kitag. ex H.Hara was used to relieve pain in ChP and QSG, but it was used to treat gynecopathy in the Chuanqing people’s medicine and some researchers found that it could treat gynecological inflammation disease because of its anti-inflammatory properties [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR55">55</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Gleditsia sinensis</italic> Lam. was used to treat osteodynia, and arthralgia rather than psychiatric disorders in ChP and QSG, and some studies found that it also had analgesic effects [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR56">56</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Lysionotus pauciflorus</italic> Maxim. was first found to treat rheumatism, and <italic toggle="yes">Paris polyphylla</italic> Sm was first found to treat cardiopathy. <italic toggle="yes">Sanguisorba officinalis</italic> L. was first found to treat diarrhea in Chuanqing people; this treatment had been corroborated in the reports about being used to treat diarrhea of humans and livestock [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR57">57</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR59">59</xref>].</p><p id="Par35">Generally, after thousands of years of development, TCM had formed a mature theoretical system, such as “the theory of Four Qi and Five Flavors, the theory of Visceral Manifestation, and the theory of Yin-Yang and Five Elements.” According to our survey, the Chuanqing people’s medicine was still in the stage of summarizing specific knowledge and experience, such as circulated in the form of rhymes.</p><p id="Par36">Southwest China is adjacent to the Southeast Asian countries of Vietnam, Laos, Myanmar, and Thailand [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR60">60</xref>]. Historically, they had often exchanged cultural practices and medicine. The traditional medicines in Southeast Asian countries were deeply influenced by TCM [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR61">61</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR64">64</xref>]. The Chuanqing people in southwestern China were also influenced by traditional Chinese medicine [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR65">65</xref>]. Therefore, the Chuanqing people and the people of Southeast Asian countries may have certain similarities in their use of medicinal plants.
