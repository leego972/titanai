Their results showed that pigmented orange contained the highest amount of vitamin C (46 mg/100 g fresh weight (fw)), whereas navels were high in hesperidin (73.8 mg/100 g fw), and satsumes had the greatest amount of narirutin (27.6 mg/100 g fw). Similarly, Bermejo et al.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref128">128</xref></sup> showed that, among different cultivars of citrus fruits such as
Clementine mandarin, Satsume mandarin, Navel orange, and Common orange,
Satsume mandarin had the highest amount of flavanone glycosides (hesperidin)
and carotenoid content (β-cryptoxanthin) with 55.82 mg/g dw
and 1278 μg/100 g dw, respectively. However, they concluded
that the vitamin C content did not significantly change among different
cultivars. Al-Juhaimi<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref129">129</xref></sup> reported similar
results with Orlando orange, Kinnow mandarine, and Eureka lemon by
means of vitamin C content. Additionally, both pulp and peel of the
citrus fruits were analyzed, and only Eureka lemon was found to contain
more phenolics in its pulp compared to the peel, just like Gorinstein
et al.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref130">130</xref></sup> reported that, compared to peeled
fruits, peels of grapefruit, orange, and lemons contained higher amounts
of polyphenols, 155, 179, and 190 mg/100 g fresh fruits, respectively. Similar results were also reported by Kim et al.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref116">116</xref></sup> They also concluded that peels of citrus fruits are rich
in both dietary fibers and phenolic contents, and therefore they might
be utilized conveniently in the food industry for different purposes.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref116">116</xref></sup></p><p>Conventional extraction methods as discussed
above have the limitations
of being time-consuming, toxicity arising from the solvents, and high
maintenance costs, and therefore researchers have turned toward greener
applications to terminate organic solvent use and decrease extraction
time and cost as well as to increase extraction efficiency.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref42">42</xref></sup> As a novel, green extraction method, the ultrasonic
extraction method is examined in different studies. Compared to other
technologies such as microwave and supercritical fluid extraction,
ultrasonic extraction is reported as being a cheaper and simpler method
for utilization. On the one hand, Sun et al.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref131">131</xref></sup> analyzed <italic toggle="yes">Citrus succosa</italic> Hort peels and reported
that, although ethanol provides a poor extraction yield for β-carotene
under conventional extraction (CE), when it is used along with ultrasound
extraction, higher extraction yields were obtained. On the other hand,
dichloromethane caused the degradation of all <italic toggle="yes">trans</italic>-β-carotene under the ultrasound treatment. Another study,
in which the peels of <italic toggle="yes">C. sinensis</italic> L. Osbeck were
examined, showed that ionic salts (ILs), which are known for their
tunable properties and biocompatibility, have a great potential for
carotenoid extraction when assisted with ultrasound. Among four different
ILs, methylimidazolium chloride was found to be the most efficient,
leading a carotenoid content of 32.08 ± 2.05 μg/g dw.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref132">132</xref></sup> Lately, Saini et al.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref133">133</xref></sup> performed a study on the optimization of ultrasonic parameters for
lutein extraction from <italic toggle="yes">Citrus reticulata</italic>, and they
concluded that time, amplitude, and temperature have significantly
affected the extraction yield. Ultrasonic extraction was also compared
with the EAE method, and the results indicated that, although EAE
provided an improved extraction yield, UAE recovered greater amounts
of total phenolics and flavonoids compared to the conventional methods.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref134">134</xref></sup> However, a combined application of UAE and
EAE further enhanced the total flavonoids and phenolic content.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref135">135</xref></sup> When MAE was compared with UAE, MAE resulted
in higher phenolic acid recovery than UAE and CE.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref136">136</xref></sup> MAE also had the advantage of reducing energy consumption
up to 27 times in comparison to the hydrodistillation method, which
is a conventionally used technique for recovery.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref137">137</xref></sup> The SFE technique is responsible for higher yields of bioactive
compounds by utilizing nontoxic carbon dioxide; however, lower diffusion
rates cause longer extraction times of the bioactive compounds into
the supercritical fluid.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref138">138</xref></sup> Lately, Priyadarshani
et al.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref139">139</xref></sup> reported up to 93% lycopene yield
by SFE from <italic toggle="yes">Citrus paradisi</italic> endocarp, depending on
the pressure, extraction time, and their interaction.</p><p>Food fortification
is described as an addition of micronutrients
to various food matrices to improve their nutritional value, which
is widely studied for citrus byproducts.
