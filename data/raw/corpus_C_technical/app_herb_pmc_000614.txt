Antiviral Activity</title><p>In 2009, it was reported that the WEX (0.01, 0.1 and 1.0 g/kg, i.g., for 10 days) possessed antiviral activity against duck hepatitis B virus, and it can delay pathological changes [<xref rid="B125-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">125</xref>]. In addition, five compounds were isolated from the fruits of <italic toggle="yes">X. strumarium</italic>, and their antiviral abilities were also evaluated. The results indicated that norxanthantolide F, 2-desoxy-6-epi-parthemollin, xanthatin, threo-guaiacylglycerol-8′-vanillic acid ether and caffeic acid ethyl ester exhibited notable activity against influenza A virus with IC<sub>50</sub> values of 6.4, 8.6, 8.4, 8.4 and 3.7 µM, respectively by a cytopathic effect (CPE) inhibition method [<xref rid="B13-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot10-molecules-24-00359"><title>5.10. Other Pharmacological Effects</title><p>Apart from the pharmacological effects displayed above, <italic toggle="yes">X. strumarium</italic> also possesses some other activities. In 2016, the CXT (10, 20, and 40 mg/kg, i.p.) isolated from fruits of <italic toggle="yes">X. strumarium</italic> showed significant anti-septic activity in animal models of Cecal ligation and puncture (CLP) operation. Meanwhile, the CXT can increase survival rates of septic mice induced by CLP and decrease TNF-α and IL-6 levels induced by LPS in serum of mice [<xref rid="B126-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">126</xref>]. After treatment with WEX (570 and 1140 mg/kg p.o., for 6 weeks), the glucose tolerance and insulin sensitivity improved, meanwhile, lipogenesis increases and lipid oxidation decreased in the liver of high-fat diet rats [<xref rid="B127-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">127</xref>]. In 2014, Lin et al. demonstrated that the EEX (75 and 300 mg/kg, p.o.) can significantly inhibit paw swelling and arthritic score and increase body weight loss and decrease the thymus index in animal model of rheumatoid arthritis induced by Complete Freund’s Adjuvant (CFA) [<xref rid="B128-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">128</xref>]. Moreover, the overproduction of TNF-α and IL-1β was notably suppressed in the serum of all EEX-treated rats. The anti-pyretic activity of MEXW (200 and 400 mg/kg, p.o.) was estimated on yeast induced hyperpyrexia, and it showed significant reduction in elevated body temperature [<xref rid="B129-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">129</xref>]. Using Maximal Electroshock (MES) and Pentylenetetrazole (PTZ) induced seizures models, the anticonvulsant activity of PEEXW was tested, and results showed that PEEXW can reduce the mean duration of extensor phase and delay onset of myoclonic spasm and clonic convulsion of treated groups at doses of 250 and 500 mg/kg [<xref rid="B130-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">130</xref>]. In 2016, Panigrah et al. explored the antiurolithiatic effect of HEEXB, and showed that HEEXB can restore the impairment induced by ethylene glycol including hyperoxaluria, crystalluria, hypocalciuria, polyurea, raised serum urea, creatinine, erythrocytic lipid peroxidise and nitric oxide, kidney calcium content as well as crystal deposition. The mechanism may be related to inhibition of various pathways involved in renal calcium oxalate formation, antioxidant property and down regulation of matrix glycoprotein, osteopontin (OPN) [<xref rid="B131-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">131</xref>]. A report in 2012 indicated the antiulcer effect of EEXL in pylorus ligation induced gastric ulcers, and its gastro-protective mechanism may be due to DNA repair, free radical scavenging and down regulation of oxidativenitrosative stress along with cytokines [<xref rid="B132-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">132</xref>].
