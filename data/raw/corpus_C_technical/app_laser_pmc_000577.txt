The Microneedle Photodynamic Therapy II trial was a randomized, single-blinded, split-face controlled, 2-arm clinical study where 32 patients with AKs on the face were randomized into two incubations arms, either 10- or 20-minute ALA incubation times, after pretreatment with a microneedle roller or a sham roller. The study results showed that PDT with microneedling pretreatment at a 20-minute ALA incubation time demonstrated equivalent efficacy regarding AK clearance compared to that of a conventional 1-hour ALA incubation time. The further benefit of accelerated treatment was that the procedure was almost painless [<xref rid="b37-dp1304a291" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>]. In a similar study, Torezan et al combined microneedling or ablative carbon dioxide lasers with MAL-PDT to increase photosensitizer penetration into the skin. The findings favored PDT with microneedling, as it generated superior cosmetic outcomes to conventional MAL-PDT for refining photodamaged skin [<xref rid="b38-dp1304a291" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>]. Vrani et al used ablative CO<sub>2</sub> fractional laser prior to MAL-PDT treatment with 1-hour incubation in 42 patients with two mirror cancerized areas of the face or scalp and compared it to conventional PDT [<xref rid="b43-dp1304a291" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>]. After one year of follow-up, the CO<sub>2</sub> fractional laser pretreatment results were equivalent to those of the conventional PDT while allowing for reduced photosensitizer occlusion time, results that were in line with previous studies [<xref rid="b43-dp1304a291" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>,<xref rid="b44-dp1304a291" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>]. Pires et al. added acoustic pressure wave ultrasound to the CO2-assisted MAL-PDT with a short incubation time for field cancerization. Overall, 638 AKs in 15 patients were treated either with MAL-PDT alone or with CO2 and acoustic wave ultrasound pretreatment. The efficacy of the novel protocol was reported to be equivalent to the conventional MAL-PDT [<xref rid="b42-dp1304a291" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>].</p><p>A randomized split-scalp study by Torezan et al suggested that topical application of calcipotriol could improve the efficiency of MAL-PDT by 30%, potentially because of the elevated PpIX levels [<xref rid="b39-dp1304a291" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>]. A bilaterally controlled trial of 17 patients proved that the pretreatment of skin areas with 5-FU for 6 days increased the PpIX levels two- to three-fold nearly after MAL-PDT leading to complete clearance by both enhanced photosensitizer accumulation and induction of p53 [<xref rid="b41-dp1304a291" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>]. With the application of 5-FU prior to PDT field treatment, the relative clearance rates of MAL-PDT increased from 45% to 75% at 3 months and from 39% to 67% at 6 months, respectively [<xref rid="b41-dp1304a291" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>]. A study of 58 patients showed that vitamin D3 deficiency could be an aggravating factor regarding the efficacy of PDT, leading to a reduction of effectiveness by nearly 20%. Meanwhile, the administration of high-dose vitamin D3 supplementation (10,000 IU daily for 5 or 14 days) before PDT treatment significantly increased the response rates from 54.4% to 72.5% [<xref rid="b40-dp1304a291" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>].</p><p>A recent study tested the efficacy of three physical pretreatment interventions compared to the standard treatment using daylight PDT. Forty patients were allocated either to standard DL-PDT, DL-PDT with microneedling, DL-PDT with CO2 laser or DL-PDT with microdermabrasion. When DL-PDT was combined with physical methods, it provided better clinical and histologic outcomes. The clearance of AKs was significantly greater 1 and 3 months post treatment with the employment of CO2 laser. Photorejuvenation was more apparent with CO2 laser and microdermabrasion pretreatment. However, only CO2 laser offered a substantial decrease in solar elastosis and an increase in collagen type 1 [<xref rid="b45-dp1304a291" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>]. Especially when it comes to organ-transplant recipients with resistant to conventional PDT lesions, adding ablative fractional laser along with DL-PDT increases the clearance rates with excellent tolerability than DL-PDT and conventional PDT alone [<xref rid="b46-dp1304a291" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>]. The regimens mentioned above appear particularly promising with good tolerance by the patients and could be considered under challenging cases for further promotion in the everyday clinical setting.</p><p>An interesting approach is inhibiting the development of precancerous lesions.
