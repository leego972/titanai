Informant consensus analysis demonstrated that some species were repeatedly cited across multiple informants, reflecting both shared cultural knowledge and strong perceived therapeutic reliability. High consensus values, particularly for <italic toggle="yes">Citrus × limon</italic> (73.6%), <italic toggle="yes">Vachellia bussei</italic> (62.3%), and <italic toggle="yes">Allium sativum</italic> (56.6%), indicate that these plants play a central role in local healthcare practices and may represent the most culturally and medicinally significant taxa in the study area (Table <xref rid="Tab5" ref-type="table">5</xref>). Similar patterns have been reported in Uganda and Kenya, where frequently cited species are closely associated with common health concerns such as malaria, gastrointestinal disorders, and respiratory infections [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR61">61</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR76">76</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR164">164</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR186">186</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR187">187</xref>].</p><p id="Par153">Repeated citations of species such as <italic toggle="yes">Citrullus colocynthis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Hydnora hanningtonii</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus hamur</italic> further underscore their cultural relevance and potential pharmacological importance. In ethnobotany, high informant consensus is widely considered a reliable indicator for prioritizing plants for bioactivity screening, as it reflects both the perceived efficacy reported by local practitioners and the consistency of knowledge across the community [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR188">188</xref>]. In Sheikh District, the prominence of <italic toggle="yes">Allium sativum</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Moringa stenopetala</italic> aligns with observations from Ethiopia and Tanzania, where these species are among the most frequently cited MPs in both markets and household healthcare systems [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR64">64</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR189">189</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR190">190</xref>].</p><p id="Par154">Interestingly, species with the highest informant consensus often exhibit multi-purpose uses. For example, <italic toggle="yes">Citrus × limon</italic> is applied for gastrointestinal ailments, respiratory infections, and as a natural antimicrobial, reflecting its versatility and strong local preference. This multifunctionality mirrors findings from Kenya and India, where plants with broad-spectrum applications tend to achieve higher citation frequencies due to accessibility, perceived effectiveness, and integration into everyday health practices [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR139">139</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR154">154</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR191">191</xref>].</p><p id="Par155">In contrast, species with moderate yet notable consensus, such as <italic toggle="yes">Commiphora myrrha</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Psidium guajava</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Commelina erecta</italic>, appear to be culturally valued for more specialized or seasonal applications. Similar trends have been documented in Nigeria and Pakistan, where certain MPs are reserved for specific or culturally significant conditions, resulting in reliable but lower informant consensus [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR6">6</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR88">88</xref>].</p><p id="Par156">From both conservation and pharmacological perspectives, species with high informant consensus represent priority targets for further research and sustainable management. Their repeated use and widespread recognition suggest therapeutic efficacy, yet al.so indicate a potential risk of overharvesting, particularly in areas where wild collection predominates. Studies from Tanzania, Cameroon, and Ethiopia emphasize the importance of integrating community knowledge with conservation strategies to safeguard both biodiversity and traditional healthcare resources [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR47">47</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR62">62</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR189">189</xref>].</p><p id="Par157">Thus, the informant consensus analysis in Sheikh District highlights plants of central cultural and medicinal importance, providing an evidence-based framework for prioritizing species in pharmacological investigations and conservation planning.
