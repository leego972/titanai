The resulting flavonoid radical obtained from the donation of hydrogen atoms is delocalized across the aromatic ring system, minimizing the reactivity and stabilizing compound.<xref rid="cit0108" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>,<xref rid="cit0109" ref-type="bibr">109</xref> This antioxidant buffering prevents the further propagation of oxidative reactions in biological membranes and cellular components. In addition to direct radical scavenging, <italic toggle="yes">P. suaveolens</italic> has been shown to modulate the activities of endogenous antioxidant enzymes. The extracts enhanced the expression and activity of SOD and catalase (CAT) enzymes, which are responsible for converting superoxide radicals to hydrogen peroxide, water, and oxygen.<xref rid="cit0110" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>,<xref rid="cit0111" ref-type="bibr">111</xref> This enzymatic defense mechanism reduces the ROS burden and preserves cellular redox homeostasis. Moreover, the tannins present in <italic toggle="yes">P. suaveolens</italic> may chelate transition metals such as Fe<sup>2</sup>⁺ and Cu<sup>2</sup>⁺, reducing the Fenton reaction and producing highly reactive hydroxyl radicals from hydrogen peroxide. Tannins contribute an additional layer of antioxidant protection owing to metal-catalyzed radical formation. Collectively, these biochemical and molecular processes support the strong antioxidant capacity of <italic toggle="yes">P. suaveolens</italic> and substantiate its traditional use in the prevention and treatment of oxidative stress-related ailments. The dual function of scavenging free radicals and enhancing enzymatic defenses positions <italic toggle="yes">P. suaveolens</italic> as a promising natural antioxidant candidate in phytopharmaceutical development.</p><p><italic toggle="yes">P. suaveolens</italic> exhibits potent antioxidant effects through direct radical scavenging and enhancement of endogenous defence systems. Polyphenolic compounds, such as flavonoids and tannins, donate hydrogen atoms to neutralize ROS such as superoxide anions (O<sub>2</sub><sup>−</sup>•) and hydroxyl radicals.<xref rid="cit0106" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>,<xref rid="cit0107" ref-type="bibr">107</xref>,<xref rid="cit0112" ref-type="bibr">112</xref> These actions are potentiated by the upregulation of antioxidant enzymes such as SOD, CAT, and glutathione peroxidase.
