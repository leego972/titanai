</sup></p><p>In cases of intrascleral foreign bodies, AS-OCT has been used to detect objects concealed behind subconjunctival hemorrhage or granuloma. Although UBM remains the gold standard, AS-OCT offers a less invasive and more accessible alternative, enabling effective visualization of foreign bodies within the sclera.<sup>
<xref rid="bibr52-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>
</sup> Additionally, AS-OCT has been utilized to assess deeper structures. For instance, it is used for detecting foreign bodies within the crystalline lens in suspected patients, particularly when slit-lamp examination cannot provide accurate results due to the presence of traumatic cataract.</p></sec></sec><sec id="sec8-03000605251382298"><title>Traumatic cataracts</title><p>Traumatic cataract is a significant cause of vision loss following ocular trauma, with a high estimated prevalence of 65%.<sup>
<xref rid="bibr53-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>
</sup> Both blunt and penetrating traumas can lead to the development of traumatic cataracts.<sup><xref rid="bibr53-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">53</xref><xref rid="bibr54-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr55-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">55</xref></sup> AS-OCT plays a crucial role in visualizing subtle anatomical and pathological lenticular changes, both preoperatively and intraoperatively.<sup><xref rid="bibr56-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">56</xref><xref rid="bibr57-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr58-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">58</xref></sup> It provides an objective grading of immature cataracts and detailed imaging of intralenticular structures, the posterior capsule, and zonular integrity in various types of cataracts, including traumatic and mature cataracts.<sup><xref rid="bibr59-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">59</xref><xref rid="bibr60-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr61-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr62-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">62</xref></sup></p><p>The detection of anterior lens capsule defects is a key predictor of future traumatic cataract development. However, identifying these defects using slit-lamp examination can be challenging, especially when the cornea is damaged. In such cases, AS-OCT proves to be an invaluable diagnostic tool.<sup>
<xref rid="bibr11-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>
</sup> It enables the objective assessment of lens opacity, which may directly impact vision loss, particularly in cases where corneal edema limits the effectiveness of slit-lamp biomicroscopy. Furthermore, AS-OCT allows the evaluation of the posterior capsule and zonular integrity, which is essential for aiding surgical planning, predicting intraoperative challenges, and determining the feasibility and type of intraocular lens (IOL) implantation.<sup><xref rid="bibr56-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>,<xref rid="bibr63-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>,<xref rid="bibr64-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">64</xref></sup> Without ancillary imaging techniques such as AS-OCT, it may not be possible to detect a posterior capsule tear behind an opacified lens (<xref rid="fig1-03000605251382298" ref-type="fig">Figure 1</xref>).<sup><xref rid="bibr65-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>,<xref rid="bibr66-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">66</xref></sup> Nonetheless, it is noteworthy that slit-lamp examination remains a more accessible, cost-effective, and dynamic assessment tool than AS-OCT.<sup>
