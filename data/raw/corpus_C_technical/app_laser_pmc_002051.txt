In addition, PBM therapy has been demonstrated to enhance local blood flow and lymphatic drainage, which can accelerate wound healing by delivering oxygen to the tissues and clearing away waste through the lymphatic system [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR13">13</xref>].</p><p id="Par4">There may be different effects on wound healing for different wavelengths. Adamskaya et al. demonstrated rats exposed to blue light (470 nm) had faster healing attributed to the effect of blue light on nitric oxide (NO) metabolism—including the release of NO from hemoglobin complexes, with better perfusion of tissues and release of NO from mitochondrial complexes, improving mitochondria recovery [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>]. Wang et al. analyzed the effect of four wavelengths (420 nm, 540 nm, 660 nm or 810 nm) at a dose of 3 J/cm<sup>2</sup> using human adipose-derived stem cells on osteogenic differentiation of stem cells [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR15">15</xref>]. They found that blue and green light had better impact on the osteoblast differentiation process (mechanism suggested to be due to activation of light-gated calcium channel) compared with red/NIR light [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR15">15</xref>]. Red/NIR light had greatest impact on stem cell proliferation (through activation of cytochrome c oxidase in mitochondria) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR15">15</xref>].</p><p id="Par5">In a study conducted by Trelles et al., the effect of 633 nm PBM therapy was investigated in 10 subjects who had undergone upper eyelid blepharoplasty [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR16">16</xref>]. Treatment sessions were 20 min each and performed immediately after and 48 h after surgery, and twice during the week following the surgery [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR16">16</xref>]. Edema, erythema and bruising were assessed by an independent plastic surgeon and pain, was assessed by the subjects [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR16">16</xref>]. The study found a significant difference between time to complete healing on the half of the face that was treated with LED light versus the untreated half of the face, with a mean of 13.5 ± 0.34 days for the treated side and 26.8 ± 0.49 days for the untreated side [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR16">16</xref>]. In addition, the study found statistically significant differences in edema, erythema, bruising, and pain scores between the two arms. Mean edema scores were 3.7 compared to 7.6, mean erythema scores were 3.7 compared to 7.6, mean bruising scores were 4.7 compared to 8.5, and mean pain scores were 2.9 compared to 6.9 for treated versus untreated sides respectively [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR16">16</xref>]. However, at 6 weeks post-blepharoplasty, there was no significant difference in skin quality between the LED treated and untreated sides [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR16">16</xref>].</p><p id="Par6">Ablative fractional laser (AFL) is used for photo-rejuvenation and treatment of scars [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR17">17</xref>]. While generally well tolerated, there is a healing phase of several days to a week or more during which patients may experience a sunburn type sensation, oozing, and discomfort. PBM therapy has the potential to improve healing post AFL. Le Duff et al. conducted a study to investigate the effects of different wavelengths on wound healing in humans in a randomized comparative intraindividual study [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR17">17</xref>]. Each of the ten participants, with skin types I-III, had treatment with placebo, 590, 630 nm or 850 nm PBM therapy to different test spots following AFL (CO<sub>2</sub>: 10,600 nm; Fraxel Re:Pair Solta Medical/Bausch Health companies—handpiece 135 μm, 15 mm, fluence 30 mJ, and density 30%) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR17">17</xref>]. The authors did not find a statistically significant difference in erythema or transepidermal water loss at day 3 post-laser between the placebo and PBM treated wounds for any of the tested wavelengths [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR17">17</xref>].</p><p id="Par7">Studies as described above have assessed the effect of a specific wavelength on wound healing, though there is little research on the effect of a combination of wavelengths. The objective of the current study was to evaluate the effect of a device with a combination of blue (465 nm), red (640 nm and near infrared (880 nm) LED PBM therapy on wound healing after application of low-density AFL to bilateral inner biceps following up for a period of 55 days. Given the three wavelengths act through different mechanisms to improve wound healing, our hypothesis was LED PBM therapy would result in less post treatment pain and faster healing after AFL<bold>.</bold></p></sec><sec id="Sec2"><title>Methods</title><p id="Par8">This study was approved by the UC Irvine Investigational review board.
