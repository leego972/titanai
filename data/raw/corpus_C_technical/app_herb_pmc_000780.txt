A systems-based approach involves modulating a list of abnormally expressed genes in disease, in contrast to a traditional target-based approach which involves modulating the molecular state of one single protein (<xref rid="ref108" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>). This approach was first designed and introduced to the public by CMap to link drugs and diseases (<xref rid="ref13" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>). It defines a set of abnormally expressed genes in a disease, termed a disease signature, and queries it in the CMap reference database. It then searches for drugs that inversely regulate the expression of the disease signature, that is, those that decrease the expression of upregulated disease genes and increase the expression of downregulated disease genes. These drugs are considered candidates for reversing the diseased state back to the normal state.</p><p>Several studies using this approach have demonstrated its applicability to drug repositioning of herbal compounds (<xref rid="ref109" ref-type="bibr">109</xref>, <xref rid="ref110" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>). Luo <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>. (<xref rid="ref109" ref-type="bibr">109</xref>) have identified flavonoid luteolin as a therapeutic agent for ischemic stroke (IS) via inhibiting <italic toggle="yes">MMP9</italic> and activating PI3K/Akt signaling pathways. Flavonoids are common constituents of plants used in traditional herbal medicine to treat a wide range of diseases (<xref rid="ref111" ref-type="bibr">111</xref>). Luteolin was predicted by CMap analysis finding compounds that could regulate the expression of IS signature obtained from transcriptome data of IS patients. Further <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> experiments demonstrated that luteolin reduced the infarct volume in a rat model of IS. Similarly, Liu <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>. (<xref rid="ref110" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>) have utilized the CMap database to seek compounds that could mimic transcriptional changes induced by a variety of interventions to reduce endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress based on the observation that increased ER stress could develop leptin resistance and lead to obesity. As a result, they identified celastrol, a pentacyclic triterpene extracted from roots of <italic toggle="yes">Thunder of God Vine</italic> plant as a promising agent for treating obesity by increasing leptin sensitivity. They showed that celastrol could reduce ER stress and increase leptin sensitivity to reduce body weights of obese mice.</p><p>TCM102 database is also widely utilized in research to discover new indications of herbal compounds based on the systems-based approach (<xref rid="ref18" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>, <xref rid="ref19" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>). For example, Li <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>. (<xref rid="ref18" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>) have identified several vasodilators from TCM102 database. They first generated two gene signatures that involved in positive and negative regulation of blood vessel diameter by using databases GO and SEEK (search-based exploration of expression compendia) (<xref rid="ref112" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>). Compounds that inversely regulated the expression of these two gene signatures were then screened by performing GSEA on drug-induced transcriptome data in TCM102. Top 10 candidate compounds were tested for their vasorelaxant effects on vascular tension of constricted thoracic aortic rings in a rat model. Among these 10 compounds, ferulic acid exhibited the strongest vasorelaxant effect, and others also induced relaxation significantly. Finally, mechanisms of six compounds (ferulic acid, borneol, daidzin, magnolol, chenodeoxycholic acid, and artemisinin) were inferred from one integrated network representing pathways involving signature genes specifically regulated by these six compounds. As another example, Wang <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>. (<xref rid="ref19" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>) have utilized TCM102 database for the evaluation of phillyrin as an anti-cardiac fibrosis agent. Phillyrin was of interest because it was one of the major active ingredients of <italic toggle="yes">Forsythia suspensa</italic>, an herbal medicine used as an anti-inflammatory and antipyretic drug (<xref rid="ref113" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>). They first analyzed transcriptome data sets of human heart diseases and constructed a cardiac fibrosis (CF)-related gene functional module (CFGM), which had a set of genes containing three known CF markers, <italic toggle="yes">Postn</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Ddr2</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Pdgfra</italic>, and their co-expressed genes.
