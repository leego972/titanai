Also, there is a chance that the solubility and dissolution rates could be sped up, which would mean that there would be more of the drug near cancer cells [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR71">71</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="Sec24"><title>Antioxidant activity</title><p id="Par37">Natural antioxidants, such as those found in algae extracts, are gaining popularity as a potential alternative to synthetic antioxidants, which have been linked to a number of health problems. As a result, the DPPH and ABTS procedures were used to evaluate the antioxidant impact of ChlE and ChlE-NS [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR77">77</xref>]. These methodologies were chosen because of their low cost, high speed, and relative ease of use. The findings were shown in terms of percentage radical scavenging activity and IC<sub>50</sub> (ug/mL) values in Table <xref rid="Tab5" ref-type="table">5</xref> and Fig. <xref rid="Fig7" ref-type="fig">7</xref>. Both ChlE and ChlE-NS had a concentration-dependent antioxidant impact, as shown by their antioxidant activity compared to ChlE, ChlE-NS exhibited a higher scavenging capacity for both DPPH and ABTS radicles. ChlE-NS outperforms ChlE because, by reducing particle size to the nano-range, the concentration gradient and surface area are enhanced, resulting in a significant increase in dissolving velocity [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR78">78</xref>].
