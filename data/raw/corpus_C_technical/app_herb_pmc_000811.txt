Paris polyphylla</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Paris polyphylla</italic> (called “Love Apple”) belongs to family Liliaceae and contains 24 species throughout the world [<xref rid="B74-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">P. polyphylla</italic> is mostly used by Indian and Chinese traditional medicine system for having potential anticancer properties. <italic toggle="yes">P. polyphylla</italic> consists of important secondary metabolites such as polyphyllin D, formosanin C, β-ecdysterone, dioscin, daucosterol heptasaccharide, oligosaccharides, octasaccharide, protogracillin, trigofoenoside A, yunnanosides G-J, padelaoside B, pinnatasterone, and other saponins [<xref rid="B75-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>]. Steroidal saponins are the main active components because of its structural diversity and bio-activities such as antitumor, immune-stimulator, analgesic, and hemostatic properties [<xref rid="B76-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>,<xref rid="B77-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>,<xref rid="B78-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">78</xref>,<xref rid="B79-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">79</xref>,<xref rid="B80-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>,<xref rid="B81-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>].</p><p>Aqueous and ethanol extracts of <italic toggle="yes">P. polyphylla</italic> showed potential antitumor activity against human liver carcinoma (HepG2 and SMMC-7721) cell line, human gastric (BGC-823) cell line, human colon adenocarcinoma (LoVo and SW-116) cell line, and human esophagus adenocarcinoma (CaEs-17) cell lines. Ethanolic extract showed a strong inhibitory effect with IC<sub>50</sub> values ranging from 10 µg/mL to 30 µg/mL [<xref rid="B82-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">82</xref>]. Extract of <italic toggle="yes">P. polyphylla</italic> also showed an antitumor effect in esophageal cancer ECA109 cells by increasing the connexin26 mRNA and protein expression. Studies reported that <italic toggle="yes">P. polyphylla</italic> extracts increased the <italic toggle="yes">Bad</italic> genes expression and decreased the expression of <italic toggle="yes">Bcl-2</italic> genes, inhibiting the growth of ECA109 cells by proliferation and inducing cell apoptosis [<xref rid="B83-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">83</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec3dot11-biomolecules-10-00047"><title>3.11. Perilla frutescens</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Perilla frutescens</italic>, commonly called perilla or Korean perilla or Beefsteak plant, is widely distributed in Vietnam, China, Japan, and most Asian regions belong to the Labiatae family [<xref rid="B84-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">84</xref>,<xref rid="B85-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">85</xref>]. Economically, one of the most significant crops, cultivation of <italic toggle="yes">P. frutescens</italic> in China and some other Asian countries is more than 2000 years old [<xref rid="B86-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>,<xref rid="B87-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">87</xref>]. Stem, seed, and leaf parts of <italic toggle="yes">P. frutescens</italic> have been used to treat poisoning, cold, bloating, and headache [<xref rid="B74-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>,<xref rid="B88-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">88</xref>]). Multiple in vivo and in vitro studies have been conducted to evaluate the anticancer and antitumor potential of <italic toggle="yes">P. frutescens</italic>. Leaf extract of <italic toggle="yes">P. frutescens</italic> showed the highest anticancer activity in HepG2 cells through cell proliferation inhibition and upregulation of apoptosis-related gene expression [<xref rid="B89-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">89</xref>].
