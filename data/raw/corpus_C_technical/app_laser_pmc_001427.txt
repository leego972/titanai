Sawada and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr61-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">61</xref></sup> and Hirano and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr40-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">40</xref></sup> have directly compared wide-field OCTA to FA, showing a high sensitivity and specificity of OCTA for retinal nonperfusion areas (NPAs) and retinal NV. Both studies utilized 12 × 12 mm<sup>2</sup> SS-OCTA scans, although the study by Hirano and colleagues was enhanced with extended field imaging to offer an even greater field of view. In the report by Sawada and colleagues, the sensitivity and specificity of OCTA for NPA were 0.98 and 0.82, respectively, with the sensitivity and specificity for NV being 1.0 and 0.97, respectively. Hirano and colleagues reported a sensitivity and specificity of 0.96 and 1.0 for NPA and a sensitivity and specificity of 0.79 and 0.96 for NV, respectively. Separately, Schaal and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr62-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">62</xref></sup> compared widefield OCTA imaging to color fundus photography and reported an increased detection rate for intraretinal microvascular abnormalities with OCTA.</p></sec><sec id="section8-2515841419840249"><title>Age-related macular degeneration</title><p>OCTA is an effective tool for the identification of CNV associated with neovascular age-related macular degeneration (AMD),<sup><xref rid="bibr26-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>,<xref rid="bibr63-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">63</xref></sup> although reported sensitivities and specificities of OCTA have varied.<sup><xref rid="bibr64-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">64</xref></sup> This may be due to differing study distributions of type 1 and type 2 CNV, which appear to be more readily apparent on OCTA than Type 3 CNV and polypoidal complexes.<sup><xref rid="bibr64-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">64</xref></sup></p><p>Given its high level of image detail, OCTA has been advantageous in the study of the structure of CNV and its changes in response to antiangiogenic agents. Different morphologic appearances of CNV have been described, including ‘medusa’ and ‘sea-fan’ forms, which generally consist of smaller vessels radiating from a larger feeder vessel.<sup><xref rid="bibr65-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">65</xref></sup> These larger vessels remain stable despite changes in lesion size with antiangiogenic treatment.<sup><xref rid="bibr64-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">64</xref></sup></p><p>Multiple studies have been conducted to identify OCTA biomarkers for CNV activity. Souied and colleagues found that active CNV lesions requiring treatment were more likely to have a combination of high-risk features including a well-defined shape (lacy-wheel or sea-fan shaped), branching of many small capillaries, the appearance of anastomoses and loops, the presence of a peripheral arcade at the vessel termini, and the presence of a hypointense halo surrounding the lesion on OCTA.<sup><xref rid="bibr66-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">66</xref></sup> In their study of type 1 CNV, Al-Sheikh and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr67-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">67</xref></sup> similarly found that branching of small vessels and the presence of peripheral arcades were more prevalent in active <italic toggle="yes">versus</italic> quiescent lesions and also reported that fractal dimension (a measure of complexity of CNV lesions) was significantly different between active and quiescent lesions.
