The use of KR as an optogenetic tool [<xref rid="pone.0144617.ref006" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0144617.ref007" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>] and photosensitizer for PDT was proposed [<xref rid="pone.0144617.ref008" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>].</p><p>It has been shown in mammalian cell cultures that the mechanism of cell death depends especially on the intracellular location of the KR. For example, KR expressed in mitochondria induced apoptosis after illumination [<xref rid="pone.0144617.ref001" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>]. With KR localized in the plasma membrane the cells died through necrosis [<xref rid="pone.0144617.ref009" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>], while for KR located in a lysosome the cell death pathway depended on the light intensity and dose [<xref rid="pone.0144617.ref010" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>]. When KR was expressed in nuclei, exposure to light led to a blockage of cell division [<xref rid="pone.0144617.ref006" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>].</p><p>In our earlier studies we found that KR can be used to damage cancer cells <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic>. Substantial dystrophic cellular changes were observed in KR-expressing HeLa tumors inoculated subcutaneously into immunodeficient mice [<xref rid="pone.0144617.ref008" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>]. However, to achieve a cytotoxic effect a rather intensive treatment regimen was required: the tumors were exposed to illumination with a yellow continuous wave (CW) laser (593 nm, 150 mW/cm<sup>2</sup>, 270 J/cm<sup>2</sup>) daily for 7 days, which was only really feasible on slowly growing tumors. It is apparent that any further development of this approach for PDT would require optimization of the treatment regimen and the testing of it on other tumor models, including those transplanted into immunocompetent mice.</p><p>The current work was aimed at the selection of the treatment regimen for PDT of CT26 mouse tumors expressing KR. The treatment parameters were selected such that they would cause satisfactory photobleaching of the KR in subcutaneous tumors without any excessive temperature effects on the skin surface. The PDT was implemented at 593 nm, 150 mW/cm<sup>2</sup>, 270 J/cm<sup>2</sup> for the CW laser, and at 584 nm, 225 mW/cm<sup>2</sup>, 337 J/cm<sup>2</sup> for the pulsed laser on the days 6, 7, and 8 of tumor growth. Histological examination of the tumor tissue was performed on the day following the last irradiation and tumor growth was followed for a further two weeks.</p></sec><sec sec-type="materials|methods" id="sec002"><title>Materials and Methods</title><sec id="sec003"><title>Cell cultures</title><p>CT26, murine colon carcinoma, stably expressing KR in fusion with histone H2B (CT26-KR) and a non-expressing counterpart were used. The cell lines were cultured in DMEM with 10% FCS, 1% glutamine, 10 units/mL penicillin and 10 μg/mL streptomycin. The cells were collected for injection by adding 1 mL of trypsin-EDTA (25%) to the 25 mm<sup>2</sup> plate for 5 min at 37°C.</p><p>The CT26-KR cell line was obtained by lentiviral transduction. For lentiviral transduction a NheI-blunt PCR fragment containing the H2B–tKR open reading frame was cloned into a NheI- and EcoRV-digested pRRLSIN.EF1.WPRE vector with a modified multiple cloning site. The vector was kindly provided by Prof. Didier Trono (E´cole Polytechnique Fe´de´ rale de Lausanne, Lausanne, Switzerland). The lentiviral particles for mammalian cell infection were obtained according to standard procedure. For lentiviral infection, CT26 cells were plated on d = 35 mm cell culture dishes (SPL Life sciences, Korea) at a density of 2.5х10<sup>4</sup> cells/dish in DMEM with 10% FCS, 1% glutamine, 10 units/mL penicillin and 10 <italic toggle="yes">μ</italic>g/mL streptomycin. After 24 h culturing, the medium was changed for the medium with viral particles.</p></sec><sec id="sec004"><title>Analysis of fluorescence of CT26-KR cells</title><p>The fluorescence of the infected cells was analyzed 5–7 days post–infection, using flow cytometry. For flow cytometric analysis, cells were washed with PBS and re-suspended to the final density of 2 x 10<sup>5</sup> cells/mL. Analysis was carried out using Cytomics FC500 flow cytometer, equipped with an air-cooled argon-ion laser operating at 488 nm (Beckman Coulter). The following detection parameters were used: 6 mW laser power and 620 nm band pass filter (FL3 channel). A minimum of 5000 events were collected for each sample. The portion of the cell population with the highest fluorescence intensity was selected with FACS (brightest 30%).</p><p>For sterile cell sorting, 2×10<sup>6</sup> cells were re-suspended in PBS with 5% FCS at a density of 5×10<sup>5</sup> cells/mL. The cell suspension was then filtered through a 70 <italic toggle="yes">μ</italic>m nylon mesh cell strainer.
