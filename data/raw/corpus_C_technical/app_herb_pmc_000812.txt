Platycodon grandiflorus</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Platycodon grandifloras</italic>, commonly known as balloon flower, or Chinese bellflower, belongs to the family Campanulaceae, which is distributed through Northeast Asia. The rhizomes of <italic toggle="yes">P. grandiflorus</italic> are very effective and are used as a traditional medicine in China, North Korea, and Japan for treatment of different diseases like cough, sore throat, phlegm, and other ailments [<xref rid="B93-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">93</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">P. grandiflorus</italic> contains many biologically active compounds which include saponins, flavonoids, anthocyanins, phenolics, and polysaccharide. These compounds have significant immune-stimulatory [<xref rid="B94-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>], anti-inflammatory [<xref rid="B95-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>], hepatoprotective [<xref rid="B96-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>], and antitumor activities. The antitumor activity of <italic toggle="yes">P. grandifloras</italic> was shown in a dose-dependent manner by reducing PKC enhancement of matrix metallopeptidases (MmP-9 and MmP-2), which caused the death of HT-80 cells [<xref rid="B97-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>]. Yu and Kim [<xref rid="B98-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">98</xref>] isolated platycodin D from the root of <italic toggle="yes">P. grandiflorus</italic> and treated MCF-7 cells with a concentration of 5–100 µM which reduce cell viability and proliferation in a dose-dependent and time-dependent manner as compared with controlled cells. Induction of anticancer activity was observed because of caspase 8 and 9 activation and PARP cleavage. In addition, platycodin D upregulate cellular levels of protein Bax and Bcl-2 and downregulate the activation of caspase-9. Further, it also induces proteolytic activation of Bid (a protein of the proapoptotic Bcl-2 family).</p><p>Platycodin D is a triterpene saponin isolated from the roots of <italic toggle="yes">P. grandiflorus</italic> shows cytotoxic effects on the human leukemia cells. It inhibited telomerase activity and showed a cytotoxic effect in a dose-dependent manner with a concentration of 10–20 µM. This was shown to be achieved through downregulating the expression of human telomerase reverse transcriptase (hTERT). The results of the study by Kim et al. [<xref rid="B99-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>] showed that suppression of telomerase activity and cytotoxic effect on leukemia cells by platycodin D was through post-translational and transcriptional inhibition of hTERT.
