Synthetic DMARDs such as MTX, Hydroxychloroquine (HCQ), Sulfasalazine (SSZ), Clofazimine (CLO), Leflunomide (LEF), Actarit, and Azathioprine show various side effects including myelosuppression, hepatic cirrhosis, interstitial pneumonitis, and gastrointestinal disorders (Zhao et al. <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR120">2021a</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR121">b</xref>).</p><p id="Par20">Adalimumab and infliximab are the most commonly used biological DMARDs, which mimic the effects of natural biological agents generated by the immune system of the body by acting as TNF-α inhibitors (Barrios et al. <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR15">2017</xref>; Rein and Mueller <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR93">2017</xref>; Schoellnast et al. <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR98">2006</xref>). These frequently target the CD molecules of antigen-presenting cells, soluble cytokines, and B cells. The mechanism of action of biological DMARDs involves antagonism of TNF-α, IL-1 or IL-6 receptor. These agents reduce the formation of rheumatoid factors and antibodies, downregulate T cells activation and deplete B cells. Although these agents are effective, their usage is limited due to various associated side effects like hypersensitivity, renal disease, hypertension, cardiac complications, gastrointestinal damage, respiratory difficulty, ulcer, and immune suppression, leading to opportunistic infections.
