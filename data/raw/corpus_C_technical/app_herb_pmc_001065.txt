Other Activities</title><p>The ethanol extract of <italic toggle="yes">D. aegyptium</italic> revealed antifertility activity, evident through reduced sperm count after treatment as well as reduced weight of reproductive organs and serum hormonal levels [<xref rid="B216-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">216</xref>]. The compound(s) responsible for this activity are not known [<xref rid="B216-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">216</xref>]. Additionally, the ethanol extracts of this plant exhibited antipyretic activities in rats (300 mg/kg) [<xref rid="B182-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">182</xref>]. Daily oral treatment with aqueous extract of <italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic> (400 mg/kg) decreased the quantity of calcium oxalate deposited in the kidneys, resulting in anti-urolithiasis effects [<xref rid="B217-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">217</xref>]. Ojha and co-workers (2010) evaluated the anticoagulant activity of <italic toggle="yes">I cylindrica</italic> methanol extracts (100, 200 and 400 mg/kg) and concluded that this may act on the extrinsic cascade of clotting by binding to antithrombin [<xref rid="B218-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">218</xref>]. This extract increased prothrombin time significantly after first, second and third oral administration [<xref rid="B218-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">218</xref>]. The methanol extract of <italic toggle="yes">P. karka</italic> also showed central nervous system depressant activity throught the reduction of sleep latency and increased duration of sleep [<xref rid="B219-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">219</xref>]. The aqueous and ethanol extracts of <italic toggle="yes">S. spontaneum</italic> stems showed reduction in the motor activities of the rats tested, indicating central nervous system depressant properties [<xref rid="B220-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">220</xref>]. The methanol extract from <italic toggle="yes">I. cylindrica</italic> roots exhibited dose-dependent anthelmintic activity (10–80 mg/50 mL) against <italic toggle="yes">Pheretima nosthuma</italic> (earthworms) compared with the control anthelmintic drug [<xref rid="B221-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">221</xref>]. These extended lists of activities enlighten the great potential of halophytic grasses.</p></sec></sec><sec id="sec4-ijms-20-01067"><title>4. Conclusion, Discussion and Future Perspectives</title><p>In this review, we systematically went through the chemical composition and biological activities of halophytic grasses up to now. The identification of 300 compounds was summarized and present; among them, several chemical families were revealed (<xref ref-type="app" rid="app1-ijms-20-01067">Table S1</xref>). Nonetheless, research on halophytes from Poaceae is still in its infancy and needs to be supplemented with further investigation on chemical composition, since only 14% of the diversity of the <italic toggle="yes">taxa</italic> was studied regarding this aspect. Moreover, the available information regarding some of the studied species is still scarce. This is evidenced by the different degree of compounds identified among the <italic toggle="yes">taxa</italic> so far, but also in the number of chemical classes described. For instance, in <italic toggle="yes">B. dactyloides</italic>, only cinnamic, benzoic and other short chain carboxylic acids and derivatives were described. Similarly, in <italic toggle="yes">H. mucratom</italic>, only fatty acids and derivatives were reported and in <italic toggle="yes">S. pyramidalis</italic> solely carbohydrates. <italic toggle="yes">C. dactylon</italic> is the only species with far more detailed characterization of its chemical composition (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="ijms-20-01067-f002">Figure 2</xref>) with 11 chemical families reported. In addition, from a chemical point of view, most of the studies lack a structural identification by nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR). Even with scarce studies, it is evident that halophytic grasses exhibit a wide range of chemical families, as well as a great diversity of compounds (<xref ref-type="app" rid="app1-ijms-20-01067">Table S1</xref>), some of them being recognized for their biological activities with importance in pharmaceutical and nutritional areas. Nonetheless, only in-depth work on the phytochemical profile using modern chromatographic techniques such as high-performance liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (HPLC-MS) and gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS) as well as the isolation and characterization of the compounds will allow further speculation on their application in pharmaceutical and/or agrifood industry.
