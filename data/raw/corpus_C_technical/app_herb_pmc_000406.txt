In an enzyme-based assay, andrographolide inhibited M<sup>pro</sup> (the SARS-CoV-2 main protease), with an IC<sub>50</sub> of 15 µM, possibly through formation of a covalent bond with the active site at the Cys145 amino acid residue. It is postulated that this compound would attack the virus through multiple pathways, including viral entry, replication, protein synthesis, and protein expression. Andrographolide has also shown binding affinity with the S protein and the ACE2 receptor, hence, it may inhibit viral entry. This compound has been isolated from a fingerroot of <italic toggle="yes">Boesenbergia rotunda</italic> that is commonly used in Southeast Asia and China as a culinarily spice. The rhizome has various pharmacological properties, such as antibacterial, antitumor, anti-allergic, and antioxidant. Panduratin A, as a major active compound, has shown antiviral activity against HIV [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR41">41</xref>] and Dengue virus [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR42">42</xref>]. The study showed that the antiviral activity against SARS-CoV-2 was superior to the FDA-approved drug, hydroxychloroquine. The value of cytotoxicity against the normal Vero E6 cell line was 14.71 µM [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR26">26</xref>]. However, molecular targets for this compound have not been well explored.</p><p id="Par19">Baicalin and baicalein, active compounds isolated from <italic toggle="yes">Scutellaria baicalensis</italic>, are used in TCM for treatment of respiratory disorders, heat clearing, detoxification, fire purging, and viral diseases, including hepatitis. The herb has shown antitumor, antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and broad-spectrum antiviral activity against various viruses, such as Zika, HIV, DENV, and H1N1. Baicalin and baicalein have shown inhibitory activity against the 3CL<sup>pro</sup> main protease of SARS-CoV-2, with IC<sub>50</sub> values of 6.41 µM and 0.94 µM, respectively. The anti-SARS-CoV-2 activity of baicalein has been shown to be superior to that of baicalin [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR43">43</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR44">44</xref>]. In a cell assay model using SARS-Co-2-infected Vero E6 cells, baicalein exhibited IC<sub>50</sub> and EC<sub>50</sub> values of 0.39 and 2.94 µM, respectively. The CC<sub>50</sub> of baicalein against Vero E6 cells was &gt; 200 µM, which is categorized as low cytotoxicity. Baicalein reportedly has closed activity with chloroquine, with an EC<sub>50</sub> of 2.71 µM [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR43">43</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR45">45</xref>]. This compound reportedly inhibited viral replication by blocking 3CL<sup>pro</sup> via in an in vitro study, and a molecular docking study showed that the 6-OH and 7-OH of its structure interacted with a carbonyl group of Leu141 and an amide group of Gly143 of 3CL<sup>pro</sup>, respectively [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR43">43</xref>]. In addition, baicalein also has been reported to inhibit the RdRp SARS-CoV-2 in an in vitro assay. In the subsequent molecular docking study, inhibition was not predicted on the active site of the enzyme since it is not an analog of a nucleoside. An in silico study showed that the compound had binding affinities with the Asn705 and His133 residues of RdRp on the palm subdomain and nucleotidyltransferase, respectively [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR46">46</xref>].</p><p id="Par20">Furthermore, cardiac glycosides, including digoxin, digitoxin, and ouabain, also have shown good properties for treating COVID-19 disease.
