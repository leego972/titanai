and its <xref rid="sec014" ref-type="sec">Supporting Information</xref> files.</p></notes></front><body><sec sec-type="intro" id="sec006"><title>Introduction</title><p>Gonioscopy, developed in the 1800s [<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref001" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>], is essential for the diagnosis of certain types of glaucoma, particularly angle-closure glaucoma [<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref002" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref003" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>]. Angle-closure glaucoma is especially important in Asian countries, where it is more common, including in Japan, and less common among Caucasians [<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref004" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>].</p><p>Gonioscopy enables angle assessment via color imaging and facilitates static and dynamic examinations, making it useful for evaluating angle structures [<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref005" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>]. However, owing to the complexity of the procedure and the required proficiency, it is not commonly performed in clinical practice [<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref005" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>,<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref006" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>]. Moreover, the evaluation of gonioscopic features reportedly varies depending on examiner expertise [<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref007" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>]. Furthermore, capturing and storing numerous detailed gonioscopic images of the entire angle is difficult. These factors highlight the need for relatively noninvasive devices that can replace or supplement gonioscopy and can be operated by examiners such as orthoptists.</p><p>As alternatives to gonioscopy, anterior-segment optical coherence tomography (AS-OCT) and ultrasound biomicroscopy (UBM) can be performed noninvasively and are reportedly useful for evaluating the anterior chamber and iridocorneal angle structures [<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref008" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>–<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref011" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. In particular, AS-OCT is highly useful because of its short examination time. However, AS-OCT and UBM cannot assess trabecular meshwork pigmentation or distinguish between organic and functional angle closure. Therefore, these tests have not yet replaced gonioscopy.</p><p>The GS-1 Gonioscope (GS-1; NIDEK, Gamagori, Japan) is a device capable of automatically capturing color photographs of the entire angle (16 directions) (<xref rid="pone.0332590.g001" ref-type="fig">Fig 1</xref>). Reportedly, it enables rapid examinations with relatively low invasiveness [<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref012" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>–<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref015" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>]. Therefore, it is considered useful for monitoring temporal changes in angle structures, and its application in evaluating peripheral anterior synechiae formation following outflow reconstruction surgery has been reported [<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref016" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>].
