# PMC: low level laser therapy healing review — Overview

align="left" colspan="1" rowspan="1">Irradiation time (s)</td><td align="left" colspan="1" rowspan="1">10 s</td></tr><tr><td align="left" colspan="1" rowspan="1">Distance</td><td align="left" colspan="1" rowspan="1">1 cm from the tissue</td></tr><tr><td align="left" colspan="1" rowspan="1">Angulation</td><td align="left" colspan="1" rowspan="1">90 degree</td></tr><tr><td align="left" colspan="1" rowspan="1">Energy density/ fluence (J/cm<sup>2</sup>)</td><td align="left" colspan="1" rowspan="1">3.82 J/cm<sup>2</sup></td></tr><tr><td align="left" colspan="1" rowspan="1">Power density (W/ cm<sup>2</sup>)</td><td align="left" colspan="1" rowspan="1">38.2 W/cm<sup>2</sup></td></tr></tbody></table></table-wrap></p></sec><sec id="Sec20"><title>Postoperative instructions</title><p id="Par29">The area was examined for 2 to 3 min after suturing of the area for any fresh bleeding. The patients were given written post-operative instructions and medications. Amoxicillin (500 mg 3 times a day for 5 days) and ibuprofen (3 times a day for 3 days) were prescribed. The patients were refrained from retracting the lips and cheeks and to avoid brushing or flossing in the grafted area for 6 weeks. To achieve chemical plaque control, all the patients were asked to rinse with 0.2% chlorhexidine gluconate solution for 2 weeks. The sutures were removed after 2 weeks post-surgery. Essex appliance was delivered to the patient with minimal soft tissue contact during healing phase, thus preventing the surgical site from undue routine damaging forces. After 3 months, the patient was referred to Department of Prosthodontics for rehabilitation with fabrication of the final prosthesis. The postoperative follow-up was done after 3 months.</p></sec><sec id="Sec21"><title>Parameters recorded</title><p id="Par30">Following clinical parameters and patient satisfaction response were recorded at baseline (before surgery), 1 week, 4 weeks, and 12 weeks.</p></sec><sec id="Sec22"><title>Clinical parameters</title><p id="Par31"><italic toggle="yes">Palatal tissue thickness</italic> was measured at six fixed points (5 and 7 mm from the gingival margin) using an acrylic stent as explained earlier. To standardize the parameter recorded acrylic stent was positioned at various time intervals to mark the points. An endodontic file with rubber cursor was inserted on the marked point perpendicularly until it reaches palatine bone plate under local anesthesia administered using insulin syringe.</p><sec id="Sec23"><title>Consistency, color match, contour, and scar</title><p id="Par32">The “Modified Manchester Scar Scale (MSS)” was modified to assess consistency, color match, contour, and scar of the palatal wound on the donor site. Consistency was scored as “firm and resilient” and “soft and edematous;” color as “absolute match;” “slight mis-match;” “obvious mis-match,” and “gross mis-match to the surrounding muscosa/skin;” contour as “flush with surrounding skin,” “slightly proud/indented,” “hypertrophic,” and “keloid;” and scar was recorded as “present” and “absent” respectively by visually comparing with the adjacent non-operated site [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR20">20</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="Sec24"><title>Patient satisfaction analysis</title><p id="Par33">In form of pain, burning sensation and postoperative discomfort at baseline (after surgery), 1 week, 4 weeks, and 12 weeks. Postoperative pain intensity was assessed using a 100‐mm line VAS Score with two end‐points representing “absent,” and “present.” The participants were asked to rate their pain by placing a mark on the VAS line corresponding to their postoperative pain level [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR21">21</xref>]. Patient post-operative discomfort was analyzed through air spray for 5 s over the operated site. Sensitivity to air spray was measured as “comfort” and “discomfort” before applying anesthesia to palatal mucosa [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR22">22</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="Sec25"><title>Complications</title><p id="Par34">Various complications were evaluated at baseline (after surgery), 1 week, 4 weeks, and 12 weeks. Discoloration of palatal muscosa (<italic toggle="yes">Ecchymosis</italic>) after surgery is measured visually and recorded as “no ecchymosis,” “mild ecchymosis (patch measuring less than 1 cm),” and “severe ecchymosis (patch measuring more than 1 cm).” The presence of necrosis in the palatal flap was clinically assessed with periodontal probe and recorded as “no necrosis,” “less than 30% of necrosis of the total surface of the elevated flap” and “more than 30% of necrosis of the total surface of the elevated flap” [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR23">23</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Swelling and edema</italic> assessment and measurement were performed subjectively in the clinical routine and recorded as “no swelling” and edema, “presence of mild swelling, and edema” and “presence of severe swelling and edema seen on palatal site.” <italic toggle="yes">Infection</italic> was assessed on visual examination and “no pus discharge” and “presence of pus discharge.”</p></sec></sec><sec id="Sec26"><title>Statistical analysis</title><p id="Par35">Statistical data was analyzed using SPSS (Statistical package for social science) Version 22.0, statistical Analysis Software.
