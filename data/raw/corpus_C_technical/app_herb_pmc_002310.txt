Other Reported Biological Activities</title><p>Certain bioactivities of <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> remain insufficiently characterized and studied to date. Many of these activities are strongly associated with the antioxidant capacity of the extracts derived from this plant. For example, evidence suggests that <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> exhibit anti-inflammatory effects. However, other parts of the plant, such as the oils extracted from the seeds, have demonstrated even greater potential in this regard [<xref rid="B104-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">104</xref>]. In fact, this plant has been traditionally used in Asian medicine for the treatment of inflammatory disorders [<xref rid="B18-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>]. Regarding the compounds responsible for this bioactivity, various studies have demonstrated the influence of different terpenoids (e.g., squalene, lupeol, and vitamin E) and saponins, revealing a close relationship between inflammation and tumorigenesis. These properties are attributed to the significant inhibition of nitric oxide production [<xref rid="B20-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>,<xref rid="B50-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>]. Moreover, the exploration of the biosynthesis pathway of methyl commate B through metabolomics may provide insights into new bioactive compounds with anti-inflammatory potential. Understanding these metabolic pathways could uncover additional components in the plant extracts that contribute to their anti-inflammatory activity by modulating key inflammatory processes [<xref rid="B50-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> have also shown antifungal activity [<xref rid="B31-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>]. This bioactivity is attributed to the presence of difference bioactive compounds, with particular emphasis on camellidin I and II, which were effective against <italic toggle="yes">Pestalotiopsis longiseta</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Pyricularia oryzae</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Cochliobolus miyabeanus</italic> [<xref rid="B51-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>]. Despite this antifungal activity, the presence of the fungus <italic toggle="yes">Rosellinia</italic> sp. PF1022 has been reported in <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic>. However, the presence of this fungus may be of industrial interest since it produces a metabolite (cyclooctadepsipeptide PF1022A) known for its antifungal activity, which has significant potential for developing parasitic treatments [<xref rid="B105-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">105</xref>].</p><p>Another reported bioactivity in <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> is their antiviral capability, with activity demonstrated against key viruses such as AIDS [<xref rid="B44-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>], porcine epidemic diarrhea virus [<xref rid="B20-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>,<xref rid="B43-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>], or human immunodeficiency virus type 1 protease [<xref rid="B44-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>]. This bioactivity is associated with the presence of various phenolic compounds, particularly highlighting camelliatannin H. However, this bioactivity is lower than the reported for <italic toggle="yes">CJ</italic> fruit [<xref rid="B44-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>]. These compounds can interfere with viral replication, modulating immune responses and blocking viral entry into host cells, suggesting their potential as natural therapeutic agents against various viral infections [<xref rid="B85-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">85</xref>,<xref rid="B106-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>]. In addition, the occurrence of oleanane-type triterpenes in <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> prompts the need to assess if the leaves exhibit antiviral effects against coronavirus as reported for <italic toggle="yes">CJ</italic> flowers [<xref rid="B43-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>].</p><p>In addition to its antiviral activity, <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> has demonstrated skin protection properties against external factors such as UVB rays and reactive oxygen species. Studies show that <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> exhibits anti-photoaging effects, reducing the impact of UV-induced premature aging in concentrations ranging from 0.125 to 10.0 mg/mL [<xref rid="B86-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>].
