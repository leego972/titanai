Most identified phytochemical species have been previously reported for PMG phenogenotypes from Italy (37, 38), Croatia (39), Spain (40), and the USA (41). It is noteworthy that the PC profile (molecular diversity and content) of PMGs depends on the fruit´s ripening stage, phenogenotype, pre/post-harvest conditions, and extractive solvent, among other factors [<xref rid="B37-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>]. Nevertheless, the flavonoid: total PC and ANTO: flavonoid ratios and ANTO fingerprint (cyanidin &gt; delphinidin &gt; pelargonidin, di &gt; mono glucosides) are quite conserved among PMGs of different phenogenotypes and regions [<xref rid="B8-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>,<xref rid="B37-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>,<xref rid="B38-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>,<xref rid="B39-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>,<xref rid="B40-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>,<xref rid="B41-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>]. Also, while red PMG was a richer source of ANTO and organic acids, white and pink were richer in phenolic acids (<xref rid="app1-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="app">Table S1; see Supplementary Materials</xref>).</p><p>Ellagitannins and ANTO have been the most investigated PC in PMGs because of their well-known antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, cardioprotective and anticancer properties [<xref rid="B42-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>,<xref rid="B43-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>,<xref rid="B44-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>]. Particularly, ellagitannins [<xref rid="B43-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>] and ANTO [<xref rid="B44-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>] have potent antiproliferative and pro-apoptotic activity against several cancer cells including those of the colon, breast, lung, cervix, prostate, liver, and oral/esophageal, to name a few. Although the extractive methodology and chromatographic conditions used in this study did not allow the identification of complex ellagitannins (e.g., punicalagin), as it has been reported for “Wonderful” and “Mollar de Elche” varieties by others [<xref rid="B37-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>,<xref rid="B38-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>,<xref rid="B39-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>,<xref rid="B40-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>,<xref rid="B41-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>] that the presence of ellagic acid and two of its glycosides in all three varieties of PMG (pink &gt; red, white; <xref rid="app1-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="app">Table S1</xref>) accounts for this. PMG´s ellagitannin-derived compounds (e.g., ellagic and gallagic acids, and urolithins A/B and B) not only have a high RSC but also antimutagenic activity [<xref rid="B42-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>,<xref rid="B45-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>]. Gallic acid and its glucosides (present in pink/white &gt; red PMG; <xref rid="app1-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="app">Table S1; see Supplementary Materials</xref>) and gallotannins are also bioactive against many cancer cells, including abnormal colonocytes [<xref rid="B46-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>]. Lastly, even though monomeric ANTO in white PMG fall below detection/quantification limits with the pH-differential spectrophotometric method [<xref rid="B17-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>], it was possible to identify them by UPLC-DAD-MS<sup>2</sup>, evidencing the lack of sensitivity/accuracy of the spectrophotometric method.</p><p>According to Nicolson et al. [<xref rid="B47-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>], PMG small flavonoids (e.g., catechin, epicatechin, quercetin, naringenin) have strong electron-acceptor properties, making them potent inhibitors of the NF-κB pathway involved in several carcinogenic processes. In this study, the five most abundant flavonoids were phellatin, quercitrin, phlorizin, D-(+)-catechin, and corilagin (<xref rid="app1-biomolecules-12-01649" ref-type="app">Table S1; see Supplementary Materials</xref>), although each PMG sample had a differential (relative abundance) flavonoid fingerprint: red [quercitrin&gt; (-)-epicatechin], pink (phellatin, &gt; corilagin, &gt; astragalin), and white [D-(+)-catechin, phlorizin].
