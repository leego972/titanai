This activity is attributed to the presence of triterpenoids and other bioactive compounds that could interfere with cell proliferation and promote apoptosis in tumor cells [<xref rid="B20-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>].</p><p>In addition, <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> extracts have been found to have significant anti-inflammatory properties, attributed to their high concentration of terpenoids [<xref rid="B50-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>]. These compounds act by inhibiting nitric oxide production and reducing levels of prostaglandin E and tumor necrosis factor-<italic toggle="yes">α</italic>, in addition to decreasing the expression of proinflammatory enzymes such as cyclooxygenase-2 and inducible nitric oxide synthase [<xref rid="B142-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">142</xref>]. These actions suggest certain potential in the development of treatments for inflammatory diseases. These extracts have also demonstrated effectiveness against various pathogenic bacteria such as <italic toggle="yes">S. aureus</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">E. coli</italic>, as well as fungi such as <italic toggle="yes">Candida albicans</italic>. This effect is due to that <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> extracts are rich in polyphenols and saponins, which possess the ability to inhibit the growth of these pathogens, suggesting their potential in the development of natural antibiotics and antifungals [<xref rid="B31-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>,<xref rid="B41-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>,<xref rid="B90-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">90</xref>,<xref rid="B143-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">143</xref>]. These antimicrobial effects open new possibilities for the treatment of infections.</p><p>Other less explored potential pharmaceutical applications include allergy medications and drugs with anti-hyperuricemic activity. This is supported by research indicating that ethanolic extracts of <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> have inhibitory activity on xanthine oxidase, a key enzyme in uric acid production, and reduce blood uric acid levels in mouse models, suggesting their potential for the treatment of hyperuricemia and gout [<xref rid="B19-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>]. Furthermore, the compound okicamelliaside isolated from <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> has shown inhibitory effects on mast cell degranulation, a key process in allergic reactions. In vivo studies have shown that both the leaf extract and okicamelliaside can reduce vascular permeability in models of allergic conjunctivitis and decrease sneezing frequency in models of allergic rhinitis, indicating their potential in the development of anti-allergy treatments [<xref rid="B144-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">144</xref>].</p><p>In conclusion, despite <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> being a promising source of bioactive compounds, there is limited information available on its potential. Therefore, additional research is required to fully explore its industrial applications. The potential of <italic toggle="yes">CJ</italic> in various industries is vast and constantly under investigation. While its use in cosmetics is well established, the food and pharmaceutical industries are still exploring ways to incorporate its benefits into commercially viable products. Growing investment in research and development suggests that in the coming years we could see a significant expansion in the application of this plant in the global market. These may include the development of nutraceuticals, the incorporation of <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> compounds into formulations to extend the shelf life of food products, or the formulation of cosmetic products based on its demonstrated bioactive properties.</p></sec></sec><sec id="sec6-nutrients-17-03382"><title>6. Future Perspectives</title><p>The development of <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic>-based applications, alongside the growing demand for natural ingredients and environmentally sustainable products, indicates a strong potential for continued market growth in this sector [<xref rid="B145-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">145</xref>]. This is reflected in the cosmetics market, where natural cosmetics had revenues of around USD 40 billion in 2021 [<xref rid="B146-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">146</xref>]. A similar growth is observed in the food industry, where not only the sustainability of products, but also the development of functional foods, plays a fundamental role, being both of them in high demand [<xref rid="B147-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">147</xref>,<xref rid="B148-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">148</xref>]. The natural food additives market is estimated to experience continued growth of 6.2% annually through 2030, reaching a market value of EUR 9.1 billion in 2021. This represents a significant portion of the global food and beverage market, which is valued at approximately EUR 2.56 billion [<xref rid="B149-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">149</xref>].
