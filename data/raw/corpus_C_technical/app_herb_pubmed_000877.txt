Aminoacyl sulfonamide assembly in SB-203208 biosynthesis.

Sulfonamide is present in many important drugs, due to its unique chemical and 
biological properties. In contrast, naturally occurring sulfonamides are rare, 
and their biosynthetic knowledge are scarce. Here we identify the biosynthetic 
gene cluster of sulfonamide antibiotics, altemicidin, SB-203207, and SB-203208, 
from Streptomyces sp. NCIMB40513. The heterologous gene expression and 
biochemical analyses reveal unique aminoacyl transfer reactions, including the 
tRNA synthetase-like enzyme SbzA-catalyzed L-isoleucine transfer and the GNAT 
enzyme SbzC-catalyzed β-methylphenylalanine transfer. Furthermore, we elucidate 
the biogenesis of 2-sulfamoylacetic acid from L-cysteine, by the collaboration 
of the cupin dioxygenase SbzM and the aldehyde dehydrogenase SbzJ. Remarkably, 
SbzM catalyzes the two-step oxidation and decarboxylation of L-cysteine, and the 
subsequent intramolecular amino group rearrangement leads to N-S bond formation. 
This detailed analysis of the aminoacyl sulfonamide antibiotics biosynthetic 
machineries paves the way toward investigations of sulfonamide biosynthesis and 
its engineering.
