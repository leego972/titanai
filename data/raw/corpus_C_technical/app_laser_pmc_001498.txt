However, it must be kept in mind that quadrants or clock-hours thicknesses are characterized by higher test–retest variability, and, therefore, a greater structural change is required to distinguish true progression from fluctuation.<sup><xref rid="bibr39-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">39</xref></sup> Furthermore, glaucoma follows characteristics patterns of OCT progression, whose recognition at the thickness and total deviation maps may help the clinician discriminating between variability and progression.<sup><xref rid="bibr47-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>,<xref rid="bibr48-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">48</xref></sup></p><p>Compared to peripapillary OCT parameters, macular OCT measures reach the floor later on and may be useful to monitor patients along the entire spectrum of the disease, including advanced stages.<sup><xref rid="bibr42-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>,<xref rid="bibr49-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">49</xref><xref rid="bibr50-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr51-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">51</xref></sup> Belghith and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr49-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">49</xref></sup> were the first to report that mGCIPL thickness may detect progression in advanced glaucoma better than optic nerve measures, such as pRNFL, and further studies corroborated these findings. In the Advanced Imaging for Glaucoma Study, Zhang and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr42-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">42</xref></sup> investigated the utility of pRNFL and macular GCC measures to detect progression in all the spectrum of glaucoma severity and found that pRNFL had little utility in patients with advanced glaucoma as opposed to GCC thickness, which retains high sensitivity throughout all the course of disease. Shin and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr50-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">50</xref></sup> showed that Glaucoma Progression Analysis (GPA) of the GCIPL may be used to track glaucoma progression better than that of pRNFL. In a cohort of patients with advanced glaucoma, Lavinsky and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr51-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">51</xref></sup> compared the rates of changes of VF MD, visual field index (VFI), pRNFL, and mGCIPL and found that all the indices but pRNFL rate detected a significant negative rate of change.</p><p>There is no consensus on which macular measure (e.g. thickness of GCC, ganglion cell layer (GCL), GCIPL, or full macular thickness (FMT)) best monitors glaucoma progression. A real change must exceed the test–retest variability to be detectable. Previous studies have shown that within-session variability, rather than between-session variability, is responsible for most of the total test–retest variability in macular OCT imaging, and variability is low and uniform (approximately 3 µm) regardless the macular measure considered.<sup><xref rid="bibr52-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>,<xref rid="bibr53-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">53</xref></sup> As the relative variability is related to normal layer thickness, thinner outcome measures (i.e. GCL and GCIPL) have higher relative variability than thicker ones (i.e. GCC and FMT).<sup><xref rid="bibr52-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>,<xref rid="bibr53-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">53</xref></sup> In addition, thinner measures are limited by narrower dynamic ranges and increased variability in severe glaucoma, likely related to segmentation errors which are more frequent with thinner retinal layers.<sup><xref rid="bibr54-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">54</xref></sup> In yet unpublished studies, GCC was the best outcome measure to monitor glaucoma progression along the entire disease spectrum and provided the best longitudinal structure–function relationship with central VF (Nouri- Mahdavi et al. “Comparison of rates of progression of macular OCT parameters in glaucoma.” Paper presented at: 2018 ARVO annual meeting, Honolulu, HI, USA. Mohammadzadeh et al. “Longitudinal structure-function relationship in the macula.” Paper presented at: 2019 ARVO annual meeting, Vancouver, Canada).</p><p>Although MRW is a promising measure to diagnose patients with early glaucoma, few studies suggest that it may perform worse or, at least, equal to pRNFL to monitor disease progression. Bowd and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr55-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">55</xref></sup> estimated the measurement floor of macular GCIPL, pRNFL, and MRW and found that the latter two hit similar measurement floor, which is reached far before macular GCIPL.
