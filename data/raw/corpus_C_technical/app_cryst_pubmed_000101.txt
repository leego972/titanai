Nanoparticle-Mediated Targeted Drug Delivery to Remodel Tumor Microenvironment 
for Cancer Therapy.

Advanced research has revealed the crucial role of tumor microenvironment (TME) 
in tumorigenesis. TME consists of a complicated network with a variety of cell 
types including endothelial cells, pericytes, immune cells, cancer-associated 
fibroblasts (CAFs), cancer stem cells (CSCs) as well as the extracellular matrix 
(ECM). The TME-constituting cells interact with the cancerous cells through 
plenty of signaling mechanisms and pathways in a dynamical way, participating in 
tumor initiation, progression, metastasis, and response to therapies. Hence, TME 
is becoming an attractive therapeutic target in cancer treatment, exhibiting 
potential research interest and clinical benefits. Presently, the novel 
nanotechnology applied in TME regulation has made huge progress. The 
nanoparticles (NPs) can be designed as demand to precisely target TME components 
and to inhibit tumor progression through TME modulation. Moreover, 
nanotechnology-mediated drug delivery possesses many advantages including 
prolonged circulation time, enhanced bioavailability and decreased toxicity over 
traditional therapeutic modality. In this review, update information on TME 
remodeling through NPs-based targeted drug delivery strategies for anticancer 
therapy is summarized.
