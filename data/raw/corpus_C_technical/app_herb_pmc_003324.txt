# PMC: ethnobotany medicinal plants Africa Asia — Overview

<italic toggle="yes">graveolens</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">A</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">fistulosum</italic> L. have been used by traditional Chinese and Indonesian people to reduce blood pressure and cough, respectively [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref030" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0235886.ref031" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>]. Finally, nine species were reported to have low SUV (0.01) in the present study (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="pone.0235886.g002">Fig 2</xref>). High or low SUV may be due to extensive or minimum ethnobotanical uses of the reported species, respectively. Similar results were also reported by Hussain <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>. [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref032" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>], where the highest SUV represents the most exploited medicinal plants used to treat a specific ailment.</p><p>In total, 30 medicinal plant species have been recorded in our study. All belong to 20 different families, with Poaceae and Zingiberaceae being dominant in the study area (each consisting of four species) followed by Apiaceae (three species). The remaining families were represented by one or two species (<xref rid="pone.0235886.t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>). Poaceae and Zingiberaceae were the most representative medicinal plant families in our study. This finding might be due to the high accessibility of these species in that region. This further supports that dominant plant families and species are commonly used by local people for disease treatment [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref032" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>]. Moreover, most of the species within both families are cultivated by people of Tengger in the Ngadisari village. The occurrence of dominant plant species and families in the study area is also related to favorable climate and environmental conditions [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref033" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0235886.ref034" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>]. As a result of the abundance, these species are commonly used as a basic ingredient of <italic toggle="yes">Jamu—</italic>an Indonesian traditional medicine [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref035" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>]. Sharifi-Rad <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>. [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref036" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>] also described that plants from the Zingiberaceae family are a potential source of bioactive phytochemical.</p><table-wrap
