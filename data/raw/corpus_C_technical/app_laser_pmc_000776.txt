Such deformable/flexible/elastic liposomal formulations have increased drug permeability through the skin [<xref rid="B45-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>]. Taken together, lipid-based vesicular systems have the potential to improve the topical delivery of PSs by simply incorporating new components into the liposomal formulation. </p><p>Tertiary and quaternary mixtures of oil, water, and surfactants, the latter of which can be supplemented with a phospholipid, have a long history of applications on the skin. Such combinations form vesicular or sponge phases and often microemulsions. However, microemulsions are far less form-adaptable than hybrid lipoidal vesicles, as the latter not only has a flexible membrane but also can adjust their inner volume easily to vesicle shape changes [<xref rid="B26-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>].</p><sec id="sec3dot2dot1-pharmaceuticals-14-00772"><title>3.2.1. ALA and Derivatives</title><p>As previously indicated, ALA has problems of instability and low skin penetration. Different strategies to enhance ALA penetration for improved PDT results have been investigated. Liposomal delivery and synthesis of ALA esters are some of the most extensively studied systems [<xref rid="B46-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>]. Liposomes provide an enhanced ALA passage through the SC, resulting in more precise drug targeting into diseased cells. Liposome-encapsulated ALA transformation into PpIX is also higher in AK and BBCs than in normal adjacent skin [<xref rid="B47-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>].</p><p>Although the superiority of liposomal PSs was not initially demonstrated [<xref rid="B48-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">48</xref>], in 2001, Pierre et al. proposed modified liposomes having lipid composition similar to the mammalian SC to optimize ALA transdermal delivery in skin cancers [<xref rid="B49-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>,<xref rid="B50-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>]. The liposomal vesicles containing 5.7% ALA showed higher skin retention (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.05) on the epidermis with a decrease of skin permeation compared to an aqueous solution [<xref rid="B50-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>]. Likewise, ALA liposomal formulations resulted in prolonged ALA-induced PpIX accumulation, as well as better epidermal targeting [<xref rid="B51-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>]. Additionally, enhanced ALA penetration with hydrophobic derivatives of ALA (ALA-esters, ALA-amino acid derivatives, and ALA dendrimers) showed promising results in vitro [<xref rid="B52-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>]. However, although some ALA-esters yielded strong in vivo results, only the MAL formulation is currently used in cutaneous malignancies [<xref rid="B34-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>].</p><p>Approximately a decade later, Dyaderm<sup>®</sup> (a non-invasive fluorescence imaging system) combined with ALA encapsulated liposomes were used to detect early NMSCs on the face, neck, chest, back, and hands of patients treated with UV light or heavy outdoor workers [<xref rid="B53-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>]. The diagnostic skin fluorescence system using liposomal encapsulated ALA offered the possibility for early detection of NMSC and was helpful at pre-clinical stages. After the commercial development of the liposomal ALA and its methyl ester (Metvix<sup>®</sup>) for PDT, it was found that liposome-entrapped precursors induced the expression of the proteolytic enzymes metalloproteinases (MMPs) in BCC [<xref rid="B54-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">54</xref>]. The MMP-3 expression was blocked after using an MMP-3 inhibitor, suggesting a route to improve topical PDT effectiveness.</p><p>In parallel to the advance of conventional liposomal systems, research and development of deformable/flexible/elastic lipid vesicles as tDDS were considered for ALA-PDT towards skin cancer. Critical studies in applying ethosomes as ALA tDDS have also been developed [<xref rid="B55-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>,<xref rid="B56-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>]. As a lipid vehicle, ethosomes contain higher concentrations of ethanol and lipids.
