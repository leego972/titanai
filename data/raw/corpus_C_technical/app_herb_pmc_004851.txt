These findings can be reconciled with the observed beneficial effects of adaptogens in behavioral, mental, and aging-associated disorders. Combining two or more active substances in one mixture significantly changes deregulated genes profiles: synergetic interactions result in activation of genes that none of the individual substances affected, while antagonistic interactions result in suppression some genes activated by individual substances. These interactions can have an influence on transcriptional control of metabolic regulation both on the cellular level and the level of the whole organism. BY</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><sec><title>Introduction</title><p>The term adaptogen was introduced in scientific literature in the 1950s to refer to substances that increase the “state of non-specific resistance” under stress conditions (Lazarev, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B61">1958</xref>; Lazarev et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B62">1959</xref>). The adaptogen concept was based on the theory of stress (Selye, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B108">1950</xref>), initially defined as a state of threatened homeostasis, and a general adaptation syndrome characterized by a non-specific response of the organism to diverse stressors (physical, emotional, environmental, etc.). It has been postulated that adaptogens should be safe and are able to normalize body functions irrespective of the nature of stressors (Brekhman II and Dardymov, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B13">1969</xref>). On the verge of the new millennium, the definition of adaptogens was updated to “<italic toggle="yes">a new class of metabolic regulators which increase the ability of an organism to adapt to environmental factors and to avoid damage from such factors</italic>” (Panossian et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B97">1999a</xref>). The concept of adaptogens is now generally accepted by the scientific community (EMEA/HMPC/102655/, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B30">2007</xref>; Samuelsson and Bohlin, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B103">2009</xref>), although it has yet to gain prominence in mainstream pharmacology. In this context recent research progress has taken two main forms:
<list list-type="bullet"><list-item><p>Convincing clinical evidence has been generated on the efficacy of adaptogens, based on clinical trials performed in accordance with the International Conference in Harmonization Regulations (ICH) standards for good clinical practice (Davydov and Krikorian, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B25">2000</xref>; Goulet and Dionne, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B38">2005</xref>; Sarris, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B105">2007</xref>; Bleakney, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B9">2008</xref>; Panossian and Wikman, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B92">2009</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B93">2010</xref>; Dwyer et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B28">2011</xref>; Hung et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B48">2011</xref>; Iovieno et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B49">2011</xref>; Sarris et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B107">2011</xref>; Chan, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B17">2012</xref>; Ishaque et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B50">2012</xref>),</p></list-item><list-item><p>Some key molecular mechanisms of adaptogen activity were defined (Panossian et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B86">2007</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B94">2009</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B95">2012</xref>).</p></list-item></list></p><p>The stress-protective activity of adaptogens is associated with regulation of homeostasis both on:
<list list-type="bullet"><list-item><p>the system level <italic toggle="yes">via</italic> several mechanisms of action which are linked to the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis, and the cellular level <italic toggle="yes">via</italic> activation of molecular chaperones, mainly hsp70 proteins, and the regulation of key mediators of the stress response, including neuropeptide Y (NPY), cortisol, nitric oxide, stress-activated protein kinase JNK, and forkhead box O transcription factor (Panossian et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B86">2007</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B94">2009</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B95">2012</xref>; Wiegant et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B130">2009</xref>).</p></list-item></list></p><p>An important role of CNS system in stress is generally accepted, since the stress concept has been defined by Selye (Fink, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B34">2000</xref>).
