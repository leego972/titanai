Acute stress (short-term extreme situations requiring a fight-or-flight response) is a survival strategy in healthy individuals [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref004" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>–<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref006" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>]. However, prolonged unmanageable stress called chronic stress leads to many diseases like cardiovascular disease [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref007" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>], diabetes [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref008" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0285012.ref009" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>] and depression [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref008" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0285012.ref010" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>]. Managing chronic stress involves the removal of stressors, gaining more knowledge about the stress, and taking supplements that help reduce the stress. Removing the stressor is not always possible (E.g., work stress). Gaining more knowledge about stress needs more time and effort, which could be a daunting task for many of us in today’s fast-paced world. Intake of supplements to reduce stress is a simple task and could get accomplished by the majority in today’s scenario. Indian tradition has left us with a medicinal plant that could reduce our stress in day-to-day life. Drinking water soaked with the leaves of this plant has been given as a “Prashad” in many Hindu temples to calm the people and make them peaceful during their short visits to these temples.</p><p><italic toggle="yes">Ocimum tenuiflorum</italic> L. (Synonym: <italic toggle="yes">Ocimum sanctum</italic> L.) commonly known as Tulsi or Holy Basil is the plant used for managing stress. It is a sacred Indian medicinal plant with longstanding traditional use in multiple Indian systems of medicines such as Ayurveda, Siddha, and Unani [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref011" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Ocimum tenuiflorum</italic> has protected against iron-induced testicular toxicity by counteracting redox imbalance [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref012" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>] and exhibited better seizure control, memory retention, oxidative stress reduction, and neuronal structure preservation [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref013" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>]. It showed antistress effects in different animal models [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref014" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>] and prevented a stress-induced decline in macrophage function [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref015" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>]. In a human clinical study, the plant effectively managed symptoms of general stress when used at a dose of 1200 per day for six weeks [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref016" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>]. Various phytochemicals, namely vicenin-2, orientin, cynaroside, betulinic acid, genistein with syringic acid, rosmarinic acid, eugenol, carnosic acid, oleanolic acid, ursolic acid, luteolin, and apigenin were confirmed to be present in <italic toggle="yes">Ocimum tenuiflorum</italic> [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref017" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>]. Though available scientific literature indicated the antistress property of different extracts of <italic toggle="yes">Ocimum tenuiflorum</italic>, there are hardly any studies that evaluated the antistress property of <italic toggle="yes">Ocimum tenuiflorum</italic> extract at a low dose of ≤50 mg/kg body weight in mice.
