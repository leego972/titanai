<italic toggle="yes">Ginkgo biloba</italic> extract (GBE) contains terpenoids, flavonoids, alkylphenols, polyprenols, and organic acids. The standardized GBE, EGb761, is pharmacologically prepared containing ginkgo flavonoids (primarily quercetin, kaempferol, and isorhamnetin) comprising 22–24% of the GBE, 6% terpenoids (3.1% ginkgolides A, B, C, and J and 2.9% bilobalide), and &lt;5 ppm ginkgolic acid (Figures <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig5">5(d)</xref>, <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig5">5(e)</xref>, and <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig5">5(f)</xref>). Fitzl et al. showed that 100 mg/kg/d EGb761 orally administered for 12 weeks significantly reduced the increase of interstitial volume and collagen fibers in a diabetic rat heart [<xref rid="B71" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>, <xref rid="B72" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>]. Furthermore, EGb761 treatment improves the hypoxia tolerance of diabetic myocardium and myocardial microvessels [<xref rid="B73" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>, <xref rid="B74" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>]. Saini et al. [<xref rid="B75" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>] demonstrated that EGb761 at a dose of 50 mg/kg/d for 3 weeks significantly attenuated the index of lipid peroxidation and oxidative stress in diabetic rats and inhibited the opening of mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP), ultimately leading to improvement of cardiomyopathy.</p></sec><sec id="sec4.4"><title>4.4. Resveratrol</title><p>Resveratrol (3,5,4′-trihydroxylstilbene), a natural polyphenol present in red wine and grapes, is capable to reduce blood glucose level in STZ-induced diabetic rat [<xref rid="B76" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>, <xref rid="B77" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>] (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig5">Figure 5(c)</xref>). GLUT-4 translocation and glucose uptake are increased in STZ-induced diabetic rat myocardium by orally administrated resveratrol at a dose of 2.5 mg/kg/d for 2 weeks, the mechanism involving activation of AMPK and AKt cascades by resveratrol [<xref rid="B78" ref-type="bibr">78</xref>]. Resveratrol prevents high-glucose cultured neonatal rat cardiomyocyte apoptosis by inhibiting NADPH-derived ROS production and by alleviating the reduction of cardiac antioxidant enzyme activities, possibly mediated by AMPK-related signaling pathway [<xref rid="B79" ref-type="bibr">79</xref>]. Yar et al. showed that 10 mg/kg/d resveratrol intraperitoneal injection for 4 weeks ameliorated diabetic heart failure by increasing the expression of SIRT1 [<xref rid="B80" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>]. A special diet enriched with resveratrol at 0.067% (the consumption of resveratrol is estimated to be less than 100 mg/kg/d) for 12 weeks effectively restores SERCA2<italic toggle="yes">α</italic> expression and cardiac function in diabetic mice, associated with STIR1 activation [<xref rid="B52" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec4.5"><title>4.5. Astragalus Polysaccharides (APS)</title><p>APS is a main active extract from the traditional Chinese medicinal herb Astragalus membranaceus. Chen et al. demonstrated that APS improved cardiac function and myocardial collagen deposition by inhibiting the local chymase-Ang II system and Ang II-activated ERK1/2 in diabetic cardiomyopathy in hamsters [<xref rid="B81" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>–<xref rid="B83" ref-type="bibr">83</xref>]. They also showed that APS can ameliorate myocardial glucose metabolism disorders in diabetic hamster by promoting expression of myocardial GLUT-4 gene and inhibiting level of PPAR <italic toggle="yes">α</italic> [<xref rid="B84" ref-type="bibr">84</xref>].
