Cicarma <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> [<xref rid="B96-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>] demonstrated that a short course (24 h) of calcitriol pretreatment before MAL-PDT elevated the intracellular PpIX in A-431 cells. Furthermore, cell damage after exposure to blue light was significantly higher in calcitriol treated cells. Increased photoinactivation correlated with higher levels of PpIX in the calcitriol-pretreated cells. Likewise, Anand <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> [<xref rid="B97-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>] using mouse models of SCC reported that calcitriol administered prior to ALA increased the efficacy of PDT. Anand <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> [<xref rid="B98-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">98</xref>] also tested a possible strategy to overcome the problem of hypercalcemia by substituting natural dietary vitamin D3 for calcitriol. Oral D3 supplementation enhanced PpIX levels, and cell death in subcutaneous A431 tumors mediated by PDT. These results support the brief use of oral administration of cholecalciferol as a safe neoadjuvant to ALA-PDT. In BCC and UVB-induced SCC mouse models, they identified an increase in tumor-specific accumulation of PpIX due to vitamin D preconditioning of up to six-fold <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic>. In addition, increased expression of proliferation (42-fold) and differentiation (145-fold) markers was identified in BCCs, all leading to superior tumor destruction (18.3-fold) with the combination of both treatments, compared to ALA-PDT [<xref rid="B99-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>].</p><p>Furthermore, in our previous works, one of the factors that seemed related to the lack of response to PDT was the overexpression of EGFR [<xref rid="B100-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">100</xref>]. For this reason, association of PDT with an EGFR blocker may help to reduce treatment resistance [<xref rid="B101-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">101</xref>]. Weyergang <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> [<xref rid="B102-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>] evaluated the combination of PDT plus EGFR targeting drugs in A-431 cells, an EGFR positive cell line of human epidermoid skin carcinoma. PDT and Tyrphostin revealed synergistic cytotoxicity, whereas Erlotinib or Cetuximab induced an antagonistic effect on cell survival. Neoadjuvant EGFR targeting therapies and PDT induced a synergistic inhibition of ERK as well as synergistic cytotoxicity only when the EGFR targeting monotherapies caused a prolonged ERK inhibition.</p></sec><sec id="sec3dot3-ijms-16-25912"><title>3.3. Basal Cell Carcinoma</title><p>Different treatments have been combined with PDT to increase its efficacy and overcome resistances (<xref ref-type="table" rid="ijms-16-25912-t003">Table 3</xref>). Jeremic <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> [<xref rid="B103-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">103</xref>] treated 10 BCC and eight SCC previous to surgical excision; four BCC demonstrated a complete response after an average of two PDT treatments, whereas the remaining lesions demonstrated a partial response after three PDT sessions with a maximum reduction of the tumor area of 88%. These tumors were then excised with clear histologic margins without recurrence in one year. The results suggest that for those NMSC without a complete response to PDT, neoadjuvant PDT can substantially reduce tumor burden, allowing for less morbid surgical excisions with histologically clear margins. In another study, Torres <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> [<xref rid="B104-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">104</xref>] showed that two PDT sessions, one week apart, reduced in 30%–50% the size of all tumors. After two and a half years, all patients remained without recurrence and the cosmetic outcome was considered very good.
