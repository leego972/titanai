Expected changes in tissue caused by cancer, relevant to OCT, include altered cellular arrangement; density and size of nuclei and organelles; proliferation and changes in the organization of the extracellular matrix; and changes in the blood and lymphatic microvasculature.<xref rid="r84" ref-type="bibr"><sup>84</sup></xref><sup>,</sup><xref rid="r85" ref-type="bibr"><sup>85</sup></xref> McLaughlin et al.<xref rid="r9" ref-type="bibr"><sup>9</sup></xref> were the first to apply parametric OCT attenuation imaging to assess cancer <italic toggle="yes">ex vivo</italic>, extracting an attenuation coefficient for each A-scan and visualizing the spatially distributed attenuation values as an <italic toggle="yes">en face</italic> image (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="f10">Fig. 10</xref>). Although they quantified only the relative values of attenuation coefficient, the demonstration on malignant human axillary lymph nodes from breast cancer patients (<inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math275"><mml:mrow><mml:mi>n</mml:mi><mml:mo>=</mml:mo><mml:mn>2</mml:mn></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula>) indicated the presence of contrast between malignant and healthy non-neoplastic tissue. <xref ref-type="fig" rid="f10">Figure 10</xref> shows one such example indicating the differentiation of residual healthy tissue (circled regions) in a malignant lymph node as the low attenuation coefficient regions in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="f10">Fig. 10(b)</xref>, which is difficult to identify using the original OCT image in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="f10">Fig. 10(c)</xref>. This contrast was attributed to the changes in size and texture of cell nuclei resulting from the neoplastic transformation.<xref rid="r86" ref-type="bibr"><sup>86</sup></xref> Their method was further developed and applied by Scolaro et al.<xref rid="r17" ref-type="bibr"><sup>17</sup></xref> for imaging the absolute attenuation coefficients in axillary lymph nodes (<inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math276"><mml:mrow><mml:mi>n</mml:mi><mml:mo>=</mml:mo><mml:mn>4</mml:mn></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula>). They summarized the OCT attenuation coefficients of various tissue subtypes, as included in <xref rid="t003" ref-type="table">Table 3</xref>, to guide the classification of different tissue types within the lymph node.
