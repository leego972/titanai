Previous studies on phototoxicity of GFPs showed the low efficiency for triplet state formation and singlet oxygen production by EGFP [<xref rid="pone.0144617.ref018" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>]. Nevertheless, the experiments by Momiyama et al. [<xref rid="pone.0144617.ref019" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>] and Kimura et al. [<xref rid="pone.0144617.ref020" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>] indicated that fluorescent proteins GFP and RFP increased the efficacy of ultraviolet C on cancer cells and, consequently, enhanced PDT.</p><p>KR is the only GFP-like protein that displays strong phototoxicity, exceeding that of EGFP by at least 1,000-fold. This property allows it to be considered as a potential genetically encoded photosensitizer for the PDT of cancer.</p><p>In this research we examined the effects of both CW and pulsed lasers on KR-expressing colon carcinoma CT26 in mice, and selected treatment regimen effective from the point of view of antineoplastic action. Significant cellular damage and inhibition of tumor growth was caused in CT26-KR tumors by irradiation with the pulsed laser at 337 J/cm<sup>2</sup> at a wavelength of 584 nm.</p><p>The immediate question regarding KR as a therapeutic agent is the best method of delivery of genetic material encoding the protein, or of the protein itself, to the tumor. A small number of systems for KR plasmid delivery to cancer cells have been developed—an adenovirus-mediated system [<xref rid="pone.0144617.ref021" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>], the use of a polymeric micelle-encapsulating quantum dots [<xref rid="pone.0144617.ref022" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>], and the use of chitosan complexes each with a negatively-charged poly(γ-glutamic acid) [<xref rid="pone.0144617.ref023" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>], but at the moment these authors' papers have only reported expression of KR in the gene-transfected cells <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> with a resulting suppression of cell proliferation and viability following irradiation with light.
