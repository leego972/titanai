From a financial point of view, it is easier and less expensive to scale-up the US technology compared to other techniques like microwave (MW), pulsed electric fields (PEF), high voltage electric field (HVEF), and high-pressure processing (HPP) methods [<xref rid="b0150" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>]. Further, the technology generates better yield and thus improves the economics of the extraction process. For instance, Tsaltaki, Katsouli, Kekes, Chanioti and Tzia [<xref rid="b0200" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>] compared the recovery of bioactive compounds from Damiana leaves (<italic toggle="yes">Turnera diffusa</italic>) by using US, MW, heat reflux (CON), and soxhlet (SOX) extraction methods using 50% ethanol. In that study, US (20 kHz 40 °C, and 15 min) achieved the highest total phenolic yield 203.96 mg GAL/ dry leaves (DL), compared to MW (300 W, 50 °C, and 15 min) and SOX (2.4 × 10<sup>6</sup> Pa, 100 °C, and 6 h) with 191.36 and 161.62 mg GAL/DL. Similarly, the US increased (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.05) the Ginsenside recovery from ginseng (<italic toggle="yes">Panax ginseng</italic>) herb by 31.1%, 19.5%, and 12.1% compared to CONV, MW, and SOX methods, respectively. Additionally, Panadare, Gondaliya and Rathod [<xref rid="b0520" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>] found that US pre-treatment (150 s at 30 W) increased oil yield by 11% from <italic toggle="yes">Annona squamosal</italic> herb compared to CON methods.</p><p id="p0210">Carbone, Macchioni, Petrella and Cicero [<xref rid="b0560" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>] compared the use of US and MW for the extraction efficiency of bioactive compounds from hop herb (<italic toggle="yes">Humulus lupulus</italic>). They mentioned that MW (2.45 GHz, 2.4 × 10<sup>6</sup> Pa, 210 °C, and 1 min) resulted in a higher extraction power for its phenolic contents (95 mg GAL/g) than US (40 kHz, 25 °C, and 30 min) with 25 mg GAL/g. This study suggested that in terms of yield, the efficacy of the US will depend on the plant/ material used, and thus optimization reported in the literature cannot be extrapolated to other materials. In the study of Carbone and colleagues [<xref rid="b0550" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>], it is important to highlight that US treatment did not use high power like MW (2.45 GHz, 210 °C, 2.4 × 10<sup>6</sup> Pa), which could have a negative effect on the phytochemicals structure and function. In terms of selectivity, treating rosemary leaves with US (19.5 kHz,140 W) showed remarkably high (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.05) recovery of carnosic acid and rosmarinic acid contents (13%, 6.8% of the dried extract) compared to MW (100 °C, 20 bar) [<xref rid="b0150" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>].
