Effect of the AYW-KK-04 on the Nrf2-Keap1 and BDNF Pathways in UCMS-Exposed Mice</title><p>Quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) was employed to evaluate the transcriptional levels of genes related to neuroplasticity and oxidative stress regulation, including BDNF, CREB, Nrf2, and Keap1, in the frontal cortex and hippocampus of mouse brains. As illustrated in <xref rid="pharmaceuticals-19-00339-f002" ref-type="fig">Figure 2</xref>, vehicle-treated UCMS mice exhibited a significant reduction in BDNF and CREB mRNA expression in both brain regions compared with non-stressed control mice. In contrast, daily administration of the AYW-KK-04 formulation at a dose of 800 mg/kg or vitamin E markedly restored gene expression, resulting in significant upregulation of BDNF and CREB transcripts in the frontal cortex and hippocampus (<xref rid="pharmaceuticals-19-00339-f002" ref-type="fig">Figure 2</xref>a,b).</p><p>To elucidate the molecular basis of the antioxidant activity of AYW-KK-04, the transcript levels of Nrf2 and Keap1 were initially analyzed using real-time PCR. As presented in <xref rid="pharmaceuticals-19-00339-f002" ref-type="fig">Figure 2</xref>c,d, mice subjected to UCMS and receiving vehicle treatment displayed a marked suppression of Nrf2 mRNA expression, concomitant with a significant elevation of Keap1 transcripts in both the hippocampus and frontal cortex when compared with the non-stressed control group. Notably, treatment with 800 mg/kg AYW-KK-04 formula or vitamin E effectively attenuated these effects by significantly upregulating Nrf2 mRNA and modulating Keap1 expression. These results suggest that 800 mg/kg/day AYW-KK-04 formula or vitamin E can activate the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway, potentially enhancing the cellular defense against chronic stress-induced oxidative damage.</p></sec><sec id="sec2dot5-pharmaceuticals-19-00339"><title>2.5. Effect of the AYW-KK-04 Formula on Oxidative Stress and Antioxidant Enzyme Activities in the Brains</title><p>In line with the transcriptional activation of the Nrf2 signaling pathway, the functional activities of the antioxidant enzymes superoxide dismutase (SOD) and catalase (CAT) were subsequently assessed. Prolonged exposure to UCMS resulted in a significant decline in SOD and CAT activities in both the hippocampus and frontal cortex when compared with non-stressed controls. Conversely, treatment with the AYW-KK-04 formulation at a dose of 800 mg/kg/day or with vitamin E markedly enhanced the activities of these enzymes relative to the UCMS plus vehicle group (*** <italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.001) (<xref rid="pharmaceuticals-19-00339-f003" ref-type="fig">Figure 3</xref>a,b).</p><p>To further verify whether the reinforcement of the antioxidant defense system was associated with a reduction in oxidative injury, levels of malondialdehyde (MDA), a widely recognized indicator of lipid peroxidation, were quantified. As illustrated in <xref rid="pharmaceuticals-19-00339-f003" ref-type="fig">Figure 3</xref>c, mice exposed to UCMS displayed a pronounced elevation in MDA levels relative to non-stressed controls, reflecting substantial oxidative stress. In contrast, administration of the AYW-KK-04 formulation at 800 mg/kg/day or vitamin E significantly attenuated MDA concentrations in both the hippocampus and frontal cortex when compared with the UCMS plus vehicle group (*** <italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.001). Collectively, these results indicate that the AYW-KK-04 formula confers effective protection against chronic stress-induced oxidative damage by modulating the Nrf2/Keap1 signaling pathway, enhancing antioxidant enzyme activities, and consequently limiting lipid peroxidation (<xref rid="app1-pharmaceuticals-19-00339" ref-type="app">Supplement S4–S6</xref>).</p></sec><sec id="sec2dot6-pharmaceuticals-19-00339"><title>2.6.
