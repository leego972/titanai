While latex use is well established across African traditional medicine, the breadth of conditions treated in Sheikh District illustrates a refined local knowledge of plant chemistry and pharmacodynamics, consistent with ethnobotanical practices observed in Africa and Asia [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR3">3</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR19">19</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR74">74</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR157">157</xref>].</p><p id="Par148">Several species exhibited notable therapeutic versatility. <italic toggle="yes">Balanites aegyptiaca</italic> roots were used for asthma, bark for snakebite, and fruits for diarrhea, highlighting the species’ cultural and medicinal significance. Preventive and culturally embedded practices were also observed, such as chewing <italic toggle="yes">Cissus rotundifolia</italic> roots to prevent snakebite or using <italic toggle="yes">Punica granatum</italic> leaves and seeds mixed with milk to treat “eye evil.” These practices illustrate the integration of spiritual and physiological concepts in traditional healthcare [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR20">20</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR78">78</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR95">95</xref>].</p><p id="Par149">Ethnonutritional and chronic disease management practices were additionally recorded. Seeds of <italic toggle="yes">Cordeauxia edulis</italic> were chewed to address malnutrition, while leaves of <italic toggle="yes">Moringa stenopetala</italic> were used to manage diabetes, demonstrating the dual role of certain plants in both nutrition and medicinal therapy. These findings highlight the dynamic, context-specific nature of traditional healthcare in Sheikh District and emphasize the empirical foundation of indigenous knowledge systems.</p><p id="Par150">By explicitly documenting new reports for Somaliland and rare uses for East Africa, this study provides a valuable baseline for pharmacological research, comparative ethnobotanical studies, and conservation planning. These findings underscore the urgency of preserving indigenous medicinal knowledge, as locally refined practices may inform modern drug discovery, culturally sensitive healthcare interventions, and sustainable resource management at regional and global scales.</p><p id="Par151">
