Special attention should be directed to 9,11-octadecadienoic acid (<bold>96</bold>) due to its dual antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity. Mechanistic studies have shown that this isomer suppresses COX-2 expression, reduces pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and TNF-α, and activates PPARγ-dependent lipid-regulating pathways [<xref rid="B144-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">144</xref>]. Related conjugated dienes, such as 13-KODE ((9Z,11E)-13-oxooctadeca-9,11-dienoic acid), exert similar anti-inflammatory effects via inhibition of NF-κB/MAPK signaling and activation of Nrf2 [<xref rid="B146-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">146</xref>].</p><p>In our view, the identification of rare fatty acids such as 9,11-octadecadienoic acid positions <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> as a potential source of specialized lipid mediators beyond nutritional relevance. These metabolites may contribute to the anti-inflammatory and metabolic regulatory effects traditionally attributed to this species. However, the lack of in vivo validation, bioavailability data, and mechanistic studies remains a major gap in current research. Future investigations should prioritize the pharmacokinetics, metabolic stability, and synergistic interactions of these fatty acids—particularly in comparison with other Araliaceae taxa—to substantiate their potential for phytotherapeutic or nutraceutical applications.</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot9-molecules-30-03460"><title>5.9. Newly Identified Volatile and Lipophilic Constituents in Polyscias fruticosa</title><p>Recent phytochemical investigations of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> leaf extracts have led to the identification of several volatile and lipophilic metabolites previously unreported within the <italic toggle="yes">Polyscias</italic> genus [<xref rid="B4-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>]. These compounds were characterized by gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC-MS) in two solvent-derived fractions: n-hexane (PFLH) and dichloromethane (PFLD) [<xref rid="B10-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>]. In total, five newly identified constituents were detected, with the majority either exclusively present or more abundant in the PFLD fraction, highlighting the influence of solvent polarity on metabolite recovery (<xref rid="molecules-30-03460-t008" ref-type="table">Table 8</xref>).</p><p>The presence of unique volatile and lipophilic compounds in PFLD highlights the underexplored chemical space of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, especially with regard to less polar but pharmacologically active constituents that may escape detection in aqueous or ethanol-based extracts.</p><p>Among these, 3-hydroxy-4,5-dimethylfuran-2(5H)-one (<bold>110</bold>), a furanone-type lactone, stands out due to its known antioxidant and antimicrobial activities. Detected in the PFLD at 0.95 ± 0.03%, this semi-volatile compound may contribute significantly to the pharmacological properties of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> extracts. Although this compound has been reported in other medicinal plants, its occurrence in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> may reflect species-specific stress-related biosynthetic pathways that deserve further study.</p><p>Additionally, four aliphatic hydrocarbon-related compounds were identified: octanal (<bold>111</bold>), hexadecanal (<bold>112</bold>), 3-methylene-7,11-dimethyl-1-dodecene (<bold>113</bold>), and linoleyl alcohol (<bold>114</bold>) (<xref rid="molecules-30-03460-t008" ref-type="table">Table 8</xref>). Octanal, a short-chain aldehyde with a citrus aroma, exhibits potent antibacterial and cytotoxic effects, with IC<sub>50</sub> values below 20 µg·mL<sup>−1</sup> against selected cancer cell lines [<xref rid="B147-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">147</xref>,<xref rid="B148-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">148</xref>]. Hexadecanal (palmitaldehyde), a saturated long-chain aldehyde, is a known lipid peroxidation byproduct and sphingolipid metabolism intermediate, linked to oxidative stress mechanisms and inflammatory responses relevant to vascular health [<xref rid="B148-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">148</xref>,<xref rid="B149-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">149</xref>].</p><p>3-methylene-7,11-dimethyl-1-dodecene (<bold>113</bold>), an unsaturated hydrocarbon likely of terpenoid origin, may act as a semiochemical involved in plant–environment interactions, particularly in defense signaling.
