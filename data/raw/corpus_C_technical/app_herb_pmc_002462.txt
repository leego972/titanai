It is important to select the best extraction method and appropriate extraction solvents in relation to the intended use of the final medicinal plant extract (such as identification or separation of bioactive compounds and test for biological activities) since they have influence on the extraction yield [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref015" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>]. However, the extractive yield is not a measure of biological activities and thus, higher yield of NSS oil extracted using hexane does not imply a higher antimicrobial activity than NSS oil extracted with absolute ethanol.</p><p>Furthermore, the oil extracted using absolute ethanol was greasy and viscous. This may be accountable for the differences in extraction yield of the oil obtained in the current study in addition to the extraction time, temperature, and the strength of the types of solvents used [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref029" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0272457.ref030" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec025"><title>Qualitative screening of phytochemicals from different extracts of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds</title><p>The phytochemical screening was achieved through biochemical testing on different extracts (petroleum spirit, ethyl acetate, methanol, and aqueous extracts) of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds to detect the presence of major natural or medicinally active components. This qualitative screening test showed different results in different extracts of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds as shown in <xref rid="pone.0272457.g001" ref-type="fig">Fig 1</xref> and <xref rid="pone.0272457.t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref>. Cardiac glycosides were only detected in methanol extract whereas saponins were only detected in the aqueous extract.
