In a randomized, split-side, noninferiority study comparing DL-PDT with either BF‐200 ALA or MAL for the treatment of Grade I–II AKs, clearance rates after 12 weeks were comparable following one treatment session (79.8 ± 23.6% and 76.5 ± 26.5%, respectively; <italic toggle="yes">P</italic> &lt; 0.0001 for noninferiority) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR60">60</xref>]. The sides treated with BF‐200 ALA gel also had lower 1‐year recurrence rates (19.9%) compared with sides treated with MAL cream (31.6%, <italic toggle="yes">P</italic> = 0.01) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR60">60</xref>]. Another randomized split-face trial of 13 patients with a total of 177 Grade I–III AKs compared treatments using either BF-200 ALA or 16% MAL cream followed by DL-PDT and found both regimens effectively cleared AKs (by 85% with BF-200 ALA and 74% with MAL; <italic toggle="yes">P</italic> = 0.099), suggesting that DL-PDT activation is not limited to only one group of photosensitizers [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR64">64</xref>].</p><p id="Par30">A potential limitation of DL-PDT is its reliance on natural sunlight, which is affected by seasonal variations, ambient temperature fluctuations, and UVB emission. A small, retrospective, proof-of-concept pilot study overcame this limitation by using 35-min exposure to an indoor DL-PDT multilight lamp device emitting 415 nm (blue), 585 nm (yellow), and 635 nm (red) wavelengths sequentially to mimic the solar radiation spectrum after CO<sub>2</sub> AFR pretreatment and a 45–60-min incubation with BF-200 ALA [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR65">65</xref>]. An average of 3.5 months after one session, 72% of patients with field cancerization of the scalp, face, neck, and/or dorsal hand (defined as &gt; 5 AKs) were noted to be in complete remission, but the maximal pain intensity reported was severe (mean ± SD, 9.0 ± 2.0 out of 10.0). Because less pain was reported in other studies using either BF-200 ALA [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR28">28</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR60">60</xref>] or ablative fractional laser (AFL) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR53">53</xref>] in conjunction with ALA, the authors attributed this finding to potential free radical byproducts from AFL-irradiated PpIX [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR65">65</xref>]. Interestingly, treatment of patients with mild-to-moderate AK on the face and scalp with BF-200 ALA photosensitizer and indoor simulated daylight resulted in an individual AK clearance rate of 67–100% and an overall lesion clearance rate of 85%, with half of participants reporting no pain at all in a small (<italic toggle="yes">n</italic> = 12) prospective open-label trial [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR59">59</xref>].</p></sec></sec></sec><sec id="Sec14"><title>Organ Transplant Recipients</title><p id="Par31">Patients who have received organ transplants are usually on immunosuppressive drugs and are at increased risk of developing NMSC, especially cSCC [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR58">58</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR79">79</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR80">80</xref>]. In OTR, cSCCs tend to be more aggressive and more highly metastatic than in the general population, highlighting the need for prevention in these patients [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR58">58</xref>].</p><p id="Par32">Data suggest the efficacy of conventional PDT is lower in OTR patients, with complete response rate at 48 weeks after 20% 5-ALA solution for 5 h followed by visible light illumination of 0.55 (95% CI 0.38–0.71) versus 0.72 (95% CI 0.54–0.86) in immunocompetent controls [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR81">81</xref>]. While there is limited evidence for the treatment of NMSCs in OTR candidates [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR82">82</xref>], systematic reviews of randomized clinical trials have found that complete clearance rates are superior following MAL-PDT (40–76.4%) versus imiquimod (27.5–62.1%), diclofenac (41%), and 5-FU (11%) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR82">82</xref>], and support use of PDT for preventing additional AKs/SCCs at least 3 months after treatment as well as achieving complete clearance of AK/SCC [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR80">80</xref>].</p><p id="Par33">Advantages of PDT compared with surgery in OTRs include potential for treating larger affected areas and no documented reports of infection after PDT treatment. In recipients with lower pain thresholds, alternative modalities such as DL-PDT or AFL-assisted PDT can be employed to minimize treatment-associated pain [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR58">58</xref>].
