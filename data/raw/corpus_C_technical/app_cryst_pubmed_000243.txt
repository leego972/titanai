Metal-free, low-cost, organic photocatalytic graphitic carbon nitride (g-C3N4) 
has become a promising and impressive material in numerous scientific fields due 
to its unique physical and chemical properties. As a semiconductor with a 
suitable band gap of ~2.7 eV, g-C3N4 is an active photocatalytic material even 
after irradiation with visible light. However, information regarding the 
toxicity of g-C3N4 is not extensively documented and there is not a 
comprehensive understanding of its potential adverse effects on human health or 
the environment. In this context, the term "toxicity" can be perceived in both a 
positive and a negative light, depending on whether it serves as a benefit or 
poses a potential risk. This review shows the applications of g-C3N4 in 
sensorics, electrochemistry, photocatalysis, and biomedical approaches while 
pointing out the potential risks of its toxicity, especially in human and 
environmental health. Finally, the future perspective of g-C3N4 research is 
addressed, highlighting the need for a comprehensive understanding of the 
toxicity of this material to provide safe and effective applications in various 
fields.
