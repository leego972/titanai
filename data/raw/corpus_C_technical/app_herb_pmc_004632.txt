Objective tests include measuring mental speed/information processing speed and attention using sensitive and robust computerized tests [<xref rid="B5-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>,<xref rid="B7-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>].</p><p>Brain fatigue symptoms can vary in intensity and daytime. The most common signs include an abnormal loss of mental energy, prolonged recovery time, difficulty concentrating, memory problems, mental fog, slowness of thinking, reduced cognitive performance, increasing degree of mental fatigue over the day, difficulty in starting activities, emotional lability and irritability, stress sensitivity, etc. (see <xref rid="pharmaceuticals-18-00261-t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref>).</p><p>Symptoms of brain and mental fatigue can overlap but have key differences, often reflecting each condition’s physical versus psychological nature. Below is a breakdown of the differences.</p><p>Symptoms of brain fatigue (physical exhaustion of the brain) include the following:</p><list list-type="bullet"><list-item><p>Cognitive slowness: slower reaction times or delayed thinking processes.</p></list-item><list-item><p>Mental fog: difficulty processing information or understanding concepts.</p></list-item><list-item><p>Headaches or pressure: sensations in the head after cognitive tasks.</p></list-item><list-item><p>Memory issues: forgetfulness or inability to recall recent information.</p></list-item><list-item><p>Visual disturbances: blurred vision, light sensitivity, or difficulty focusing the eyes.</p></list-item><list-item><p>Physical tiredness: feeling physically drained after mental exertion, even without significant physical activity.</p></list-item><list-item><p>Coordination problems: in severe cases, mild issues with hand-eye coordination or balance.</p></list-item></list><p>These symptoms are often related to overexertion of brain activity or neurological conditions and can worsen with lack of rest.</p><p>Symptoms of mental fatigue (psychological exhaustion) include the following:<list list-type="bullet"><list-item><p>Lack of motivation: feeling demotivated to start or continue tasks, even simple ones.</p></list-item><list-item><p>Irritability: becoming easily frustrated, annoyed, or emotionally reactive.</p></list-item><list-item><p>Difficulty concentrating: struggling to focus on tasks, especially those requiring sustained attention.</p></list-item><list-item><p>Indecisiveness: making decisions, even about trivial matters, is more demanding.</p></list-item><list-item><p>Emotional numbness: feeling emotionally detached, disinterested, or overwhelmed by small things.</p></list-item><list-item><p>Sleep disturbances: insomnia or restless sleep due to racing thoughts or stress despite feeling tired.</p></list-item><list-item><p>Burnout-like symptoms: feeling mentally and emotionally exhausted, often linked to chronic stress or overload.</p></list-item></list></p><p>While brain fatigue often presents with physical symptoms such as slowed cognitive function, mental fatigue tends to be more tied to emotional and motivational issues such as irritability, lack of focus, and emotional exhaustion. However, the two can often coexist, especially in high-stress situations.</p><p>Neuropsychological examination often yields results within normal limits in cases of brain fatigue, even if the patient describes significant problems in everyday life [<xref rid="B117-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">117</xref>].</p><p>A wide variety of techniques, methods, and tools were implemented to detect and assess mental fatigue, such as self-reporting questionnaires, attention network tests, heart rate variability, salivary cortisol levels, electroencephalogram, brain imaging, and saccadic eye movements [<xref rid="B2-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="B95-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>,<xref rid="B113-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>,<xref rid="B114-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>,<xref rid="B115-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">115</xref>,<xref rid="B118-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">118</xref>].</p><p>Various self-assessments and diverse types of questionnaires were developed to detect and assess mental fatigue, including the following:<list list-type="bullet"><list-item><p>Occupation fatigue exhaustion recovery (OFER) scale;</p></list-item><list-item><p>Visual analog scale to evaluate fatigue severity (VAS-F);</p></list-item><list-item><p>Fatigue assessment scale (FAS);</p></list-item><list-item><p>Multidimensional fatigue inventory (MFI);</p></list-item><list-item><p>Fatigue severity inventory (FSI);</p></list-item><list-item><p>Mental fatigue scale (MFS).</p></list-item></list></p><p>Overall, the pros of mental fatigue detection methods include multiple brief questionnaires, which are reliable, easy to administer, and accessible.
