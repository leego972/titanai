Introduction</title><p>The genus <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> (Hong Jing Tian; Crassulaceae) consists of more than 200 species, of which approximately 20, including <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea, Rhodiola alterna, Rhodiola brevipetiolata, Rhodiola crenulata, Rhodiola kirilowi, Rhodiola quadrifida, Rhodiola sachalinensis</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola sacra</italic>, are used as traditional medicines in Asia [<xref rid="b1-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>]. These plants grow mainly in the Himalayan belt, Tibet, China, and Mongolia, but are also cultivated in Europe and North America and are available on the market as dietary supplements [<xref rid="b2-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="b3-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> plants extracts are traditionally used in tonics and adaptogen, antidepressant, and anti-inflammatory drugs [<xref rid="b2-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="b4-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>]. The most widely known is <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic>, which is also called the “golden root” or “roseroot.” The pharmacological effects of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic>, including its role in increasing longevity, stimulating the central nervous system, and elevating work performance as well as its cardio-, neuro-, and hepatoprotective effects and immunotropic, antiviral, anti-inflammatory, and antibacterial activities, have been studied extensively [<xref rid="b1-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="b5-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> has been used for a long time in Eastern Europe and Asia to enhance physical and mental performance. It is also used in Eastern Europe and Asia as a traditional medicine to stimulate the nervous system, alleviate depression and fatigue, enhance work performance, and prevent high-altitude sickness, mountain malhypoxia, and anoxia [<xref rid="b6-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>], and in Russia and Mongolia for treating long-term illness and weakness caused by infection [<xref rid="b7-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>]. In addition, <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> has been proven to have cardiovascular protection effects [<xref rid="b8-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>,<xref rid="b9-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. In recent years, the root extracts of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> have been used as drinks, food additives, and in commercial pharmaceutical preparations offered worldwide [<xref rid="b10-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> plants contain polyphenols such as flavonoids, proanthocyanidines, tyrosol, and cinnamyl alcohol, as well as glycosides, organic acids, essential oils, sugars, fats, alcohols, and proteins [<xref rid="b5-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>].
