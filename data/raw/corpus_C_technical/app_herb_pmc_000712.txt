AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) suppresses NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B cascade through inhibition of IKK and decreased I<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B<italic toggle="yes">α</italic> degradation. Mitogen-Activated Protein Kinase (MAPK) Cascade</title><p>Accumulated evidences have demonstrated that MAPK cascade including extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2 (ERK1/2), c-Jun N-terminal protein kinase (JNK), and p38 MAPK are involved in the diabetic complications [<xref rid="B21" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>]. p38 MAPK consists of four isoforms including p38<italic toggle="yes">α</italic>, p38<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>, p38<italic toggle="yes">γ</italic>, and p38<italic toggle="yes">δ</italic>. P38<italic toggle="yes">α</italic> is the major form expressed in a healthy heart, and p38<italic toggle="yes">β</italic> displays lower expression. p38 MAPK especially p38<italic toggle="yes">α</italic> MAPK contributes to the development of DCM owing to inflammation, oxidative stress, apoptosis, hypertrophy, metabolic abnormalities, and disordered Ca<sup>2+</sup> handling. High glucose promotes the expression of protein kinase C (PKC) in the neonatal rat cardiomyocytes, leading to the upregulation of ROS, MAPK, and NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B [<xref rid="B22" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>]. ROS activates p38 MAPK, which in turn, promotes the production of ROS; alternatively, downregulation of p38 MAPK can inhibit ROS generation and oxidative stress [<xref rid="B23" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>]. p38<italic toggle="yes">α</italic> MAPK has been shown to promote the cardiomyocyte apoptosis by activating STAT1 and NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B and contribute to cardiomyocyte hypertrophy through activating the GATA4 transcription factor. MAPKPK-2 (MK2), a p38 MAPK downstream target, is responsible for downregulation of SERCA2a, FFA accumulation, and NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B activation in the development of DCM. On the contrary, the antiapoptotic function of p38<italic toggle="yes">β</italic> MAPK in DCM has been reported [<xref rid="B21" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>].</p><p>ERK1/2 signaling pathway is also known as the Ras-Raf-MEK-ERK cascade. ERK1/2 pathway is triggered by the activation of Ras at the myocyte membrane. The detrimental effects of ERK1/2 in a diabetic heart are manifested as oxidative stress, apoptosis, hypertrophy, and myocardial fibrosis. ERK1/2 activated by high glucose is associated with cardiac hypertrophy in DCM [<xref rid="B24" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>]. ROS and ET-1 stimulate the ERK1/2 cascade; hence, antioxidant agents block the ROS generation and ERK1/2 activation as well as cardiac hypertrophy [<xref rid="B25" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>]. The hyperglycemia-induced increased TGF-<italic toggle="yes">β</italic> is suppressed by ERK1/2 inhibitor U0126, suggesting that ERK1/2 mediates upregulation of TGF-<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>, closely related to cardiomyocyte fibrosis [<xref rid="B26" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>]. Interestingly, Zhang et al. showed that fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21) protects the diabetic heart from cardiac apoptosis, remodeling, and dysfunction by activating ERK1/2, and this protection effect could be abolished by ERK1/2 inhibitor PD98059 [<xref rid="B27" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>].
