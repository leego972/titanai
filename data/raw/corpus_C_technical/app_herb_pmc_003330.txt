Leaves are common and favorite parts used for medicinal treatment preparation because of easy handling and sustainability [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref057" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0235886.ref058" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>]. The latter is linked to the survival rate of medicinal plants. Removing the leaves biomass within reasonable limits does not interfere with the plant life, compared to collecting the stem, root, or whole plant, which may risk the plant life [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref059" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>]. Moreover, many reports have showed that leaves contain diverse plant secondary metabolites [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref060" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>]. In the present study, no data have been obtained for the use of flowers as medicinal materials. This might offer other perspectives for further investigation. Furthermore, our data have also shown the use of more than one plant part from the same plant species. For instance, <italic toggle="yes">J</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">curcas</italic> leaves and stem have been used to treat hyperuricemia and mouth ulcer, respectively.</p><p>People from the Ngadisari village use many methods to prepare plant parts before using them as herbal medicine. The decoction is considered the main mode of preparation (40.9%), followed by pounding (15.9%) and burning (13.6%). Meanwhile, eating raw (9.1%) and smearing (6.8%) contribute and of the total mode of preparation in the present study. Other miscellaneous modes of preparations constitute the remaining 13.6% (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="pone.0235886.g004">Fig 4</xref>). Some other studies have also mentioned the same results, where the most common method of preparation is decoction [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref061" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>–<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref063" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>]. Simple, easy handling and inexpensive are the major reasons why this mode of preparation is widely used by society [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref064" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>]. Moreover, other reports also demonstrated that decoction might increase the efficiency of plant extraction and therefore increase its bioactivity [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref065" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>].</p><fig id="pone.0235886.g004" orientation="portrait" position="float"><object-id pub-id-type="doi">10.1371/journal.pone.0235886.g004</object-id><label>Fig 4</label><caption><title>Mode of preparation of the medicinal plants used by the people of Tengger in Ngadisari village, Indonesia.</title></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="pone.0235886.g004.jpg"/></fig><p>Some plants can be prepared without any processing. For example, leaves of <italic toggle="yes">A</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">graveolens</italic> are eaten raw to reducing hypertension symptoms and leaves of <italic toggle="yes">A</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">vulgaris</italic> are applied directly by clogging into the nose to stop nosebleeds. All this local knowledge is preserved and applied by the people of Tengger in Ngadisari village. This practice is common in other regions in Indonesia such as in Madura and Bali [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref005" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0235886.ref066" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>].</p></sec></sec><sec sec-type="conclusions" id="sec017"><title>Conclusions</title><p>Our results highlighted the use of medicinal plants by people from the Ngadisari village, Indonesia. A total of 30 medicinal plant species were recorded in the present study. They belong to 20 different families, where Poaceae and Zingiberaceae were the most representative families. A high number of plant species were used for treating internal medical diseases, respiratory-nose, ear, oral/dental, and throat problems. Leaves were the most popular plant part used and decoction was the most common method of preparation. These findings indicated potential roles of medicinal plants used in the Ngadisari village. Furthermore, our study characterized the cultural values of the people of the Ngadisari village.
