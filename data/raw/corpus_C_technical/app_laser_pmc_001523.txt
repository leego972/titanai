To achieve the significant depth range required by our application (on the order of tens of centimeters), we selected a quasi-linear-in-<italic toggle="yes">k</italic>, swept laser (Insight, Inc.) with a sweep frequency of 100 kHz and centered at 1.3 µm. The all-semiconductor laser cavity is programmatically tuned to produce linear-in-<italic toggle="yes">k</italic> sweeps during known intervals on a 400 MHz clock [<xref rid="r18" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>]. Each sweep in <italic toggle="yes">k</italic> is calibrated and consists of tens of transition periods to complete the total sweep bandwidth. During these transition periods the laser wavenumber is updated for the next wavenumber interval and is not well controlled. Hence, the samples acquired during the transition are called “invalid points” and discarded in post-processing. Although the analog laser sweep is nonlinear-in-<italic toggle="yes">k</italic> due to the non-constant sweep profile, once the invalid points are removed in post-processing, <italic toggle="yes">dk</italic> is constant. In the analog domain, the laser sweep is highly repeatable and results in interferograms with a non-constant, but narrow temporal bandwidth. This laser then provides the generally limited interferometric bandwidth required by EFS-OCT.</p><p>Two Insight lasers with different specifications were used to assess the performance of EFS-OCT.
