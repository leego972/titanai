# PMC: ethnobotany medicinal plants Africa Asia — Overview

The lechuguilla production [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B50">50</xref>], wood use [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B51">51</xref>], amole root used as shampoo[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B52">52</xref>], ixtle fiber extraction [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B53">53</xref>], consumption of wild beans [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B54">54</xref>], laurel oils uses [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B55">55</xref>] recorded in the north, central and south of Mexico are also common practices of routine use in Rayones; all of them are similarly used in Rayones for same purposes. People still produce lechuguilla fibers, however, this practice is almost vanished because of the low prices of the fibers. Several roots (<italic toggle="yes">Agave</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Opuntia</italic>) are commonly used for make soap, food or for medicinal use in a similar way to the <italic toggle="yes">chicana</italic> (<italic toggle="yes">Hechtia</italic>) roots [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B40">40</xref>], and other species of <italic toggle="yes">Agave</italic> from northwestern Mexico [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B56">56</xref>]. In Tamaulipas, 610 useful species were recorded, including 334 medicinal and 154 timber species [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B57">57</xref>], almost 30% of them are found in Rayones. For the arid areas of Tamaulipas, 53 medicinal species have been recorded [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B58">58</xref>], many of these species are present and used in Rayones in a similar way, especially the herbaceous and shrub species. In Coahuila, the ethnic Kikapoo uses at least 150 useful plant species, 26 of them are widely used as medicinal [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B59">59</xref>], almost 12% of them are commonly used in Rayones. In northwestern Mexico, the Raramuri ethnia uses dozens of medicinal plants, and has been compared to those found in city markets [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B60">60</xref>], some of them are used as a poisonous plants [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B61">61</xref>], only one species (<italic toggle="yes">Solanum douglasii</italic>) is used as a poisonous plant in Rayones. In Chihuahua it is known that many Raramuri kids know up to 40 different useful species [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B62">62</xref>], greater amount than that in younger individuals interviewed in Rayones. Raramuri women know at least 356 different useful species, highlighting those medicinal, forage, food, fuel and construction [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B17">17</xref>]. In Sonora, where Pima and Yaqui ethnicity persist, still uses several plants as living fences [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B63">63</xref>] and many species are used as food [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B64">64</xref>], several of these genera are also used in Rayones for the same purpose. Of the nearly seventy medicinal plants recorded on the market of Monterrey [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B65">65</xref>], almost 60% of them are used in Rayones in similar way, especially the medicinal species. Almost 70% of plants recorded in Rayones are used also in similar way in the Cumbres de Monterrey National Park [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B11">11</xref>], and almost 80% of the useful flora recorded in Rayones was recorded in the southern area of Nuevo León, México [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B12">12</xref>].</p></sec></sec><sec><title>Consent</title><p>Written informed consent was obtained from the patient for the publication of this report and any accompanying images.</p></sec><sec><title>Competing interests</title><p>The authors declare that they have no competing interests.</p></sec><sec><title>Authors’ contributions</title><p>The field work and database confection was carried out by EEC, MGL, BSM, MRS, and JVQ, data analysis was carried out by EEC, HGR, CCA, DGU, ICS and ACP.
