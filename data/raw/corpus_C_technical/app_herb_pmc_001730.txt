[<xref rid="B17-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>] on the ethanolic extract of the leaves collected in Saudi Arabia was active against resistant Gram-positive bacteria such as <italic toggle="yes">Micrococcus</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Methicillin-resistant S. aureus</italic> (MRSA) at 200 mg/mL. The most susceptible bacteria were <italic toggle="yes">K. pneumonia</italic>, MRSA, and <italic toggle="yes">B. subtilius</italic>. The diameters of the inhibition zones of the extract against these bacterial strains were 29.33 mm, 27.66 mm, 22.66 mm, and 23.33 mm, respectively. The positive control streptomycin inhibition zones against the above bacteria were 25 mm, 20 mm, 19 mm, and 27 mm, respectively. The results indicated the extract was as active as the 10 μg/disc of streptomycin. The extract was also reported to be active against the pathogenic Gram-negative <italic toggle="yes">E. coli</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">P. aeruginosa</italic> with inhibition zones of 25.66 mm and 25.66 mm, respectively. The inhibition zones were compared to the positive control 10 μg/disc of streptomycin, which suggested that the activity of the extract against these three Gram-negative bacteria was comparable to streptomycin. </p><p>HPLC-based analysis of the active extract showed that it mainly contained hydroxybenzoic acids (reported in % <italic toggle="yes">w</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">w</italic> of crude extract) such as gallic acid <bold>25</bold> (0.00543), syringic acid <bold>28</bold> (0.00037), chlorogenic <bold>40</bold> (trace), <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-hydroxybenzoic <bold>31</bold> (0.0002), vanillic acid <bold>29</bold> (0.0002), <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-coumaric acid <bold>37</bold> (0.00083), and salicylic acid <bold>32</bold> (0.00013). Possible Mechanisms of Action (MOA) of Antibacterial Compounds Identified in <italic toggle="yes">A. saligna</italic></title><p>Flavonoids’ MOAs for the inhibition or killing of bacteria has been suggested in three modes based on their structures [<xref rid="B74-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>]. The MOA of identified antibacterial compounds in <italic toggle="yes">A. saligna,</italic> such as quercetin <bold>3</bold>, kaempferol <bold>22</bold>, myricetin <bold>10</bold>, and rutin <bold>6</bold>, might occur via the destruction of the bacterial cytoplasmic membrane [<xref rid="B75-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>] by a perforation mechanism [<xref rid="B76-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>], decreasing membrane fluidity [<xref rid="B77-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>], or disrupting lipid bilayers and membrane barriers. They could also inhibit energy metabolism [<xref rid="B78-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">78</xref>] by inhibiting NADH cytochrome c reductase. They might also inhibit nucleic acid synthesis [<xref rid="B79-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">79</xref>] by inhibiting the DNA gyrase enzyme. </p><p>Other suggested MOAs associated with bacterial resistance include inhibiting biofilm formation and e-flux pumps. For instance, rutin <bold>6</bold> at a 50 μg/mL concentration inhibited the biofilm formation of <italic toggle="yes">E. coli, S. aureus</italic> [<xref rid="B80-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>], and <italic toggle="yes">Streptococcus suis</italic> at 19.5 μg/mL [<xref rid="B81-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec3dot2dot2-molecules-28-04396"><title>3.2.2.
