Because PDT cannot penetrate lesions beyond 2 mm deep, the use of surgical curettage to reduce tumor depth to less than 2 mm followed by subsequent PDT may be beneficial in the treatment of BCC [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR23">23</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>]. Data suggest that debulking thicker tumors prior to PDT treatment increases complete response rate after 3 years of follow-up relative to older studies where pretreatment debulking may not have been performed [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR30">30</xref>].</p><p id="Par12">Studies utilizing 5-ALA or MAL have demonstrated promising results for the treatment of BCC (Table <xref rid="Tab2" ref-type="table">2</xref>) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR31">31</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR33">33</xref>]. In a 3-year, prospective, open-label trial of 174 adult patients with dermoscopically/histologically confirmed superficial BCC and nodular BCC, combining curettage of visible tumor (sparing dermis and adjacent normal skin) with 16% MAL cream and photoactivation with red light-emitting diode (LED) light for 23 min resulted in overall 3-year clearance rates of 96.1% after a mean of 2.6 sessions for superficial BCC and 95.2% after a mean of 2.7 sessions for nodular BCC [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>]. An open-label trial assessing 12% 5-ALA-PDT gel with a 3-h incubation period and photoactivation with red light for the treatment of superficial BCCs on the head and neck, trunk, and extremities achieved a complete response rate of 95.8% at 3 months after the second treatment session and a 3-year recurrence-free survival rate of 88.3% [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR31">31</xref>]. In another interventional cohort study of histologically confirmed BCCs &lt; 2 mm deep in adult patients, 20% 5-ALA ointment with a 4-h incubation time under occlusion and red light illumination resulted in complete response for 47/54 lesions (87%) after only one session and for an additional 5/54 lesions after two sessions [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR32">32</xref>]. In a small pilot interventional study [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR33">33</xref>], three patients with basal cell nevus syndrome (Gorlin syndrome) underwent six ALA-PDT treatments in three biweekly (day 1 and day 7) sessions spaced 2 months apart. Each tumor was treated with 20% ALA, and matching contralateral treatment zones were illuminated with red light and blue light after a 4-h incubation.
