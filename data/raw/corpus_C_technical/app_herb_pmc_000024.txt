Conclusions</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Euclea natalensis</italic> is an important and frequently used herbal medicines in tropical Africa. The species is widely used for human diseases and ailments such as abdominal pains, antidote for snake bites, diabetes, diarrhoea, malaria, roundworms, stomach problems, toothache and venereal diseases. Recent research on <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> focused primarily on antimicrobial properties of the crude extracts of the species, naphthoquinone and pentacyclic terpenoid compounds isolated from the species. Literature studies revealed that naphthoquinone and pentacyclic terpenoid compounds isolated from <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> such as shinanolone <bold>13</bold> exhibited antibacterial effects [<xref rid="B50-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>], shinanolone <bold>13</bold>, 20(29)-lupene-3β-isoferulate <bold>14</bold>, octahydroeuclein <bold>15</bold> and β-sitosterol <bold>16</bold> exhibited antifungal effects [<xref rid="B53-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>], isodiospyrin <bold>3</bold>, neodiospyrin <bold>10</bold>, diospyrin <bold>11</bold>, 7-methyljuglone <bold>12</bold> and shinanolone <bold>13</bold> exhibited antimycobacterial effects [<xref rid="B17-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>,<xref rid="B39-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>,<xref rid="B52-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>,<xref rid="B55-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>,<xref rid="B65-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>,<xref rid="B66-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>], diospyrin <bold>11</bold> and 7-methyljuglone <bold>12</bold> exhibited cytotoxic effects [<xref rid="B17-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>]. The compound 7-methyljuglone <bold>12</bold> appears to be the most efficient antimycobacterial of all the compounds that have been isolated from <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> so far. Any future research on <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> should consolidate its ethnomedicinal usage with its phytochemistry and pharmacological effects if ethnopharmacological potential of the species is to be fully realized. Such further research should assess mechanisms of actions, clinical effectiveness and proper dosage for the documented ethnomedicinal uses and associated pharmacological activities. Based on current information, the ethnomedicinal uses and documented pharmacological effects of the species show that there is not enough systematic data on phytochemistry and pharmacological effects for the majority of the ethnomedicinal applications of the species. There is still need for research on phytochemical, bioactive compounds and other medicinal ingredients and minerals that can be used to explain the wide use of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> as herbal medicine in tropical Africa. Future studies should also focus on the mechanism of biological activities and structure–function relationships of bioactive constituents of the species. Likewise, animal studies and clinical studies are to a large degree missing and should be carried out to determine the potential of this plant to be used in human medicine.</p></sec></body><back><ack><title>Acknowledgments</title><p>Research reported in this publication was supported by the National Research Foundation (NRF) and Govan Mbeki Research and Development Centre (GMRDC), University of Fort Hare.
