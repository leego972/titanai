Antidiabetic Effects</title><p>Diabetes mellitus is a metabolic disorder and global health concern with complicated factors. The rapid development of modern society leads to unhealthy eating behavior, less physical activities, and overloaded stress management, which increases the risk of diabetes in adolescents and young adults [<xref rid="B62" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>]. In addition, the occurrence of diabetes increases the risk of complications that are still costly to be controlled by current drugs, such as diabetic retinopathy and nephropathy [<xref rid="B63" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>]. Numerous studies reveal that many herbal medicines and their bioactive compounds show significant hypoglycemic effects mainly by regulating glucose metabolism, improving insulin sensitivity and insulin resistance, and restoring the damaged pancreas [<xref rid="B64" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> as a medicinal herbal plant has a long history of being used to attenuate the symptoms of diabetes which is also called “Xiaoke” disease in China. The hypoglycemic efficacy of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> makes it a common ingredient in Xiaoke decoction for type 2 diabetes treatment [<xref rid="B65" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>].</p><p>Some enzymes are involved in glycemic control, such as <italic toggle="yes">α</italic>-glucosidase and <italic toggle="yes">α</italic>-amylase, and they have been developed as therapeutic targets for type 2 diabetes prevention and treatment [<xref rid="B66" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>]. Several main antidiabetic compounds were identified based on the inhibition of <italic toggle="yes">α</italic>-glucosidase and <italic toggle="yes">α</italic>-amylase activities in <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic>. The crude extract of its stems was reported to have IC50 values of 78.1 <italic toggle="yes">μ</italic>g/mL on <italic toggle="yes">α</italic>-glucosidase activity and 116.7 <italic toggle="yes">μ</italic>g/mL <italic toggle="yes">α</italic>-amylase activity. Moreover, there were six compounds associated with <italic toggle="yes">α</italic>-glucosidase inhibition, such as N-p-coumaroyltyramine and 3,4,4′-trihydroxy-5-methoxybibenzyl. And 3,4-dihydroxy-4′,5-dimethoxybibenzyl was the only identified compound with <italic toggle="yes">α</italic>-amylase inhibitory activities [<xref rid="B67" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>]. Type 2 diabetes is tightly related to abnormal metabolisms, such as hepatic glucose metabolism, insulin resistance, and low-grade inflammation. <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> polysaccharides could decrease the levels of fasting blood glucose, insulin, glycated serum protein, and serum lipid profile and alleviate pancreatic injury as well as the dysregulated metabolism of bile acids and amino acids in type 2 diabetic rats [<xref rid="B36" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>]. In addition, it could regulate the hepatic glucose metabolism via the glucagon-mediated signaling pathways as well as the liver-glycogen structure in HFD/STZ-induced diabetic mice [<xref rid="B68" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>]. Furthermore, the polysaccharides reduced the fasting blood sugar levels in mice by increasing insulin in serum and stimulating the glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) secretion which is an important hormone regulator in the progression of diabetes. And the in vitro study showed that the stimulated GLP-1 secretion may be related to the Ca<sup>2+</sup>/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase (CaMK) and p38-MAPK pathways in the murine enteroendocrine cell line STC-1 cells [<xref rid="B69" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> extracts could prevent STZ-induced type 1 diabetes in mice, which increased the level of liver glycogen and taurine and upregulated energy and amino acid metabolism [<xref rid="B70" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>].</p><p>Apart from type 1 and type 2 diabetes, diabetic complications are also recognized as a severe health concern. <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> polysaccharides were demonstrated to ameliorate diabetic cataracts in rats, and it could reduce the severity of the opacity of the lens by downregulating the microRNA-125b and MAPK signaling pathways, in which the level of microRNA-125b was positively correlated with the levels of ERK1, ERK2, Raf, and Ras [<xref rid="B71" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec4.3"><title>4.3.
