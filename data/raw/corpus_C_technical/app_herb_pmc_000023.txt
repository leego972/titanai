Cytotoxicity and Toxicity</title><p>Lall et al. [<xref rid="B17-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>] evaluated cytotoxicity of crude chloroform extract of the roots of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic>, diospyrin <bold>11</bold> and 7-methyljuglone <bold>12</bold> by exposing different concentrations of samples to green monkey kidney cells (Vero) and a mouse macrophage cell line, J774A.1. Cytotoxicity results for the Vero cell line showed that the crude extract and diospyrin <bold>11</bold> had 50% maximal inhibitory concentration (IC<sub>50</sub>) values of 64.87 and 17.78 µg/mL, respectively. The concentration of 7-methyljuglone <bold>12</bold> that effected a 90% reduction of growth of <italic toggle="yes">Mycobacterium tuberculosis</italic> Erdman within J774.1 macrophages was 0.57 µg/mL [<xref rid="B17-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>]. Similarly, Lall et al. [<xref rid="B18-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>] evaluated cell toxicity of root extracts of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> by determining the effect of the crude extracts and diospyrin <bold>11</bold> on the monolayers of primary vervet monkey kidney (VK) cells. The dose of the plant samples that inhibited 50% cell growth (ID<sub>50</sub>) after the incubation period was 0.1 mg/mL and 0.2 mg/mL for acetone and water extracts, respectively. The compound diospyrin <bold>11</bold> exhibited an ID<sub>50</sub> value of 0.02 mg/mL on VK cells. The water extract from the roots of the plant was the least toxic to cell cultures and inhibited the replication of HSV-1 moderately at a concentration of 0.2 mg/mL [<xref rid="B18-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>]. More et al. [<xref rid="B31-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>] evaluated cytotoxicity of ethanol leaf extracts of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> using the XTT (sodium 3′-[1-(phenyl amino-carbonyl)-3,4-tetrazolium]-bis-[4-methoxy-6-nitro) benzene sulphonic acid hydrate) assay method. The extracts showed cytotoxicity activity on the Vero cell line with IC50 value of 285.1 ± 4.9 µg/mL [<xref rid="B31-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>]. Mahapatra et al. [<xref rid="B71-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>] evaluated cytotoxicity activities of the compound 7-methyljuglone <bold>12</bold> isolated from the roots of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> and its synthetic derivatives using the XTT assay method. Cytotoxicity results for the Vero cell line showed that the compound 7-methyljuglone <bold>12</bold> and synthesized compounds had EC<sub>50</sub> values ranging from 2.5 µg/mL to 36.1 µg/mL [<xref rid="B71-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>]. Kishore et al. [<xref rid="B80-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>] evaluated cytotoxicity activities of 7-methyljuglone <bold>12</bold> isolated from the root extract of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> and a series of its derivatives on MCF-7, HeLa, SNO and DU145 human cancer cell lines using the XTT method. Most of the 7-methyljuglone derivatives exhibited significant toxicity on HeLa and DU145 cell lines with IC<sub>50</sub> values ranging from 5.3 µM to 10.1 µM [<xref rid="B80-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>]. Lall et al. [<xref rid="B67-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>] evaluated in vitro cell cytotoxicity using cell lines (primary peripheral blood mononuclear cells, U937 monocytes and Chang liver cells), acute and sub-acute toxicity were carried out on eight-week-old female Balb/c mice by administering ethanolic shoot extracts of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> orally. During the study conducted on the cytotoxic effect of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> on peripheral blood mononuclear cells, U937 monocytes and Chang liver cells, <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> extract showed no cellular toxicity with IC<sub>50</sub> values ranging from 131.3 ± 1.67 µg/mL to 208.9 ± 10.3 µg/mL. An IC<sub>50</sub> value below 50 µg/mL has been considered to be moderately toxic and samples with a toxicity value higher than 100 µg/mL have been considered to be non-toxic [<xref rid="B67-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>].
