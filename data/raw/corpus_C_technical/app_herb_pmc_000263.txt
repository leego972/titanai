The plants were collected from their natural habitats or from the “<italic toggle="yes">Attarin</italic>” or the herb shops. A small portion of plant parts (100 g) were extracted and tested in different <italic toggle="yes">in-vitro</italic> bioassays related to AD. The medicinal uses of the listed plants (Table <xref ref-type="table" rid="T1">1</xref>) were discussed in detail by numerous scholars as Ibn Sina “Avicenna”, Ibn El Beitar, El Baironi, Al Antaki, Al Mo’tamed [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B11">11</xref>-<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B14">14</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B20">20</xref>].</p><p>The inhibition effect of the methanolic extracts from the 23 different extracts on AChE activity was screened (Table <xref ref-type="table" rid="T2">2</xref>, Figure <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F1">1</xref>). The screening was performed at a concentration of 1000 μg/ml and the activity guidelines of our program only considered the extracts as active if they only inhibited the enzyme more than 50%. The screening showed different effects on AChE activity as shown in Table <xref ref-type="table" rid="T2">2</xref>. Extracts from <italic toggle="yes">M. officinalis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">B. sacra</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Z. officinalis</italic> activated AChE more than 45%. Only, two species namely <italic toggle="yes">A. vasica</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">P. harmala</italic> inhibited AChE by 86 and 90% respectively (Figure <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F1">1</xref>). Further testing and analyses of the inhibition of AChE by <italic toggle="yes">A. vasica</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">P. harmala</italic> revealed the IC<sub>50</sub> values of 294 and 68 μg/ml
