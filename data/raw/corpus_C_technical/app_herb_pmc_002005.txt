Anti-Osteoclastogenic Potential of Polyscias fruticosa in Bone Health</title><p>The maintenance of skeletal homeostasis relies on a delicate balance between bone-forming osteoblasts and bone-resorbing osteoclasts. Disruption of this balance in favor of osteoclastic bone resorption underlies the pathogenesis of several skeletal disorders, including osteoporosis, rheumatoid arthritis, and Paget’s disease [<xref rid="B175-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">175</xref>].</p><p>Osteoclasts originate from monocyte/macrophage precursors and undergo differentiation in response to key cytokines—macrophage colony-stimulating factor (M-CSF) and receptor activator of nuclear factor kappa-B ligand (RANKL). Engagement of RANKL with its receptor RANK activates intracellular cascades, such as the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathways and nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NF-κB), which in turn upregulate essential transcription factors, including c-Fos and NFATc1, driving osteoclastogenesis [<xref rid="B176-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">176</xref>].</p><p>Tran et al. [<xref rid="B19-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>] investigated the anti-osteoclastogenic effects of ethanol extract from <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> leaves (EEPL) in both bone marrow-derived macrophages (BMMs) and RAW264.7 murine macrophage cells. Treatment with EEPL at concentrations ranging from 12.5 to 100 μg·mL<sup>−1</sup> markedly inhibited RANKL-induced osteoclast differentiation in a dose-dependent manner. At the highest tested concentration (100 μg·mL<sup>−1</sup>), the extract suppressed the formation of tartrate-resistant acid phosphatase-positive (TRAP<sup>+</sup>) multinucleated cells by more than 85% relative to the RANKL-only control (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.001).</p><p>EEPL also disrupted actin ring formation and significantly reduced the resorption pit area, indicative of impaired osteoclast function (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.01 and <italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.001 for higher doses).</p><p>This suppression of both osteoclastogenesis and resorptive capacity indicates functional inhibition beyond mere cytotoxicity.</p><p>These functional outcomes were accompanied by decreased mRNA and protein expression of osteoclast-associated markers, including TRAP, matrix metallopeptidase-9 (MMP-9), cathepsin K (CtsK), c-Fos, and NFATc1. Mechanistically, EEPL selectively inhibited the phosphorylation of p38 and JNK MAPKs while leaving ERK1/2 signaling unaffected, suggesting targeted interference with RANKL-mediated downstream signaling events [<xref rid="B19-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>,<xref rid="B176-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">176</xref>].</p><p>The antiresorptive activity of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> is comparable to that of other phytotherapeutics with known bone-protective properties. For example, ethanol extracts of <italic toggle="yes">Osmanthus fragrans</italic> have been shown to inhibit osteoclastogenesis by reducing intracellular reactive oxygen species and attenuating both NF-κB and MAPK signaling pathways [<xref rid="B176-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">176</xref>].</p><p>Likewise, <italic toggle="yes">Perilla frutescens</italic> hexane leaf extract inhibited osteoclast differentiation through suppression of MMP-9 expression and RANKL pathway modulation in both cellular and animal models [<xref rid="B177-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">177</xref>].</p><p>Such comparisons underscore the growing relevance of botanicals in bone health management, particularly those modulating osteoimmune crosstalk.</p><p>Given the adverse effects associated with conventional antiresorptive agents such as bisphosphonates, including osteonecrosis of the jaw and atypical femoral fractures, plant-based therapeutics like EEPL offer an attractive, multitarget alternative with a favorable safety profile.</p><p>Notably, <italic toggle="yes">P.
