Xeroderma Pigmentosum (XP)</title><p>XP is a genetic disorder with a DNA repair defect characterized by extreme sensitivity to ultraviolet (UV) light. The extreme sensitivity to UV light results in multiple skin cancers, such as AK, squamous cell carcinoma or melanoma at a young age. A publication in Africa showed promising results of dPDT for AK in XP patients [<xref rid="B64-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>]. A total of 13 XP children were applied with either ALA or MAL followed by two hours of indoor light exposure. Weekly imiquimod was not discontinued prior to and after PDT. Crusting and scaling at the treated area were seen for two days. Improvement was seen after two weeks and no adverse events were seen at three-months follow-up [<xref rid="B64-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>]. Alicia et al. studied the efficacy of cPDT on dermal fibroblasts from XP [<xref rid="B65-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>]. Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAF) and normal fibroblasts were immersed in MAL (1mM) for five hours and then exposed to different light doses (from 1.8 to 16.7 J/cm<sup>2</sup>) with a 384 light-emitting diode (LED) matrix emitting 636 nm ± 17 nm red light. Results showed a significant difference in the decrease of survival between CAF and normal fibroblasts at 40 and 5%, respectively. The authors concluded that the selective cytotoxicity might be related to the decrease of inactive caspase-3 followed by increased expression of cleaved Poly ADP Ribose Polymerase (PARP) after cPDT in CAF.</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot4-molecules-25-05195"><title>5.4. Bowen’s Disease</title><p>The European consensus also recommended treating Bowen’s disease (BD, or squamous cell carcinoma in situ) with cPDT and dPDT [<xref rid="B3-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B66-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>]. Roba et al. reported the first BD patient treated with MAL-dPDT under two hours of sunlight exposure. The resolution of BD was achieved at a three-month follow-up [<xref rid="B67-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>]. A study in Brazil [<xref rid="B68-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>] enrolled 24 BD lesions which were treated with two sessions of MAL-dPDT with a one-week interval. 25% of BD showed complete remission and 33% showed &gt;75% partial response. Lesions located at sun-exposed areas, such as the upper limbs and head/neck area, showed better response than those on the trunk and lower limbs. Adverse events were minimal and tolerable. We treated patients successfully who suffered from chronic arsenism with multiple BDs with dPDT (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="molecules-25-05195-f006">Figure 6</xref>, unpublished data).</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot5-molecules-25-05195"><title>5.5. Basal Cell Carcinoma</title><p>Wiegell et al. in Denmark treated 32 superficial or small nodular basal cell carcinoma (BCC) with two sessions of MAL-dPDT with a one-week interval and achieved 74% complete response (CR) at 12-month follow-up [<xref rid="B69-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>]. The skin was exposed to 2.5 h of sunlight with a mean effective light dose of 10.8 J/cm<sup>2</sup>. No pain was reported in 72% of the treatments. The pain was mild (0–4/10 visual analog scale) if reported. All patients reported a good or excellent cosmetic outcome. MAL-dPDT combined with imiquimod 5% for superficial BCC was reported to be superior to dPDT alone at 12-month follow-up (CR response of 91.3% versus 83.4%) [<xref rid="B70-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>]. dPDT was not recommended for nodular BCC. Fai et al. reported no response after dPDT on the nodular BCC formed in the treatment field of AK [<xref rid="B46-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>,<xref rid="B49-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot6-molecules-25-05195"><title>5.6. Benign Skin Diseases</title><p>dPDT has been applied to benign skin lesions such as acne vulgaris and photorejuvenation. A randomized clinical study, conducted in China, compared cPDT and dPDT for acne vulgaris and revealed no statistical difference in objective treatment response rate between the dPDT group and cPDT group at weeks two, four and six [<xref rid="B71-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>] with a two-week treatment interval.
