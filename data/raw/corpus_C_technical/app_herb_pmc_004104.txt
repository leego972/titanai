Antagonism of CRF1 by <italic toggle="yes">O. tenuiflorum</italic> interrupts this cascade at the pituitary level [<xref rid="B69-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>]. In addition, tulsi enhances parasympathetic nervous system activity, thereby reducing sympathoadrenal system (SAS) activation and promoting relaxation and recovery [<xref rid="B70-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>].</p><p>In vitro studies have confirmed that <italic toggle="yes">O. tenuiflorum</italic> extract directly inhibits cortisol release from H295R adrenocortical cells following stimulation with forskolin (an adenylyl cyclase activator) in a dose-dependent manner (1–100 μg/mL), reducing cortisol levels by 42% at the highest tested concentration [<xref rid="B68-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>]. This finding indicates a dual mechanism of action: a central effect (CRF1 antagonism) and a peripheral effect (direct inhibition of adrenal steroidogenesis) [<xref rid="B68-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>]. The reduction in cortisol release is mediated by decreased expression of key steroidogenic enzymes, including CYP11A1 (cholesterol side-chain cleavage enzyme) and 3β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (3β-HSD) [<xref rid="B68-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>].</p><p>Eugenol (4-allyl-2-methoxyphenol), the major constituent of <italic toggle="yes">O. tenuiflorum</italic> essential oil (70–80% of its composition), exhibits strong antioxidant activity through activation of the Nrf2 signaling pathway and upregulation of antioxidant enzymes such as superoxide dismutase (SOD), catalase, and glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px) [<xref rid="B35-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>]. Eugenol suppresses oxidative stress in neurons via direct scavenging of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and modulation of mitochondrial function, thereby protecting against neurodegeneration [<xref rid="B36-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>]. Furthermore, eugenol exerts anti-inflammatory effects by inhibiting NF-κB and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) activation, leading to reduced production of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α [<xref rid="B35-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>].</p><p>Ursolic acid, a pentacyclic triterpenoid from <italic toggle="yes">O. tenuiflorum</italic> leaves, exhibits neuroprotective effects through modulation of apoptotic pathways and increased expression of brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) and neurotrophin-3 in the hippocampus [<xref rid="B71-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>]. Ursolic acid inhibits activation of caspase-3 and caspase-9, prevents loss of mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm), and increases the Bcl-2/Bax ratio, thereby protecting neurons against oxidative stress-induced apoptosis [<xref rid="B71-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>]. This mechanism explains the antidepressant effects and cognitive improvements observed following <italic toggle="yes">O. tenuiflorum</italic> supplementation [<xref rid="B71-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>].</p><p>Clinically, <italic toggle="yes">O. tenuiflorum</italic> (300–600 mg/day of leaf extract for 60 days) reduces symptoms of generalized anxiety by 35–58% compared with placebo in randomized controlled trials [<xref rid="B69-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>]. Tulsi decreases serum cortisol levels by 18–24%, improves psychological stress parameters (Perceived Stress Scale, PSS), and enhances sleep quality and overall well-being [<xref rid="B69-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>]. In individuals exposed to occupational stress, tulsi supplementation (400 mg/day for 6 weeks) improved memory, attention, and concentration by 20–30% compared with baseline values [<xref rid="B69-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">O. tenuiflorum</italic> also exhibits immunomodulatory activity by increasing T- and B-lymphocyte proliferation, natural killer (NK) cell activity, and production of Th1 cytokines (IFN-γ, IL-2), thereby strengthening cell-mediated immunity [<xref rid="B69-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>]. In addition, tulsi shows hepatoprotective, cardioprotective, and antidiabetic effects through modulation of the AMPK, PI3K/Akt, and PPAR-γ signaling pathways [<xref rid="B69-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>]. Traditional Ayurvedic uses of <italic toggle="yes">O. tenuiflorum</italic> include the treatment of asthma, bronchitis, malaria, diarrhea, dyspepsia, and skin diseases, which are supported by contemporary pharmacological studies [<xref rid="B69-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec3dot7-nutrients-18-00931"><title>3.7.
