Khan et al. [<xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0023" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>] reported that LLLT 830 and 810 nm and LED 660 and 850 nm all stimulated colony formation. However, lower doses of LLLT at 830 and 810 nm produced a more pronounced increase in both the number and size of epithelial colonies.</p></sec></sec><sec id="cbf70161-sec-0140"><label>3.3</label><title>In Vivo</title><sec id="cbf70161-sec-0150"><label>3.3.1</label><title>Macroscopic Analysis</title><p>Six studies performed macroscopic analyses [<xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0025" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0027" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0031" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0032" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0033" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0034" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>]. All studies reported a reduction in wound area following LLLT (633–810 nm) and LED (530–640 nm). Two studies found no significant differences in wound closure between LED and LLLT in diabetic and healthy animals [<xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0025" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0031" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>]. De Sousa et al. [<xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0033" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>] reported enhanced re‐epithelialization with LED at 530 nm compared with other LED wavelengths and LLLT, conversely, one study demonstrated superior effects of LLLT [<xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0026" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>]. In animal burn models, LLLT promoted faster wound healing [<xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0032" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0034" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="cbf70161-sec-0160"><label>3.3.2</label><title>Microscopic Analysis</title><p>Ten studies performed microscopic analyses [<xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0026" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0027" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0028" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0029" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0030" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0031" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0032" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0034" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0035" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0036" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>]. Both LLLT (660–810 nm) and LED (635–808 nm) promoted improved cellular organization, enhanced fibroblast proliferation, angiogenesis, collagen accumulation, epidermal migration, and increased expression of Proliferating Cell Nuclear Antigen (PCNA) and Transforming Growth Factor‐β2 (TGF‐β2). Two studies reported reduced neutrophil infiltration and increased lymphocyte and macrophage counts with LED and LLLT [<xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0034" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0035" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>]. Conversely, LED did not increase collagen fiber content but improved structural organization compared to LLLT [<xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0029" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>]. LLLT 660 nm induced better tissue organization and regeneration than LLLT and other LEDs wavelengths [<xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0030" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>]. LLLT promoted keratinized epithelium with parallel collagen and spindle‐shaped fibroblasts, reducing inflammation in non‐anemic animals at Day 14, whereas LED produced similar results in anemic animals [<xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0031" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>]. LLLT 780 nm yielded greater type I collagen deposition than LED and others LLLT wavelengths [<xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0035" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="cbf70161-sec-0170"><label>3.3.3</label><title>Molecular Analysis</title><p>Four studies assessed protein expression [<xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0026" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0027" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0029" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0034" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>]. LLLT (660–904 nm) and LED (460–850 nm) modulated proteins involved in proliferation, collagen synthesis, migration, VEGF formation, and extracellular matrix regeneration [<xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0027" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0029" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>, <xref rid="cbf70161-bib-0034" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>].
