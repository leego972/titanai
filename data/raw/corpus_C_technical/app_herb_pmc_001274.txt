Natural products within this framework are advantageous due to their multifaceted forms and inherent molecular diversity, which generate many pharmacological effects [<xref rid="B58-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>,<xref rid="B59-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>,<xref rid="B60-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>].</p><p>A study by Wang et al. (2022) demonstrated that nanoparticles can be used for co-delivery of these drugs in cancer therapy [<xref rid="B61-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>]. In recent studies, mesoporous nanoparticles were used to deliver 5-fluorouracil (5-FU), a chemotherapy drug used for chemotherapy and curcumin (<xref rid="ijms-25-08792-f006" ref-type="fig">Figure 6</xref>). This combination led to cell cycle arrest and apoptosis in laryngeal tumours (Hep-2 cells) [<xref rid="B62-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>].</p><p>Drug repositioning and repurposing existing drugs for new therapeutic applications presents an opportunity for phytochemicals. Bioactive phytochemicals such as the taxanes (<bold>14</bold>), ellipticine (<bold>15</bold>), camptothecin (<bold>16</bold>), combretastatin (<bold>17</bold>), podophyllotoxin (<bold>19</bold>), homoharringtonine (<bold>20</bold>) (<xref rid="ijms-25-08792-f007" ref-type="fig">Figure 7</xref>), and others are reported for their potential anticancer effects on various neoplastic diseases [<xref rid="B63-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>].</p><p>Moreover, phytochemicals have been applied in cancer immunotherapy and vaccines and used as immune checkpoint inhibitors [<xref rid="B64-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>]. The FDA has confirmed the use of natural products and immunotherapeutic approaches in cancer treatment (described above). Moreover, the process of discovering cancer drugs has been accelerated by natural products. Approximately 47% of anti-tumour drugs have been reported to be derived from natural products [<xref rid="B52-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>,<xref rid="B65-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>,<xref rid="B66-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>,<xref rid="B67-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>,<xref rid="B68-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>,<xref rid="B69-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>,<xref rid="B70-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>,<xref rid="B71-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>,<xref rid="B72-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>]. Several studies have shown that natural compounds are capable of reducing estrogen receptor alpha (ER-α) levels, angiogenesis suppression proliferation and metastasis inhibition, apoptosis, and cell cycle arrest of breast tumours [<xref rid="B52-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>,<xref rid="B73-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>,<xref rid="B74-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>,<xref rid="B75-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>,<xref rid="B76-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>].</p><p>Moreover, phytochemicals targeting pathways like Hh, Notch, and Wnt/β-catenin and cancer stem cell resistance mechanisms show promise in reducing chemotherapy resistance. Therefore, it is crucial to assess plant-derived compounds’ safety, efficacy, and pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic properties [<xref rid="B77-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec2dot3-ijms-25-08792"><title>2.3. Phytochemicals as Antimicrobials</title><p>The search for novel antibiotics should be accelerated as there are new microbial resistance determinants in bacteria, some of which have no effective remedies [<xref rid="B78-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">78</xref>]. Microbial pathogens have developed self-defence machinery, which protects them against antimicrobial drugs, antibiotics, and pesticides (<xref rid="ijms-25-08792-f005" ref-type="fig">Figure 5</xref>). These mechanisms are active in pathogenic microbes, especially antibiotic-resistant phenotypes, ensuring their protection against a wide range of antibiotics [<xref rid="B79-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">79</xref>,<xref rid="B80-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>].</p><p>Plants have been shown to possess antimicrobial activities, even in their crude form. The crude extracts or powders can further be purified to enhance potencies [<xref rid="B81-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>]. Several medicinal plant species are distributed across the African region and have been shown to possess some antimicrobial properties, e.g., <italic toggle="yes">Hibiscus calyphyllus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Cassia abbreviata</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Dicoma anomala</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Securidosa longipendunculata</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Lippia javanica</italic>, to name a few [<xref rid="B82-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">82</xref>].
