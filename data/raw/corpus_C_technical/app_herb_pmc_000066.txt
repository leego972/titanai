Anti-Rheumatism Effect</title><p>Studies showed that decoction, and an alcoholic extract of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> roots are used to treat rheumatism [<xref rid="B45-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>,<xref rid="B190-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">190</xref>].</p></sec></sec></sec><sec id="sec6-molecules-21-00331"><title>6. Pharmacokinetics</title><sec id="sec6dot1-molecules-21-00331"><title>6.1. Absorption</title><p>The bioavailability of the constituent eurycomanone was investigated in animal research [<xref rid="B65-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>]. Following intravenous injection, eurycomanone was detected in the plasma, declining to zero within 8 h. Following oral administration, C<sub>max</sub> and T<sub>max</sub> values were 0.33 ± 0.03 mcg/mL and 4.40 ± 0.98 h, respectively. The plasma concentration was lower following oral administration <italic toggle="yes">vs.</italic> intravenous administration, even at a much higher oral dose (five times the dose). The authors concluded that eurycomanone is poorly bioavailable orally (10.5%).</p><p>In animal research, less than 1% of the constituent 9-methoxycanthin-6-one was found to be absorbed orally [<xref rid="B100-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">100</xref>].</p><p>Following oral administration of a standardized extract (Fr 2) of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic>, 13 α(21)-epoxy-eurycomanone had a higher C<sub>max</sub> than eurycomanone (1.61 ± 0.41 mcg/mL <italic toggle="yes">vs.</italic> 0.53 ± 0.10 mcg/mL) [<xref rid="B62-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>,<xref rid="B86-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>,<xref rid="B191-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">191</xref>]. The absolute bioavailability was also higher due to increased membrane permeability (higher log Kow value of −0.43 <italic toggle="yes">vs.</italic> −1.46 at pH 1). Following oral administration of a standardized extract (Fr 2) of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic>, eurycomanol and 13α,21-dihydroeurycomanone were not detected in plasma [<xref rid="B62-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec6dot2-molecules-21-00331"><title>6.2. Distribution</title><p>In animal research, the volume of distribution (<italic toggle="yes">V<sub>d</sub></italic>) of eurycomanone was relatively high (0.68 ± 0.30 L/kg), suggesting that it is well distributed in the extravascular fluids [<xref rid="B65-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec6dot3-molecules-21-00331"><title>6.3. Excretion</title><p>Following intravenous injection, the mean elimination rate constant (<italic toggle="yes">k<sub>e</sub></italic>) and clearance (<italic toggle="yes">CL</italic>) for eurycomanone were 0.88 ± 0.19 h<sup>−1</sup> and 0.39 ± 0.08 L/h/kg, respectively [<xref rid="B65-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec6dot4-molecules-21-00331"><title>6.4 CYP Inhibition</title><p><italic toggle="yes">In vitro</italic> evaluation of the modulatory effects of eurycomanone, an active constituent of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> on cytochrome P450 (CYP) isoforms CYP1A2, CYP2A6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2E1 and CYP3A4 were conducted by Pan <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> They indicated that eurycomanone did not potently inhibit any of the investigated CYP isoforms, with IC50 values greater than 250 μg/mL, hence appears to be little likelihood of drug-herb interaction via CYP inhibition [<xref rid="B192-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">192</xref>].</p><p>Recent, CYP inhibition study of <italic toggle="yes">E.</italic>
<italic toggle="yes">longifolia</italic> by Han <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, showed that <italic toggle="yes">E. logifolia</italic> has a weak, concentration-dependent inhibitory effect on CYP1A2, CYP2A6, and CYP2C19 isozymes, showing IC<sub>50</sub> values of 324.9, 797.1, and 562.9 μg/mL, respectively. It needs careful attention in taking <italic toggle="yes">E.</italic>
<italic toggle="yes">longifolia</italic> extracts products with conventional drugs [<xref rid="B193-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">193</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec6dot5-molecules-21-00331"><title>6.5 Half-Life</title><p>Following intravenous administration of a standardized extract (Fr. 2) of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic>, 13α(21)-epoxyeurycomanone had a longer biological half-life than eurycomanone (0.75 ± 0.25 h <italic toggle="yes">vs.</italic> 0.35 ± 0.04 h), due to a lower elimination rate constant [<xref rid="B62-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>].
