Antiviral Activities</title><p>Lall et al. [<xref rid="B18-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>] evaluated antiviral activities of acetone and water extracts of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> and the compound diospyrin <bold>11</bold> isolated from this species against herpes simplex virus Type 1 (HSV-1). The acetone extract of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> showed moderate antiviral activity against HSV-1, at concentrations of 0.1 to 0.02 mg/mL as shown by the reduction of virus-induced cytopathogenic effects and the protection of cells in a cell viability assay. The compound diospyrin <bold>11</bold> exhibited no inhibitory effects while water extracts exhibited weak activity at a concentration of 0.2 mg/mL which corresponded to a 42% cytopathic effect [<xref rid="B18-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>]. Mahapatra et al. [<xref rid="B71-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>] evaluated the HIV-1 reverse transcriptase inhibition activities of the compound 7-methyljuglone <bold>12</bold> isolated from the roots of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> and its synthetic derivatives against recombinant HIV-1 enzyme using non-radioactive HIV-RT colorimetric assay. The compound 7-methyljuglone <bold>12</bold> and synthesized compounds exhibited potent inhibitory activities ranging from 70% to 100% at 100 µg/mL [<xref rid="B71-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>]. These findings will provide baseline data to future research focusing on correlating the traditional use of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> as herbal medicine for viral infections and the antiviral properties of the species.</p></sec></sec><sec id="sec5dot2-molecules-22-02128"><title>5.2. Antidiabetic Activities</title><p>Nkobole et al. [<xref rid="B72-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>] evaluated antidiabetic activities of acetone root extracts of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> by assessing in vitro α-glucosidase and α-amylase enzyme assays. The plant extract demonstrated inhibition of 92.6 ± 0.04% and 74.5 ± 0.04% at 0.2 mg/mL on α-glucosidase and α-amylase, respectively. The compounds lupeol <bold>2</bold> and β-sitosterol <bold>16</bold> isolated from the stem bark of <italic toggle="yes">Terminalia sericea</italic> Burch. ex DC. stem bark were evaluated for antidiabetic activities using α-glucosidase and α-amylase enzyme assays. Compounds lupeol <bold>2</bold> and β-sitosterol <bold>16</bold> inhibitory activities on α-glucosidase with 50% maximal inhibitory concentration (IC<sub>50</sub>) values of 66.5 µM and 66.5 µM, respectively [<xref rid="B72-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>]. Against α-amylase, the compounds lupeol and β-sitosterol exhibited moderate activities with IC<sub>50</sub> values of 140.7 µM and 216.0 µM, respectively.
