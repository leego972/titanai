Introduction</title><p>The <italic toggle="yes">Brassicaceae</italic> family, commonly called <italic toggle="yes">crucifers</italic>, stands out as one of the most frequently cultivated and consumed all over the world with around 338 genera and more than 3700 species [<xref rid="B1-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>]. In recent years, <italic toggle="yes">cruciferous</italic> vegetables have attracted increased attention as they represent an excellent source of nutrients (carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, vitamins, and minerals) and health-promoting phytochemicals (phenolics, flavonoids, and glucosinolates) responsible for the prevention and treatment of various diseases via several biological qualities, including anti-obesity, antioxidant, anticancer, antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and antidiabetic activities [<xref rid="B1-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B2-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="B3-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B4-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>].</p><p>Among all the <italic toggle="yes">Brassicaceae</italic> family genera, the <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> (B.) genus is the most known and the most important one [<xref rid="B5-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>].
