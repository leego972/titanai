# PMC: phytochemical bioactive compound anticancer — Overview

scoparone can mitigate cardiac ischemia/reperfusion injury in <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> settings (<xref rid="B129" ref-type="bibr">129</xref>). Scoparone also has anti-atherogenic effects including the inhibition of vascular smooth muscle cell proliferation and migration (<xref rid="B130" ref-type="bibr">130</xref>, <xref rid="B131" ref-type="bibr">131</xref>), inhibition of platelet aggregation (<xref rid="B132" ref-type="bibr">132</xref>), and the attenuation of atherosclerotic plaque formation and dyslipidemia in hyperlipidemic diabetic rabbits (<xref rid="B133" ref-type="bibr">133</xref>, <xref rid="B134" ref-type="bibr">134</xref>). One study also found that scoparone decreased peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARγ) activity, expression of PPARγ target genes, and lipid accumulation in differentiating 3T3-L1 adipocytes (<xref rid="B135" ref-type="bibr">135</xref>). In cultured rat mesangial cells, high glucose-induced production of extracellular matrix proteins was reduced with scoparone treatment (<xref rid="B136" ref-type="bibr">136</xref>), a finding with potential implications for renal diabetic complications.</p><p>As is the case for SCOPA extracts and traditional preparations, hepatoprotective effects have been reported for scoparone in NASH (<xref rid="B137" ref-type="bibr">137</xref>, <xref rid="B138" ref-type="bibr">138</xref>) and in conditions of hepatotoxicity or liver injury induced by carbon tetrachloride or alcohol (<xref rid="B139" ref-type="bibr">139</xref>–<xref rid="B141" ref-type="bibr">141</xref>). Consistent with folk uses of SCOPA in the treatment of jaundice and cholestasis, scoparone also promotes bilirubin clearance through activation of the constitutive androstane receptor (CAR) (<xref rid="B142" ref-type="bibr">142</xref>). Interestingly, CAR activation has been shown to improve insulin sensitivity, glucose metabolism, and hepatic lipid accumulation in leptin-deficient <italic toggle="yes">ob/ob</italic> mice (<xref rid="B143" ref-type="bibr">143</xref>), and to prevent obesity, insulin resistance, and hepatic steatosis in HFD-fed mice (<xref rid="B144" ref-type="bibr">144</xref>). CAR agonism has been proposed as a therapeutic target for obesity, insulin resistance, and diabetes (<xref rid="B142" ref-type="bibr">142</xref>–<xref rid="B146" ref-type="bibr">146</xref>). In a recent study, a panel of natural and synthetic coumarin derivatives was screened for the ability to activate CAR, and scoparone was found to improve glucose tolerance in leptin receptor-null <italic toggle="yes">db/db</italic> mice (<xref rid="B147" ref-type="bibr">147</xref>).</p><p>Anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties of scoparone have been demonstrated in a wide range of conditions, and some of the pathways impacted by these effects have been described. Studies in the murine RAW 264.7 macrophage cell line demonstrated that scoparone could attenuate the LPS- or IFNγ-induced production of inflammatory cytokines, as well as iNOS and cyclooxygenase 2 (COX2) protein levels and corresponding NO and prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) release (<xref rid="B148" ref-type="bibr">148</xref>). Similar results were obtained in a human monocyte cell line, in which scoparone attenuated phorbol-12-myristate-13-acetate (PMA)-induced inflammatory cytokine production by inhibiting nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NF-κB) activation (<xref rid="B149" ref-type="bibr">149</xref>).</p><p>In a mouse model of acute lung injury, pulmonary edema, histological changes, and LPS-mediated inflammatory cytokine production were improved by scoparone <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic>, while <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> experiments in alveolar macrophages revealed that the compound’s anti-inflammatory effects were mediated through the toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4)/NFκB pathway (<xref rid="B150" ref-type="bibr">150</xref>). Anti-inflammatory effects of scoparone in a rat model of colitis have also been reported (<xref rid="B151" ref-type="bibr">151</xref>), while in BV2 microglial cells, scoparone attenuated LPS-induced neuroinflammatory responses by blocking interferon regulatory factor 3 (IRF3) and extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) activation (<xref rid="B126" ref-type="bibr">126</xref>). In a mouse model of acute seizures, scoparone preserved blood-brain barrier integrity, prevented inflammation and apoptosis, and inactivated the phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)/AKT signaling pathway <italic toggle="yes">in vivo.</italic> This same study determined that scoparone could also inhibit astrocyte activation elicited by LPS (<xref rid="B152" ref-type="bibr">152</xref>).</p><p>In addition to interfering with inflammatory pathways, scoparone has been shown to provide protection from oxidative stress, as demonstrated in 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) and lipid peroxidation assays <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> (<xref rid="B151" ref-type="bibr">151</xref>, <xref rid="B153" ref-type="bibr">153</xref>), and to inhibit production of ROS and preserve antioxidant enzyme activity in response to several types of oxidative stressors (<xref rid="B154" ref-type="bibr">154</xref>, <xref rid="B155" ref-type="bibr">155</xref>).
