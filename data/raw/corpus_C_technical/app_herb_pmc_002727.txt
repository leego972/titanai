In few cases, the plant materials are mixed with feed and/or potash to improve palatability.</p></sec><sec><title>Discussion</title><p>During this study, a total of 248 questionnaires were used to collect information from both semi-literate and non literate livestock farmers. The respondents comprised of individual livestock farmers having mainly backyard farms made up of heterogeneous species of animals and practicing the semi-intensive management system. Poultry farmers who keep local chickens manage them on free range system while the exotic breeds are reared under intensive system. From table <xref ref-type="table" rid="T1">1</xref>, <italic toggle="yes">Adansonia digitata </italic>and <italic toggle="yes">Khaya senegalensis </italic>were the most common plants reported to be used in the management of diarrhoea in livestock with frequencies of 49 (19.76%) and 43 (17.34%) respectively. <italic toggle="yes">Adansonia digitata </italic>is very common in the northern parts of Nigeria, and earlier works had published its application in diarrhoea, malaria and cough [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B28">28</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B29">29</xref>]. The anthelminthic effect of <italic toggle="yes">Khaya senegalensis </italic>(mahogany) has been reported [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B30">30</xref>], which may justify its use in diarrhoea management. Only <italic toggle="yes">Cucumis metuliferus </italic>and <italic toggle="yes">Solanum dasyphyllum </italic>were mentioned as herbal remedies used in diarrhoea management in poultry.
