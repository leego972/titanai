# PMC: adaptogen stress response HPA axis — Overview

ADAPT-232 significantly activates HSF1, a protein known to initiate the synthesis of Hsp72 (Wu, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B76">1995</xref>), However, ADAPT-232 is inactive in “silent” cells transfected by HSF1-siRNA which effectively inhibits baseline levels of HSF1 expression (Figure <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F3">3</xref>A).</p><p>Interferences RNA technology (RNAi) was also used to abrogate the expression of HSF1 (using HSF1-siRNA) in order to clarify what is upstream of NPY, HSF1, and Hsp72 activation. Figure <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F5">5</xref> demonstrated that ADAPT-232 and an active constituent salidroside induce the release of Hsp72 <italic toggle="yes">via</italic> a mechanism dependent on the upregulation of HSF1. Pre-treatment of human neuroglia cells HSF1-siRNA, which silences/knock down the expression of intracellular HSF1, before treatment with ADAPT-232 or salidroside, resulted in a significant suppression of Hsp72 and NPY release (Figure <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F5">5</xref>). We further demonstrated that pre-treatment of human neuroglia cells with NPY-siRNA, which silences/knock down the expression of intracellular NPY, before treatment with ADAPT-232 or salidroside resulted in a significant suppression of Hsp72 expression and release (Figures <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F3">3</xref>A, <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F3">3</xref>B and <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F5">5</xref>).In a separate set of experiments, we demonstrated that the treatment of human neuroglia cells with NPY peptide dose-dependently increases the expression and release of Hsp72 (Figure <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F6">6</xref>). These results are in agreement with recent findings that NPY stimulates Hsp72 in rat renal vascular smooth muscle (Zhong et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B78">2003</xref>).</p><fig id="F6" position="float" orientation="portrait"><label>Figure 6</label><caption><p><bold>Effect of NPY on Hsp72 release from human neuroglia cells</bold>. The general mechanism of activation and regulation of Hsp72 by HSF1 in the brain and aging is well elucidated (Asea and Brown, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B7">2008</xref>; Calderwood et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B14">2009</xref>). However, the mechanism activation of Hsp72 by NPY still remains uncertain. The expression of NPY receptors on glial cells has been demonstrated (Canto Soler et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B15">2002</xref>; Hashimoto et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B25">2011</xref>). Recently, the Y(1) receptor has been demonstrated to play an essential role in mediating NPY-induced NO and IL-1β production in microglia (Ferreira et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B23">2010</xref>). However, further studies are required to elucidate the exact mechanism of action and the signaling pathway utilized by NPY receptors to transducer it beneficial effects.</p><p>Taken together our studies suggest that the ADAPT-232 and its active constituent salidroside act on NPY expression <italic toggle="yes">via</italic> a mechanism dependent on the upregulation of HSF1 expression, which is upstream of Hsp72 expression, which is upstream of Hsp72 release (Figure <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F7">7</xref>). Our hypothetical model suggest that the release of Hsp72 take place <italic toggle="yes">via</italic> a mechanism dependent on the upregulation of NPY, which is upstream of Hsp72 and all other mediators of stress response involved in effects of ADAPT-232 (Figure <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F7">7</xref>).
