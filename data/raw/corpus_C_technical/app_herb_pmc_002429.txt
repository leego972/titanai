Pyrrole alkaloids are a class of naturally occurring compounds that contain a five-membered ring composed of four carbon atoms and one nitrogen atom found in various plants, fungi, and marine organisms where it serves as a chemical defense against predators and protection against environmental stresses (<xref rid="ref-60" ref-type="bibr">Singh &amp; Majik, 2019</xref>). <xref rid="ref-34" ref-type="bibr">Liu et al. (2021)</xref> recently identified and isolated three pairs of novel enantiomeric pyrrole alkaloids—(2′ R)-Caffeicpyrrole A and (2′ S)-Caffeicpyrrole A, (2′ R)-Caffeicpyrrole B, and (2′ S)-Caffeicpyrrole B, and (2′ R)-Caffeicpyrrole C and (2′ S)-Caffeicpyrrole C—from the leaves of <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> where it confirms its efficacy as an antifeedant and growth-inhibitory agent against <italic toggle="yes">Henosepilachna vigintioctomaculata,</italic> an invasive herbivore of <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic>.</p><fig position="float" id="fig-4" orientation="portrait"><object-id pub-id-type="doi">10.7717/peerj.17112/fig-4</object-id><label>Figure 4</label><caption><title>Chemical structure of important alkaloids isolated and identified from <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic>.</title><p>(A) Pyrrole alkloids. Also, methylprotodioscin, isolated from the aerial parts of <italic toggle="yes">S</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">rostratum</italic>, demonstrated its capability to induce cytotoxic effects on human cancer cells (<xref rid="ref-11" ref-type="bibr">Bah et al., 2004</xref>). In another study, solanine and chaconine were isolated and identified from various parts of <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum,</italic> demonstrating their potential in controlling pests, especially those with chewing and sucking mouthparts on a variety of vegetable crops (<xref rid="ref-76" ref-type="bibr">Zhang et al., 2012</xref>).</p></sec><sec><title>Flavonoids</title><p><italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic>, classified in the Androceras section series, is recognized for its abundance of flavonoids (<xref rid="ref-28" ref-type="bibr">Huang et al., 2017</xref>). Important flavonoids have been isolated and identified from <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> (<xref rid="fig-5" ref-type="fig">Fig. 5</xref>). Quercetin is one of the major flavonoids primarily sourced from the aerial part of <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> that plays a crucial role in neutralizing free radicals in the body, promotes seed germination and plant development in both stressful and non-stressful environments (<xref rid="ref-28" ref-type="bibr">Huang et al., 2017</xref>; <xref rid="ref-44" ref-type="bibr">Omar et al., 2018</xref>). As a plant-derived aglycone, quercetin has demonstrated superior disease-fighting abilities compared to curcumin, exhibiting higher reduction potential, a 3.5-fold increase in total antioxidant capacity, and a reduction in reactive oxygen species (ROS) and nitric oxide (NO) in LPS-stimulated human THP-1 acute monocytic leukemia cells (<xref rid="ref-77" ref-type="bibr">Zhang et al., 2011</xref>). Additionally, quercetin mitigates mycotoxin-induced endoplasmic reticulum stress and apoptosis, providing cellular protection (<xref rid="ref-71" ref-type="bibr">Yang et al., 2020</xref>). Another aglycone flavonoid, kaempferol, has been successfully isolated from <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> (<xref rid="ref-29" ref-type="bibr">Ibarra-Alvarado et al., 2010</xref>; <xref rid="ref-28" ref-type="bibr">Huang et al., 2017</xref>). Kaempferol demonstrates potential in treating obesity-related ailments by promoting lipolysis and suppressing adipogenesis in 3T3-L1 adipocytes, a mouse pre-adipocyte cell line (<xref rid="ref-40" ref-type="bibr">Muni Swamy et al., 2020</xref>). It has also been reported to inhibit the growth of pancreatic cancer cells through apoptosis induction (<xref rid="ref-74" ref-type="bibr">Zhang et al., 2008</xref>).
