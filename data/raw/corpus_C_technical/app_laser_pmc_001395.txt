Changes in certain vascular parameters, such as the vascular area, showed similarity to that found in other VoC models upon their stimulation with pro-inflammatory culture conditions.<sup><xref rid="cit23" ref-type="bibr">23,24</xref></sup> However, our model showed a faster vessel regression compared to others.<sup><xref rid="cit23" ref-type="bibr">23,24</xref></sup> This could be due to the relatively immature state of our vessels at the time of treatment. In other studies, extreme glucose levels were only introduced after establishing the vascular network.<sup><xref rid="cit23" ref-type="bibr">23,24</xref></sup> In our case, extreme glucose levels were added as our vessels were still in development. During this phase, the addition of extreme glucose concentrations probably led to an increase in oxidative stress during development, which eventually led to a faster regression of the vessels.<sup><xref rid="cit59" ref-type="bibr">59,60</xref></sup> In addition, we found that our iPSC-derived endothelial cells generally live shorter (up to 7 days) in fibrin hydrogel compared to well-established cell lines,<sup><xref rid="cit57" ref-type="bibr">57,61</xref></sup> such as human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) or human retinal microvascular endothelial cells (HRMVECs) that were cultured up to 51 and 28 days, respectively.<sup><xref rid="cit24" ref-type="bibr">24,62</xref></sup> As there are known differences between iPSC-derived ECs and ECs from other sources,<sup><xref rid="cit63" ref-type="bibr">63</xref></sup> possible further organ-specific differentiation could improve the maturation, function, morphology and lifetime of resulting vascular networks.<sup><xref rid="cit52" ref-type="bibr">52</xref></sup> Another method to extend vessel lifetime could be by adding continuous flow to the model. In a similar model, it was found that continuous flow extended lifetime of vessels and induced recovery of regressed vessels.<sup><xref rid="cit62" ref-type="bibr">62</xref></sup> Therefore, developing organ-specific mature cells and culturing them for an extended period of time under continuous flow could help in modeling chronic disease states. Only then is it possible to model diseases such as DR that develop slowly over time in mature vessels with plasma glucose levels between 7 mmol L<sup>−1</sup> and 11 mmol L<sup>−1</sup> in the choroid.<sup><xref rid="cit64" ref-type="bibr">64</xref></sup></p><p>Regarding the VEGF treatment, we observed that increased VEGF levels in culture medium could induce neovascularization in our VoC model, analogous to how increased VEGF concentrations contribute to CNV in patients with wet-AMD.<sup><xref rid="cit65" ref-type="bibr">65</xref></sup> Moreover, anti-VEGF therapy, such as Avastin® (bevacizumab), is a well known treatment to reduce disease progression in wet-AMD.<sup><xref rid="cit66" ref-type="bibr">66</xref></sup> Although typical <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> concentrations of VEGF in the serum of patients with late-stage AMD are approximately 260 pg mL<sup>−1</sup>, higher levels of VEGF are also found in other systemic diseases, such as diabetes mellitus.<sup><xref rid="cit65" ref-type="bibr">65</xref></sup> In this study, we used higher VEGF concentrations to accelerate vessel growth according to how this is typically performed in similar experimental culture systems.<sup><xref rid="cit22" ref-type="bibr">22,23</xref></sup></p><p>In addition to well-known vascular network measurements such as vessel length and branching, we introduced the ‘vascularity index’ as a new parameter for analyzing the networks in our VoC models. The vascularity index is a measure of the vascular density in the <italic toggle="yes">xz</italic>- and/or <italic toggle="yes">yz</italic>-plane and is defined by dividing the luminal volume by the hydrogel volume. Our definition of the vacularity index is derived from the choroidal vascularity index (CVI) which is used in clinical imaging by OCT and is defined as the fraction of luminal area with respect to the total choroidal area. Over the last decade, the CVI has been clinically identified as a potential biomarker for various retinal diseases.<sup><xref rid="cit10" ref-type="bibr">10–13,67,68</xref></sup> As current retina-on-chip systems capture structures of the retina and include read-outs relevant to tissue health and disease,<sup><xref rid="cit21" ref-type="bibr">21</xref></sup> measuring the CVI could be a valuable addition as a read-out that may enable side-by-side comparisons between patients and personalized retina-on-chip systems in the future.<sup><xref rid="cit69" ref-type="bibr">69</xref></sup></p><p>In our study, we demonstrated that OCT could reveal similar vessel structures as compared to confocal microscopy. However, there is a significant difference in transversal resolution (<italic toggle="yes">xy</italic>) and noise between both imaging modalities.<sup><xref rid="cit3" ref-type="bibr">3,25</xref></sup> The lower transversal resolution (<italic toggle="yes">xy</italic>) of OCT compared to fluorescence microscopy could be considered as a limitation of the correlation that we performed.
