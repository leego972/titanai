Bowd and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr55-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">55</xref></sup> estimated the measurement floor of macular GCIPL, pRNFL, and MRW and found that the latter two hit similar measurement floor, which is reached far before macular GCIPL. Gardiner and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr56-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">56</xref></sup> compared pRNFL and MRW in terms of signal-to-noise ratio, which indicates how well a method discriminates a true change from variability; they found that MRW measure has a lower signal-to-noise ratio, and, therefore, it may require a larger magnitude of change than pRNFL to identify significant progression. The conclusions of these theoretical studies were confirmed by Belghith and colleagues,<sup><xref rid="bibr49-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">49</xref></sup> who showed that both mGCIPL and pRNFL detect more progression than MRW in a cohort of patients with advanced glaucoma. Future studies need to clarify whether MRW can be useful to monitor early glaucoma.</p></sec><sec id="section4-2515841419899822"><title>Optical coherence tomography angiography: detection of early glaucoma</title><p>OCTA has provided insights into the relationship between neuronal and vascular changes in glaucomatous eyes. Initially, the attention was focused on the peripapillary VD in patients with established glaucoma, and capillary dropouts in these areas corresponding to pRNFL defects were thoroughly demonstrated.<sup><xref rid="bibr13-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">13</xref><xref rid="bibr14-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr15-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr16-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>,<xref rid="bibr57-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">57</xref></sup> The diagnostic ability of peripapillary VD has been shown to be lower or, at best, equal to pRNFL thickness, and it is still uncertain how this measure may improve the clinical care of glaucoma patients.<sup><xref rid="bibr16-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>,<xref rid="bibr57-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>,<xref rid="bibr58-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">58</xref></sup></p><p>More recently, OCTA-based studies have also investigated the macular region and found that glaucomatous eyes have a significantly lower superficial vascular complex (SVC) VD at the macula than healthy eyes. In contrast, no significant difference was found in VD of the intermediate and deep capillary plexuses at the macula.<sup><xref rid="bibr59-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">59</xref><xref rid="bibr60-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr61-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">61</xref></sup> The SVC has been found to supply blood to the nerve fiber layer and GCL. Thus, these studies suggested that vascular changes associated with glaucoma occurred preferentially among the vessels that feed the superficial layers of the retina. Similar findings have also been reported in pre-perimetric glaucoma.<sup><xref rid="bibr61-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>,<xref rid="bibr62-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">62</xref></sup></p><p>Takusagawa and colleagues,<sup><xref rid="bibr59-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">59</xref></sup> noticed that a macular area with low perfusion in the SVC corresponded in shape, size, and location to areas of detectable GCC thinning and VF defects. This indicates that there is an intimate correspondence between vascular and structural defect.</p><p>Whether macular OCTA parameters are useful in the early diagnosis of glaucoma patients is still under debate. Same studies demonstrated high diagnostic performance of macular OCTA parameters in detecting glaucoma, comparable to those typical of the pVD.<sup><xref rid="bibr59-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">59</xref><xref rid="bibr60-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr61-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">61</xref></sup> In a previous study, we compared diagnostic properties of structural OCT and OCTA at the peripapillary and macular regions, and we found that structural parameters (i.e. pRNFL and macular GCIPL) had the best diagnostic performance, followed by radial peripapillary capillaries VD, and, far beyond, macular SVC VD.<sup><xref rid="bibr16-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">16</xref></sup> In this study, high correlation was reported between peripapillary VD and pRNFL, but not between macular VD and mGCIPL in glaucomatous patients. Richter and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr63-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">63</xref></sup> also reported that peripapillary superficial retinal layer VD has higher diagnostic abilities compared to macular parameters. Wan and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr64-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">64</xref></sup> evaluated the diagnostic performance of macular OCT and OCTA in a large cohort of Chinese patients and, once again, found that macular OCTA performed considerably worse than structural measures.
