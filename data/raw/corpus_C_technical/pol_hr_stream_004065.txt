He recognized the suzerainty of the Abbasid Caliphate, but ruled as a de facto independent prince.

The Andalusis also occupied several of the Cyclades during these early years. Michael II's admiral Ooryphas managed to evict the Arabs from the Aegean islands, but failed to retake Crete. The overtures of Theophilos () to Abd ar-Rahman II of Córdoba, proposing a joint action against the Andalusi exiles, came to nothing, and in October 829, the Andalusis destroyed an imperial fleet off Thasos, undoing much of the work of Ooryphas and opening the Aegean and its coasts to pillage. Later the Andalusis attacked Euboea (), Lesbos (837), and the coasts of the Thracesian Theme, where they destroyed the monastic centre of Mount Latros.  They were heavily defeated, however, by the local strategos, Constantine Kontomytes.

After the death of Theophilos in 842, another expedition to recover Crete was launched under the personal leadership of the powerful logothetes and regent Theoktistos. Although it succeeded in occupying much of the island, Theoktistos had to abandon the army due to political intrigues in Constantinople, and the troops left behind were slaughtered by the Arabs.

From numismatic evidence, it appears that Abu Hafs died in  and was succeeded by his son, Shu'ayb.
