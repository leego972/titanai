ETHNOPHARMACOLOGICAL RELEVANCE: Multidrug-resistant organisms (MDROs) pose a 
severe global health threat, significantly complicating the treatment of 
bacterial infections. Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM), with its long history 
and rich clinical experience in treating infectious diseases, offers a unique 
and promising approach to enhance anti-infective efficacy and overcome 
resistance mechanisms when combined with antibiotics. The combination represents 
a novel strategy to combat MDROs.
AIM OF THE STUDY: This review summarizes the efficacy of TCM (including active 
ingredients, herbs and prescriptions) combined with antibiotics against MDROs 
based on in vitro and clinical evidence, and outlines their advantages, 
challenges and future directions.
MATERIALS AND METHODS: The review consulted the electronic databases PubMed and 
Chinese National Knowledge Infrastructure. keywords used for the search included 
"herb," "TCM," "Chinese herbal medicine," "antibiotics," "mechanism," "clinical 
studies," "in vitro experiments," and their combinations. Relevant literature 
was collected from 2002 to 2025.
RESULTS: This review demonstrates that TCM-antibiotic combinations exhibit 
significant synergistic antibacterial effects, markedly enhancing clinical 
outcomes and effectively reducing antibiotic resistance. The primary therapeutic 
effects include overcoming bacterial resistance, reduced treatment duration, and 
lower incidence of adverse effects. However, the future research should also pay 
more attention to the action mechanisms and the relationship between dosage and 
efficacy for the reliability of the applications.
CONCLUSION: TCM-antibiotic combinations have promising clinical application 
value for addressing MDROs infections. By integrating the multi-target actions 
of TCM with the rapid bactericidal effects of antibiotics, this approach offers 
a synergistic solution that enhances clinical outcomes and mitigates the 
emergence of resistance. Future research should focus on translating these 
findings into clinical practice through rigorous mechanistic studies and 
well-designed clinical trials, ultimately aiming to develop optimized 
therapeutic protocols for the effective management of MDROs infections.
