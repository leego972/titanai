Discussion</title><p>LLLT has emerged as a noninvasive modality to promote tissue regeneration, modulate inflammation, and accelerate chronic wound healing [<xref rid="B43-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>]. At the cellular level, LLLT enhances the mechanisms essential for tissue repair [<xref rid="B44-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>]. Several studies have confirmed the beneficial effects of LLLT on oral wound healing, even in pathological conditions such as DM [<xref rid="B45-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>]. Diabetes predisposes patients to oral mucosal erosion and ulceration, particularly at the interface with dental prostheses [<xref rid="B10-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>]. Delayed wound healing in individuals with DM not only causes discomfort and a higher risk of infection but also compromises prosthetic outcomes, often necessitating repeated treatment and the redesign of restorations.</p><p>In the present study, animals injected with alloxan developed persistent hyperglycemia (&gt;28.2 mmol/L) and significant body weight loss, consistent with previously validated models of chemically induced type 1 diabetes [<xref rid="B46-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>,<xref rid="B47-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>]. These systemic changes reflected a catabolic metabolic state that impaired tissue repair mechanisms [<xref rid="B48-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">48</xref>]. Our findings further demonstrate that diabetes significantly delays wound healing and disrupts systemic redox homeostasis, as evidenced by increased TOS and OSI along with decreased TAC and SOD activity (<xref rid="medicina-61-01651-t003" ref-type="table">Table 3</xref>). Importantly, the inclusion of non-ulcerated healthy (Group I) and diabetic (Group IV) controls enabled us to account for systemic metabolic effects, ensuring that the observed differences primarily reflect wound-related and treatment-specific responses. These results align with the well-established role of hyperglycemia in impeding normal healing [<xref rid="B49-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>,<xref rid="B50-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>]. Diabetic rats with mucosal ulcers showed severely delayed healing (by contrast, healthy rats achieved near-complete healing by day 10) and elevated oxidative stress markers, consistent with literature suggesting the effects of hyperglycemia [<xref rid="B51-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>].</p><p>The LLLT-treated diabetic animals exhibited significantly improved wound healing, with closure rates comparable to non-diabetic controls by day 10. These findings support previous studies that show that LLLT can promote mechanisms of wound healing even under metabolic stress [<xref rid="B52-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>,<xref rid="B53-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>].
