Mutans</italic> and the fluoride release.</td><td colspan="1" rowspan="1">El Sharkawy [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR79">79</xref>]</td></tr></tbody></table></table-wrap></p><p id="Par16"><italic toggle="yes">Salvadora persica</italic> (<italic toggle="yes">S. persica</italic>, Miswak), is an evergreen shrub, cultivated in India, Pakistan, Amman, and Egypt. Miswak extract possesses different antimicrobial and antifungal properties due to the presence of trimethylamine, chlorides, fluoride, saponins, flavonoids, and phenols. Kabil et al. evaluated the efficacy of adding chlorhexidine gluconate (CHX) or aqueous miswak (<italic toggle="yes">Salvadora persica</italic>) extract on the clinical performance and the antibacterial activity of conventional glass ionomer cement (GIC). CHX and miswak improved significantly the antibacterial properties without seriously affecting the clinical performance of the GIC up to 6 months of follow-up [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR65">65</xref>].</p><p id="Par17"><italic toggle="yes">Disocorea altissimo</italic> is from the genus <italic toggle="yes">Dioscorea</italic> that is indigenous to Brazil, Bolivia, Peru, Central America, and the Caribbean, <italic toggle="yes">Dioscorea</italic> species reported promising antioxidant, antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and thrombolytic activities. Mechanical properties and antimicrobial activity of a glass ionomer cement (GIC) incorporated with an antimicrobial agent from the aerial parts of <italic toggle="yes">Dioscorea altissimo</italic> have been investigated. The modified GIC was very active against <italic toggle="yes">Streptococcus mutans</italic> and had better mechanical properties than the original cement [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR66">66</xref>].</p><p id="Par18">More recently, <italic toggle="yes">Dioscorea altissimo</italic> (EA) has been further incorporated into GIC as a photosensitizer for Photodynamic therapy (PDT) against <italic toggle="yes">Streptococcus mutans</italic>. PDT has been specified as an adjunct technique to improve antimicrobial activity against dental caries causative microorganisms. EA was found to potentiate the antimicrobial action of GIC against <italic toggle="yes">S. mutans</italic> and therefore, results indicate that EA could be a potential photosensitizer for a PDT [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR67">67</xref>].</p><p id="Par19"><italic toggle="yes">Salvia officinalis</italic>, commonly known as sage, is a perennial evergreen plant native to the Mediterranean region. Sage extracts exhibit remarkable biological effects due to the presence of polyphenols, monoterpenes, diterpenes, triterpenes, and phenolic components [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR68">68</xref>]. The antimicrobial activity of GIC modified with <italic toggle="yes">Salvia officinalis</italic> extract powder (0.5%, 0.75%, 1%, and 1.25%) was examined. The study revealed a direct inhibitory activity of sage extract-modified GIC against <italic toggle="yes">S. mutans</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">L. casei</italic> in a dose-dependent manner. Therefore, the authors have claimed that the material can be used as a base, liner, and restorative material in the management of carious teeth [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR69">69</xref>].</p><p id="Par20">In 2020, a new drug delivery system based on a glass-ionomer-Brazilian pepper (<italic toggle="yes">Schinus terebinthifolius Raddi</italic>) extract combination was assessed to check the activity against pathogenic microorganisms in the oral cavity. The modified material revealed a profound antibacterial activity at non-cytotoxic concentrations for the human fibroblast MRC-5 cells. The results suggested that the modified GIC represents a new low-cost therapeutic option for use in dental treatment, especially in developing countries [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR70">70</xref>]. In the same year, the antimicrobial properties and compressive strength of GICs modified with a mixture of plant extracts (<italic toggle="yes">Salvadora persica, ficus carcia, and Olea europaea</italic>) at three different concentrations were evaluated. The extracts mixture boosted the antimicrobial activity against <italic toggle="yes">S. mutans</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">M. luteus</italic> while the compressive strength was improved by the addition of the plant extracts mixture at high concentrations [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR71">71</xref>].</p><p id="Par21">Proanthocyanidins (PA) are polyphenols that are found in many plants, such as cranberry, blueberry, and grape seeds. PA is a natural antioxidant, free-radical scavenger and it induces the formation of exogenous collagen crosslinks [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR72">72</xref>]. Proanthocyanidin from grape seed extract (Pa-rich GSE) has been shown to enhance the mechanical properties of demineralized dentin.
