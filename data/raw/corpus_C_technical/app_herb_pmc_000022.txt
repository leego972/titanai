The authors carried out five-day experimental crossover phases with volunteers wearing palatal devices containing human enamel and dentin blocks. The <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> gel was applied in a thin layer in the experimental group and was not applied in the control group. The <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> gel caused less wear in enamel in the experimental group than in the control group and a statistically significant value was found for erosion and erosion and abrasion in dentin. Therefore, <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> may play a role in the prevention of dentin loss under mild erosive and abrasive conditions [<xref rid="B78-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">78</xref>]. Hepatoprotective Effects</title><p>Lall et al. [<xref rid="B67-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>] evaluated in vitro hepatoprotective activities of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> ethanolic shoot extracts on human HepG2 cells. The hepatoprotective activities of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> extracts were tested in vivo using a rat model of isoniazid- and rifampicin-induced hepatotoxicity. <italic toggle="yes">Euclea natalensis</italic> showed a hepatoprotective effect (50% at 12.5 µg/mL) and the ability to increase T-helper 1 cell cytokines Interleukin 12, Interleukin 2 and Interferon α by up to 12-fold and the ability to decrease the T-helper 2 cell cytokine Interleukin 10 fourfold when compared to baseline cytokine production [<xref rid="B67-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>]. These findings provide a further scientific basis to the invention relating to the ethanolic extracts from the shoots of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> exhibiting immune stimulatory activity and hepatoprotective activity in vitro and in vivo studies [<xref rid="B79-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">79</xref>]. The ethanolic extract of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> provides an immune stimulatory effect on peripheral blood mononuclear cells, selecting a Th1 immune response over a Th2 immune response. Research by [<xref rid="B79-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">79</xref>] showed that ethanolic shoot extract of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> showed significant in vitro hepatoprotective activities against <sc>d</sc>-galactosamine and in vivo studies, the extract was nontoxic, acting as hepatoprotectant against the toxic effect of some of the first line drugs.</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot8-molecules-22-02128"><title>5.8.
