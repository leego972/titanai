This finding represents a notable expansion of the known metabolomic diversity of the species and underscores the importance of including lipophilic fractions in phytochemical screening.</p><p>Tocopherols, particularly α-tocopherol, are well-established lipid-soluble antioxidants in both plant and animal systems [<xref rid="B138-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">138</xref>]. Their principal function has traditionally been attributed to the scavenging of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and the protection of membrane lipids from peroxidation. However, emerging evidence indicates that α-tocopherol also exerts biological functions independent of its antioxidant properties. These include modulation of gene expression, regulation of intracellular signaling cascades, and involvement in lipid metabolism [<xref rid="B138-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">138</xref>,<xref rid="B139-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">139</xref>]. From a phytopharmacological perspective, such pleiotropic effects may enhance the adaptogenic potential of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> and partially explain its traditional use in managing oxidative stress and inflammation.</p><p>The detection of tocopherols in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> may also reflect their physiological role in stabilizing membrane structures and influencing lipid raft composition (<xref rid="molecules-30-03460-t005" ref-type="table">Table 5</xref>). Tocopherols are known to interact with phospholipids and sterols in the lipid bilayer, potentially affecting membrane fluidity and signal transduction under stress conditions [<xref rid="B138-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">138</xref>]. This membrane-level modulation may act synergistically with sterols previously identified in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, suggesting coordinated functions of lipophilic compounds in the plant’s response to abiotic stress. Furthermore, metabolites of tocopherols, including carboxychromanols and CEHCs (2-(β-carboxyethyl)-6-hydroxychromans), have demonstrated a range of bioactivities, such as anti-inflammatory, antiproliferative, and cytoprotective effects in mammalian systems [<xref rid="B139-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">139</xref>]. Although these metabolites have not yet been reported in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, their biosynthetic plausibility invites targeted metabolomic studies.</p><p>In our opinion, the functional role of tocopherols in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> warrants further investigation not only due to their pharmacological relevance, but also their potential contribution to stress resilience and chemotaxonomic distinction within Araliaceae.</p><p>This first report of tocopherols in the <italic toggle="yes">Polyscias</italic> genus emphasizes the importance of detailed phytochemical investigation and invites further study into their functional roles in plant physiology and therapeutic potential. Future research should focus on quantification, localization, and possible synergistic interactions of tocopherols with other lipophilic constituents in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, such as sesquiterpenes, diterpenes, and triterpenoids.
