Considering that <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> extract increases the serum testosterone concentrations, there might be a potential risk from its treatment in elderly men, which might cause prostatic problems. On other hands, a randomized double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial by Ismail <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, revealed no difference between the placebo and the verum group for serum prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels [<xref rid="B114-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>]. Li <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> showed that neither mutagenicity nor clastogenicity was noted, and the acute oral LD<sub>50</sub> was more than 6 g/kg b.w for <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> extract. After 4-week subacute and 13-week subchronic exposure paradigms (0, 0.6, 1.2, and 2 g/kg b.w. per day), adverse effects attributable to test compound was not observed with respect to body weight, hematology, serum biochemistry, urinalysis, macropathology, or histopathology. However, the treatment significantly reduced prothrombin time, partial thromboplastin time, blood urea nitrogen, creatinine, aspartate aminotransferase, creatine phosphate kinase, lactate dehydrogenase, and cholesterol levels, especially in males (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.05). Calculated acceptable daily intake (ADI) for <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> extract, was up to 1.2 g/adult/day. The investigated intension of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> extract intake by Li <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> was to calculate its safety profile in health supplements. This information is useful for product development and safety management [<xref rid="B198-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">198</xref>].</p><p>From Hamoud and Qamar’s findings, it is strongly suggested that <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> Jack has no evidence of side effects or any deleterious effect on the pancreatic tissues when used orally in small quantities for more than a month. Regular <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> use at low doses does not appear to cause any toxic effect on the pancreas and could be considered a safe herbal supplement as far as the safety of the pancreas in human beings is concerned [<xref rid="B199-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">199</xref>].</p><p>No toxic symptoms were observed in TAF273-treated pregnant female rats, and their pregnancies were normal with no fetus abnormalities. After administration of a 100 mg/kg daily dose of TAF273, which is almost &gt;10-fold lower than the LD<sub>50</sub> value, no adverse effect was observed in reproductive toxicity and teratology studies in rats. The authors concluded that any human dose derived from converting the rat doses of 100 mg/kg/day or below, may be considered safe for further clinical studies [<xref rid="B200-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">200</xref>].</p><p>Chen <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, investigated the effects of standardized water extract of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> the Physta<sup>®</sup> at dose of 400 mg/day for 6 weeks, showed no significant changes in both the liver and renal functions tests, so supplementation of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> at this dosage and duration was non-toxic to the liver and renal functions [<xref rid="B117-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">117</xref>].</p><p>The Food and Drug Administration has suggested that the extrapolation of animal doses to human doses is correctly performed only through normalization to BSA, which often is represented in mg/m<sup>2</sup>. The human dose equivalent can be more appropriately calculated by using the formula as HED (mg/kg) = Animal dose (mg/kg) [Animal <italic toggle="yes">Km</italic>/Human <italic toggle="yes">Km</italic>] [<xref rid="B201-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">201</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> is considered safe as long as it is not taken in a high dose. Based on the results of previous toxicity studies, <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> is normally recommended to be administered to men at the dose of 200–400 mg daily and should be used with caution, especially in the elderly. Currently, <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> is commercially sold worldwide following this established dosage in the form of tablets for easier daily consumption [<xref rid="B195-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">195</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec7dot2-molecules-21-00331"><title>7.2. Precautions/Contraindications</title><p>Based on studies in animals suggesting that <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> reduced blood glucose in hyperglycemic animals [<xref rid="B168-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">168</xref>] and unpublished studies in humans suggesting the possibility for increased blood glucose, it should be used cautiously in patients using hypoglycemic agents.
