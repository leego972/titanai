This has shown that the dynamic range of the SB implementation was larger than that of TD version by a factor given by the ‘number of saturation charges of the linear image sensor’ used in the spectrometer, i.e with the ‘number of spectral windows’.</p></sec></sec><sec><title>Disadvantages of SD-OCT in comparison with TD-OCT</title><sec><title>Sensitivity versus depth</title><p>When employing TD principles, the same sensitivity is achievable for any OPD value, i.e. for any axial position of the scattering point along the A-scan, as shown at the bottom of <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig02">Figure 2</xref>. In opposition, there is a decay of sensitivity of the SD methods with the OPD, as shown at the bottom of <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig04">Figures 4</xref> and <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig05">5</xref>. Both SB and SS methods require resolution in separating the channelled spectrum peaks and troughs, which translates into the need of large number of grating lines, <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>, and large number of pixel cameras, <italic toggle="yes">M</italic>, when using the SB-OCT method and of a narrow linewidth, δλ, for the SS used in the SS-OCT method. In fact, fundamental in limiting the axial range in both types of SD methods is the spatial extension of the interfering wavestrains, CL. This can be assimilated to an equivalent coherence length, hence the notation. In the SB, CL is given by the extension of the object and reference wavetrains after suffering dispersion (or diffraction) in the spectrometer. It should be noted that CL is much larger than the coherence length cl introduced above. In the SS-OCT case, the spatial extension, CL, of the wavetrains emitted by the swept source, is inverse proportional to the linewidth, δλ, of the instantaneas spectrum emitted.</p><p><xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig06">Figure 7</xref> illustrates the spatial relativity of dimensions. The <italic toggle="yes">Object</italic> to be investigated is made of layers separated by δ<italic toggle="yes">z</italic> and the OPD axial extension of the object is 2Δ<italic toggle="yes">z</italic> (considering for simplicity the index of refraction as 1). In TD-OCT, the coherence length, cl, of the optical source needs to be smaller than δ<italic toggle="yes">z</italic> to be able to separate the layers in depth in the object, as shown in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig03">Fig. 3</xref>. Because TD-OCT operates around OPD = 0, by moving the <italic toggle="yes">Reference Mirror</italic> in the interferometer in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig02">Figure 2</xref>, maximum of sensitivity is moved from one layer in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig03">Figure 3</xref> to the next. Therefore, all layers can be interrogated by the same sensitivity. This is not the case in SD-OCT.</p><p>To ensure interference of the local reference wave with both the waves coming from the top of the object as well as with the waves returned from the deepest layer, their spatial extension, CL, needs to exceed the OPD axial range 2Δ<italic toggle="yes">z</italic>, CL &gt; 2Δ<italic toggle="yes">z</italic> as shown in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig07">Figure 7</xref>. Let us say that the <italic toggle="yes">Reference mirror</italic> is adjusted for OPD = 0 to match the top of the <italic toggle="yes">Object</italic>. As illustrated in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig07">Figure 7</xref>, total overlap of interfering wavetrains, object and reference, happens at OPD = 0 only, whereas for the maximum depth, tails only of object and reference wavetrains are superposed. The sensitivity depends on the amount of overlap of the two interfering wavetrains, object and reference, therefore, the sensitivity in SD-OCT decays with depth. However, because the wavetrains are longer than the axial range, 2Δ<italic toggle="yes">z</italic>, they will all interfere to some extent with the reference wavetrain. In this way, all OPD values within the axial range 2Δ<italic toggle="yes">z</italic> are interrogated at once. At any particular time, for any given optical frequency of the SS, wavetrains from all depths are returned back to the photodetector.</p><fig id="fig07" position="float" orientation="portrait"><label>Fig. 7</label><caption><p>SD-OCT: Relative size of the CL extension of interfering wavetrains, required depth resolution δ<italic toggle="yes">z</italic>, and axial range Δ<italic toggle="yes">z</italic> determined by the axial extension of the <italic toggle="yes">Object</italic> to be investigated.</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="jmi0247-0209-f7.jpg"/></fig><p>The wavetrain from the bottom of the object is shown with more cycles than the wavetrain from the top of the object, as the picture in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig07">Figure 7</xref> represents a frozen aspect in time of returning wavetrains from the top and bottom of the object.
