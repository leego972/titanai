Specifically, these components have to be C-coated for the 1050–1620 nm wavelength interval (as presented in this article for our 1050 nm and our 1310 nm OCT systems) and B-coated for the 650–1050 nm wavelength interval (as necessary for an OCT system centered at 780 nm).</p></list-item><list-item><p>The designs, as well as the manufacturing methods for the parts that are not off-the-shelf, were chosen in order to offer the readers the possibility to built easily their own OCT handheld probe: (a) almost entirely from off-the-shelf components (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig1-0954411914543963">Figures 1</xref> and <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig2-0954411914543963">2</xref>), with only one adaptor, that is, the GS mount shown in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig4-0954411914543963">Figure 4(d)</xref>; (b) in a more ergonomic configuration, but with more parts to be manufactured (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig3-0954411914543963">Figure 3</xref>). This aim of our work was the reason for not choosing manufacturing methods that could be more easily applied, such as rapid prototyping.<sup><xref rid="bibr27-0954411914543963" ref-type="bibr">27</xref></sup>
</p></list-item><list-item><p>Testing of the probes was performed in this study on dental prosthesis with a mobile SD-OCT setup developed in our labs. The defects in the material were clearly identified, as well as the type of material used in the dental construction. We have done another, previous demonstration of the feasibility and ergonomy of one of the probes (i.e. the one shown in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig2-0954411914543963">Figure 2</xref>) for OCT investigations in ear, nose, and throat,<sup><xref rid="bibr30-0954411914543963" ref-type="bibr">30</xref></sup> as mentioned before. Future work will continue towards in vivo investigations in the oral cavity, both on hard tissue (teeth) and soft tissue (gums).</p></list-item></list><p>This research will also continue in our groups with other applications and with other handheld scanning probes with different complexities, appropriate for various biomedical imaging techniques. Of particular importance is the development of handheld probes that correlate OCT with confocal microscopy,<sup><xref rid="bibr36-0954411914543963" ref-type="bibr">36</xref></sup> or with laser scanning ophtalmoscopy.<sup><xref rid="bibr38-0954411914543963" ref-type="bibr">38</xref></sup> The scope is to take advantage of the conjugated performance of two techniques at once, especially in <italic toggle="yes">en face</italic> imaging.
