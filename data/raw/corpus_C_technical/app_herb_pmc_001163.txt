Agri-Food By-Products as Rich Sources of Bioactive Compounds for Biopesticide Applications</title><p>The scarcity of natural resources coupled with environmental pollution has raised global awareness about the need to address, or at least mitigate, this issue due to its negative consequences. The agri-food industry is a major contributor, generating large amounts of organic waste and consuming scarce natural resources such as freshwater. It is crucial to develop new technologies for the reuse of these waste materials.</p><sec id="sec5dot1-molecules-31-00996"><title>5.1. Olive-Oil By-Products</title><p>Olive oil is a widely consumed commodity globally, owing to its numerous health benefits and its critical role as an ingredient in other industries such as cosmetics. The olive tree belongs to the Oleaceae family, with <italic toggle="yes">Olea europaea</italic> L. being the only species capable of producing fruits that are suitable for olive oil production [<xref rid="B65-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>]. According to data released by the International Olive Council (IOC), the EU accounts for approximately 70% of the world’s olive oil production, with a market value of US$13.496 billion worldwide in 2024/25, with Spain being the largest producer [<xref rid="B66-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>]. It is noteworthy that this market has tripled in the last 60 years, reaching a production of 3.6 million tons in 2024/25 [<xref rid="B67-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>].</p><p>Olive oil production begins with the maintenance and care of olive trees, including pruning, weeding, pesticide application, and others [<xref rid="B65-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>,<xref rid="B68-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>]. Concurrently with harvesting, olives undergo a pre-cleaning process, generating branches (both thin and thick), leaves, and wood, which remain in the field. Subsequently, the olives are briefly stored and transported to mills. At the mills, olives are washed again to remove impurities and foreign materials, such as leaves, stones, damaged olives, and soil, among others [<xref rid="B65-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>]. Then, the olives undergo milling to break the stones, peel cells, and pulp (releasing vacuoles containing oil droplets), producing a homogeneous paste with stone fragments [<xref rid="B65-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>]. This paste is then transported to the malaxation phase to undergo physical and biochemical phenomena, correlated with the product’s quality and nutritional properties [<xref rid="B65-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>,<xref rid="B69-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>]. The paste proceeds to the separation phase, which can occur through three different processes: pressing and centrifugation in two or three phases [<xref rid="B65-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>,<xref rid="B69-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>]. Finally, various olive oil grades are obtained, ranging from extra virgin to lampante, generating olive mill wastewater, olive stones, and pomace.</p><p>The reviewed studies indicate that the olive oil extraction process generates large quantities of waste, with 80% of the olive weight turning into residues [<xref rid="B70-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>,<xref rid="B71-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>]. The major compounds in olive oil are fatty acids, followed by lignans and triterpenoids, whereas olive oil by-products contain high concentrations of phenolics, flavonoids, and secoiridoids, as shown in <xref rid="molecules-31-00996-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>, along with low concentrations of tocopherols, fatty acids, and phytosterols, among others.</p><p>Regarding leaves and branches, it is estimated that pruning/harvesting annually generates a significant amount of olive by-products, about 25 kg per tree or between 1.5- and 3-tons ha<sup>−1</sup>, requiring further research to improve this value [<xref rid="B69-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>,<xref rid="B72-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>].
