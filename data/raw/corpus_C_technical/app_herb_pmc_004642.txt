Overview of Stress-Protective and Anti-Fatigue Effects of Adaptogens</title><sec id="sec4dot1-pharmaceuticals-18-00261"><title>4.1. Adaptogens and Adaptive Stress Response</title><p>The term adaptogen is primarily associated with the notion of adaptability introduced by George Canguilhem in 1943 [<xref rid="B185-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">185</xref>], who defined adaptability as the ability of an organism to alter itself or its responses to the changed environment or circumstances and assumed that adaptability shows the ability to learn and improve from experience—repeated mild exposure or low doses of stress result in the increased resistance of cells and organisms to subsequent stress exposure, resulting in an adaptation that favors survival. The adaptation phenomenon to repetitive low-level stress was first described by Hans Selye in 1936 using rats exposed to low temperatures, low oxygen tension, muscular exercise, adrenaline, and morphine. Several nonspecific reactions were evoked (thymus atrophy, adrenal hyperplasia, stomach ulceration, increased secretion of cortisol and catecholamines, etc.), which Selye termed the general adaptation syndrome [<xref rid="B85-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">85</xref>]. In a broad aspect, adaptation is an active process of responding to challenges, and adaptedness resulting from this process means achieving a positive outcome, i.e., survival and reproduction, in the face of adversity. In this context, the term adaptogen refers to a time-dependent physiological process of adaptation of the organism in response to repeated administration of a botanical that triggers intracellular and extracellular adaptive signaling pathways and adaptive stress response (<xref rid="pharmaceuticals-18-00261-f004" ref-type="fig">Figure 4</xref>) [<xref rid="B31-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>,<xref rid="B32-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>,<xref rid="B186-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">186</xref>,<xref rid="B187-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">187</xref>].</p><p>In 1957, Lazarev introduced the term “adaptogen” for some chemicals, increasing the so-called “state of nonspecific resistance” of the organism [<xref rid="B25-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>,<xref rid="B87-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">87</xref>,<xref rid="B188-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">188</xref>] and implementing “Ginseng-like” [<xref rid="B189-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">189</xref>] tonic, stress-protective, harmless botanicals normalizing body function [<xref rid="B190-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">190</xref>], increasing the adaptability to damaging environmental factors [<xref rid="B25-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>,<xref rid="B68-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>]. The concept of adaptogens has been thoroughly reviewed regarding physiology, pharmacology, toxicology, and potential medical uses [<xref rid="B25-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>,<xref rid="B26-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>,<xref rid="B31-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>,<xref rid="B33-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>,<xref rid="B34-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>,<xref rid="B66-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>,<xref rid="B67-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>,<xref rid="B87-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">87</xref>,<xref rid="B88-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">88</xref>,<xref rid="B89-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">89</xref>,<xref rid="B186-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">186</xref>,<xref rid="B190-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">190</xref>,<xref rid="B191-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">191</xref>,<xref rid="B192-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">192</xref>,<xref rid="B193-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">193</xref>]. The definitions of adaptogens were upgraded and updated depending on the progress in neuroscience, molecular biology, pharmacology, and the knowledge in phytotherapy research [<xref rid="B25-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>].
