Title: Disruptive Innovation in an Institutionalized Environment: Space-based ADS-B in the Air Traffic Management Industry

Abstract:
Air Traffic Management (ATM) is a highly institutionalised industrythat often prevents its service providers, Air Navigation Service Providers (ANSPs), from embracing innovations at a pace that would be expected from their high-technology operational environment.One of the inducing factors that can facilitate the institutional change are disruptive technologies, such as the space-based surveillance. Yet, several institutional pressures currently act as barriers to their mainstream introduction into the ATM market.ANSPs are often forced to conform to coercive and mimetic pressures stemming from the global regulations and leading players on the market, which results in ANSPs being mostly public entities and often delivering the ATM service inefficiently.This article argues that space-based surveillance can act as a disruptive innovation, but besides the technology itself, other inducing factors for institutional change may be required to transform the industry, namely: • Coercive institutional pressures that are currently applied by regulators should be eased up to enable more flexible regulatory framework defining technical protocols for the provision of the ATM service rather than static standards. Also, a change in the incentivisation ofANSPs needs to be introduced to facilitate more innovative ATM provision. • Regulators and policy makers should promote full commercialisation of ANSPs, hence enabling established members to become ‘institutional entrepreneurs’ and embark on new practices. • Competition among ANSPs should be fostered to create market dynamics, enabling exits of incumbent organisations and entries of newcomers. Thus, where feasible, the competition in or at least for the market should be introduced.
