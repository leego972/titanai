# PMC: adaptogen stress response HPA axis — Overview

activity of the ginseng saponins is believed to be mediated through the blocking of ACTH action in the adrenal gland (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b17-dti-2-2007-039">Kim et al. 2003a</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b18-dti-2-2007-039">b</xref>) and by inducing NO production in the brain (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b16-dti-2-2007-039">Kim et al. 1998</xref>). Administration of a single dose of <italic toggle="yes">P. ginseng</italic> increased working capacity in rats by up to 132% (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b10-dti-2-2007-039">Filaretov et al. 1988</xref>): interestingly, although the effect of repeated administration over a 7 day period was more pronounced (179%), it was not accompanied by further changes in blood cortisone level. Additionally, ginseng saponins have been shown to affect brain monoamine levels in heat-stressed mice causing a reduction in the stress-induced increase of noradrenalin and serotonin. Similar effects on the HPA axis under both normal and stress conditions were reported in experiments involving another tetracyclic triterpene glycoside, namely, cucurbitacin R diglucosides an active principle of <italic toggle="yes">B. alba</italic>, giving rise to a stress-protective and stimulating effect in animals (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b34-dti-2-2007-039">Panossian et al. 1997</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b36-dti-2-2007-039">1999b</xref>).</p><p>The action of a steroid hormone is mainly attributed to its binding with a receptor, which results in the activation of the nuclear genome and subsequent alterations in protein synthesis. However, such a mechanism cannot account for the rapid effects of some adaptogens, particularly the single dose effects of <italic toggle="yes">S. chinensis</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">R. rosea</italic> (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b31-dti-2-2007-039">Panossian and Wagner, 2005</xref>). Moreover it has been shown that these adaptogens are able to enhance resistance in simple organisms, such as developing snail (<italic toggle="yes">Lymnaea stagnalis</italic>) embryos, silk worm (<italic toggle="yes">Bombix mori</italic>) larvae, round worms (<italic toggle="yes">Caenorhabditis elegans</italic>), and isolated cells (Reuber H35 hepatoma and isolated cardiac cells), against various conditions including cold-induced viral infection, <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus thuringiensis</italic> infection<italic toggle="yes">,</italic> stress induced by menadione, formalin, heat, and toxic metal ions (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b6-dti-2-2007-039">Chernykh et al. 1985</xref>; <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b2-dti-2-2007-039">Boon-Niermeijer et al. 2000</xref>).</p><p>Such results clearly cannot be explained in terms of regulation of endocrine, immune or CNS systems, but rather confirm that adaptogens are universal enhancers of non-specific resistance of living organisms at various levels of organization, and that they can adapt cells and organisms to stress by mechanisms of regulation of intracellular communications. Whilst the effects of adaptogens on the HPA axis (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b9-dti-2-2007-039">Filaretov et al. 1986</xref>; <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b20-dti-2-2007-039">Kimura and Sumiyoshi, 2004</xref>), on NO (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b38-dti-2-2007-039">Park et al. 1996</xref>; <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b37-dti-2-2007-039">Panossian et al. 1999c</xref>) and eicosanoids (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b33-dti-2-2007-039">Panossian et al. 1988</xref>; <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b29-dti-2-2007-039">Okhura et al. 1990</xref>; <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b40-dti-2-2007-039">Park et al. 2005</xref>) are well documented, nothing is known about the involvement of SAPK/JPK, and its phosphorylated form, in the mechanism of action of plant adaptogens.</p><p>The results of the present study show that immobilization stress increases the levels of p-JNK in the blood cells of rabbits significantly (P &lt; 0.05) up to 200–300% compared with the initial basal level. Previously, a single 15 min session of forced swimming was found to increase p-JNK (p-JNK1 and/or p-JNK2/3) levels in all regions of the brain (hippocampus, neocortex, prefrontal cortex, amygdala and striatum) by <italic toggle="yes">ca</italic> 2–5-fold (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b43-dti-2-2007-039">Shen et al. 2004</xref>). Moreover, acute sessions of bicycle ergometry have been reported to produce increases in signaling intermediates from the SAPK/JNK pathways (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b50-dti-2-2007-039">Williamson et al. 2003</xref>). Interestingly, it was found that older men exhibited significantly (P &lt; 0.05) higher resting levels of p-SAPK/p-JNK compared with younger men, but lower comparative levels after a session of the resistance exercise.</p><p>In the present study, the blood levels of p-SAPK/p-JNK in rats that had been treated with multiple doses of the adaptogens <italic toggle="yes">R. rosea</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">S. chinensis</italic> and rhodioloside remained practically unchanged after the period of acute stress.
