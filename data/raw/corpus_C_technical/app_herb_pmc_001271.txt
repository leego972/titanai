Examples of Approved Commercial Phytochemical Drugs</title><p>Several plant-derived drugs on the market have been developed to treat various diseases (<xref rid="ijms-25-08792-f001" ref-type="fig">Figure 1</xref>), e.g., apomorphine (<bold>1</bold>) is made semi-synthetically from morphine isolated from <italic toggle="yes">Papaver somniferum</italic> L. While initially investigated as a potential non-addictive morphine replacement, the pharmacology of <bold>1</bold> is distinct, acting as a dopamine receptor agonist and it is now approved for the treatment of Parkinson’s disease [<xref rid="B19-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>]. Arteether (<bold>2</bold>) is a semisynthetic drug derived from artemisinin from <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia annua</italic> and is used to treat malaria. Arteether is oil soluble, has a long elimination half-life, and is more stable than artemisinin [<xref rid="B20-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>]. Galantamine (<bold>3</bold>) is an Amaryllidaceae alkaloid from <italic toggle="yes">Galanthus woronowii</italic> and an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor used in Alzheimer’s treatments [<xref rid="B18-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>]. Tiotropium is a muscarinic receptor antagonist from <italic toggle="yes">Atropa belladonna</italic> that has been used to treat asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) [<xref rid="B21-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>]. Other examples include anthocran, cysticlean, and monoselect macropcarpon from <italic toggle="yes">Vaccinium</italic> spp., which are being used to treat urinary tract infections. GutGuard is a standard product that was derived from <italic toggle="yes">Glycyrrhiza glabra</italic> extract, and Parodontax is a product that was developed from <italic toggle="yes">Commiphora myrrha</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Echinacea purpurea</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Krameria triandra</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Matricaria recutita</italic> extracts. <italic toggle="yes">Mentha arvensis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">M. piperita</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Salvia officinalis</italic> essential oils have all been used to treat oral infections [<xref rid="B22-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>].</p><p>Even in recent times, natural products play a role in drug development, with 6 of 53 new products approved by the FDA in 2023 having been inspired from natural products (<xref rid="ijms-25-08792-f002" ref-type="fig">Figure 2</xref>) [<xref rid="B23-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>]. Examples of small molecules approved include bexagliflozin (<bold>4</bold>) (Brenzavvy<sup>TM</sup>) and sotalgliflozin (<bold>5</bold>) (Inpefa<sup>TM</sup>), sodium-glucose co-transporter inhibitors that are synthetic analogues of the natural product phlorizin isolated initially from apple trees. Brenzavvy<sup>TM</sup> has been authorized for glycemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes, and Inpefa<sup>TM</sup> has been approved as a cardiovascular therapeutic. The synthetic steroids zuranolone (<bold>6</bold>) (Zurzuvae<sup>TM</sup>) and vamorolone (<bold>7</bold>) (Agamree<sup>TM</sup>) were respectively approved for the treatment of postpartum depression and Duchenne muscular dystrophy.</p><p>Filsuvez<sup>TM</sup> is an extract of birch terpenoids that was approved in 2023. The topical gel consists of pentacyclic triterpenes (<xref rid="ijms-25-08792-f003" ref-type="fig">Figure 3</xref>), namely, betulin (<bold>8</bold>) (72–88%), lupeol (<bold>9</bold>) (2.4–5.7%), betulinic acid (<bold>10</bold>) (2.6–4.2%), erythrodiol (<bold>11</bold>) (0.5–1.2%), and oleanolic acid (<bold>12</bold>) (0.3–0.8%). The gel treats partial-thickness wounds with Junctional and Dystrophic Epidermolysis Bullosa (JEB and DEB). This is the first treatment approved for wounds associated with the rare disease JEB [<xref rid="B23-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>].</p><p>Natural products (NPs) or their derivatives contribute a substantial proportion of drugs that successfully progress through clinical trials to approval.
