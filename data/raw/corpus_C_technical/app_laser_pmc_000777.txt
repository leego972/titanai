Critical studies in applying ethosomes as ALA tDDS have also been developed [<xref rid="B55-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>,<xref rid="B56-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>]. As a lipid vehicle, ethosomes contain higher concentrations of ethanol and lipids. The presence of ethanol in the formulation allows drug solubilization (<xref rid="pharmaceuticals-14-00772-t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref>). It creates deformable lipid structures that can more easily promote a more profound skin permeation of both hydrophilic and lipophilic drugs (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="pharmaceuticals-14-00772-f003">Figure 3</xref>C). Thus, they have superior drug skin retention and permeation enhancement as well as improved pharmaceutical properties, including stability at room temperature, high entrapment efficiency, and greater compatibility with the SC [<xref rid="B57-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>]. In vivo experiments by Fang et al. indicated that the penetration ability of ethosomes was superior to that of liposomes [<xref rid="B58-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>]. The enhancements of all the formulations ranged from 11- to 15-fold compared to that of the control (ALA in an aqueous solution) in PpIX intensity. Colorimetry detected no erythema in the irradiated skin, favoring ALA-ethosomes for clinical use as a tDDS in anticancer PDT [<xref rid="B58-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>]. Additionally, the ALA ethosomal formulation showed a 3.64-fold higher PpIX detection in mouse skin than the aqueous formulation [<xref rid="B59-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>].</p><p>More recently, cationic ALA-loaded ultra-deformable liposomes, also known as transfersomes (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="pharmaceuticals-14-00772-f003">Figure 3</xref>B), showed higher stability and permeability than other tested liposomal formulations, enabling ALA delivery to deeper skin layers [<xref rid="B49-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>] (<xref rid="pharmaceuticals-14-00772-t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref>). Moreover, cationic transfersomes allowed higher retention of ALA in cells and improved the induction of PpIX, indicating better photosensitizing properties compared to other hybrid lipoidal vesicles.</p><p>In 2015, Bragagni et al. developed ALA-loaded niosomes, which are amphiphilic vesicles formed by synthetic non-ionic surfactants (<xref rid="pharmaceuticals-14-00772-t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref>). These lipid-based vesicles offer a series of potential advantages over liposomes, including more significant physical and chemical stability, longer shelf life, greater ease of production and storage, lower cost, and broader formulation versatility. Ex vivo permeation and penetration studies on excised human skin revealed that niosomal formulations were significantly more effective in improving ALA tDDS than aqueous drug solutions, leading to an 80% increase of drug permeation with 100% of the drug retained in the skin [<xref rid="B60-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec3dot2dot2-pharmaceuticals-14-00772"><title>3.2.2. Temoporfin (mTHPC)</title><p>Liposomal formulations have included a broad spectrum of second-generation PSs. Bendsoe et al. reported the human use of liposomal mTHPC in a topical gel formulation for PDT to treat NMSC [<xref rid="B61-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>]. Subsequently, Johansson et al. reported a novel, selective, and in-depth distribution of a liposomal mTHPC formulation applied for 4 and 6 h in a murine skin tumor model [<xref rid="B62-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>]. The results suggested that this PS formulation could be interesting for topical administration of mTHPC, decreasing the effects of extended skin photosensitivity associated with systemic mTHPC administration. Possible toxicities using a liposomal mTHPC were investigated, revealing no general adverse effects with cats after treatment [<xref rid="B63-pharmaceuticals-14-00772" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>]. However, a low degree of toxicity was demonstrated in 15% of the test subjects.</p><p>Although liposomal mTHPC has shown limited clinical value, this potent second-generation synthetic PS with hybrid lipoidal vesicles displayed outstanding performance as tDDS in anticancer PDT. To illustrate this, Dragicevic-Curic et al. repeated the topical application of mTHPC-loaded invasomes-vesicles, containing a mixture of terpenes or a single terpene with ethanol in addition to phospholipids (<xref rid="pharmaceuticals-14-00772-t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref>), onto the skin of mice bearing subcutaneously implanted human colorectal tumor HT29 followed by photoirradiation.
