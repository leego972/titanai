The NYC Dominican community also frequently uses complex plant mixtures extracted in alcohol (<italic toggle="yes">botellas</italic>) or boiled for an extensive time in water (<italic toggle="yes">bebedizos</italic>) to treat reproductive and genitourinary conditions, including gynecological infections. <italic toggle="yes">Botellas</italic> used by Dominicans for genitourinary conditions are typically taken internally, and <italic toggle="yes">bebedizos</italic> are consumed during puerperium to cleanse the uterus after childbirth [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>]. The NYC Haitian community most frequently reported extracting plants in water and using them as an intimate wash to treat gynecological infections. Understanding cultural differences in how groups of people treat, understand, and interpret disease is crucial information for medical professionals who have patients of different cultures [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR46">46</xref>]. Physicians also need to know for which health conditions people preferentially use medicinal plants, and what these plants are through taxonomic authentication [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR34">34</xref>].</p><p id="Par35">Vaginal cleansing was another treatment that had cultural importance for Haitian women. The use of medicinal plants and other natural and/or synthetic agents to clean, wash, and tighten the vagina is common in Haitian and other cultures with African descent [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR47">47</xref>]. Vaginal cleansing is associated with an increased risk of vaginal and STI infections, such as bacterial vaginosis, HIV, and human papillomavirus [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR26">26</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR48">48</xref>]. Increased risk is linked with inflammation, stripping of the protective vaginal mucosa, as well as the disruption of the vaginal microbiota [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR49">49</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR50">50</xref>]. When used intravaginally, compounds from plants and other substances, like permanganate, come in direct contact with the vaginal microbiota. The vaginal microbiota is primarily composed of beneficial <italic toggle="yes">Lactobacillus</italic> bacteria that naturally produce lactic acid and bacteriocins that protect against infection. When the microbiota is disrupted, women are more susceptible to recurrent and more severe infections [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR51">51</xref>]. Plants used for cleansing are not the only treatment applied intravaginally. Both NYC Haitians and Dominicans [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR45">45</xref>] frequently apply plants for gynecological infections directly to the vagina as washes, douches, or steams. There has been very little ethnopharmacological research into the effect of medicinal plants on the beneficial vaginal bacteria. We previously identified plants used for gynecological infections and vaginal cleansing that were used by the Dominican community in NYC and likely important to the Haitian community [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR45">45</xref>]. This survey has identified the top five plant species used to treat gynecological infections that warrant further investigation for their effect on the vaginal microbiota: <italic toggle="yes">Cajanus cajan, Cecropia peltata</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Mentha</italic> spp.<italic toggle="yes">, Ricinus communis</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Syzygium aromaticum.</italic></p><p id="Par36">The Haitian survey instrument included specific questions about plants used as women’s health preventatives, hormonal imbalances, and gynecological cancers that were not included explicitly in the Dominican survey, which may have influenced the frequency of reports in the intercultural comparison. Women’s health preventatives were defined as plants not used for a specific illness but instead to support overall wellness for reproductive health. The number of use reports for these preparations shows the importance of preventative medicine for Haitian women. There were also women’s health conditions participants reported that could not easily be self-diagnosed, such as gynecological cysts/fibroids and cancers. However, Haitian participants mentioned frequently in interviews that they would go to the doctor to receive a diagnosis and then self-treat or seek the help of a healer to treat the condition with medicinal plants. Furthermore, when participants reported using plants for cancer, it was typically reported as cancer prevention. <italic toggle="yes">Dysphania ambrosioides</italic> (L.) Mosyakin &amp; Clemants (<italic toggle="yes">simen kontra</italic>, Mexican tea) was frequently taken as a daily tea for cancer prevention because it is believed to be “good for everything.” Similarly, the fruit of <italic toggle="yes">Annona muricata</italic> L. (<italic toggle="yes">korosol</italic>, soursop) was eaten daily to prevent cancer. Both plants have research supporting their potential for cancer preventative properties.
