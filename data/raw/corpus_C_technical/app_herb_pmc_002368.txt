In particular β-CD complexes formed with the root extract at 25 °C and α-CD complexes with the aerial part at 40 °C showed high antioxidant capacities in several assays, including ABTS, DPPH, FRAP, CUPRAC, and phosphomolybdenum (PBD). Therefore, using multiple assays is essential to comprehensively evaluate the antioxidant potential of the samples. Overall, root extracts demonstrated stronger antioxidant activity, especially when encapsulated under optimal conditions, while cyclodextrin encapsulation helps preserve antioxidant capacity, β-cyclodextrin showed better interaction with phenolic compounds, particularly in root samples. Although β-cyclodextrin appeared slightly more effective in some cases, the differences between β-CD and α-CD treatments were not substantial. This suggests that both types of cyclodextrins can be effectively used for encapsulation. The absence of large variations in activity indicates that the phytochemicals in <italic toggle="yes">L. bellidifolium</italic> are compatible with both α- and β-cyclodextrin structures. Nevertheless, the slightly higher activity observed in non-encapsulated extracts suggests that some loss of antioxidant compounds may occur during encapsulation, possibly due to partial entrapment or limited release of active compounds.</p><p id="Par65">Furthermore, encapsulation with β-CD significantly enhanced the antioxidant activity of plant extracts. This finding is consistent with previous reports. For example, β-CD complexes with tea catechins (EGCG and GCG) doubled the antioxidant activity compared to free catechins, as measured by the DPPH assay. This result showed the ability of β-CD to improve the bioactivity of plant polyphenols<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR55">55</xref></sup>. Similarly, a study on the rutin-β-CD complex showed increased antioxidant activity depending on the solvent diversity<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR56">56</xref></sup>. Moreover, encapsulation of <italic toggle="yes">Origanum compactum</italic> essential oil in β-CD metal-organic frameworks resulted in enhanced DPPH scavenging activity and improved thermal stability<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR57">57</xref></sup>. In our findings, antioxidant activity was higher with β-CD encapsulation, while lower values were observed with α-CD. The results are supported by previous studies. For instance, in red clover and <italic toggle="yes">Actinidia</italic> leaf extracts, α-CD showed weaker antioxidant capacity than β- or γ-CD in ABTS, DPPH, and FRAP assays<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR58">58</xref></sup>. On the other hand, many studies also support finding, reporting that β-CD enhances solubility, stability, and antioxidant function. For example, encapsulated garlic oil, turmeric extract, and other essential oils showed increased DPPH activity and longer-lasting antioxidant effects after complexation with β-CD<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR59">59</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR61">61</xref></sup>. Overall, our results confirmed by previous evidence that β-CD is an effective encapsulation agent for enhancing the antioxidant potential of plant-derived compounds. In contrast, α-CD may contribute to molecular protection but can reduce antioxidant activity under certain conditions. This difference highlights the influence of cyclodextrin cavity size and binding interactions on the antioxidant performance of inclusion complexes.</p></sec><sec id="Sec17"><title>Phytochemical content analysis</title><p id="Par66">Detailed phytochemical analyses were carried out to identify the bioactive compounds in the pure plant extracts. A total of 32 compounds were found in the aerial and root parts of the plant. These analyses were done only on pure extracts to understand the natural potential of the plant’s bioactive molecules. In the next step, to better understand how the compounds behave after bioavailability, we selected 11 major compounds for further analysis. These included gallic acid, rutin, apigenin, quercetin, kaempferol, epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG), ferulic acid, caffeic acid, myricetin, and vanillic acid. The selection was based on the results of our study, previous literature, and data from other <italic toggle="yes">Limonium</italic> species<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR2">2</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR62">62</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR67">67</xref></sup>. A literature review was also conducted to support and validate the selection of these compounds<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR2">2</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR42">42</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR64">64</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR65">65</xref></sup>.
