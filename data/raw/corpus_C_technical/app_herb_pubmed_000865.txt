Bacterial Alkaloids Prevent Amoebal Predation.

Klapper M(1)(2), Götze S(1)(2), Barnett R(1)(2), Willing K(1)(3), Stallforth 
P(4)(5).

Author information:

(2)Junior Research Group Chemistry of Microbial Communication, Jena, Germany.

(4)Leibniz Institute for Natural Product Research and Infection Biology, HKI,

(5)Junior Research Group Chemistry of Microbial Communication, Jena, Germany.

Bacterial defense mechanisms have evolved to protect bacteria against predation 
by nematodes, predatory bacteria, or amoebae. We identified novel bacterial 
alkaloids (pyreudiones A-D) that protect the producer, Pseudomonas fluorescens 
HKI0770, against amoebal predation. Isolation, structure elucidation, total 
synthesis, and a proposed biosynthetic pathway for these structures are 
presented. The generation of P. fluorescens gene-deletion mutants unable to 
produce pyreudiones rendered the bacterium edible to a variety of soil-dwelling 
amoebae.
