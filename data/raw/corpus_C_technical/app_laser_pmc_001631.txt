To the best of our knowledge, this is the first time such a detailed design was described—although there are numerous reports of functional handheld probes. The two initial designs described were made almost entirely from off-the-shelf components that are available in any photonics laboratory. Finally, these designs led to a simpler, more robust, and more ergonomic variant of handheld scanning probe for OCT, suitable for use in clinical environments.</p><p>Several aspects should be pointed out regarding the probes:</p><list list-type="order" id="list6-0954411914543963"><list-item><p>The mass of our devices ranges from 0.28 kg (for the probe in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig2-0954411914543963">Figure 2</xref>—with a plastic handle) to 0.35 kg (for the one in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig1-0954411914543963">Figure 1</xref>). In comparison, commercially available handheld probes for OCT range from 1.5 kg (Envisu; Bioptigen) to 2.2 kg (iVue; Optovue, Fremont, CA, USA). However, one has to point out that the latter two are not actually handheld devices, as they are placed on supports with several degrees of freedom. They can be moved around on these supports and thus they are placed in front of the area of interest, but they are not actually held in the hand; their degrees of freedom as well as the actual accessibility in any area of the body are actually limited. Such devices also include several parts of the interferometer, so they are integrated only with a specific OCT system, that is, those developed by each of the companies mentioned before. Therefore, our designs should more likely be compared with the most recent ones that have reported such probes with masses of 0.42–0.5 kg.<sup><xref rid="bibr27-0954411914543963" ref-type="bibr">27</xref></sup>
</p></list-item><list-item><p>The costs of these latter probes have not been reported, but as they include MEMS, they are higher than those of our solutions, which include the less costly GSs. As it can be calculated from the data provided for the components in <xref ref-type="table" rid="table1-0954411914543963">Table 1</xref>, the total costs of our handheld probes are €1434 for the probe in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig1-0954411914543963">Figure 1</xref> and around €1340 for the probes in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig2-0954411914543963">Figures 2</xref> and <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig3-0954411914543963">3</xref>. It must be remarked that these costs include GS as the most significant part, which costs more than €850 (with driver card and driver card heatsink, both of them mounted on the fixed part of the OCT setup). The remark that has to be made is that this GS has to be included anyway in the OCT system, as well as the fiber collimator (1) and the objective lens (12); the latter two components have an overall cost of €250. Therefore, the additional cost of the handheld probe to the OCT setup is only around €250.</p></list-item><list-item><p>From a functional point of view, the handheld probes with MEMS scanners developed recently<sup><xref rid="bibr27-0954411914543963" ref-type="bibr">27</xref></sup> require a post-acquisition image correction due to the sinusoidal regime of the MEMS scanners. These devices are also limited by the fact that they are resonant scanners, therefore they have a fixed scan/oscillatory frequency. In contrast, using GSs for the lateral scanning of the sample in the OCT system has certain advantages (besides lower costs): the scan frequency of the GSs is adjustable; they can employ triangular scanning, with constant scan speed on the active portions of the scan. As demonstrated,<sup><xref rid="bibr32-0954411914543963" ref-type="bibr">32</xref></sup> the latter aspect provides images with fewer artifacts (i.e. distortion-free).</p></list-item><list-item><p>A limitation of our devices is the fact that they include only a 1D GS, therefore they can achieve only transversal images/B-scans (as demonstrated in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig5-0954411914543963">Figure 5</xref>). To perform 3D reconstructions, one needs dual-axis MEMS<sup><xref rid="bibr27-0954411914543963" ref-type="bibr">27</xref></sup> or 2D GSs;<sup><xref rid="bibr21-0954411914543963" ref-type="bibr">21</xref></sup> the latter devices are the aim of our current and future works. However, for the designs developed in this study, the aim of our design has been to obtain probes as simple and low cost as possible. Even more, these handheld probes, which include only the scanner, can be utilized in any OCT configuration (TD, SD, or SS), which is also a plus. In fact, we have already demonstrated in our groups the use of these probes in a fixed TD and in a mobile SD-OCT system—in this study, as well as in an SS-OCT system in a previous study.<sup><xref rid="bibr30-0954411914543963" ref-type="bibr">30</xref></sup>
