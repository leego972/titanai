These optical interactions include scattering, refraction, and selective absorption, influencing the depth and distribution of photobiological effects.</p><p>LLLT uses low-intensity laser light, typically ranging from one to one thousand milliwatts. It is commonly used for its tissue-healing, pain-relieving, and anti-inflammatory properties [<xref rid="B133-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">133</xref>,<xref rid="B134-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">134</xref>]. It is frequently used to treat musculoskeletal conditions, promote wound healing, and address dermatological issues [<xref rid="B3-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B95-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>]. It has also been shown to be effective in preventing oral mucositis in stem cell transplant recipients undergoing chemotherapy [<xref rid="B135-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">135</xref>]. In the treatment of musculoskeletal conditions, LLLT has shown promise in providing short-term pain relief for such conditions as rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis, chronic low back pain, neck pain, tendinopathy, chronic joint disease, and oncology-related pain [<xref rid="B95-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>,<xref rid="B128-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">128</xref>].</p><p>However, the effectiveness of LLLT in dentistry and wound healing remains controversial. This variability may be attributed to differences in treatment protocols, including wavelength selection, energy density, and irradiation time, which are not yet standardised across clinical settings [<xref rid="B80-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>]. Nevertheless, evidence suggests that the effects of LLLT are related to specific wavelengths of laser light that primarily target the respiratory enzyme cytochrome c oxidase, which plays a role in the electron transport chain within mitochondria [<xref rid="B136-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">136</xref>]. This interaction stimulates and enhances cell function, promotes wound repair, and reduces inflammation. Furthermore, cytochrome c oxidase activation results in increased ATP synthesis, enhanced cellular metabolism, and modulation of transcription factors that are involved in tissue regeneration [<xref rid="B101-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">101</xref>,<xref rid="B102-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>].</p><p>Cold laser therapy, also known as LLLT, is a non-invasive medical treatment that uses low-level lasers or light-emitting diodes (LEDs) to stimulate cell function and promote tissue healing [<xref rid="B137-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">137</xref>,<xref rid="B138-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">138</xref>]. Widely used in sports medicine and dermatology, it is particularly effective in reducing pain, controlling inflammation, and improving wound healing [<xref rid="B3-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B139-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">139</xref>]. It promotes tissue repair, reduces inflammation, and accelerates wound healing [<xref rid="B140-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">140</xref>]. Cold laser therapy is also used to treat skin conditions, such as acne, scars, and psoriasis [<xref rid="B3-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B139-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">139</xref>]. Unlike HILT, cold laser therapy operates at lower power outputs (typically &lt; 500 mW), making it safer for superficial applications and sensitive areas, such as the face or mucosal surfaces [<xref rid="B104-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">104</xref>]. The effectiveness of cold laser therapy depends on such parameters as wavelength, fluence, and treatment duration, all of which must be carefully adjusted according to the clinical indication.</p></sec><sec><title>6.2. High-Intensity Laser Therapy (HILT)</title><p>As reported by Starzec-Proserpio et al. (2022) [<xref rid="B141-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">141</xref>], HILT is becoming increasingly popular for treating chronic musculoskeletal pain, including such conditions as vulvodynia and fibromyalgia. The therapy aims to alleviate pain and enhance functionality in chronic inflammatory and degenerative conditions [<xref rid="B142-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">142</xref>]. The mechanism of action of HILT is based on biostimulation [<xref rid="B143-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">143</xref>]. High-intensity laser radiation provides cells with additional energy, thereby affecting their metabolism and increasing ATP production in the mitochondria [<xref rid="B144-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">144</xref>]. This promotes cellular repair and regeneration as well as anti-inflammatory responses, making HILT effective in rehabilitative medicine. Due to its ability to penetrate deep tissue (by several centimetres), HILT is particularly well-suited to treating large joints, deep muscle layers, and neuropathic pain syndromes [<xref rid="B90-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">90</xref>].
