# PMC: phytochemical bioactive compound anticancer — Overview

Bioactive constituents such as alkaloids, flavonoids, phenols, tannins and terpenoids are known to elicit broad antimicrobial responses against bacteria, fungi, viruses and parasites [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref003" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0272457.ref017" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0272457.ref032" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0272457.ref033" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>]. A number of studies documented that these phytochemicals also possess many pharmacological properties, including but not limited to anticancer, antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, cytotoxicity, anti-diarrheal, anti-hemostatic, anti-hemorrhoidal, anti-apoptosis, anti-aging, and growth regulation [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref031" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0272457.ref034" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>]. Cardiac glycosides possess antihypertensive properties whereas, steroids can act as antibacterial agents and their association with compounds such as sex hormones is significant. As <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds possess these important phytochemical constituents to which their pharmacological activities are ascribed [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref001" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0272457.ref002" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0272457.ref014" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0272457.ref035" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>], this signifies their potential use as medicine against microbial infections.</p></sec><sec id="sec026"><title>Quantification of phytochemicals</title><p>The TTC of extracts was calculated in terms of tannic acid equivalents (TAE) by using the standardized linear equation (<xref rid="pone.0272457.g002" ref-type="fig">Fig 2A</xref>). The correlation coefficient (R<sup>2</sup>) for the tannic acid calibration was of 0.9015. The tannin content in both aqueous and methanol extract of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds was 81.7 ± 0.9 μg TAE/g (<xref rid="pone.0272457.t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>). The means of total tannin content (<italic toggle="yes">μ</italic>g TAE/g) in <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds (NSS) extracts obtained by maceration with water and methanol) were not statistically different (F-ration: 2.14 x 10<sup>−5</sup>, p &gt; 0.05). There was also no noticeable difference in the TTC determined in aqueous and methanol extracts. Both solvents were, therefore, best in extracting tannins.</p><fig position="float" id="pone.0272457.g002" orientation="portrait"><object-id pub-id-type="doi">10.1371/journal.pone.0272457.g002</object-id><label>Fig 2</label><caption><title>Standard calibration curves for the quantification of phytochemicals in <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds extracts.</title><p><bold>(A):</bold> Calibration curve of tannic acid for the determination of total tannin content in aqueous and methanol extracts of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds. <bold>(B):</bold> Standard calibration curve of gallic acid for the determination of total phenolic content in aqueous and methanol extracts of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds. <bold>(C):</bold> Standard calibration curve of quercetin for the determination of total flavonoid content in aqueous and methanol extracts of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>.
