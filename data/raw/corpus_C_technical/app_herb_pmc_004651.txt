There is a strong rationale for targeting the leukotriene signaling system, which mediates various aspects of AD pathology (<xref rid="pharmaceuticals-18-00261-f009" ref-type="fig">Figure 9</xref>) [<xref rid="B264-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">264</xref>].</p><p>Adaptogenic herbs affecting all these issues simultaneously by a pleiotropic mechanism might be a much better strategy to treat dementia compared with the monospecific approaches of the past decades, most of which, if not all, have entirely failed. Notably, 5-LOC levels and the activity of the leukotriene pathway are elevated in the brain with age, likely contributing to age-related CNS diseases [<xref rid="B265-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">265</xref>].</p><p>The adaptogens modulate the expression of genes in stress and aging-related disorders, characterized by an imbalance between pro- and anti-inflammatory eicosanoids in low-grade systemic inflammatory conditions. A study of transcriptome-wide RNA sequencing to profile gene expression alterations in T98G neuroglia cells upon treatment with plant extracts shows that all tested adaptogen extracts revealed a specific “signature” on eicosanoid signaling and shared features for adaptogens and other anti-inflammatory plants [<xref rid="B68-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>]. The common feature for all tested anti-inflammatory plant extracts was related to the downregulation of the ALOX12 gene, which is associated with the neuroprotective action of these medicinal plants and their potential benefits in neurodegenerative diseases. <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Withania somnifera</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Eleutherococcus senticosus</italic> extracts downregulate the expression of ALOX5AP, DPEP2, and LTC4S genes involved in the biosynthesis of leukotrienes A, B, C, D, and E, resulting in inhibition of the leukotriene signaling pathway and suggesting their potential benefits in Alzheimer disease (<xref rid="pharmaceuticals-18-00261-f009" ref-type="fig">Figure 9</xref>) [<xref rid="B68-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>].</p><p>Adaptogens modulated the expression of 14 genes encoding the proteins playing key roles in eicosanoid signaling [<xref rid="B68-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>]. All of them downregulated the expression of arachidonate 12-lipoxygenase, 12S type (ALOX12). The most potent effect exhibited by Eleutherococcus is decreasing gene expression by 7.4-fold. A similar magnitude effect was observed for the BHP combination of Withania with melatonin. However, the downregulation of ALOX12 expression is not specific for adaptogens since other tested anti-inflammatory plant extracts (curcumin and Boswellia) also attenuated ALOX12 expression, suggesting that all these plants may inhibit 12-HETE-mediated neurotoxicity. Some synergistic and antagonistic interactions between Withania, Rhodiola, and melatonin have been documented [<xref rid="B67-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>,<xref rid="B68-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>], including the unexpected potential benefits of the fixed combination in Alzheimer’s disease. A promising direction of further studies is related to the use of the combination of Rhodiola with Withania (Adaptra<sup>®</sup>), which inhibits the LTC4 signaling pathway by downregulation of expression of the leukotriene C4 synthase (LTC4S) gene and upregulating the PTGER3 gene for the treatment of allergic asthma and Alzheimer disease. The correlation between the effects of anti-inflammatory or adaptogenic plants on the eicosanoid signaling pathway was related to the downregulation of ALOX12, suggesting that it is one possible mechanism of the neuroprotective action of these medicinal plants as well as their potential benefits in neurodegenerative diseases [<xref rid="B68-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec4dot2dot2-pharmaceuticals-18-00261"><title>4.2.2. The Dose Matters: Hormetic Dose-Dependent Reversal Effects of Adaptogens</title><p>The pharmacodynamics of adaptogens is characterized by their interaction with networks of endogenous regulators and mediators of adaptive stress response, a time-dependent process of maintaining homeostasis. They induce an adaptive response to stress by triggering adaptive signaling pathways in the neuroendocrine-immune complex.</p><p>The concentration of active constituents of adaptogens, e.g., <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> preparations, in the organisms varies over time after their intake [<xref rid="B266-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">266</xref>,<xref rid="B267-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">267</xref>,<xref rid="B268-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">268</xref>], and their response is reversal depending on the dose [<xref rid="B88-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">88</xref>,<xref rid="B269-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">269</xref>], commonly known as the hormetic response [<xref rid="B210-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">210</xref>,<xref rid="B211-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">211</xref>,<xref rid="B212-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">212</xref>,<xref rid="B213-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">213</xref>,<xref rid="B214-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">214</xref>,<xref rid="B215-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">215</xref>,<xref rid="B216-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">216</xref>,<xref rid="B217-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">217</xref>,<xref rid="B218-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">218</xref>,<xref rid="B219-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">219</xref>,<xref rid="B220-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">220</xref>,<xref rid="B221-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">221</xref>,<xref rid="B270-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">270</xref>,<xref rid="B271-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">271</xref>].
