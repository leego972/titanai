Introduction</title><p>Therapeutic plants are of major importance in human life due to their bioactive phytochemicals, which provide potential health benefits and possess commercial value [<xref rid="B1-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B2-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="B3-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>].</p><p>The <italic toggle="yes">Onagraceae</italic> is a large family of flowering plants with around 650 species of trees, shrubs, and herbs spread throughout approximately 17 genera [<xref rid="B4-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B5-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>]. This family is divided into two subfamilies: <italic toggle="yes">Ludwigioideae</italic>, which primarily includes the genus <italic toggle="yes">Ludwigia</italic>; and <italic toggle="yes">Onagroideae</italic>, sometimes referred to as the willowherb or evening primrose family. Many species within <italic toggle="yes">Onagroideae</italic> are known for their therapeutic and dietary benefits [<xref rid="B6-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>]. Characteristically, <italic toggle="yes">Onagroideae</italic> species have two to four (rarely three) deciduous sepals, while <italic toggle="yes">Ludwigioideae</italic> often have three to four persistent sepals, allowing for clear differentiation between them [<xref rid="B4-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>]. Several well-known garden plants belong to this family, including evening primrose (<italic toggle="yes">Oenothera</italic> L.) and fuchsia (<italic toggle="yes">Fuchsia</italic> L.). Additionally, many <italic toggle="yes">Onagraceae</italic> species are valued for their medicinal applications. Many Onagraceae species, including <italic toggle="yes">Oenothera biennis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Epilobium angustifolium</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Ludwigia octovalvis</italic>, exhibit strong antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties due to their rich flavonoid and polyphenol content. Additionally, compounds from <italic toggle="yes">Oenothera biennis</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Oenothera paradoxa</italic> have demonstrated cytotoxic effects against prostate and breast cancer cells [<xref rid="B7-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>,<xref rid="B8-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>,<xref rid="B9-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>].
