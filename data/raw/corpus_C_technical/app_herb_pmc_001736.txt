Notably, <italic toggle="yes">D</italic>-(+)-pinitol <bold>48</bold> was a potent inhibitor that was two times more active than its enantiomer (–)-pinitol <bold>49</bold>. </p><p><italic toggle="yes">D</italic>-(+)-Pinitol <bold>48</bold> has been used as a natural health supplement to provide therapeutic benefits in treating T2D. It is also a natural antidiabetic agent and insulin regulator with anti-inflammatory [<xref rid="B104-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">104</xref>] and hepatoprotective [<xref rid="B105-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">105</xref>] activities. In an animal model of diabetes, <italic toggle="yes">D</italic>-(+)-pinitol <bold>48</bold> has been described as an antidiabetic agent with an insulin-like effect that can enhance insulin activity by translocating the GLUT-4 in the mice’s skeletal muscles [<xref rid="B106-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>,<xref rid="B107-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">107</xref>]. It was demonstrated in animal and clinical studies that <italic toggle="yes">D</italic>-(+)-pinitol <bold>48</bold> effectively regulated hyperglycemia and prevented insulin resistance. The mechanism of action of <italic toggle="yes">D</italic>-(+)-pinitol <bold>48</bold> was explained via its ability [<xref rid="B108-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>] (i) to protect the pancreas against diabetes-induced oxidative stress because of its antioxidation property [<xref rid="B104-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">104</xref>]; (ii) to overcome insulin resistance by modulating the PI3kt/Akt signalling pathway in a rat model [<xref rid="B109-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">109</xref>]; and (iii) to increase glucose-induced insulin secretion by reducing the expression of the subunit alpha of AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK-<italic toggle="yes">α</italic>) that protected against triglyceride deposition in the liver [<xref rid="B110-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>]. The inhibitory activity of <italic toggle="yes">D</italic>-(+)-pinitol <bold>48</bold> against α-glucosidase would provide an additional therapeutic benefit to combat T2D.</p><p>Studies have revealed that the inhibition of the <italic toggle="yes">α</italic>-glucosidase enzyme could be achieved using flavonoids [<xref rid="B102-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>]. Proença et al. [<xref rid="B111-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">111</xref>] reported the <italic toggle="yes">α</italic>-glucosidase inhibitory activity of flavonoids, particularly those identified in <italic toggle="yes">A. saligna</italic>. Quercetin <bold>3</bold>, kaempferol <bold>22</bold>, and luteolin <bold>16</bold> were found to have IC<sub>50</sub> values of 15, 32m and 46 µM, respectively, compared to the positive control acarbose (IC<sub>50</sub> = 607 µM). At the same time, rutin <bold>6</bold> and naringenin <bold>42</bold> were found to inhibit the enzyme activity by &lt;20% and 45% at 200 µM (the highest tested concentration). Another group showed that myricetin <bold>10</bold>, quercetin 3, kaempferol <bold>22</bold>, luteolin <bold>16</bold>, and naringin <bold>42</bold> inhibited the enzyme with IC<sub>50</sub> values of 5, 7, 12, 21, and 75 µM, respectively [<xref rid="B112-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>]. Myricetin <bold>10</bold> was active with an IC<sub>50</sub> of 4.48 µM compared to the positive control acarbose (304 µM) [<xref rid="B113-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>]. Kaempferol <bold>22</bold> has also been shown to inhibit the enzyme with an IC<sub>50</sub> value of 77.42 μM [<xref rid="B102-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>].</p><sec><title>SAR of Flavonoids Required for the α-Glucosidase Inhibition</title><p>SAR investigation of flavonoids carried out by Proença et al. [<xref rid="B111-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">111</xref>] suggested that flavonoids with two phenolic groups at the A or B ring and a hydroxy group at C-3 possessed the highest α-glucosidase inhibitory activity. He et al. [<xref rid="B114-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>] and Şöhretoğlu et al. [<xref rid="B102-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>] further reiterated that the number of phenolic groups on ring B was vital for the activity. A free hydroxyl group at C-4′ was crucial for <italic toggle="yes">α</italic>-glucosidase inhibition.
