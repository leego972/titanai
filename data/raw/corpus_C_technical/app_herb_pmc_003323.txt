We also observed that five species found in this study were used to treat more than one disease in distinct categories. For instance, fennel, locally named <italic toggle="yes">Adas</italic> (<italic toggle="yes">Foeniculum vulgare</italic> Mill.) has been used to treat urticaria/hives (DD), cough (RT), and to overcome motion sickness (OT). Betelvine (<italic toggle="yes">Piper betle</italic> L.) has also been used for more than one disease including leucorrhoea (UGP), hives or urticaria (DD), and worm disease (GI). This demonstrated that the use value of these species is quite high compared with that of other medicinal plants [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref022" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec014"><title>Species and family use value</title><p>Species use value demonstrates the value of a medicinal plant species used by the people from Ngadisari village. Our results revealed that the SUV of the reported plants varied from 0.01 to 1.01 (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="pone.0235886.g002">Fig 2</xref>). Five species showed the highest SUV: <italic toggle="yes">Foeniculum vulgare</italic> Mill. (1.01), <italic toggle="yes">Aloe vera</italic> (L.) Burm. f. (0.86), <italic toggle="yes">Acorus calamus</italic> L. (0.8), <italic toggle="yes">Apium graveolens</italic> L. (0.76), and <italic toggle="yes">Allium fistulosum</italic> L. (0.71). Previous studies also demonstrated that fennel is frequently used as medicinal plants in Indonesia [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref012" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0235886.ref025" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>] and is abundantly present in this region [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref009" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. Our data showed that <italic toggle="yes">F</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">vulgare</italic> is categorized as a plant used to treat dermatological problems (DO). People of Tengger inhabiting Ngadisari village use <italic toggle="yes">F</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">vulgare</italic> to treat urticaria, hives, or itching. Our results are also in accordance with other studies that revealed <italic toggle="yes">F</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">vulgare</italic> as a traditional medicine for people suffering from itching or other dermatitis problems [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref026" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>].</p><fig id="pone.0235886.g002" orientation="portrait" position="float"><object-id pub-id-type="doi">10.1371/journal.pone.0235886.g002</object-id><label>Fig 2</label><caption><title>Species Use Value (SUV) of medicinal plants found in the Ngadisari village, Indonesia.</title></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="pone.0235886.g002.jpg"/></fig><p>Similar medicinal uses of <italic toggle="yes">Aloe vera</italic> (L.) Burm. f., <italic toggle="yes">Acorus calamus</italic> L., <italic toggle="yes">Apium graveolens</italic> L., and <italic toggle="yes">Allium fistulosum</italic> L. have been reported in previous ethnobotanical studies. For example, Reimers <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>. [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref027" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>] and Salehi <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>. [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref028" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>] reported the use of <italic toggle="yes">Aloe vera</italic> to treat hair problems. Meanwhile, the use of <italic toggle="yes">Acorus calamus</italic> L. to treat fever has been reported by Rajput <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>. [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref029" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>]. Also, <italic toggle="yes">A</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">graveolens</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">A</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">fistulosum</italic> L. have been used by traditional Chinese and Indonesian people to reduce blood pressure and cough, respectively [<xref rid="pone.0235886.ref030" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0235886.ref031" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>]. Finally, nine species were reported to have low SUV (0.01) in the present study (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="pone.0235886.g002">Fig 2</xref>). High or low SUV may be due to extensive or minimum ethnobotanical uses of the reported species, respectively.
