UC and UCB are disposable biological material/medical waste at parturition and have been used in scientifically proven procedures to isolate hMSCs with the potential to differentiate into multipotent cell types [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR13">13</xref>]. Due to the high availability of UC and UCB, hMSC requirements for transplantation could be fulfilled easily using them [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>]. Extra-embryonic tissue-derived MSCs have demonstrated immune-privileged characteristics and broader multipotent plasticity [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR15">15</xref>]. UCB-hMSCs have demonstrated the highest rates of cell proliferation and clonality and significantly lower expression of p53, p21, and p16, well known markers of senescence, compared with BM-hMSCs and adipose tissue-derived (AD) hMSCs [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR16">16</xref>]. This has made UC- and UCB-derived hMSCs more popular than those derived from BM in recent years.</p><p>In 2015, 493 MSC-based clinical trials were recorded in the database of the US National Institutes of Health, those on bone and cartilage diseases being the highest proportion and cardiovascular diseases being the second highest [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR10">10</xref>]. hMSCs are currently used in clinical studies of tissue engineering [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR17">17</xref>], clinical engraftments to repair damaged tissues, and immune disorder therapy [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR18">18</xref>]. They also have potential applications in dentistry, metabolic disorders, disorders that result in tissue degeneration, and replacing damaged tissue due to accidents. Clinical trials for osteoporosis are being carried out using hMSCs derived from different sources such as BM, adipose tissue, and UC tissue by exogenous introduction of the stem cells into osteoporosis sites [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR19">19</xref>]. Research on diabetes have utilized MSCs to produce insulin-producing cells [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR20">20</xref>], treat diabetic ulcers and limb ischemia [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR21">21</xref>], and enhance islet engraftment and survival [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR22">22</xref>]. The potential of MSCs to regenerate heart and skeletal muscle tissue has been investigated in murine models, with results leading to preclinical trials on Duchenne muscular dystrophy to replace degenerating muscle tissue and also as carriers with a functioning dystrophin gene [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR23">23</xref>]. MSCs are candidates in cancer therapy as carriers for anticancer gene therapy [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR24">24</xref>] and have proven their ability as anti-tumor agents against Kaposi’s sarcoma [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR25">25</xref>], colon carcinoma [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR26">26</xref>], Lewis Lung carcinoma, and B16F10 melanoma [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR27">27</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="Sec3"><title>Current stimulants in stem cell therapy: cytokines and growth factors</title><p>Different semi-biological and synthetic substances are currently used for the proliferation and differentiation of MSCs. During the early scientific research, components isolated from human biological sources were used for this [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR28">28</xref>] but because of ethical controversies and the long and tedious downstream processing for the isolation and purification of such substances, recombinant and synthetic components have emerged as replacements. Currently, a cocktail of dexamethasone, ascorbic acid, and β-glycerophosphate is used for in vitro osteogenic differentiation of hMSCs, while 3-isobutyl-1-methylxanthine, hydrocortisone, and indomethacin are used for adipogenic differentiation, and fibroblast growth factor and bone morphogenetic protein (BMP)-2 are used for higher rates of proliferation [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>]. Several extracellular matrix proteins have demonstrated proliferation and differentiation potential-preserving properties [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>]. A cytokine, granulocyte colony stimulating factor (G-CSF), has been used to mobilize and differentiate murine MSCs into cardiomyocytes [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR30">30</xref>].</p><sec id="Sec4"><title>Drawbacks of current stimulants</title><p>Many companies produce recombinant cytokines using <italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic> cells, plant cells, and other mammalian cells.
