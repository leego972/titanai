He had previously been hospitalised, and after 2 weeks of antibiotic therapy, which led to a reduction in the infection level, he was referred to our clinic for advanced wound care (Figure <xref rid="iwj70796-fig-0001" ref-type="fig">1A</xref>). After 1 month of combined laser and plasma therapy, the ulcer showed signs of new tissue formation and the infection significantly decreased. By the end of the first month, the wound had reduced in size and was approximately 80% granulated (Figure <xref rid="iwj70796-fig-0001" ref-type="fig">1B</xref>). After 2 months of continuous plasma injections and laser therapy, the wound achieved complete granulation (Figure <xref rid="iwj70796-fig-0001" ref-type="fig">1C</xref>). From the third month onward, plasma therapy was discontinued and treatment continued with laser therapy alone. Laser sessions were reduced from three times per week to twice per week, and silver dressings were applied between sessions to maintain wound hygiene and support healing (Figure <xref rid="iwj70796-fig-0001" ref-type="fig">1D</xref>). Throughout the treatment period, the patient's CBC, BUN, Cr and ESR were monitored weekly, and medications were adjusted accordingly.</p><p>By the fifth month, the wound was completely healed, and all laboratory values had returned to normal levels (Figure <xref rid="iwj70796-fig-0001" ref-type="fig">1E</xref>).</p></sec><sec id="iwj70796-sec-0011"><label>4.2</label><title>Case 2</title><p>A 58‐year‐old female patient presented with a severe DFU accompanied by necrosis of one toe. Upon admission, baseline laboratory investigations were performed, including, CBC, ESR, CRP, BUN, Cr and wound culture. Following the availability of laboratory results, antibiotic therapy was initiated according to the culture sensitivity (Figure <xref rid="iwj70796-fig-0002" ref-type="fig">2A</xref>). After 1 month of treatment with laser therapy and sub‐necrotic tissue plasma injections a technique we employ to target and resorb necrotic tissue in chronic wounds the wound demonstrated approximately 70% granulation. However, the necrotic tissue was still partially present (Figure <xref rid="iwj70796-fig-0002" ref-type="fig">2B</xref>). After 2 months of continuous plasma and laser therapy, the necrotic tissue was fully eliminated without the need for amputation (Figure <xref rid="iwj70796-fig-0002" ref-type="fig">2C</xref>). The wound achieved full granulation, and plasma injections were discontinued. Laser therapy was continued as the sole modality. From this point, laser sessions were reduced from three times per week to twice weekly, and during the intervals between sessions, the patient applied Medihoney wound gel to maintain a moist and antimicrobial environment (Figure <xref rid="iwj70796-fig-0002" ref-type="fig">2D</xref>). By the end of the fifth month, the wound had completely healed, with full epithelialization and restoration of tissue integrity (Figure <xref rid="iwj70796-fig-0002" ref-type="fig">2E</xref>).</p><fig position="float" fig-type="FIGURE" id="iwj70796-fig-0002" orientation="portrait"><label>FIGURE 2</label><caption><p>Diabetic foot ulcer with necrotic toe.
