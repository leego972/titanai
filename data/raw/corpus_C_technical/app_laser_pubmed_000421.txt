Biodegradable and biocompatible poly(lactic acid) optical fibers for 
fiber-guided photothermal therapy of breast cancer cells in vitro.

Breast cancer remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality 
among women worldwide, while conventional clinical treatments are often limited 
by high invasiveness, severe side effects, and insufficient spatial control over 
therapeutic energy delivery. Fiber-based optical platforms offer a compelling 
strategy for minimally invasive photothermal therapy by enabling precise and 
localized light transmission within biological tissues. Here, we report a 
scalable extrusion-thermal drawing strategy for fabricating biodegradable and 
biocompatible poly(lactic acid)-based optical fibers (BPOFs) and demonstrate 
their application as biodegradable optical waveguides for fiber-mediated 
photothermal ablation of breast cancer cells. Owing to the intrinsic 
biodegradability and biosafety of poly(lactic acid), the resulting BPOFs exhibit 
excellent structural uniformity, mechanical flexibility, and efficient 
near-infrared light guidance, with a low propagation loss of 0.33 dB cm-1 at 
808 nm. By integrating indocyanine green (ICG) at the distal fiber interface as 
a photothermal transduction layer, the system enables efficient 
optical-to-thermal energy conversion and spatially confined heat generation 
under near-infrared laser irradiation. In vitro studies reveal that BPOF-guided 
photothermal treatment induces a pronounced time-dependent cytotoxic effect on 
MCF-7 breast cancer cells, leading to significant suppression of cell viability. 
Collectively, this work highlights biodegradable polymer optical fibers as a 
versatile photonic interface for controllable light-tissue interaction, with 
promising potential for future minimally invasive photothermal applications.
