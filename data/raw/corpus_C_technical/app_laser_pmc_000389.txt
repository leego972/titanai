Furthermore, it has been hypothesized that pain could play a role in the pathogenesis of blood pressure increase after MAL-PDT, including acute postoperative hypertension and hypertensive crisis [<xref rid="B38-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>].</p><p>Tumoral thickness is the best-studied limiting factor of PDT for BCC. McKay <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> [<xref rid="B39-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>] described those tumors thicker than 0.4 mm had more risk of recurrence and, on the other hand, PDT is not recommended for nodular BCC deeper than 2 mm [<xref rid="B24-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>]. However, for superficial BCC no association was found between thickness and adnexal extension and treatment failure [<xref rid="B40-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>]. To reduce the impact of this limitation, different techniques have been used to diminish the tumor size or to increase the penetration of the photosensitizer; Christensen <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> [<xref rid="B41-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>] showed that a deep curettage which, reduced the tumor thickness by 50%, improved the outcome in patients following PDT in BCC with mean thickness of 2.3 mm (CR at three months of 93%).</p><p>Some histological subtypes of BCC are more resistant to PDT than others. Pigmented BCC responds worse due to light absorption by melanin [<xref rid="B42-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>]; the high density of collagen and the cords of cells infiltrating the connective tissue limits the penetration of PS in the morphoeic and infiltrative variants of BCC, respectively [<xref rid="B42-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>]. For these reasons, PDT is not indicated in these subtypes of tumor.</p><p>Hyperkeratosis is one of the principal inconveniences for the photosensitizer penetration. The use of keratolytic agents or curettage before PDT is mandatory for a successful PDT [<xref rid="B28-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>].</p><p>The size of the tumor is a limiting factor for some authors. Madan <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> found that giant superficial BCC [<xref rid="B43-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>] or those tumors over 4 cm in diameter respond worse to PDT [<xref rid="B44-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>,<xref rid="B45-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>]. However, there are more studies supporting that tumor diameter does not affect the outcome [<xref rid="B46-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>,<xref rid="B47-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>,<xref rid="B48-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">48</xref>,<xref rid="B49-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>,<xref rid="B50-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>]. In BD, clinical guidelines consider PDT as a first line treatment, especially in big and multiple lesions [<xref rid="B49-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>,<xref rid="B51-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>,<xref rid="B52-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>]. Whereas some authors conclude that tumor diameter is not a predictor of final response, although it has been associated with tumor relapse [<xref rid="B53-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>], others found it a negative prognostic factor [<xref rid="B54-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">54</xref>].</p><p>The site of the lesions could have a strong influence over PDT response. BCC located on the face/head has lower complete response rates compared to those on the trunk/neck region (at 24 months, 54% <italic toggle="yes">vs.</italic> 88%) [<xref rid="B48-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">48</xref>]. If the tumor is present in the H zone of the face, regardless of size, the response is also reduced; considering also that this is a risk zone for BCC, PDT is not indicated in this area [<xref rid="B48-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">48</xref>]. Regarding AK, lesions present on the limbs are more resistant to MAL-PDT than those located on the face/scalp [<xref rid="B55-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>,<xref rid="B56-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>]. Modern guidelines consider BD located on fingers as a limitation factor for the use of PDT [<xref rid="B57-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>].</p><p>Another factor that may limit the effectiveness of PDT can be patient’s age, being more effective in younger patients [<xref rid="B58-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>]. Previous history of radiotherapy in the area has been also associated with less response of BCC [<xref rid="B59-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec2dot2-ijms-16-25912"><title>2.2.
