Lansoprazole, omeprazole, esoprazole, and <italic toggle="yes">S</italic>-mephenytoin are the in vivo sensitive substrates for CYP2C19, while dextromethorphan is a substrate for CYP2D6 [<xref rid="B23-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>]. In particular, paclitaxel and <italic toggle="yes">S</italic>-mephenytoin are known to have a narrow therapeutic range, while the drugs used for respiratory illness like dextromethorphan and montelokast are quite sensitive here. Therefore, the concomitant use of these drugs with <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extracts may result in clinically significant drug interactions and requires careful attention. The dosage of <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> preparations is discussed contradictorily in the literature. In a controlled study, the efficacy was shown with low dosages (approximately 300 mg herbal substance), while in the market there are preparations with daily dosages up to approximately 1000 mg herbal substance (Committee on Herbal Medicinal Products, European Medicines Agency) [<xref rid="B4-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B10-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>,<xref rid="B14-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>]. The clinical pharmacokinetic data on <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extracts is unavailable at present. Although the absorption of <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extract from the gastrointestinal tract is shown to be somewhat inadequate for some constituents like hederacoside C and α-hederin [<xref rid="B10-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>,<xref rid="B14-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>], if the extract is assumed as a single compound, its plasma concentration could reach up around 1 mg/mL. Therefore, it might have clinically close relevance for CYP-mediated herb–drug interactions, at least for CYP2C8 and CYP2C19, considering its maximum daily dosages. </p></sec><sec id="sec4-molecules-22-01241"><title>4. Materials and Methods</title><sec id="sec4dot1-molecules-22-01241"><title>4.1. Chemicals and Reagents</title><p>HDC (&gt;98.0%) and <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extract were obtained from the Lab. of Pharmacognosy, College of Pharmacy, Yonsei University (Incheon, Korea). The extract was prepared by extracting the pulverized ivy leaves with 30% ethanol for 1 h using sonication. A voucher specimen of <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extract (HY-2016-01-05) was deposited at the Herbarium of the College of Pharmacy, Hanyang University, Ansan, Korea. <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extract contains 8.2% of HDC. The content of HDC was determined by liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) [<xref rid="B10-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>] and the representative chromatogram is shown in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="molecules-22-01241-f004">Figure 4</xref>. Pooled human liver microsomes and recombinant CYP2C8, CYP2C19, and CYP2D6 isozymes were purchased from BD Gentest (Woburn, MA, USA). Glucose-6-phosphate, β-NADP+, glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase, coumarin, phenacetin, diclofenac, midazolam, mephenytoin, dextromethorphan, ketoconazole, and terfenadine were purchased from Sigma Chemical Co. (Saint Louis, MO, USA). All other solvents used were of HPLC grade and were obtained from J. T. Baker (Phillipsburg, NJ, USA). Distilled water was prepared using a Milli-Q purification system (Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA). All standard solutions and mobile phases were passed through a 0.22 µm membrane filter before use.</p></sec><sec id="sec4dot2-molecules-22-01241"><title>4.2. CYP Inhibition Assay</title><p>CYP inhibition assays with human liver microsomes were performed for <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extract at various concentrations (0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 2.5 and 5 mg/mL) and HDC (1, 10, 100, and 500 µM) according to the method used in our previous study [<xref rid="B30-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>] (For the determination of the IC<sub>50</sub> value for CYP2D6, the concentration range of <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extract was extended up to 10 mg/mL). The reaction mixtures consisted of human liver microsomes at a concentration of 0.5 mg/mL; <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extract or HDC standard solution at various concentrations; an NADPH-generating system (NGS, containing 0.1 M glucose-6-phosphate, 10 mg/mL β-NADP+, and glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase) at 1.0 U/mL; and probe substrates (the final concentrations were at ~Km, which were 40 µM phenacetin, 2.5 µM coumarin, 5 µM dextromethorphan, 10 µM diclofenac, 160 µM mephenytoin, 10 µM paclitaxel, and 2.5 µM midazolam) in 200 µL of 0.1 M potassium phosphate buffer (pH 7.4). The reaction mixture was pre-incubated at 37 °C for 5 min without NGS and then further incubated for 30 min with NGS in a water bath.
