</p></sec><sec id="Sec24"><title>Informant consensus factor (ICF)</title><p id="Par174">The Informant Consensus Factor (ICF) analysis aims to provide insight into how local communities in Sheikh District, Sahil Region, Somaliland, perceive and employ MPs for different health conditions. For this study, reported ailments were grouped into nine major categories based on symptoms, affected body systems, and similarities in traditional treatment strategies. The ICF, which reflects the level of agreement among informants regarding plant use for specific ailments, ranged from 0.25 for wound healing to 0.77 for gastrointestinal and parasitic disorders (Table <xref rid="Tab15" ref-type="table">8</xref>). High ICF values suggest strong agreement, indicating that certain illnesses are prevalent and consistently treated with a limited set of culturally trusted plant species [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR4">4</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR89">89</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR189">189</xref>].</p><p id="Par175">Despite its usefulness, several authors have highlighted significant limitations in applying ICF. Leonti and colleagues, for example, caution that consensus values may be influenced by sampling bias, the number of informants, or social dynamics within communities, rather than strictly reflecting therapeutic efficacy [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR19">19</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR20">20</xref>]. Similarly, high ICF values may reflect cultural popularity of a remedy rather than pharmacological effectiveness. Recognizing these caveats, we apply the ICF with caution but still consider it informative, as it provides a culturally contextual overview of plant use patterns and can guide further pharmacological and conservation studies.</p><p id="Par176">Gastrointestinal and parasitic disorders recorded the highest ICF (0.77), highlighting their prominence in Sheikh District. This suggests that oral administration of plant-based remedies is the main treatment route, likely due to ease of preparation, accessibility, and the high prevalence of digestive health challenges. These findings are consistent with ethnobotanical research in Kenya, Uganda, and Ethiopia, where gastrointestinal ailments dominate traditional healthcare priorities, often linked to sanitation limitations, water scarcity, and food insecurity [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR65">65</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR95">95</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR197">197</xref>]. Similar trends are observed in India and Pakistan, where widely cited remedies such as <italic toggle="yes">Citrus limon</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Allium sativum</italic> achieve high consensus due to their digestive and antimicrobial properties [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR6">6</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR198">198</xref>].</p><p id="Par177">Dermatological problems, including ringworm and other skin infections, had the second-highest ICF (0.75), reflecting strong agreement among informants about effective topical treatments. Comparable observations from Tanzania, Nigeria, and China suggest that visible efficacy and cultural familiarity contribute to high consensus for dermatological remedies [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR136">136</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR171">171</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR182">182</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR185">185</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR190">190</xref>]. Similarly, acute and common conditions, such as malaria, arthritis, headache, and diabetes, showed an ICF of 0.72, indicating both prevalence and reliance on specific, culturally validated MPs.</p><p id="Par178">In contrast, wound healing exhibited the lowest ICF (0.25), indicating more variation in treatment approaches among practitioners. This may reflect differences in plant availability, wound type, or individual healer preferences. Low consensus for wound management has been noted in Europe, America, and Asia, where diverse plant remedies are employed for complex or culturally variable injuries, resulting in lower informant agreement [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR85">85</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR86">86</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR97">97</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR137">137</xref>].</p><p id="Par179">Sexual and delivery-related conditions, such as gonorrhea, placenta retention, and sexual dysfunction, showed a moderate ICF of 0.65.
