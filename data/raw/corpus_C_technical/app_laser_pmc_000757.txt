<sup>1</sup>
</xref>, <xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0002" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>2</sup>
</xref> of which 10%–36% of cases occur in oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC).<xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0001" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>1</sup>
</xref>, <xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0002" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>2</sup>
</xref> Consequently, OL is considered one of the most common oral mucosal diseases and premalignant disorders.</p><p>Treatment modalities for OL include scalpel excision, laser therapy, cryotherapy, topical application of bleomycin and vitamin A, and systemic administration of β‐carotene, lycopene, and retinoids.<xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0003" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>3</sup>
</xref>, <xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0004" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>4</sup>
</xref> Photodynamic therapy (PDT) is an attractive treatment for OL, since large or multiple lesions can be treated with low toxicity, minimal invasiveness, and high selectivity.<xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0005" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>5</sup>
</xref>, <xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0006" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>6</sup>
</xref> However, the penetration depth of photosensitizers into the lesion is a significant limitation for PDT efficacy.<xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0007" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>7</sup>
</xref>, <xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0008" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>8</sup>
</xref> Ablative fractional laser (AFL) has been proven to be effective through in vitro and in vivo studies.<xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0009" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>9</sup>
</xref>, <xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0010" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>10</sup>
</xref> AFL is also recommended by the guidelines for topical PDT<xref rid="lsm23534-bib-0011" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>11</sup>
</xref> for enhancing the uptake of topically applied photosensitizers and intensifying PDT response during the treatment of premalignant diseases.
