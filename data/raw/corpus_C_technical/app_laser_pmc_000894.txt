# PMC: optical coherence tomography clinical application — Overview

Ref. <xref rid="r76" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>.</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="JBO-025-040901-g007.jpg"/></fig><p>Quantitative OCT analysis of atherosclerosis was first explored by Levitz et al.,<xref rid="r77" ref-type="bibr"><sup>77</sup></xref> who demonstrated that there is quantitative OCT attenuation contrast in different <italic toggle="yes">ex vivo</italic> arterial tissue components. Two studies by van der Meer et al.<xref rid="r7" ref-type="bibr"><sup>7</sup></xref><sup>,</sup><xref rid="r78" ref-type="bibr"><sup>78</sup></xref> showed that such information could be extracted locally and, thus, be used for differentiating tissue types. The single-scattering model was applied to OCT data acquired from carotid arteries (<inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math236"><mml:mrow><mml:mi>n</mml:mi><mml:mo>=</mml:mo><mml:mn>13</mml:mn></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula>) <italic toggle="yes">ex vivo</italic> with an 800-nm time-domain scanner to quantify the attenuation of various tissue constituents.<xref rid="r78" ref-type="bibr"><sup>78</sup></xref> Differences between tissue types (lipid-rich, fibrous intimal thickening, calcification, and thrombus) were attributed to the different scatterers in these tissues, such as the highly scattering red blood cells, which lead to a high attenuation in the thrombi. These results were extended by imaging data of atherosclerosis with a time-domain system at a wavelength of around 1300 nm.<xref rid="r7" ref-type="bibr"><sup>7</sup></xref> They demonstrated the feasibility of using OCT attenuation to differentiate tissue types. Angle dependence of scattering parameters was investigated by Xu et al.,<xref rid="r8" ref-type="bibr"><sup>8</sup></xref> who demonstrated a strong dependence on imaging orientation for the highly oriented SMCs in the tunica media.</p><p>Van Soest et al.<xref rid="r16" ref-type="bibr"><sup>16</sup></xref> further implemented OCT attenuation imaging of coronary arteries (<inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math237"><mml:mrow><mml:mi>n</mml:mi><mml:mo>=</mml:mo><mml:mn>65</mml:mn></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula>) in a catheter-based OCT system, approximating <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> clinical imaging of coronary arteries. They demonstrated the differentiation of necrotic core and macrophage infiltration (<inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math238"><mml:mrow><mml:msub><mml:mrow><mml:mi>μ</mml:mi></mml:mrow><mml:mrow><mml:mi>OCT</mml:mi></mml:mrow></mml:msub><mml:mo>≥</mml:mo><mml:mn>10</mml:mn><mml:mtext>  </mml:mtext><mml:msup><mml:mrow><mml:mi>mm</mml:mi></mml:mrow><mml:mrow><mml:mo>−</mml:mo><mml:mn>1</mml:mn></mml:mrow></mml:msup></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula>) from calcific and fibrous arterial tissue (<inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math239"><mml:mrow><mml:msub><mml:mi>μ</mml:mi><mml:mi>OCT</mml:mi></mml:msub></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula>: 2 to <inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math240"><mml:mrow><mml:mn>5</mml:mn><mml:mtext>  </mml:mtext><mml:msup><mml:mrow><mml:mi>mm</mml:mi></mml:mrow><mml:mrow><mml:mo>−</mml:mo><mml:mn>1</mml:mn></mml:mrow></mml:msup></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula>) using the OCT attenuation (AFR: <inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math241"><mml:mrow><mml:mo form="prefix">≥</mml:mo><mml:mn>200</mml:mn><mml:mtext>  </mml:mtext><mml:mi>μ</mml:mi><mml:mi mathvariant="normal">m</mml:mi></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula>). <italic toggle="yes">Ex vivo</italic> data were acquired with a time-domain OCT system. <xref ref-type="fig" rid="f8">Figure 8</xref> shows an example of a coronary atherosclerotic lesion with a necrotic core behind a calcified region, identified from histology [<xref ref-type="fig" rid="f8">Fig. 8(b)</xref>] and marked in red in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="f8">Fig. 8(c)</xref>.<xref rid="r16" ref-type="bibr"><sup>16</sup></xref> The necrotic core and calcified region exhibit similar signal strengths in the structural OCT image in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="f8">Fig. 8(a)</xref>. Aided by OCT attenuation imaging in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="f8">Fig. 8(d)</xref>, the necrotic core is better contrasted with the calcified region than in the original OCT image. <italic toggle="yes">In vivo</italic> data from this study, recorded with a prototype swept-source OCT scanner, showed qualitatively and quantitatively similar attenuation patterns.
