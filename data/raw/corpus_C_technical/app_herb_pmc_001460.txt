# PMC: phytochemical bioactive compound anticancer — Overview

id="sec4dot5-molecules-27-06008"><title>4.5. Anti-Inflammatory Activity</title><p>In addition to the properties already mentioned, it has been reported that <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds, particularly broccoli seeds, showed a stronger anti-inflammatory effect higher than carrot and cucumber seeds [<xref rid="B136-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">136</xref>]. Furthermore, <italic toggle="yes">Sinapis alba</italic> seeds exhibited a significant inhibition of the mouse prostatic hyperplasia induced by testosterone propionate and the serum acid phosphatase activity by sinalbin and beta-sitosterol (16.0 and 8.0 mg/kg/day). Sinalbin and beta-sitosterol have anti-androgen and anti-inflammatory activities [<xref rid="B180-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">180</xref>,<xref rid="B181-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">181</xref>]. </p></sec><sec id="sec4dot6-molecules-27-06008"><title>4.6. Regulation of Metabolic Syndrome</title><p>Hypertension, obesity, T2D, and dyslipidemia are common metabolic syndrome disorders prevalent worldwide [<xref rid="B182-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">182</xref>]. The regulatory effects of <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds and their derivatives on metabolic syndrome have been confirmed in numerous studies.</p><p>The seed glucoraphanin diet modulated obesity in HFD-fed mice by regulating lipid metabolism genes and increasing the abundance and diversity of the gut microbiota [<xref rid="B139-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">139</xref>].</p><p>Cold-press rapeseed oil preserved a high level of bioactive compounds (MUFA, PUFA, tocopherols, and phytosterols) that were positively correlated with numerous health benefits, such as hyperlipidemia regulation [<xref rid="B183-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">183</xref>]. Its consumption considerably lessened the oxidative stress, the total cholesterol, oxidized LDL, and LDL cholesterol levels among metabolic syndrome-afflicted men [<xref rid="B184-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">184</xref>]. Additionally, the oral treatment of melatonin-rich mustard seeds in rats downregulated de novo cholesterol production by inhibiting the activity of hepatic 3-hydroxy-3-methyl-glutaryl-CoA (HMG-CoA) reductase and scavenging the hypercholesterolemia-related ROS [<xref rid="B185-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">185</xref>]. Overdosing on paracetamol acetaminophen (APAP) damaged the liver tissue, whereas the in vitro intervention of the extracted antioxidant-rich fraction from mustard seeds and their isolated phytoconstituents (quercetin, catechin, and vitamin E) successfully restored the APAP-induced toxicity in a hepatocellular carcinoma (HepG2) cell line. The hepatoprotective potential of the hydromethanolic mustard seed extract was linked to its ability to maintain the hepatocyte membrane integrity by blocking radical–macromolecule binding and reducing hepatic enzymes levels to normal values [<xref rid="B62-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec4dot7-molecules-27-06008"><title>4.7. Neuroprotective Activity</title><p>Psychological and neurodegenerative disorders include depression, anxiety, autism spectrum disorder (ASD), Alzheimer’s, Huntington’s, Parkinson’s, and schizophrenia which exhibit oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, inflammation, and neural damage [<xref rid="B186-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">186</xref>]. Current research has proven the neuroprotective benefits of <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds and their derivatives in vitro and in humans. The new nitrogenous compound, brassicalkaloid A, and the known alkaloid coixspirolactam C, isolated from <italic toggle="yes">B. napus</italic> L. seeds, displayed an anti-neuroinflammatory effect by suppressing the nitric oxide (NO) production induced by lipopolysaccharide (LPS) in BV-2 cells with IC<sub>50</sub> = 36.6 and IC<sub>50</sub> = 51.0 μM respectively, compared to the positive control NG-monomethyl-L-arginine (L-NMMA) (IC<sub>50</sub> = 17.4 μM) [<xref rid="B102-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>]. A recent clinical study on autism spectrum disorder (ASD) children revealed that the consumption of high-sulforaphane broccoli seed and sprout tablets improved their behavior and social responsiveness and identified changes in urinary metabolites correlated with clinical improvements. Consequently, sulforaphane’s neuroprotective effect probably stemmed from transcription factor (Nrf2) activation [<xref rid="B187-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">187</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds and their derived compounds have proven several health benefits in vitro, in vivo, and in clinical trials. However, additional research is needed to evaluate the potential of other isolated bioactive compounds and to investigate the unrecognized bioactivities and their action mechanisms in vitro and in vivo assays such as cardioprotective, gastroprotective, and renoprotective potentials.</p></sec></sec><sec id="sec5-molecules-27-06008"><title>5.
