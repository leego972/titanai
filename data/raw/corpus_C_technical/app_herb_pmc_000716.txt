Herbal Medicines: Promising Therapeutic for DCM</title><p>Currently, a growing number of preclinical studies provide the evidences that herbal medicines are promising therapy for DCM (Tables <xref ref-type="table" rid="tab2">2</xref> and <xref ref-type="table" rid="tab3">3</xref>). These herbs ameliorate cardiac injury of DCM owing to their antioxidant and anti-inflammation properties, via regulation of NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B and Nrf2 pathways (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig3">Figure 3</xref>).</p><sec id="sec4.1"><title>4.1. Triptolide</title><p>Extracts of Tripterygium wilfordii Hook F are effective in traditional Chinese medicine for the treatment of immune inflammatory diseases including rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, and nephritis. Triptolide as the major active ingredient for Tripterygium wilfordii Hook F exerts immunosuppressive and anti-inflammatory functions [<xref rid="B62" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>] (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig5">Figure 5(a)</xref>). Li et al. [<xref rid="B63" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>] showed that triptolide at 20 <italic toggle="yes">μ</italic>g/kg/d and 100 <italic toggle="yes">μ</italic>g/kg/d attenuated the myocardial fibrosis, cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, and restored the impaired cardiac function in the rat that underwent transverse aortic constriction, associated with decreased production of profibrotic factors TNF-<italic toggle="yes">α</italic> and IL-1<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>. Wen et al. [<xref rid="B64" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>, <xref rid="B65" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>] showed that triptolide (100, 200, or 400 <italic toggle="yes">μ</italic>g/kg/d p.o) administration for 6 weeks improved the left ventricular function of STZ-induced diabetic heart by inhibiting the expression of cardiac p38 MAPK in the upstream of NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B activation; triptolide with dose of 200 <italic toggle="yes">μ</italic>g/kg/d displayed the best improvement. Furthermore, triptolide (20 ng/ml) attenuated inflammation of H9c2 rat cardiac cell exposure to high glucose by inhibiting NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B activation. Guo et al. [<xref rid="B66" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>] showed that the left ventricle pathological structure and function of STZ-induced mice were significantly improved by triptolide (50, 100 or 200 <italic toggle="yes">μ</italic>g/kg/d p.o.) treatment for 8 weeks. The mechanism through which triptolide protects against DCM is involving inhibition of NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B/IL-1<italic toggle="yes">β</italic> and NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B/TNF-<italic toggle="yes">α</italic> cascades.</p></sec><sec id="sec4.2"><title>4.2. Curcumin</title><p>Curcumin, a component of turmeric found in the <italic toggle="yes">Curcuma longa</italic> plant, has been used in treating inflammatory diseases for centuries due to its antioxidant property (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig5">Figure 5(b)</xref>). Soetikno et al. [<xref rid="B67" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>] showed that curcumin exerts antifibrotic effect in amelioration of diabetic nephropathy owing to inhibiting PKC-<italic toggle="yes">α</italic> and PKC-<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>2, as well as the downstream cascade ERK1/2. They also demonstrated that curcumin at dose of 100 mg/kg/d for an 8-week oral administration significantly improved the left ventricular function and attenuated the progression of cardiac remodeling of STZ-induced diabetic rats by downregulating PKC-<italic toggle="yes">α</italic> and PKC-<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>2 and subsequently inactivating p38 MAPK, ERK1/2, and NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B.
