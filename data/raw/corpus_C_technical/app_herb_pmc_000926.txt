Alternative Herbal Agents in Gingivitis and Periodontitis</title><p>Mohammad Rayyan et al. (2018) assessed a 2% mucoadhesive <italic toggle="yes">Grape</italic> Seed Extract (<italic toggle="yes">GSE</italic>) gel in chronic PD [<xref rid="B165-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">165</xref>]. Over six months, the <italic toggle="yes">GSE</italic> group showed significant reductions in GI and PI compared to controls, though PoD improvements were limited. The authors suggested that higher <italic toggle="yes">GSE</italic> concentrations might yield better results [<xref rid="B165-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">165</xref>,<xref rid="B166-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">166</xref>,<xref rid="B167-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">167</xref>,<xref rid="B168-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">168</xref>]. Alefiya Shabbir Mamajiwala et al. (2018) compared CHX and cinnamon extract as ultrasonic coolant agents during scaling. Both significantly reduced bacterial contamination in aerosols, with cinnamon extract demonstrating comparable efficacy without side effects like staining. This positions cinnamon extract as a cost-effective alternative for low-resource settings [<xref rid="B169-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">169</xref>]. Eltay et al. (2021) studied a solution containing 5% <italic toggle="yes">Punica granatum</italic> extract as a supplement to non-surgical therapy for chronic gingivitis. Over four weeks, <italic toggle="yes">Punica granatum</italic> significantly reduced PI, GI, and IL-1β levels compared to placebo, demonstrating potent anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial properties without adverse effects. These findings support its potential for safe and natural periodontal therapy [<xref rid="B170-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">170</xref>]. Takkella et al. (2021) compared mouthwashes containing <italic toggle="yes">Punica granatum</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Lawsonia inermis</italic>. Both reduced salivary enzymes linked to inflammation and tissue damage, with <italic toggle="yes">punica granatum</italic> showing superior efficacy due to its bioactive compounds promoting collagen synthesis and reducing bacterial proliferation. The study recommended herbal mouthwashes as safe alternatives to alcohol-based formulations [<xref rid="B58-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec4dot5-nutrients-17-00762"><title>4.5. Propolis and Other Natural Extracts</title><p>Jaber Yaghini et al. (2019) compared <italic toggle="yes">aloe vera camellia sinensis</italic>, matrica, and CHX mouthwashes in plaque-induced gingivitis. <italic toggle="yes">Aloe vera camellia sinensis</italic> was as effective as CHX in reducing plaque and gingival inflammation while causing less dental staining. The findings highlight its promise as an alternative to conventional chemical mouthwashes [<xref rid="B171-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">171</xref>]. Hendrik Jünger et al. (2020) investigated a sage-based mouthwash in elderly patients receiving supportive periodontal care. Although improvements in gingival inflammation were observed, they were not statistically significant compared to placebo. The study suggested that the antimicrobial properties of alcohol and mint oil in the placebo might have confounded results.
