Incubation Time for Photosensitizing Agents</title><p>Sunlight exposure should be initiated no longer than 30 min after applying MAL cream to avoid a higher risk of increased pain [<xref rid="B46-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>]. Increased pain is supposed to result from a greater accumulation of PpIX before sun exposure [<xref rid="B55-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>]. However, a longer incubation time may increase MAL absorption in AK without curettage [<xref rid="B56-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot1dot5-molecules-25-05195"><title>5.1.5. Sunlight Exposure</title><p>To generate sufficient PpIX in AK, the European consensus recommended two hours of daylight exposure without interruption [<xref rid="B45-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>]. However, sunlight exposure longer than two hours is not recommended due to a higher risk of sunburn. The photosensitizing agents are then washed completely. The patients are educated to stay indoors and to apply sunscreen to avoid further sun damage. O’Mahoney et al. reported dosimetry methods measuring the light exposure in dPDT. The two devices used to measure spectral irradiance were the scanning spectroradiometer and the array spectrometer [<xref rid="B22-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>,<xref rid="B39-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>]. However, spectrometers are not readily available and can be replaced by handheld broadband radiometers and luxmeters [<xref rid="B22-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>]. The threshold for an effective dPDT dose was estimated to be 8 J/cm<sup>2</sup> for treatment applied at the irradiance of 130 W/m<sup>2</sup> [<xref rid="B22-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>]. Wiegell et al. reviewed dosimetry in dPDT and cited that the minimal threshold PpIX-effective light dose was 3.5 J cm<sup>−2</sup> when monitored with a wristwatch device [<xref rid="B22-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>,<xref rid="B47-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>]. Notably, measurements on the wrist should be considered as only 50% of the light dose measured on the scalp [<xref rid="B22-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot1dot6-molecules-25-05195"><title>5.1.6. Treatment Sessions/Follow-Up</title><p>The number of treatment sessions and the intervals between treatments vary in different studies [<xref rid="B46-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>]. Some studies performed one-time dPDT on grade 1 AKs, and two times on grade 2 and 3 AKs [<xref rid="B46-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>,<xref rid="B58-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>]. An intrapatient randomized trial involving 31 patients showed no superiority of two-treatment sessions to single sessions of MAL dPDT for AKs of the face and scalp [<xref rid="B59-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>]. The intervals between treatments varied from one day to one month [<xref rid="B46-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>]. Patients were followed up in clinics for at least half a year in case of residual or recurrent AK. Long-term follow-up for 12 months revealed similar efficacy and recurrence rates in AK on the face and the scalp with dPDT and cPDT [<xref rid="B50-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>].</p><p>Bernad et al. studied whether repeated treatments of field cancerization with dPDT were effective in preventing new AK and keratinous carcinoma in 24 patients received organ transplants [<xref rid="B60-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>]. The randomized, intrasubject controlled, evaluator-blind, split-face and/or scalp dPDT was compared to the lesion-directed cryotherapy control side. The dPDT treated side showed lower means of new lesions compared to the control side at three months, nine months and 15 months, and nonsignificance at 21 months. The results suggested the potential of repeated dPDT to prevent new AK and keratinous carcinoma in high-risk patients.</p></sec></sec><sec id="sec5dot2-molecules-25-05195"><title>5.2. Actinic Cheilitis</title><p>Actinic cheilitis (AC) is a precancerous disease on the vermilion border caused by long term sunlight exposure, with a predilection of lower lip involvement. The risk of progression to lip SCC has been reported [<xref rid="B61-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>]. Some case reports showed success in treating AC with cPDT [<xref rid="B62-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>,<xref rid="B63-molecules-25-05195" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>]. However, local anesthesia is usually needed for cPDT because the lip is full of nerve endings. Levi et al. reported complete remission of AC in 10 of 11 patients after a median of two sessions of dPDT.
