However, ALA has been reported to have poor penetration, which causes a reduced effect of PDT<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR15">15</xref></sup>. A previous study showed a CR rate of only 33.33% when using PDT with topically administered ALA to treat OL patients<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR1">1</xref></sup>. Therefore, pretreatment is clinically required to promote PDT efficacy by producing an alternative pathway to accelerate the absorption of photosensitizer precursors.</p><p id="Par24">Currently, CO<sub>2</sub> laser is usually used to pretreat malignant lesions for vaporization with the removal of stratum corneum infractions and partitions<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR17">17</xref></sup>. The reported CR to ALA-PDT combined with CO<sub>2</sub> laser was 78.3%, and the PR rate was 21.7%. The recurrence rate was 17.4% over a follow-up period of 12 months<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR15">15</xref></sup>. However, the CO<sub>2</sub> laser can cause thermal side effects<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR18">18</xref></sup> and induce dysplastic-like epithelial artifacts<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR19">19</xref></sup>. In the study, we applied YSGG pretreatment and found a response of 84.51% (60/71) CR and 15.49% (11/71) PR and recurrence of 12.68%. These results demonstrate that YSGG-PDT is more effective than CO<sub>2</sub> laser-PDT. Moreover, the pain and local ulcer observed in the study are also notably minimized and rapidly recovered compared with the use of other lasers, probably for the energy from YSGG is absorbed effectively by tissue and water, with minimal tissue charring and collateral thermal damage.</p><p id="Par25">In summary, our study shows that combining PDT with YSGG is effective in the treatment of OL, which is a promising treatment method for achieving excellent outcomes for patients.</p></sec><sec id="Sec19"><title>Brush biopsy and DNA aneuploidy detection</title><p id="Par26">A considerable number of OSCC lesions can evolve from precancerous lesions, such as OL and oral submucosal fibrosis (OSF) lesions. Therefore, early identification of high-risk OPMDs and interventions in the precancerous stage related to OSCC can reduce the morbidity and mortality of OSCC and save treatment costs<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR20">20</xref></sup>. The current histological evaluation standard is an important method for confirming the diagnosis, and there are no auxiliary tests to replace it. However, biopsy is an aggressive technique with certain surgical implications and high technical requirements for professionals; this process may also have psychological effects on the majority of patients<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR21">21</xref></sup>. In contrast, cytology shows promise. Brush biopsy examination is simple and fast<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR21">21</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR23">23</xref></sup>. Moreover, the results are similar to those of histological examination. Many studies have demonstrated that an increased nuclear size and a reduced cytoplasmic size are helpful indicators of early malignant transformation<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR24">24</xref></sup>.
