Comparative Antibacterial Efficacy</title><p>The antibacterial activity of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> compares favorably with that of other medicinal plants. In the study by Titova et al. [<xref rid="B17-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>], extracts derived from <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> suspension cultures grown in bubble-type bioreactors exhibited minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) values ranging from 250 to 2000 µg·mL<sup>−1</sup>, markedly lower than the MICs of 4000 µg·mL<sup>−1</sup> reported for greenhouse-derived leaf extracts. These values also outperform those of <italic toggle="yes">Thymus vulgaris</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Syzygium aromaticum</italic>, which exhibited MICs of approximately 3150 µg·mL<sup>−1</sup> against <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus cereus</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Rosmarinus officinalis</italic>, which required &gt;1000 µg·mL<sup>−1</sup> to inhibit <italic toggle="yes">E. coli</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic> [<xref rid="B166-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">166</xref>].</p><p>In contrast, <italic toggle="yes">Panax notoginseng</italic>, a related Araliaceae species, displayed more limited antibacterial activity, primarily targeting <italic toggle="yes">Streptococcus pyogenes</italic>, with diminished efficacy against Gram-negative bacteria [<xref rid="B167-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">167</xref>,<xref rid="B168-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">168</xref>].</p><p>These comparisons underscore the broad-spectrum potential of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, particularly due to its polyacetylene-rich composition and favorable safety profile.</p></sec><sec id="sec6dot4dot3-molecules-30-03460"><title>6.4.3. Evidence of Antibacterial Activity</title><p>Several studies have confirmed the antibacterial efficacy of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> against clinically relevant pathogens, including <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic> (including methicillin-resistant <italic toggle="yes">S. aureus</italic>, MRSA), <italic toggle="yes">E. coli</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Pseudomonas aeruginosa</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Salmonella typhi</italic> [<xref rid="B16-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>,<xref rid="B17-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>,<xref rid="B94-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>]. In particular, Titova et al. [<xref rid="B17-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>] reported superior activity of extracts obtained from in vitro suspension cultures, which produced significantly lower MICs than those derived from field-grown material.</p><p>Polyacetylene-rich fractions were consistently more effective than triterpenoid saponin fractions [<xref rid="B10-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>], suggesting that lipophilic compounds may better penetrate bacterial membranes or disrupt vital functions. Importantly, these extracts demonstrated no acute toxicity in murine models [<xref rid="B10-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>], supporting their potential safety as natural antimicrobial agents.</p></sec><sec id="sec6dot4dot4-molecules-30-03460"><title>6.4.4. Mechanisms of Antibacterial Action</title><p>The antibacterial effects of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> are believed to result from multiple synergistic mechanisms:<list list-type="bullet"><list-item><p>Disruption of bacterial membrane integrity—both saponins and polyacetylenes can increase membrane permeability, leading to osmotic imbalance and cell lysis [<xref rid="B165-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">165</xref>,<xref rid="B166-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">166</xref>,<xref rid="B167-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">167</xref>,<xref rid="B168-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">168</xref>].</p></list-item><list-item><p>Inhibition of protein and peptidoglycan biosynthesis—certain constituents may bind to ribosomal subunits or enzymes that are critical for bacterial cell wall synthesis, disrupting growth and replication [<xref rid="B17-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>].</p></list-item><list-item><p>Alteration of membrane potential and intracellular pH—some extracts interfere with bacterial membrane electrochemical gradients, leading to acidification and metabolic dysfunction [<xref rid="B165-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">165</xref>].</p></list-item><list-item><p>Inhibition of biofilm formation—compounds such as falcarinol and flavonoids impair bacterial adhesion and prevent the establishment of protective biofilm matrices [<xref rid="B17-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>,<xref rid="B94-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>].</p></list-item></list></p><p>These multitarget mechanisms may enhance effectiveness against antibiotic-resistant strains and reduce the likelihood of resistance development.
