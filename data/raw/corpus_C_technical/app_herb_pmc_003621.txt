During the visits, the first author carried out interviews with a total of 196 market vendors. After explaining the aim of the study, all vendors of medicinal plants from the 33 traditional markets were asked to participate in the research. The interviewed vendors were eighteen years or older; also, they were “mestizos” (95 %) and Saraguro indigenous people (5 %), and the large majority consisted of women (97 %).</p><p>Medicinal plants were bought from each vendor, and interviews were structured as ethnobotanical questionnaires in Spanish, being conducted by the main author with the 196 market vendors. In the field research, the first author respected the vendors who preferred to remain anonymous. The questionnaires aimed to record the specimens’ information on the following: vernacular names, medicinal uses, plant morphological structures sold, and therapeutic prescriptions. All the vendors who decided to collaborate were interviewed according to mutually agreed conditions and under Ecuador’s rights, especially with regards to the Convention on Biological Diversity (CDB) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR94">94</xref>].</p><p>This research was conducted according to the code of ethics of the International Society for Ethnobiology (ISE) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR95">95</xref>], which is also endorsed by the Society for Economic Botany (SEB). The Principle of Respect, numbered 9 in the code, recognizes the necessity for researchers to respect the integrity, morality, and spirituality of the culture, traditions, and relationships of indigenous people, traditional societies, and local communities within their worlds.</p></sec><sec id="Sec6"><title>Voucher collection and nomenclature</title><p>The nomenclature of plant families, genera, and species follows the Catalogue of Vascular Plants of Ecuador [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR96">96</xref>]. It was also compared to the TROPICOS database [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR97">97</xref>]. The 160 species were identified using the available volumes of the Flora of Ecuador [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR98">98</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR101">101</xref>] and reference material in the herbaria of the “Universidad Técnica Particular de Loja” (HUTPL) and “Universidad Nacional de Loja” (LOJA). The specimens were registered under the collection series FT (Fani Tinitana), and vouchers were deposited at HUTPL. The collection of botanical specimens sold in the 33 traditional markets authorized the Ecuadorian Ministry of the Environment (Ministerio del Ambiente del Ecuador N° 001-2013-IC-FLO-DPAP-MAE).</p></sec><sec id="Sec7"><title>Quantitative analysis</title><p>All the local therapeutic uses of medicinal plants were grouped in 12 medical categories (Table <xref rid="Tab2" ref-type="table">2</xref>), which were adapted from the catalogue of International Classification of Diseases made by the WHO [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR102">102</xref>]. In this research, each category proposed by the WHO allows grouping and systematizing the data related to ‘illness’ and ‘disease’ as well as to compare the results among other regional and international studies related to the markets’ ethnobotany [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR102">102</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR103">103</xref>]. Additionally, WHO recognizes in each medical category the health practice systems of traditional populations [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR46">46</xref>].
