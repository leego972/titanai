Introduction</title><p>Adaptogens belong to a category of physiologically active compounds or botanicals that trigger physiological processes of adaptability to stressors [<xref rid="B1-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B2-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="B3-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>]. More than 110 medicinal plants were found to exhibit adaptogenic activity [<xref rid="B2-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>]. The number of plants reported as being adaptogenic has increased exponentially during the past decades. However, only a few of them comply with the most characteristic features, as shown in <xref rid="pharmaceuticals-18-01346-t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref>, an upgrade from [<xref rid="B2-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>].</p><p>In the first definition of adaptogens dated 1958, a toxicologist, Lasarev, focused on the so-called “state of non-specific resistance of organism” [<xref rid="B4-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B5-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>] that is associated with the notions of “adaptability” [<xref rid="B6-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="B7-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>] defined by Georges Cunghilaim in 1943 and “stress”, Cannon [<xref rid="B8-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>] and Hans Selye [<xref rid="B9-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>,<xref rid="B10-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>].
