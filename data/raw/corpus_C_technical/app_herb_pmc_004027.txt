<list list-type="bullet"><list-item><p id="Par128">Ashwagandha as an adaptogen.</p></list-item></list>
</p><p id="Par129">Through a number of mechanisms of action connected to the homeostatic preservation of the hypothalamicpituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis and the regulation of important mediators of the stress response, ashwagandha is regarded as an adaptogen that aids in the restoration of homeostasis by counteracting external stimuli as nonspecific regulators [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR158">158</xref>]. The ideal adaptogen should minimize the negative effects of stress, perform safely and beneficially even when administered in doses greater than necessary, and be devoid of undesirable side effects, such as not impairing bodily functions beyond what is necessary [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR64">64</xref>]. Researchers have hypothesized that Ashwagandha may reduce cortisol levels, hence reducing the HPA axis activity. Particularly, the effect of WS on GABA and serotonergic pathways may reduce the activation of the HPA axis, diminishing the secretion of corticotropin-releasing hormone and adrenocorticotropic hormone from the hypothalamus and pituitary, respectively, resulting in lower levels of cortisol released from the adrenal cortex [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR159">159</xref>]. These findings are uncertain in populations with low cortisol levels [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR160">160</xref>], but they appear to exist in populations with high or moderate levels of cortisol [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR6">6</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR28">28</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR138">138</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR161">161</xref>]. However, although this offers a reasonable mechanistic explanation for how Ashwagandha may elevate testosterone levels through modulation of the HPA axis and reduction of cortisol current evidence does not definitively confirm that this is the predominant pathway for all individuals [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR160">160</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR162">162</xref>]. The association tends to be stronger at higher dosages and is particularly evident in individuals who are experiencing substantial stress [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR160">160</xref>]. As an alternative, WS supplementation may also alter the activity of critical enzymes in physiological processes linked to the creation of testosterone, increasing levels in people with low cortisol levels [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR11">11</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR160">160</xref>], but not in people with high cortisol levels [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>]. Because of their broad biological activities, the anti-inflammatory and Odant properties of aqueous, organic, and hydroalcoholic extracts of <italic toggle="yes">Withania somnifera</italic> may also contribute to their impact on testosterone levels [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR163">163</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR165">165</xref>]. These findings suggest the use of ashwagandha as an adaptogen to balance and rebalance biomarker levels and produce hormonal level balances.</p><p id="Par130">
