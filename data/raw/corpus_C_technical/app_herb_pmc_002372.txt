While α-amylase catalyzes the breakdown of starch into oligosaccharides, its inhibition generally requires a direct and accessible interaction of active compounds with the enzyme’s catalytic site<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR71">71</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR72">72</xref></sup>. Encapsulation with cyclodextrins likely masked some of these functional groups, reducing the inhibitory effect compared to the free extracts. In contrast, α-glucosidase, which catalyzes the hydrolysis of disaccharides into glucose, was strongly inhibited across all samples, and encapsulation—particularly with β-CD—appeared to enhance this activity by improving the solubility and stability of phenolic compounds, thereby increasing their availability to the enzyme<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR71">71</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR72">72</xref></sup>. These findings indicate that cyclodextrins can modulate enzyme inhibition in a mechanism-dependent manner, reducing activity when access to catalytic sites is hindered (α-amylase) but enhancing it when solubility and stability favor enzyme binding (α-glucosidase).</p><p id="Par79">The enzyme inhibition results obtained in this study are in line with previous reports on various <italic toggle="yes">Limonium</italic> species. In particular, the relatively low α-amylase and moderate α-glucosidase inhibitory activities observed in <italic toggle="yes">L. bellidifolium</italic> agree with studies on <italic toggle="yes">L. contortirameum</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">L. virgatum</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">L. axillare</italic>, which also demonstrated dual enzyme inhibition but with moderate potency (IC₅₀ values ranging from 0.6 to 1.2 mg/mL for α-amylase and lower values for α-glucosidase)<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR73">73</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR74">74</xref></sup>.</p><p id="Par80">Moreover, the stronger α-glucosidase inhibition compared to α-amylase observed in our samples, especially in β-CD encapsulated aerial parts, supports the selective inhibitory behavior reported for <italic toggle="yes">L. spathulatum</italic>, whose hydroethanolic extract showed potent α-glucosidase inhibition (IC₅₀ = 0.04 mg/mL), even stronger than that of the standard drug acarbose<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR67">67</xref></sup>. This suggests that <italic toggle="yes">Limonium</italic> species may selectively inhibit glucosidase, which is particularly valuable in managing postprandial hyperglycemia<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR67">67</xref></sup>.</p><p id="Par81">The tyrosinase inhibitory activities of <italic toggle="yes">L. bellidifolium</italic> extracts were notably high in both pure and encapsulated forms. The pure extracts exhibited the strongest activities, with the root extract showing the highest inhibition, followed closely by the aerial part extract. Among the encapsulated samples, the root extract encapsulated with β-cyclodextrin at 25 °C showed the highest activity, followed by the β-CD encapsulated aerial part at 40 °C. The α-CD encapsulated root and aerial part extracts displayed lower inhibition respectively. These findings demonstrate that <italic toggle="yes">L. bellidifolium</italic> possesses strong tyrosinase inhibitory potential, particularly in its pure form. However, encapsulation with cyclodextrins, especially α-CD, appears to reduce this activity. The root extracts consistently exhibited higher inhibition than the aerial parts, suggesting that root-derived compounds may play a more prominent role in tyrosinase inhibition.</p><p id="Par82">The tyrosinase inhibition findings in our study are also supported by existing literature. Several <italic toggle="yes">Limonium</italic> species, including <italic toggle="yes">L. tetragonum</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">L. boitardii</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">L. cercinense</italic>, have been reported to exhibit strong tyrosinase inhibitory activity due to their high phenolic and flavonoid content<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR75">75</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR76">76</xref></sup>. Notably, isolated flavonoid glycosides such as myricetin 3-galactoside and quercetin 3-O-β-galactopyranoside from <italic toggle="yes">L. tetragonum</italic> have shown effective inhibition of tyrosinase and melanogenesis<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR75">75</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR76">76</xref></sup>.
