Interdisciplinary research, including drug development initiatives based on diverse bioactivities, is essential to fully unlock the potential of modern medicine (<xref rid="t0002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>).</p><sec id="s0008-s2001"><title>Antimicrobial Activity</title><p>Indonesia’s rich biodiversity presents an array of plant species that have potential as alternative therapeutic agents. <italic toggle="yes">P. suaveolens</italic> contains a variety of phytochemicals, including flavonoids, steroids, terpenoids, alkaloids, saponins, tannins, and essential oils, with antibacterial and antimicrobial properties.<xref rid="cit0077" ref-type="bibr">77</xref> Antimicrobial agents function by inhibiting or eliminating the growth of pathogenic microorganisms, such as bacteria, fungi, viruses, and protozoa. These agents are widely used in clinical medicine, public health, sanitation, and agriculture to prevent and control infections. Research on <italic toggle="yes">P. suaveolens</italic> has shown that extracts from its outer shell possess potent antimicrobial activity against <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic> (<italic toggle="yes">S. aureus), Escherichia coli</italic> (<italic toggle="yes">E. coli</italic>), and <italic toggle="yes">Candida albicans</italic> (<italic toggle="yes">C. albicans</italic>). The efficacy of the extracts was confirmed by agar diffusion assays. Distinct zones of inhibition were observed around the treatment wells in contrast to the DMSO-treated negative control without inhibitory activity. The extract produced inhibition zones measuring between 16.17 and 18.17 mm against <italic toggle="yes">S. aureus</italic>, showing strong antibacterial potency.<xref rid="cit0016" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>,<xref rid="cit0031" ref-type="bibr">31</xref> Statistical analyses confirmed a concentration-dependent response, and the activity was attributed to bioactive constituents such as alkaloids, tannins, saponins, and steroids.</p><p>Similar inhibitory effects were observed against <italic toggle="yes">E. coli</italic>, with the suppression of bacterial proliferation recorded at increasing concentrations of 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100%.<xref rid="cit0031" ref-type="bibr">31</xref> Furthermore, the extract exhibited strong antifungal activity against <italic toggle="yes">C. albicans</italic>, thereby emphasizing its broad-spectrum antimicrobial potential. GC-MS analysis of <italic toggle="yes">P. suaveolens</italic> reported over 50 phytochemical constituents, including lupan-3-yl acetate, 1,3,4,5-tetrahydroxycyclohexanecarboxylic acid, DL-threitol, and vitamin E, which are known for their antimicrobial activity by disrupting microbial growth and metabolism.<xref rid="cit0046" ref-type="bibr">46</xref> These compounds are summarized in <xref rid="t0004" ref-type="table">Table 4</xref>, which supports their potential use as natural antimicrobial agents. The antimicrobial activity <italic toggle="yes">of P. suaveolens</italic> is mediated by a complex array of bioactive compounds. These constituents act synergistically to disrupt the bacterial membrane integrity, increase membrane permeability, and induce cell lysis. Additionally, many compounds interfere with bacterial protein biosynthesis. For example, flavonoids can bind to bacterial enzymes and structural proteins, impairing critical cellular functions, and inhibiting proliferation. Alkaloids exert their effects by targeting ribosomal subunits and inhibiting translation of essential proteins. The dual-action membrane disruption and inhibition of protein synthesis are shown in <xref rid="f0003" ref-type="fig">Figure 3</xref> and collectively show the pharmacological relevance of <italic toggle="yes">P. suaveolens</italic> as an antimicrobial agent.
