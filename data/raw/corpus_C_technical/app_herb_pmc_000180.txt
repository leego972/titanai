A special focus has been placed on the relationships among the tested materials, mechanisms of action, techniques, and processing conditions.</p></sec><sec id="s0010"><label>2</label><title>Herbs and spices definition</title><p id="p0045">Herbs and spices are botanical raw materials that have active components that could be used in pharmaceutics, cosmetics, food additives, and health supplements <xref rid="b0055" ref-type="bibr">[11]</xref>. Herbs are used by complementary medicine therapists to treat various diseases. Many plant-based poly or mono herbal formulas are used for various non-communicable diseases like cardiovascular diseases and cancers <xref rid="b0060" ref-type="bibr">[12]</xref>. The words “herbs” and “spices” have many definitions but the most common are those which consider herbs to be obtained from the green parts of a plant, for example stems and leaves such as tea, mints, and thymus. On the other hand, spices are produced from other structures such as seeds, flowers, fruits, barks, or roots, for example garlic, turmeric, cinnamon, and black pepper <xref rid="b0065" ref-type="bibr">[13]</xref>, <xref rid="b0070" ref-type="bibr">[14]</xref>, <xref rid="b0075" ref-type="bibr">[15]</xref>. These plant materials have high contents of phytochemicals; including phenolic, carotenoid, flavonoid, and volatile components that exhibit antimicrobial, antioxidant, and other biological activities [<xref rid="bib626" ref-type="bibr">[16]</xref>, <xref rid="b0080" ref-type="bibr">[17]</xref>, <xref rid="b0085" ref-type="bibr">[18]</xref>]. The long historical use of herbs and spices document their safe use, thus they are generally recognized as safe (GRAS) materials. This is a great advantage to use herbs and spices as natural alternatives to chemical additives [<xref rid="b0090" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="s0015"><label>3</label><title>Ultrasound technology</title><p id="p0050">Ultrasound is defined as any mechanical sound waves at frequencies &gt;20 kHz, which is beyond the human hearing threshold. Compression and expansion waves generated by US lead to positive and negative pressures that can create cavities in the treated materials [<xref rid="b0095" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>]. The formed cavities release high energy and generate high-pressure and localized extremely high temperatures [<xref rid="b0100" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>]. The produced shock wave energy hydrolyzes water into free radicals (H· and ·OH) and forms hydrogen peroxide (H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub>) as a byproduct. The main parameters that control the effectiveness of the treatment are the US velocity, attenuation, and acoustic impedance [<xref rid="b0105" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>].</p><sec id="s0020"><label>3.1</label><title>Ultrasound generation and treatments</title><p id="p0055">The US waves are formed from the conversion of mechanical oscillation of a high-frequency electrical field, which has an elastic effect (stretching and compressing) on the deformation of ferroelectric materials [<xref rid="b0110" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>]. For materials' physicochemical measurement, the US transducer emits pulses against one side of the material being analyzed and these waves are transmitted into the material. The time that the pulses take to pass through the material and return to a detector is measured and the echo velocity in the material is calculated [<xref rid="b0115" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>]. This phenomenon is affected by several factors, such as the chemical composition and the structural configuration of the material.</p><p id="p0060">Ultrasound can be categorized according to the used frequency (kHz) and the generated energy intensity power (W). Also, it could be categorized based on sound intensity (W/m<sup>2</sup>) or sound energy density (Ws/m<sup>3</sup>) [<xref rid="b0110" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>]. According to the used frequency, it can be categorized into high and low frequencies. High-frequency and intensity US are those using frequency higher than 100 kHz, and intensity from 10 to 1000 W/cm<sup>2</sup>, whereas the low-frequency and intensity US uses frequency lower than 100 kHz, and intensity lower than 3 W/cm<sup>2</sup> [<xref rid="b0120" ref-type="bibr">[25]</xref>, <xref rid="b0125" ref-type="bibr">[26]</xref>].</p><p id="p0065">Different combinations of frequency and intensity power are used for different US applications. High-frequency and intensity ultrasound combination is generally used for non-food applications such as soft tissue surgery, diagnostic imaging, or drug delivery [<xref rid="bib627" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>].
