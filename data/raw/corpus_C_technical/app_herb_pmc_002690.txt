The other use of cannabis in cancer is related to its ability to increase appetite, a phenomenon very useful in cancer patients where appetite is normally suppressed. The list of plants obtained and their traditional uses can be compared with what came out of other studies that concentrated on the cytotoxicity of certain medicinal plant extracts. For example, <italic toggle="yes">Kigelia africana</italic> (Lam.) Benth.<bold><italic toggle="yes">,</italic></bold> highlighted by some practitioners in this study’ has been found from other studies to be useful for skin ailments (eczema, psoriasis, fungal infection, boils), diarrhoea, haemorrhoids, fever, malaria, diabetes as well as having anti-cancer properties [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>]. The bioactive constituents found to be present in all the plant parts include iridoids, naphthoquinones, flavonoids, terpenes and pheneylethanoglycosides [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR65">65</xref>]. The stem bark and fruit extracts were found to be cytotoxic against cancer cell lines [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>]. Another study revealed anticancer activity of the fruit extract against cervical and colorectal cancer cell lines [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR30">30</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Hibiscus sabdariffa</italic> L. and <italic toggle="yes">Pseudolachnostylis maprouneifolia</italic> Pax [Agg]. have been reported for their use as cytotoxic agents, among other activities [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR32">32</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR56">56</xref>]. The high polyphenol content in dried leaf extract of <italic toggle="yes">Hibiscus sabdariffa</italic> L. have been linked to its activity against leukaemia and gastric carcinoma [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR57">57</xref>]. Studies have also shown <italic toggle="yes">Hibiscus sabdariffa</italic> L. leaf extract-induced apoptosis in human prostate cancer cells [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR58">58</xref>]. Other medicinal plants that have been reported for their actual or potential anti-cancer activities include <italic toggle="yes">Hypoxis hemerocallidea</italic> Fisch.&amp;C.A.Mey [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR66">66</xref>], [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR67">67</xref>]., <italic toggle="yes">Sutherlandia frutescens</italic> (Lam.)R.BR [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR50">50</xref>]. as well as <italic toggle="yes">Solanum incanum</italic> L. [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR54">54</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Hypoxis hemerocallidea</italic> Fisch.&amp;C.A.Mey.<italic toggle="yes">,</italic> also referred to as the miracle plant [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR68">68</xref>], apart from other documented activities such as hypoglycaemic and antioxidant properties [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR41">41</xref>]; has been shown to have activity against cancerous and premalignant cancer cells [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR42">42</xref>]. The herbal extract of <italic toggle="yes">Solanum incanum</italic> L. has been found to possess anticancer, antinociceptive, antipyretic, antispasmolytic, oretic, hypoglycemic and antimicrobial activities [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR54">54</xref>]. Notable active components thought to be responsible for the anticancer activity are glycoalkaloids; which include solamargine, solasodine and solasomine. Solarmagine was found to act by inducing cancer cells apoptosis [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR55">55</xref>]. Cancer bush, <italic toggle="yes">Sutherlandia frutescens</italic> (L.)R.Br<italic toggle="yes">,</italic> is native to South Africa and has been traditionally used in the treatment and prophylaxis of cancer [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR43">43</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR49">49</xref>].
