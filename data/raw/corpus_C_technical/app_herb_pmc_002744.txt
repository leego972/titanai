In our study, the informant consensus of medicinal plant usage with the Monpa ethnic group resulted in F<sub>IC </sub>factors ranging from 0.17 to 0.56 per illness category (Table <xref ref-type="table" rid="T1">1</xref>). The consensus analysis revealed that the category dermatological disorders have the highest F<sub>IC </sub>factor of 0.56 and the gastro-intestinal diseases have intermediate F<sub>IC </sub>(0.43), indicating greater homogeneity among informants. The highest F<sub>IC </sub>value for dermatological and gastro-intestinal diseases categories could be related to the high occurrence of skin-related and gastritis problems in the study area. The F<sub>IC </sub>of local knowledge for disease treatment depended on the availability of plant species and the occurrence of diseases in the study area. In the literature, high informant consensus (F<sub>IC </sub>0.875) was also recorded among the snakebite healers of Kamba in Africa [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B33">33</xref>] and treating 'mich' or febrile diseases (F<sub>IC </sub>0.80) among Northwestern Ethiopia [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B34">34</xref>]. The fidelity value (FL) of a plant species for a specific disease in the study area varied between 30 and 100%. The maximum FL of 100% expressed by <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia nilagirica</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Azadirachta indica</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Allium sativum</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Cannabis sativa</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Clerodendrum colebrookianum</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Gymnocladus assamicus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Lindera neesiana</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Ocimum sanctum</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Psidium guajava</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Saccharum officinarum</italic>, for wound healing and scabies, stomach disorder and diarrhea, bone fracture, diarrhea in cattle, high blood pressure, soap and ethno-veterinary, intestinal worms, wounds, diarrhea, and jaundice, respectively, indicated the 100% choice of most healers or plant practitioners for treating such diseases. The literature search on ethnopharmacological use showed that many of the species of plants with 100% FL were used to treat ailments in other parts of the world (See Table <xref ref-type="table" rid="T3">3</xref>). A specific example includes <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia nilagirica </italic>[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B35">35</xref>], <italic toggle="yes">Azadirachta indica </italic>[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B36">36</xref>], <italic toggle="yes">Allium sativum </italic>[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B37">37</xref>], <italic toggle="yes">Clerodendrum colebrookianum </italic>[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B38">38</xref>-<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B40">40</xref>], <italic toggle="yes">Gymnocladus assamicus </italic>[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B41">41</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B42">42</xref>]<italic toggle="yes">Lindera neesiana </italic>[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B43">43</xref>], <italic toggle="yes">Ocimum sanctum </italic>[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B44">44</xref>], <italic toggle="yes">Psidium guajava </italic>[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B45">45</xref>-<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B47">47</xref>], <italic toggle="yes">Momordica charantia </italic>[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B48">48</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B49">49</xref>], <italic toggle="yes">and Rhododendron arboreum </italic>[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B50">50</xref>]. However, the pharmacological properties of an individual plant can be significantly altered in the presence of other plant species in compound medicines. <italic toggle="yes">Psidium guajava </italic>is one of the most recorded plant species used to treat diarrhoea in developed countries [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B45">45</xref>]. On the other hand, the lowest FL of 30% indicated less preferred species by the traditional healers for treating specific ailment.
