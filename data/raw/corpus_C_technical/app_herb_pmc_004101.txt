Studies in honeybees have demonstrated that <italic toggle="yes">E. senticosus</italic> root extract (0.4 mg/mL) is one of the most effective adaptogens in enhancing immune resistance and improving survival in nosemosis [<xref rid="B44-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>].</p><p>Clinically, <italic toggle="yes">E. senticosus</italic> reduces symptoms of asthenia by 25–40% compared with placebo, improves quality of life, and lowers serum cortisol levels after 4–8 weeks of supplementation [<xref rid="B43-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>]. Panax ginseng C.A. Meyer</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Panax ginseng</italic>, also known as Korean or Asian ginseng, is one of the most important adaptogens used in Traditional Chinese Medicine for more than 2000 years to strengthen Qi, enhance vitality, and treat stress-related disorders (<xref rid="nutrients-18-00931-f005" ref-type="fig">Figure 5</xref>) [<xref rid="B50-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Panax ginseng</italic> contains ginsenosides (triterpenoid saponins) as its principal bioactive constituents responsible for adaptogenic activity. More than 40 different ginsenosides have been identified, including Rb1, Rg1, Rg3, Rc, Rd, and Re [<xref rid="B51-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>,<xref rid="B52-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>,<xref rid="B53-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>]. Ginsenosides are classified into two main groups: protopanaxadiol (PPD; e.g., Rb1, Rc, Rd) and protopanaxatriol (PPT; e.g., Rg1, Re), which exhibit distinct and sometimes opposing pharmacological effects [<xref rid="B50-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>].</p><p>Ginsenosides Rc and Rg3 and related compounds modulate the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis through inhibition of FK506-binding protein 51 (FKBP51) at the glucocorticoid receptor (GR), resulting in a reduction in depressive-like behaviors in mice exposed to chronic unpredictable mild stress (CUMS) [<xref rid="B54-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">54</xref>]. FKBP51 negatively regulates GR sensitivity; its inhibition by ginsenosides enhances nuclear translocation of the glucocorticoid–GR complex, leading to normalization of HPA axis activity and attenuation of hypercortisolemia [<xref rid="B54-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">54</xref>]. Ginsenoside Rg1 acts as a functional ligand of the glucocorticoid receptor, exerting both genomic and non-genomic effects via GR binding and induction of angiogenesis [<xref rid="B55-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>,<xref rid="B56-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>].</p><p>Total saponins from <italic toggle="yes">P. ginseng</italic> exert antidepressant effects by suppressing the CX3CL1/CX3CR1 signaling axis and inhibiting phosphorylation of the mitogen-activated protein kinases p38 and JNK in the hippocampus of CUMS-exposed rats [<xref rid="B57-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>]. CX3CL1 (fractalkine) is a neuron-derived chemokine that, through interaction with CX3CR1 on microglia, activates inflammatory cascades leading to excessive microglial activation and release of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α [<xref rid="B57-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>]. Total saponins inhibit this axis, thereby reducing neuroinflammation and restoring monoaminergic neurotransmitter balance (serotonin, noradrenaline, dopamine) in the brain [<xref rid="B57-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>].</p><p>The neuroprotective mechanisms of ginseng include modulation of the GABA–benzodiazepine (GABA-BZD) receptor system, which protects against oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and neuroinflammation induced by sleep deprivation in 72 h sleep deprivation models [<xref rid="B58-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Panax quinquefolius</italic> (American ginseng) increases the expression of GABA<sub>A</sub> receptor subunits (α1, α2, β2) and reduces levels of ROS, malondialdehyde (MDA), and nitric oxide (NO) in the cerebral cortex and hippocampus [<xref rid="B58-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>]. This GABAergic mechanism explains the anxiolytic and calming effects of ginseng without the sedative side effects characteristic of benzodiazepines [<xref rid="B58-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>].</p><p>Ginsenoside Rc enhances mitochondrial biogenesis by increasing the activity of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma coactivator 1α (PGC-1α), resulting in increased ATP synthesis and reduced reactive oxygen species (ROS) production in skeletal muscle cells [<xref rid="B59-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>].
