The same extract was found to show CRF1 antagonist activity of 63% at 100 μg/ml concentration and cortisol release inhibition of 89% at 100 μg/ml concentration. Methanol extract of the roots of <italic toggle="yes">Ocimum sanctum</italic> was reported to increase swimming time in mice at an intraperitoneal dose of 400 mg/kg body weight [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref047" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>]. Though there are many studies available on the antistress activity of <italic toggle="yes">Ocimum tenuiflorum</italic>, no animal studies are available on the extract at lower doses from 6.25 mg/kg to 50 mg/kg body weight in rats or mice.</p><p>In line with previously reported studies of <italic toggle="yes">Ocimum tenuiflorum</italic>, this study confirmed the antistress potential of <italic toggle="yes">Ocimum tenuiflorum</italic> but at lower doses in mice and rats, which substantiates the improved efficacy of the extract used in this study. The antistress activity was also substantiated by its stress hormone lowering effect observed in the cortisol release assay and antagonism at the CRF1 receptor. The results presented in this study serve as a plausible mechanism of the stress management property of the <italic toggle="yes">Ocimum tenuiflorum</italic> extract (Holixer<sup>TM</sup>) observed in the recent 8-week randomized double-blind placebo-controlled study [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref018" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>]. In the clinical study, the <italic toggle="yes">Ocimum tenuiflorum</italic> extract (Holixer<sup>TM</sup>) significantly reduced stress as measured by the Perceived Stress Scale (PSS), improved sleep as measured by the Athens Insomnia Scale (AIS), and reduced hair cortisol in adults experiencing stress at a daily dose of 250 mg/day.</p><p>This study also has few limitations. We could have subjected the animals to chronic stress paradigm before subjecting them to forced swim test that might have produced additional data in chronic stressful condition. Also, adaptogenic biomarkers such as molecular chaperons (e.g., Hsp70), stress-activated c-Jun N-terminal protein kinase (JNK1), Forkhead box O (FoxO) transcription factor, and nitric oxide (NO) would have been a value addition.</p></sec><sec sec-type="conclusions" id="sec019"><title>5 Conclusion</title><p>Overall, the results demonstrated that the standardized extract of <italic toggle="yes">Ocimum tenuiflorum</italic> exhibits antistress benefits at a very low dose as evident in the two <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> studies and its antistress activity could be due to the inhibition of cortisol release and antagonistic effect on the CRF1 receptors thereby regulating the HPA axis.
