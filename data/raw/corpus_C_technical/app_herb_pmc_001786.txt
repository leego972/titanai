# PMC: phytochemical bioactive compound anticancer — Overview

</title><p>Metabolic syndrome is a cluster of risk factors for cardiovascular disease and diabetes. Obesity, insulin resistance, and diabetes promote hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and vascular damage, thereby increasing risks of coronary artery disease, stroke, and peripheral vascular disease. SCOPA has been used as an anti-hypertensive, a vasodilator, and an anti-hypercholesterolemic agent in traditional medicine (<xref rid="T1" ref-type="table">
<bold>Table 1</bold>
</xref>), and data from modern studies have been consistent with these historical uses. As is the case for SCOPA’s hepatoprotective properties, investigations have focused on SCOPA-containing TCM preparations or on single compounds isolated from SCOPA, but Cho et al. have shown that diet supplementation with an aqueous SCOPA extract lowered blood pressure and produced other favorable effects in spontaneously hypertensive rats (<xref rid="B88" ref-type="bibr">88</xref>). Beneficial effects included reductions in angiotensin converting enzyme (ACE) activity, angiotensin II (AngII) levels, and lipid peroxidation in serum. Given these observations, it is plausible that SCOPA’s effects on the cardiovascular system could mitigate some of the complications of metabolic syndrome or diabetes. </title><sec id="s4_1"><title>Coumarins</title><p>SCOPA is rich in plant coumarins (<xref rid="B93" ref-type="bibr">93</xref>). The three related coumarins scoparone (6,7 - dimethoxycoumarin), scopoletin (7-hydroxy-5-methoxycoumarin), and esculetin (6,7 - dihydroxycoumarin) are found in many <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia</italic> species and are considered major components of SCOPA (<xref rid="B36" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>, <xref rid="B41" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>). Many coumarins have potent anti-inflammatory or antioxidant effects that account for a wide range of bioactivities (<xref rid="B94" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>). In addition, natural and synthetic coumarins are under investigation as promising treatments for many conditions, cancer in particular (<xref rid="B95" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>–<xref rid="B102" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>). The therapeutic potential of these coumarins is supported by molecular docking analyses (<xref rid="B94" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>, <xref rid="B103" ref-type="bibr">103</xref>–<xref rid="B112" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>) and structure-activity relationship (SAR) studies (<xref rid="B111" ref-type="bibr">111</xref>, <xref rid="B113" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>–<xref rid="B117" ref-type="bibr">117</xref>).</p><sec id="s4_1_1"><title>Scoparone</title><p>Scoparone, a prominent compound in TCM preparations, has long been known to have hypotensive and vasodilatory properties (<xref rid="B118" ref-type="bibr">118</xref>–<xref rid="B125" ref-type="bibr">125</xref>) and has been found to have several additional favorable cardiovascular effects, including inhibition of ACE activity <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> (<xref rid="B126" ref-type="bibr">126</xref>) and reduction in AngII-induced myocardial changes in rodents, cultured myocytes, and cardiac fibroblasts (<xref rid="B127" ref-type="bibr">127</xref>, <xref rid="B128" ref-type="bibr">128</xref>). Furthermore, there is evidence that scoparone can mitigate cardiac ischemia/reperfusion injury in <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> settings (<xref rid="B129" ref-type="bibr">129</xref>). Scoparone also has anti-atherogenic effects including the inhibition of vascular smooth muscle cell proliferation and migration (<xref rid="B130" ref-type="bibr">130</xref>, <xref rid="B131" ref-type="bibr">131</xref>), inhibition of platelet aggregation (<xref rid="B132" ref-type="bibr">132</xref>), and the attenuation of atherosclerotic plaque formation and dyslipidemia in hyperlipidemic diabetic rabbits (<xref rid="B133" ref-type="bibr">133</xref>, <xref rid="B134" ref-type="bibr">134</xref>).
