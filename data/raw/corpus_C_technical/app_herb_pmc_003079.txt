Traditional healers and elders serve as the main custodians of this knowledge, which is predominantly transmitted orally within families, reflecting a strong cultural framework for health management and ecological stewardship. The findings emphasize the intricate connections between cultural resilience, sustainable resource use, and ecological integrity in semi-arid forest landscapes.</p><p id="Par254">The Karamara forest serves not only as a reservoir of biodiversity but also as a living archive of Somali ethnomedicine. Integrating this knowledge into broader health and conservation frameworks can enhance community wellbeing while promoting sustainable management of forest resources. Efforts to document and safeguard indigenous medicinal knowledge are urgent, particularly through participatory education, intergenerational transfer programs, and cultural heritage initiatives within local schools and community organizations.</p><p id="Par255">Species identified as culturally significant warrant pharmacological evaluation to validate bioactivity and safety, enabling collaboration between ethnobotanists, pharmacologists, and traditional healers to bridge traditional wisdom with modern medicine. Conservation strategies, including community-managed reserves, sustainable harvesting guidelines, and home-garden cultivation, should align with local ecological practices and cultural norms to reduce pressure on wild populations.</p><p id="Par256">Policy frameworks should recognize and support traditional medicine, ensuring equitable benefit-sharing, institutional backing for knowledge protection, and integration into primary healthcare. Future research should adopt longitudinal and ecological approaches to monitor key species, assess market value chains, and examine the socio-economic dimensions of traditional medicine in pastoral and semi-pastoral communities.</p><p id="Par257">By highlighting the interplay of cultural knowledge, medicinal plant diversity, and ecological stewardship, this study highlights the importance of biocultural conservation as a foundation for sustainable healthcare and the preservation of indigenous knowledge systems.</p></sec></body><back><fn-group><fn><p><bold>Publisher’s note</bold></p><p>Springer Nature remains neutral with regard to jurisdictional claims in published maps and institutional affiliations.</p></fn></fn-group><ack><title>Acknowledgements</title><p>We sincerely thank the people of Jigjiga district for generously sharing their knowledge of medicinal plants and for their warm hospitality throughout our research.
