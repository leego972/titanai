# PMC: adaptogen stress response HPA axis — Overview

rid="B3-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B14-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>,<xref rid="B35-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>];</p></list-item><list-item><p>predict drug–herb interactions [<xref rid="B100-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">100</xref>], adverse events [<xref rid="B101-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">101</xref>], and potential toxic effects [<xref rid="B102-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>,<xref rid="B103-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">103</xref>,<xref rid="B104-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">104</xref>,<xref rid="B105-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">105</xref>].</p></list-item></list></p><p>Unlike conventional medicines, botanical adaptogens comprise multi-component active compounds and phytochemicals, whose interactions lead to novel and unexpected pharmacological activities due to their synergistic and antagonistic effects [<xref rid="B14-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>,<xref rid="B33-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>,<xref rid="B35-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>,<xref rid="B81-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>,<xref rid="B106-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>]. Emerging research also highlights how extraction methods can significantly influence the physicochemical and functional characteristics of bioactive plant-derived components, such as dietary fibers from <italic toggle="yes">Rubus chingii</italic> Hu., suggesting that extraction processes may similarly impact the efficacy and bioavailability of botanicals [<xref rid="B107-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">107</xref>].</p><p>Similarly, the pharmacological activity of a combination of several plants or herbal extracts, such as those in botanical hybrid preparations (e.g., TCM formulas), results in a new biologically active substance with unique pharmacological characteristics [<xref rid="B35-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>].</p><p>Primary outcomes from gene expression studies of adaptogens in isolated brain cells indicate that adaptogens activate adaptive stress-response canonical pathways, modulating numerous physiological processes and the progression of stress-induced and aging disorders [<xref rid="B3-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>]. In addition, various adaptogens regulate the expression of many other genes involved in neurogenesis, angiogenesis, innate immunity, and inflammaging, which play a crucial role in stress-induced and aging-related disorders [<xref rid="B2-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="B93-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">93</xref>].</p><p>Network pharmacology provides a possible mechanism of action. Network analysis must be conducted in combination with bioinformatic databases and experimental pharmacology, based on the results of gene expression and transcriptomics arrays obtained in vitro or in vivo studies, and validated, e.g., by PCT technique.</p><p>Molecular docking approaches in silico studies are not a stand-alone technique. Without functional assays (e.g., enzymatic inhibition) and binding studies (e.g., surface plasmon resonance, isothermal calorimetry, or microscale thermophoresis), there is no evidence to support the accuracy of in silico results. Otherwise, they remain hypothetical.</p><p>Various challenges in network pharmacology for natural products and phytotherapy research, which should be addressed [<xref rid="B81-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>,<xref rid="B106-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec2dot4-pharmaceuticals-18-01346"><title>2.4. Pitfalls in Adaptogen Research: Inadequate Assignment of Some Plants to Adaptogens or Insufficient Scientific Data</title><p>Polyvalence of some medicinal plants often leads to unsupported claims that they are adaptogens, as is the case with <italic toggle="yes">Eurycoma longifolia</italic>. While it is recognized in traditional medicine across Southeast Asia and regulated in markets like the EU, the official pharmacopeial status appears to be limited to three countries: Malaysia, Indonesia, and Vietnam. Several publications claim that <italic toggle="yes">Eurycoma longifolia</italic> Jack is an adaptogen [<xref rid="B108-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>,<xref rid="B109-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">109</xref>,<xref rid="B110-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>,<xref rid="B111-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">111</xref>,<xref rid="B112-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>,<xref rid="B113-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>,<xref rid="B114-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>]. However, there is no scientific evidence supporting these statements. The authors refer to:<list list-type="bullet"><list-item><p>Traditional use of “Malaysian Ginseng” as an aphrodisiac that stimulates sexual desire, antimalarial, anti-diabetic, antimicrobial, and antipyretic activities, and its polyvalent activity in various diseases [<xref rid="B115-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">115</xref>,<xref rid="B116-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">116</xref>],</p></list-item><list-item><p>Gonadotropin-induced increase in testosterone in vitro studies and elevated blood testosterone in animals and human studies [<xref rid="B57-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>,<xref rid="B110-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>,<xref rid="B117-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">117</xref>,<xref rid="B118-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">118</xref>], possibly associated with an increase in male fertility and ergogenic activity [<xref rid="B109-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">109</xref>,<xref rid="B110-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>,<xref rid="B112-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>,<xref rid="B113-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>,<xref rid="B119-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">119</xref>,<xref rid="B120-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">120</xref>,<xref rid="B121-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">121</xref>].</p></list-item></list></p><p>However, that is not a sufficient characteristic criterion to classify Eurycoma as an adaptogen.
