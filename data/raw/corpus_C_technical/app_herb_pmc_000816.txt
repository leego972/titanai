Tripterygium wilfordii </title><p><italic toggle="yes">Tripterygium wilfordii</italic> of the family Celastraceae is also known as “Thunder God Vine,” and is native to Korea, China, and Japan. It is commonly used for the treatment of multiple diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, nephritis, asthma, and cancers [<xref rid="B133-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">133</xref>,<xref rid="B134-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">134</xref>,<xref rid="B135-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">135</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">T. wilfordii</italic> produces important bioactive compound triptolide which is used as an immunosuppressive and anti-proliferative agent [<xref rid="B134-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">134</xref>]. It has a five-membered unsaturated lactone ring and is used against different breast cancer cells by activation of pro-apoptotic compounds by modulating several signaling pathways [<xref rid="B136-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">136</xref>]. </p><p>In vitro studies showed anti-proliferative and pro-apoptotic activities against tumor cell lines [<xref rid="B137-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">137</xref>,<xref rid="B138-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">138</xref>,<xref rid="B139-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">139</xref>]. He et al. [<xref rid="B140-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">140</xref>] studied the in vitro properties of triptolide of <italic toggle="yes">T. wilfordii</italic> activity by using human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs). The IC<sub>50</sub> value for HUVECs proliferation was 45 nM. The results of semi-quantitative RT-PCR and Western blot analysis in human umbilical vein endothelial cells showed that triptolide cause downregulation of proangiogenic Tie2 and VEGFR-2 expression after 24 h at 50 nM concentration. At high concentration (100 nM), the VEGFR-2 mRNA expression was completely blocked. But on the other hand, the knockdown of Tie2 decreases the inhibitory activities of triptolide on endothelial network formation. The overall results showed that anticancer properties of triptolide are directly correlated with the blockage of two endothelial receptor-mediated signaling pathways. Triptolide showed more antagonistic activities against the proliferation of HUVECs as compared to normal cells like skin keratinocytes HaCaT cells and other liver cells L-02 [<xref rid="B141-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">141</xref>].</p><p>Celastrol is another important compound of <italic toggle="yes">T. wilfordii</italic>, having cytotoxic activity against a broad range of cancer cells [<xref rid="B142-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">142</xref>]. It demonstrates its anticancer activities by blocking the NF-κB via targeting IκB kinase and TAK1-induced NF-κB activation [<xref rid="B143-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">143</xref>,<xref rid="B144-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">144</xref>]. Other studies showed that triptolide of <italic toggle="yes">T. wilfordii</italic> has anticancer properties in many model systems such as experiments have also demonstrated triptolide’s therapeutic efficacy in several model systems including neuroblastoma in nude mice model [<xref rid="B145-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">145</xref>], other xenografts of human melanoma, breast cancer, bladder cancer, gastric carcinoma [<xref rid="B146-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">146</xref>]. </p></sec><sec id="sec3dot18-biomolecules-10-00047"><title>3.18. Tussilago farfara</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Tussilago farfara</italic> (commonly called coltsfoot) is one of the important medicinal plants, grown in Europe and various regions of western and central Asia, commonly used against cancer. It possesses a high quantity of flavonoids and other phenolic compounds and some trace elements (Zn, Mg, and Se). The presence of these substances plays a key role in the anticancer activities of this plant. Maximum scavenging activity was recorded in water extract as compared to ethanol extract. It shows a 20.9% antioxidant activity. Further, this plant showed maximum antioxidant activity both using DPPH and yeast model [<xref rid="B147-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">147</xref>]. The quercetin-glycosides isolated from the flower bud of <italic toggle="yes">T. farfara</italic> shows the highest antioxidant activity [<xref rid="B148-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">148</xref>].
