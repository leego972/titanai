Copyright 2017, Royal Society of Chemistry)</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" id="MO7" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="12951_2021_862_Fig6_HTML.jpg"/></fig></p></sec><sec id="Sec12"><title>X-ray excitation</title><p id="Par23">X-ray has been as an innovative excitation source for in vivo optical imaging in recent years as the excellent merits of negligible scattering and deep depth penetration [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR88">88</xref>]. Some phosphors can be activated by X-ray photons to generate light by triggering the luminescent centers. Yang et al. firstly proposed X-ray as external optical excitation to activate PEG-functionalized SrAl<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub>:Eu<sup>2+</sup> PLNPs for imaging deep tissue (up to 2.5 cm) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR65">65</xref>]. Hao et al. employed X-ray to activate ZnGa<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub>:Cr PLNPs for renewable NIR PersL imaging of deep-tissue [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR89">89</xref>]. To synthesize the controllable morphology of PLNPs, Yang et al. developed kiwifruit-like structures of SiO<sub>2</sub>@ZnGa<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub>:Cr@SiO<sub>2</sub> with the assistant of silica template. Then the X-ray irradiated PLNPs showcased the excellent PersL performance and long-term imaging from deep tissue [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR90">90</xref>]. Zhang et al. designed MgGeO<sub>3</sub>:Mn<sup>2+</sup>,Yb<sup>3+</sup>,Li<sup>+</sup> (MGO) PLNPs with NIR-I and NIR-II emission. Under the activation of soft X-rays, MGO can be visualized from deep tissue [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR91">91</xref>]. Yeh et al. developed PEGylated ZnGa<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub>:Cr<sup>3+</sup> concave nanocubes with highly passive targeting and X-ray excitation for PersL imaging of deep-seated orthotopic hepatic tumors [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR92">92</xref>]. The uniform nanocubes showed stable NIR radioluminescence after repeated X-ray excitation (Fig. <xref rid="Fig7" ref-type="fig">7</xref>a–c) With the low-dose of X-ray excitation (0.5 Gy), these PEGylated PLNPs clearly depicted the orthotropic hepatic tumors from in vivo and ex vivo PersL imaging (Fig. <xref rid="Fig7" ref-type="fig">7</xref>d,e).
