It is now distributed in Vietnam, Laos, Cambodia, the Philippines, Indonesia, Papua New Guinea, India, China, and many Pacific and Caribbean islands, including Cuba, Puerto Rico, and the Virgin Islands [<xref rid="B2-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>]. In Cuba, the plant has been cultivated since at least the late 19th century and is commonly used in urban landscaping as a hedge or potted ornamental. It prefers well-drained, fertile soils and thrives under full sun or partial shade, although its growth is generally slow and benefits from periodic pruning and organic amendments.</p><p>Genetic and morphological studies conducted in Vietnam revealed high phenotypic plasticity and significant intraspecific variation within <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>. Using leaf morphology and internal transcribed spacer (ITS) rDNA sequencing, over 20 morphotypes were differentiated among 23 accessions, including several subspecies and potential hybrids. These findings support the hypothesis of regional domestication and extensive cultivar development driven by traditional medicinal use and esthetic selection [<xref rid="B30-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>].</p><p>According to Plants of the World Online (POWO) [<xref rid="B31-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>], <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> has 18 accepted synonyms, reflecting historical taxonomic revisions. Notable examples include <italic toggle="yes">Panax fruticosus</italic> L., <italic toggle="yes">Nothopanax fruticosus</italic> (L.) Miq., <italic toggle="yes">Aralia fruticosa</italic> (L.) L.H. Bailey, and <italic toggle="yes">Tieghemopanax fruticosus</italic> (L.) R.Vig. The name <italic toggle="yes">Polyscias</italic> is derived from the Greek “poly” (many) and “skia” (shade), referring to the plant’s dense, spreading foliage [<xref rid="B2-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>].</p><p>From an ethnopharmacological standpoint, <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> is a widely used medicinal species. In Vietnam, it is traditionally known as “đinh lăng” and used as a tonic, adaptogen, and treatment for inflammation, respiratory disorders, and fatigue [<xref rid="B9-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. In Indonesian jamu medicine, it features in postpartum and gynecological formulations. Ethnobotanical records from the Philippines and Oceania describe its use as an antipyretic, analgesic, and diuretic, applied as decoctions or topical poultices [<xref rid="B9-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. The widespread use of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> in traditional medicine aligns with its confirmed phytochemical richness, including oleanane-type triterpenoid saponins (e.g., polysciosides A–K), chikusetsusaponin IVa, flavonoids, polyacetylenes, and sterols [<xref rid="B3-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B4-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B5-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>,<xref rid="B6-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="B7-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>,<xref rid="B8-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>,<xref rid="B9-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>,<xref rid="B10-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>,<xref rid="B11-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. Pharmacological studies have confirmed its antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, antidiabetic, immunomodulatory, and antimicrobial effects [<xref rid="B12-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>,<xref rid="B13-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>,<xref rid="B14-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>,<xref rid="B15-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>,<xref rid="B16-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>,<xref rid="B17-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>].</p><p>Overall, <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> represents a phytochemically and ethnomedically important species. Its morphological plasticity, wide geographic distribution, and medicinal versatility make it a promising subject for integrative research spanning taxonomy, molecular phylogenetics, and evidence-based phytotherapy.</p></sec><sec id="sec4-molecules-30-03460"><title>4.
