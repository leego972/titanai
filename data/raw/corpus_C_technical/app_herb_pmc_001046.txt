Introduction</title><p>The Poaceae is a large family of monocotyledonous plants, commonly recognized as grasses, representing the most important group of crops [<xref rid="B1-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>]. This family encompasses several noteworthy cultivated species such as <italic toggle="yes">Triticum aestivum</italic> L., <italic toggle="yes">Oryza sativa</italic> L., <italic toggle="yes">Zea mays</italic> L. and <italic toggle="yes">Hordeum vulgare</italic> L. [<xref rid="B2-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>]. It comprises of 7500 species [<xref rid="B3-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>] distributed worldwide and with a wide spectrum of climatic adaptations. Remarkably, grasses also show extreme ranges in salinity tolerance, from salt sensitive (glycophytic) to extremely tolerant (halophytic) [<xref rid="B4-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>]. For instance, <italic toggle="yes">Poa annua</italic> L. is highly sensitive, <italic toggle="yes">Paspalum dilatatum</italic> Poir. is moderately salt tolerant and <italic toggle="yes">Cynodon dactylon</italic> (L.) Pers. is completely tolerant (true halophyte) [<xref rid="B4-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>]. A completely salt tolerant species is defined by the ability to complete a life cycle in a salt concentration of at least 200 mM of NaCl [<xref rid="B5-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>]. Nowadays, 1560 species from 550 genera and 117 families [<xref rid="B6-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>] are known to have salt-tolerance, among these the Poaceae family includes 65 genera and 148 species [<xref rid="B7-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>] of halophytes.</p><p>Halophytes can keep and acquire water, protect cells from the damage caused by the accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS), and maintain ion homeostasis in salty stressed environments through a variety of adaptations [<xref rid="B5-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>]. These include the biosynthesis of different biocompounds that can be useful due to their biological activities, such as antioxidant, antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and antitumoral [<xref rid="B8-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>].
