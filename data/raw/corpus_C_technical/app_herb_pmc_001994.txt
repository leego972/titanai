Mucolytic and Antitussive Activity</title><p>Effective control of mucus hypersecretion and cough is critical in the management of symptomatic asthma, particularly in mucus-dominant phenotypes. In a study conducted by Koffuor et al. [<xref rid="B18-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>], two complementary animal models were employed to assess the mucolytic and antitussive effects of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>: (1) ammonium chloride (NH<sub>4</sub>Cl)-induced bronchial secretion in ICR mice and (2) citric-acid-induced cough in guinea pigs. An aqueous root extract of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> (PFE) was administered orally at doses of 100, 200, and 400 mg·kg<sup>−1</sup> body weight.</p><p>In the NH<sub>4</sub>Cl-induced mucus model, semi-quantitative evaluation using phenol red dye revealed a significant reduction in bronchial secretion. PFE at 400 mg·kg<sup>−1</sup> reduced mucus volume by 49.7% versus control, with efficacy comparable to bromhexine, a reference mucolytic agent. The dose-dependent nature of this response indicates a consistent pharmacodynamic effect of the extract.</p><p>In the citric-acid-induced cough model, PFE significantly reduced the number of cough episodes by 67% and prolonged the latency to first cough by 82%, compared to the vehicle-treated group. These results indicate both suppression of chemically induced cough and possible modulation of airway sensory responsiveness. The antitussive effect was comparable to that of dihydrocodeine, a centrally acting reference drug.</p><p>Biochemical safety assessments, including serum levels of alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), creatinine, and urea, showed no significant deviations from normal values, confirming the absence of systemic toxicity following subacute oral administration of PFE at doses up to 400 mg·kg<sup>−1</sup>.</p><p>Triterpenoid saponins and glycosides may underlie the mucolytic effect, potentially by promoting mucociliary clearance and epithelial hydration. Although the precise mechanism of the antitussive action remains unclear, the observed effects suggest peripheral modulation of the cough reflex, possibly through desensitization of vagal afferents or through the regulation of TRPV1 and TRPA1 ion channels, both of which are involved in mediating airway sensory signaling [<xref rid="B18-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>].</p><p>In our opinion, these findings support the therapeutic relevance of <italic toggle="yes">Polyscias fruticosa</italic> in managing airway symptoms such as mucus hypersecretion and cough. Nonetheless, further studies are needed to elucidate its underlying mechanisms and confirm the observed effects in clinical settings.</p></sec><sec id="sec6dot1dot3-molecules-30-03460"><title>6.1.3. Bronchodilatory and Antihistaminic Effects</title><p>The ability to induce bronchial smooth muscle relaxation and counteract the effects of bronchoconstrictors such as histamine and acetylcholine is central to the pharmacological management of asthma. In a study by Koffuor et al. [<xref rid="B15-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>], the bronchodilatory effects of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> extract (PFE) was evaluated in guinea pigs subjected to bronchospasm induced by inhalation of histamine (5.8 × 10<sup>−2</sup> mg·mL<sup>−1</sup>) and acetylcholine (2.0 × 10<sup>−2</sup> mg·mL<sup>−1</sup>). Following provocation, animals received oral doses of PFE at 100, 250, and 500 mg·kg<sup>−1</sup>, and responses were compared to those of reference drugs: mepyramine (an H<sub>1</sub> receptor antagonist) and atropine (a muscarinic M<sub>3</sub> receptor antagonist).</p><p>PFE significantly prolonged the time to onset of pre-convulsive dyspnea (PCD) in a dose-dependent manner, with increases ranging from 70% to 110%. It also reduced recovery time by 44–63% compared to control, confirming effective bronchodilation. These bronchodilatory effects were corroborated by in vitro experiments using isolated guinea pig ileum, in which PFE attenuated contractile responses to exogenously applied histamine and acetylcholine, indicating potential antagonism at H<sub>1</sub> and M<sub>3</sub> receptors [<xref rid="B15-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>].</p><p>These effects may be mediated by flavonoids, sesquiterpenes, and alkaloids that modulate G protein-coupled receptors regulating airway tone. Additionally, PFE may stabilize mast cells, reducing the release of bronchoconstrictors such as histamine, prostaglandins, and leukotrienes.</p><p>No adverse effects were reported at oral doses up to 500 mg·kg<sup>−1</sup>, supporting the safety profile of the extract under subacute exposure conditions [<xref rid="B15-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>,<xref rid="B18-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>].</p><p>Collectively, the data support the bronchodilatory and antihistaminic potential of <italic toggle="yes">P.
