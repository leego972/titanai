2025</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><sec id="Sec1"><title>Background</title><p id="Par16">Herbal medicine boasts a deep-rooted history and has been a key therapeutic resource for numerous cultures across the globe [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR1">1</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR2">2</xref>]. Many communities in Asia, Latin America, and Africa, such as those in Ethiopia, depend on traditional medicine, especially herbal remedies, for their healthcare needs due to constraints in accessing modern medical services and the cultural acceptance of these practices [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR3">3</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR4">4</xref>]. The Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) highlights Ethiopia's rich ethnic diversity, which plays a significant role in the country's extensive variety of around 6,000 plant species used for health and livelihood purposes [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR5">5</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR6">6</xref>]. Ethiopia, located in Sub-Saharan Africa, has a population that heavily relies on native plants for their health and livelihoods [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR7">7</xref>]. The country is home to various ethnic groups with millennia of ethnobotanical knowledge, beliefs, and practices related to local flora. Traditional medicine is a vital component of Ethiopia's healthcare system [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR3">3</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR8">8</xref>]. However, despite some organized studies on ethnomedicine, the development of therapeutic products remains limited, and traditional knowledge is threatened by urban migration, industrialization, environmental degradation, and changing lifestyles [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">9</xref>]. It is estimated that around one-third of Ethiopia’s plant families have applications in traditional medicine, catering to the diverse ethnic groups throughout the nation [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">9</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR10">10</xref>]. Traditional remedies are essential, with about 80% of the population depending on them, and approximately 95% of these remedies based on plants [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR11">11</xref>]. Preserving the traditional knowledge of medicinal plants is crucial not only for maintaining cultural heritage and biodiversity but also for supporting contemporary healthcare and drug development [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR13">13</xref>].</p><p id="Par17">Traditional medicinal practices often provide a more affordable alternative to modern healthcare [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR3">3</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR15">15</xref>]. Indigenous knowledge includes the collective understanding, skills, and practices that local communities have developed over generations regarding the use and management of plants [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR15">15</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR16">16</xref>].
