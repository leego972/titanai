These efficacies of LLLT have been proven to accelerate dermal wound healing in the medical fields by numerous studies [<xref rid="B22-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>,<xref rid="B23-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>,<xref rid="B24-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>]. In contrast to the dermal tissue, the oral cavity is a special wet environment in which wound healing occurs in warm saliva [<xref rid="B25-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>]. The saliva contains numerous microorganisms and food debris, which might possibly act as a medium for infectious bacteria [<xref rid="B26-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>]. After surgical extraction of third molars, the incision wound in the oral mucosa remain under high risk of postoperative infection and inflammatory reactions [<xref rid="B27-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>]. Therefore, to prevent infection and promote healing of the mucosal wounds, we investigated the effect of 915 nm laser irradiation on mucosal wound healing in animal and human clinical studies.</p><p>Recently, lasers have been successfully used for clinical treatment of various intraoral diseases. It has been demonstrated that LLLT reduces size and related pain of oral lichen planus (OLP) and recurring aphthous stomatitis (RAS) [<xref rid="B28-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>,<xref rid="B29-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>]. Furthermore, lasers are being increasingly incorporated into conventional treatment for oral diseases as a part of surgical and postoperative therapy. Additional LLLT following root planing reduces gingival inflammation in chronic periodontitis [<xref rid="B4-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>]. Superpulsed LLLT reduces postoperative pain after extraction of a tooth [<xref rid="B30-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>]. However, the effect of LLLT with 915 nm wavelength diode laser on intraoral mucosal wound healing after surgical incision has not yet been reported.</p><p>In the present study, the in vivo preclinical findings indicate that 915 nm LLLT accelerated intraoral mucosal wound healing, and repeated irradiation of laser shows better results than a single irradiation. These results are consistent with those of the study which reported that repeated laser therapy after scaling showed better results compared to a single laser therapy after scaling group and scaling only group [<xref rid="B31-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>]. Multiple sessions of diode laser therapy showed a faster and greater tendency to reduce inflammatory responses. Such results might be explained by the cumulative effect of laser irradiation, by which every newly irradiated dosage postoperatively stays in the target tissue [<xref rid="B11-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. These findings from the animal study were reflected in the clinical study with a protocol of multiple irradiation of a 915 nm laser—0.5 W power, 187.5 J/cm<sup>2</sup> in fluency, and 6 sets of 50 s long LLLT on the day of extraction and on postoperative days 1 and 7. To prevent excess heat production on the mucosa, a 1 min discontinuation was considered on the mucosal incision lines.</p><p>The limitation of the animal study is that the wounds were not identical between animal and clinical studies. The intraoral wound of animal was an abrasion wound, whereas the human wound was a mucoperiosteal incisional wound. Furthermore, intraoral mucosal thickness and healing potential are different between those two species. Therefore, we used some different parameters of 915 nm laser irradiation in animal and clinical studies because the wounds were not identical.</p><p>According to the overall analysis of efficacy, the LLLT and control groups showed significant changes with time, meaning that both groups healed over time as expected. However, the statistical difference between the two groups was not significant. Clinically, faster immediate wound healing was observed in the LLLT group than in the control group on postoperative day 1, although the degrees of the difference were not that significant in terms of <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-value (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &gt; 0.05). The analysis of each parameter displayed similar results: Severity figures for swelling and dehiscence of incision wound were all clinically better immediately in the LLLT group than in the control group. These results correspond well with those of the previous study with 940 nm LLLT on days 2 and 7 after extraction, which reported that there was less swelling and trismus in the LLLT group than in the control group according to the clinical outcomes, although there was no significant difference between the experimental and control groups in terms of <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-value [<xref rid="B15-medicina-55-00405" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>].
