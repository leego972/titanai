If no problems are identified during the scientific review, the product obtains its biopesticide registration. The process typically takes around 12 to 18 months, with low financial fees involved [<xref rid="B56-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>].</p><p>It should be emphasized that, despite being derived from natural sources, biochemical biopesticides are not exempt from rigorous safety evaluation. Regulatory authorities require detailed toxicological, ecotoxicological, and environmental persistence and transformation data to assess the potential risks for non-target organisms, including pollinators, aquatic species, soil microbiota, and mammals [<xref rid="B56-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>,<xref rid="B58-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>]. Parameters such as acute and chronic toxicity, persistence, degradation pathways, and residue behavior must be carefully examined before approval. This regulatory investigation reflects the recognition that a natural origin does not inherently preclude adverse biological effects, and that risk assessment remains a central pillar in ensuring safe and sustainable implementation.</p><p>In the EU, these product groups are covered by EU Plant Protection Regulation No. 1107/2009 together with Regulation No. 396/2005 and Directive 2009/128/EC [<xref rid="B59-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>,<xref rid="B60-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>,<xref rid="B61-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>]. The term “biopesticide” is not included in any European regulation, as Regulation No. 1432/2017 introduced the terms “basic substances” and “low-risk substances” [<xref rid="B61-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>,<xref rid="B62-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>]. Product release begins with the approval of its active substance, requiring the applicant to submit a dossier to the Rapporteur Member State containing information on the physicochemical, toxicological, and ecotoxicological properties, as observed in <xref rid="molecules-31-00996-f004" ref-type="fig">Figure 4</xref> [<xref rid="B59-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>,<xref rid="B60-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>,<xref rid="B61-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>,<xref rid="B62-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>]. This dossier is assessed, and if there are no inconsistencies, the evaluation of the active substance begins. Upon completion, the Member State issues an assessment report of the substance to the European Commission and The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) for their review [<xref rid="B15-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>,<xref rid="B59-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>,<xref rid="B60-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>,<xref rid="B61-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>,<xref rid="B62-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>]. If no inconsistencies are observed, the European Commission issues a review report to the Standing Committee on Plants, Animals, Food, and Feed (SCFCAH), conducting a vote for approval or rejection. This process can take between 30 and 42 months, depending on its complexity [<xref rid="B15-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>,<xref rid="B59-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>,<xref rid="B60-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>,<xref rid="B61-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>,<xref rid="B62-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>]. Due to this rigorous authorization process, there are currently about 18 basic substances and 10 low-risk substances released for use [<xref rid="B63-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>]. Low-risk active substances have an initial approval of 15 years with a review for an additional 15 years, while basic substances theoretically have approval for an unlimited period [<xref rid="B15-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>,<xref rid="B59-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>,<xref rid="B60-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>,<xref rid="B61-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>,<xref rid="B62-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>]. After approval, authorization for product marketing must be requested and conducted by the Member States, with the applicant specifying which Member State will carry out this evaluation, providing some product information, as shown in the flowchart in <xref rid="molecules-31-00996-f003" ref-type="fig">Figure 3</xref> [<xref rid="B15-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>,<xref rid="B59-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>,<xref rid="B60-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>,<xref rid="B61-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>,<xref rid="B62-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>].
