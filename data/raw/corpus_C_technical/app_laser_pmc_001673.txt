BY-NC</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><sec id="sec0005"><label>1</label><title>Introduction</title><p>Optical coherence tomography (OCT) was proposed for the first time in the 1990 s by Fujimoto [<xref rid="ref001" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>]. Analogous to B-scan ultrasound imaging, OCT acquires cross-sectional images by measuring the intensity of back-scattered light from biological tissue. As the echo delay time is too short to measure due to the speed of light, OCT uses a low coherence light source and an interfering setup with the biological tissue in its sample arm.</p><p>OCT was introduced firstly in a time-domain modality, called as time-domain OCT (TD-OCT) [<xref rid="ref002" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>], as shown in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="xst-27-xst190559-g001">Fig. 1</xref>(a). It uses a broadband source and a Michelson interferometer. The light backscattered from biological tissue interferes with light reflected from the reference mirror when the optical distance difference between the two light beams is less than the coherence length decided by the broadband source. The reference mirror moves periodically along the direction of light propagation (called as axial direction), forming axial scan and acquiring the information of biological tissue within the depth of scan. The interfering light is received by a photodetector which generates A-lines signals. The 3D OCT image is acquired through the cruising of sample probe across the tissue surface perpendicular to the axial direction. TD-OCT is able to achieve an axial resolution of 10<italic toggle="yes">μ</italic>m or less. The drawback of this modality is that the moving reference mirror limits the imaging speed.</p><fig id="xst-27-xst190559-g001" orientation="portrait" position="float"><label>Fig.1</label><caption><p>Demonstration of several OCT modalities: (a) Time-domain OCT (b) Spectral-domain OCT (c) Swept source OCT.</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="xst-27-xst190559-g001.jpg"/></fig><p>Fourier-domain OCT (FD-OCT) is another modality which has two branches, spectral-domain OCT (SD-OCT) [<xref rid="ref003" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>] and swept source OCT (SS-OCT) [<xref rid="ref004" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>], both need no reference mirror movement. The SD-OCT, as shown in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="xst-27-xst190559-g001">Fig. 1</xref>(b), uses a spectrometer instead of photodetector to acquire the interfering spectrum, and gets the A-lines through inverse Fourier-transform (IFFT). The SS-OCT, as shown in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="xst-27-xst190559-g001">Fig. 1</xref>(c), uses a swept light source which sweeps the bandwidth in a sweeping period and outputs a sequence of narrow-band (several hundredth of a nanometer) light. A photodetector acquires the interfering light sequence and arranges them together according to time received as the interfering spectrum.
