The national measles vaccination coverage rate in 2002 was just 74% [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR82">82</xref>], leaving over a quarter of all Ugandan children unprotected against measles; this number is likely to be considerably higher within the study area, given regional disparities in health-related outcomes and access and availability of treatment [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR83">83</xref>]. Bbaale [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR84">84</xref>] notes that rates of both diarrhea and acute respiratory infection are highest in the northern region, which he conjecturally linked to the history of civil conflict and displacement ending in 2008.</p><p id="Par77">While measles was the malady most cited by number of taxa and number of combined use reports, this ranking is based specifically on the 27 use reports from Teso (Amuria District), the highest value (by a factor of two) of any malady by district. The highly contagious disease measles (rubeola) is caused by a single-stranded, negative sense RNA virus in the genus <italic toggle="yes">Morbillivirus</italic> of the family Paramyxoviridae, which also includes other parainfluenza viruses, <italic toggle="yes">e.g.,</italic> mumps, rubella, and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV); measles results in a wide variety of health complications including pneumonia, blindness and chronic neurological conditions—and, although largely preventable by vaccine, measles remains responsible for an estimated 100,000 deaths each year [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR85">85</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR89">89</xref>]). Given the limited coverage of measles vaccination, these figures underline the relevance of locally available ameliorative treatments to manage the symptoms of the disease [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR90">90</xref>].</p><p id="Par78">While a wide variety of phytochemical compounds have been found to inhibit viral replication via various pathways [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR91">91</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR92">92</xref>], very few compounds are specifically linked to inhibition of the measles virus itself. Recently identified as antiviral agents with therapeutic value [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR93">93</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR94">94</xref>], non-flavonoid phenolic coumarins and their derivatives (including pyranocoumarins) have been found to inhibit measles virus replication [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR95">95</xref>]. Although the mechanism of action is not understood, coumarin-like compounds have been found to interfere with or inhibit the viral enzymes of other, single-stranded positive sense Ribonucleic acid (RNA) viruses including Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR85">85</xref>]. According to the compositional studies consulted, coumarins and coumarin derivatives have been reported as present in 9 of the 27 taxa cited by respondents as being used in treatment of measles (Table <xref rid="Tab2" ref-type="table">2</xref>).</p><p id="Par79">Diarrhea was the highest-ranked malady by taxon and use report in three of the four cultures (Lango, Acholi and Ethur), by a factor of two over the second-ranked malady in those cultures. Diarrhea is the second leading cause of death globally in children under five years old, and a leading cause of malnutrition in that age group; nearly 1.7 billion cases of childhood diarrheal disease result in an estimated 525,000 child deaths each year [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR96">96</xref>]. Ugandans suffer the highest mortality rate of children under 5 years old in the East Africa region [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR97">97</xref>], with diarrhea accounting for over 20% of child deaths, against a global average of 8.6% [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR98">98</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR99">99</xref>]. At the time of the data collection, prevalence of diarrhea within the northern region was the highest in Uganda, at any moment affecting 29.3% of the population [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR100">100</xref>], while a 2018 study specific to Agago District found the proportion children under 5 suffering from diarrhea to be over 40% [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR98">98</xref>].</p><p id="Par80">Causative organisms of acute diarrhea include viruses (notably <italic toggle="yes">Rotavirus</italic>), Gram-positive and Gram-negative strains of bacteria (notably <italic toggle="yes">Shigella</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Campylobacter</italic> spp., <italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic>), and protozoa (notably <italic toggle="yes">Giardia lamblia</italic>) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR101">101</xref>]. Phytochemical compounds effective in treating diarrhea include polyphenol catechins and tannins [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR102">102</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR103">103</xref>] and the flavonol quercetin [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR104">104</xref>], specifically through increased colonic water and electrolyte reabsorption [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR103">103</xref>].</p><p id="Par81">Cough was ranked at second in terms of use reports in all for regions, including Teso with 16 use reports.
