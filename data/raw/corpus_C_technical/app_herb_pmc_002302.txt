Prominent examples include 1-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>-D-(6-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-<italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-hydroxybenzoyl) glucopyranoside, heterophylliin A, and casuariin [<xref rid="B34-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>,<xref rid="B37-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>]. Tannins have also been identified in <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> (<xref rid="nutrients-17-03382-t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref>), specifically camelliatannins. These molecules have as their basic structure an epicatechin nucleus, to which a C-glucosyl ellagitannin unit is attached [<xref rid="B38-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>]. The main camelliatannins described include compounds A, B, C, D, F, G and H [<xref rid="B38-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>,<xref rid="B39-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>,<xref rid="B40-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>]. In addition, it was also possible to identify proanthocyanidins and procyanidins in <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> [<xref rid="B36-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>]. Other minor phenolic compounds in this matrix include phloretin 2′-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>-D-glucopyranoside and camellianoside [<xref rid="B34-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>,<xref rid="B35-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>].</p><p>In conclusion, the leaves of <italic toggle="yes">CJ</italic> are rich in phenolic compounds, which contribute to their notable antioxidant properties. The total concentration of phenolic compounds in the leaves has been reported to vary depending on factors such as environmental conditions and plant maturity. The phenolic compound content is higher in young leaves than in mature leaves, reaching a value of up to 74.30 mg/g dry weight, with a lower content in other parts of the plant [<xref rid="B41-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>]. These findings underscore the potential of <italic toggle="yes">CJ</italic> leaves as a valuable natural source of bioactive phenolic compounds.</p></sec><sec id="sec3dot2-nutrients-17-03382"><title>3.2. Pigments</title><p>The pigments present in <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> are divided primarily into two large groups: chlorophylls and carotenoids. Each of these groups has a unique chemical composition that gives it specific properties for both the plant and the development of various applications in medicine and industry [<xref rid="B45-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>]. However, it is important to note that, although not all compounds contributing to plant coloration are classified as pigments, certain phenolic compounds, such as flavonoids, can exert a minor influence on <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> coloration [<xref rid="B13-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>].</p><sec id="sec3dot2dot1-nutrients-17-03382"><title>3.2.1. Chlorophylls</title><p>In <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic>, the pigments found in the highest concentrations are chlorophylls, which are responsible for the characteristic green coloration of these leaves. This group plays a fundamental role in the process of photosynthesis, being responsible for the absorption of light in the red and blue wavelengths. Therefore, they are essential molecules for the transformation of light energy into chemical energy for the plant [<xref rid="B46-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>]. The types of chlorophyll present in <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> include chlorophyll a and chlorophyll b [<xref rid="B47-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>]. The results indicate that the distribution of chlorophyll in the chloroplasts of different <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> tissues varies according to adaptation to different light intensities. While spongy tissue is adapted to capture light under low-intensity conditions, palisade tissue is better prepared to handle high light intensities, which may also influence the relative amount of chlorophyll in each chloroplast type. This suggests that spongy tissue has a higher amount of chlorophyll a/b, which could be related to a greater capacity to capture light under low light intensity conditions [<xref rid="B48-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">48</xref>]. Based on the greater absorption in spongy tissue and the efficiency in capturing direct light in palisade tissue, <italic toggle="yes">CJ</italic> cultivation conditions could be modified to optimize chlorophyll production, adjusting factors such as light intensity and light distribution in the different microenvironments of the plant, thus favoring greater photosynthetic efficiency [<xref rid="B49-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>].
