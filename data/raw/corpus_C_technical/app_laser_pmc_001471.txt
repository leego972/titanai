I: inferior, N: nasal, S: superior, T: temporal.</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="pone.0332590.g003.jpg"/></fig></sec><sec sec-type="conclusions" id="sec013"><title>Discussion</title><p>In this study, we examined the concordance of angle-closure evaluations between GS-1 and AS-OCT. A higher proportion of AS-OCT images were judged as angle closure compared with GS-1 images. Consequently, in certain cases, AS-OCT indicated notably narrow angles, whereas GS-1 enabled visualization of the trabecular meshwork.</p><p>AS-OCT and gonioscopy have been compared in several previous studies [<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref017" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>–<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref021" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>]. Although the AS-OCT devices used in those studies varied, the criteria for angle closure were similar. Angle closure was defined as contact between the scleral spur and the iris with AS-OCT, whereas it was defined as Scheie classification grade III–IV with gonioscopy. In all of those reports, angle closure was more frequently judged with AS-OCT than with gonioscopy [<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref017" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>–<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref021" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>], with κ-values reported as moderate [<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref020" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>].</p><p>In this study, the criteria for angle closure differed from those in the abovementioned reports [<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref017" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>–<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref021" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>], and automated rather than manual gonioscopy was used. However, AS-OCT still resulted in a higher proportion of angle-closure judgments. Conversely, Cohen’s κ was lower than the moderate agreement reported in previous studies, a notable difference.</p><p>Several factors may explain this discrepancy. One potential factor is the use of GS-1 instead of manual gonioscopy. During GS-1 imaging, the device emits light, which enters the pupil and may induce constriction. Angles reportedly appear wider under bright conditions than under dark conditions [<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref025" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>,<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref026" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>]. Therefore, although GS-1 examinations performed under bright conditions allow 360° color imaging that is useful for documenting features such as pigmentation, they may not be suitable for screening or diagnosing angle closure. A similar influence has been suggested in a previous report using devices such as the EyeCam [<xref rid="pone.0332590.ref022" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>].
