2024</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><sec id="Sec1"><title>Introduction</title><p id="Par5">Optical coherence tomography (OCT) is a non-invasive imaging technique that uses low coherence light to provide high-resolution images of retinal structures. The past several decades have seen significant growth in the capabilities of new and existing imaging modalities, and OCT is a clear example of this progress, as OCT can now provide images at a resolution of approximately 10 µm/pixel in lateral scan mode [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR1">1</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR2">2</xref>]. Due to its high resolution, this imaging modality has several applications in the field of ophthalmology, particularly the ability to obtain high-resolution images of retinal structures for diagnostic purposes. Indeed, OCT has revolutionized the clinical management of many retinal diseases, including age-related macular degeneration (AMD) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR3">3</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR5">5</xref>], diabetic retinopathy [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR6">6</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR8">8</xref>], and retinal vein occlusion [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">9</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR10">10</xref>]. Over the past two decades, OCT has become the most commonly used imaging technique in ophthalmology; in 2017, approximately 6.74 million scans were performed in the USA on Medicare recipients alone [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR3">3</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR11">11</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>]. In chronic retinal pathologies such as AMD, individual patients can receive follow-up care as frequently as every few weeks over the course of years [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR13">13</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>], resulting in vast amounts of imaging data. This provides the ideal opportunity to develop artificial intelligence (AI)‒based tools to support clinical decision-making, with the majority of these tools based on deep learning and/or computer vision (in which a computer derives information from images, videos, or other inputs) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR15">15</xref>].
