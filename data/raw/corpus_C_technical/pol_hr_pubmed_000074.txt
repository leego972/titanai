Since 1945, the reason for humanitarian crises and the way in which the world 
responds to them has dramatically changed every 10 to 15 years or less. 
Planning, response, and recovery for these tragic events have often been ad hoc, 
inconsistent, and insufficient, largely because of the complexity of global 
humanitarian demands and their corresponding response system capabilities. This 
historical perspective chronicles the transformation of war and armed conflicts 
from the Cold War to today, emphasizing the impact these events have had on 
humanitarian professionals and their struggle to adapt to increasing 
humanitarian, operational, and political challenges. An unprecedented 
independent United Nations-World Health Organization decision in the Battle for 
Mosul in Iraq to deploy to combat zones emergency medical teams unprepared in 
the skills of decades-tested war and armed conflict preparation and response 
afforded to health care providers and dictated by International Humanitarian Law 
and Geneva Convention protections has abruptly challenged future decision-making 
and deployments. (Disaster Med Public Health Preparedness. 2019;13:109-115).
