Kessel et al. concluded no significant difference comparing PDL-PDT with cPDT in a split-face manner on 57 patients (<xref rid="cit0009" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>). At 6 months, 62% of PDL-PDT-treated and 64% of cPDT-treated AKs had healed, and at 12 months, the corresponding values were 48% and 56%. The study limitations included a non-blinded investigation and a non-randomized study design, and newly developed lesions were not differentiated from persistent ones, as in our study.</p><p>Alexiades-Armenakas et al. (<xref rid="cit0010" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>) treated 41 patients with AKs with PDL-PDT. In these patients, 90% of the PDL-PDT-treated lesions had healed at 8 months, 0% in the laser-only control group. Only 10 patients completed the follow-up of 8 months, limiting the reliability of the results. Kim et al. (<xref rid="cit0011" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>) treated 30 patients with PDL-PDT with a limited 3-month follow-up. The lesion complete clearance rates were 67% for PDL-PDT and 73% for cPDT, with a non-significant difference. The lesions were treated up to 5 times at 1–2 weeks apart. Karrer et al. (<xref rid="cit0008" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>) treated 24 patients. The lesion complete clearance rates for both treatments were 79% vs 84%, but the follow-up was limited to one month. At our clinic, a prior licentiate thesis on 51 lesions treated with PDL-PDT and 86 with cPDT, showed complete clearance for 71% of lesions treated with PDL-PDT vs 90% for cPDT at a 6-month follow-up (<xref rid="cit0014" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>). In this study, both treatments were repeated after 2 weeks. In all previous studies, PDL-PDT has a lower clearance rate, but it is mostly statistically insignificant. Our PDL-PDT lesion clearance rates are the weakest, with a clearance rate of 48% at 6 months compared with 62–92% in previous studies. However, in Alexiades-Armenakas et al. (<xref rid="cit0010" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>), the patients’ complete clearance rate for extremity lesions was also low (17% compared with 10% in our study).</p><p>Mutually, in all previous studies, PDL-PDT caused significantly less pain during treatment than cPDT. Pain on the visual analogue scale (VAS) in these studies was 1.7–2.6 in PDL-PDT and 4.2–6.5 for cPDT. Our corresponding mean NRS values of 2.3 and 4.1 are in line with the previous results.</p><p>For PDL-PDT treatment, the same laser parameters were used as in the study by Kessel et al. (<xref rid="cit0009" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>). The other studies’ parameters were also similar; however, Karrer et al. (<xref rid="cit0008" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>) used a higher 18 J/m2 fluence, Alexiades-Armenakas et al. (<xref rid="cit0010" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>) and Ruohoalho et al. (<xref rid="cit0014" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>) a larger spot size 10 and Kim et al. (<xref rid="cit0011" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>) and Karrer et al. (<xref rid="cit0008" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>) a PDL-PDT wavelength of 585 nm. A weak or negligible association was found between pain values and outcome (CC/PC). An association could suggest that the biological dose achieved in PDL-PDT is lower than in cPDT. However, since PDL-PDT pulses are of a short 10 ms duration compared with 433 s in cPDT, it may not be adequate to evaluate treatment efficacy by the experienced pain. When comparing light doses in both treatments, it is lower in PDL-PDT, with 18.2 J/cm<sup>2</sup> (7 J/cm<sup>2</sup> × 2 × 1.3) compared with 37 J/cm<sup>2</sup> in cPDT (<xref rid="cit0015" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>), which suggests that additionally doubling the PDL-PDT dose could improve the treatment outcome. In addition, PpIX adsorption is higher at the 630 nm wavelength in cPDT compared with the 595 nm in PDL-PDT (<xref rid="cit0015" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>). Thus, PDL-PDT at 585 nm could be more effective, as was also concluded by Karrer et al. (<xref rid="cit0008" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>). Some studies (<xref rid="cit0011" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>, <xref rid="cit0014" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>) used multiple treatments 1–2 weeks apart, achieving higher clearance rates. However, a need for multiple treatments would give PDL-PDT a major disadvantage compared with cPDT; based on previous studies cPDT needs one treatment only for AKs (<xref rid="cit0007" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>). Other downsides to PDL-PDT treatment include relatively high costs if the clinic does not currently have a PDL laser device, and operating the device requires expertise.</p><p>The current study also shows low clearance rates for the cPDT-treated lesions, with a lesion clearance rate of 73% vs 56–90% in previous studies. The clearance rates were low, even though we differentiated new lesions from persistent ones at follow-up.
