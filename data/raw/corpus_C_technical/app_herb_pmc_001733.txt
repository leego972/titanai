The extract was active against the fungi in decreasing order: <italic toggle="yes">A. flavus&gt; A. ochraceus &gt; P. funiculosum &gt; P. ochrochloron &gt; A. niger&gt; C. albicans.</italic> MIC values of the extract against these fungi strains were 0.30, 0.38, 0.43, 0.44, 0.48, and 0.58 mg/mL, respectively (<xref rid="molecules-28-04396-t005" ref-type="table">Table 5</xref>). The extract was most active against <italic toggle="yes">A. flavus</italic> with an MIC value of 0.33 mg/mL compared to the positive control ketoconazole (MIC of 0.20 mg/mL). </p><p>The leaf extract was also fungicidal across the tested fungi, with MFC values ranging from 0.91 to 1.42 mg/mL (<xref rid="molecules-28-04396-t005" ref-type="table">Table 5</xref>). The identified pure compounds, such as rutin <bold>6</bold>, hyperoside <bold>9</bold>, miquelianin<bold>7</bold>, and <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-coumaric acid <bold>37,</bold> were also screened. These compounds showed potent antifungal activities against all tested fungi, which were comparable to the positive control ketoconazole (MIC 0.10–2.05 mg/mL). As previously mentioned, rutin <bold>6</bold>, hyperoside <bold>9</bold>, gallic acid <bold>25</bold>, miquelianin <bold>7</bold>, isoquercetin <bold>8</bold>, <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-coumaric <bold>37</bold>, and quercetin <bold>3</bold> were the main compounds detected in the HPLC-based analysis of the extract [<xref rid="B13-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>]. However, gallic acid <bold>25</bold> and isoquercetin <bold>8</bold> were not screened in the assay. Nevertheless, gallic acid <bold>25</bold> was reported to inhibit the growth of <italic toggle="yes">A. solani</italic> [<xref rid="B51-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>,<xref rid="B88-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">88</xref>] and has been suggested as a green fungicide [<xref rid="B94-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>]. </p><p>In addition, these identified compounds have been shown to have antifungal activities elsewhere against various phytopathogen fungi. For instance, rutin <bold>6</bold> and quercetin <bold>3</bold> were active against <italic toggle="yes">C. albicans</italic> [<xref rid="B72-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>,<xref rid="B95-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>]. Hyperoside <bold>9</bold> has been reported to be active against <italic toggle="yes">Alternaria alternata</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Pestalotia guepini</italic>i, <italic toggle="yes">Fusarium avenaceum</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Drechslera</italic> sp., and <italic toggle="yes">Epicoccum nigrum</italic> [<xref rid="B96-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-Coumaric acid <bold>37</bold> was reported to be active against <italic toggle="yes">Botrytis cinnerrea</italic> [<xref rid="B97-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>]. These phytochemicals identified by Elansary et al. [<xref rid="B13-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>] in their methanolic leaf extract (<xref rid="molecules-28-04396-t003" ref-type="table">Table 3</xref>) would be synergistically responsible for the observed fungal activity.</p><p>The antifungal activities found in the methanolic leaf extract by Elansary et al. [<xref rid="B13-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>] were slightly different concerning the activity order compared to the ethanolic leaf extract investigated by Gumgumjee et al. [<xref rid="B17-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>]. Interestingly, the leaves used in both studies were collected in the same region (Saudi Arabia). However, the compositions of the identified compounds in the alcoholic extracts were markedly different (<xref rid="molecules-28-04396-t003" ref-type="table">Table 3</xref> and <xref rid="app1-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="app">Supplementary Table S1</xref>). For instance, Gumgumjee et al. [<xref rid="B17-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>] mainly reported hydroxybenzoic acids and hydroxycinnamic acids in their extract. In contrast, the identified compounds in the extract of the Elansary group [<xref rid="B13-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>] were mainly flavonoids.
