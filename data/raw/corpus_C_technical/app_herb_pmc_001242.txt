Other Activities</title><p>Nanotechnology, which involves the development and controlled manipulation of molecules with length scales in the 1- to 100-nanometers range, is an emerging field that is rapidly gaining impetus with applications in drug delivery, molecular imaging, catalysis, and for diagnostic purposes, among others [<xref rid="B90-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">90</xref>]. Recently there has been growing interest in the “green synthesis” of nanoparticles through the use of microbes, plants, or plant parts for the bioreduction of metal ions into their elemental nanoparticle form [<xref rid="B91-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">91</xref>,<xref rid="B92-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">92</xref>,<xref rid="B93-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">93</xref>]. Not only has green synthesis given more stable nanoparticles compared to other methods, but also it has been scalable and eco-friendly without any requirement for high temperatures and pressure or the use of toxic chemicals. So far, <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> leaf extracts have been used in the green synthesis of silver [<xref rid="B6-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="B94-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>,<xref rid="B95-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>,<xref rid="B96-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>], gold [<xref rid="B95-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>], and zinc nanoparticles [<xref rid="B97-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>] (<xref rid="molecules-27-04240-f003" ref-type="fig">Figure 3</xref>). Indeed, during the formation of nanoparticles, the extract of <italic toggle="yes">Z. nummalaria</italic> functions as a reducing and stabilizing agent [<xref rid="B96-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>]. This increases the potential activity of the nanoparticles. </p><p>Silver nanoparticles are mostly used as anti-microbial agents in the medical field mainly due to their strong growth inhibitory effects against fungi, bacteria and viruses, while having low toxicity to human cells. These nanoparticles have been utilized in food storage, textile coatings, as well as water treatment [<xref rid="B99-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>]. Khan et al. were the first to synthesize silver nanoparticles using <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> aqueous leaf extract as the reducing and capping agent [<xref rid="B94-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>]. Results showed that the prepared nanoparticles exhibited better antioxidant activity compared to the leaf extract. Antibacterial activity against <italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Pseudomonas aeruginosa</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Salmonella typhi</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus cereus</italic>, as well as antifungal activity against <italic toggle="yes">Aspergillus niger</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Aspergillus flavus</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Candida albicans</italic> were also enhanced. Similar enhancements were reported in another study, also using <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> aqueous leaf extracts, where increased antioxidant and antibacterial activity against the cariogenic <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Streptococcus mutans</italic> [<xref rid="B95-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>], <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus subtitles</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Pseudomonas aerugionsa</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Klebsiella pneumonia</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Streptococcus aureus</italic> were reported [<xref rid="B6-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>].
