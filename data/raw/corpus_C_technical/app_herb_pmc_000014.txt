Antimicrobial Activities</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Euclea natalensis</italic> is widely used as herbal medicine for a wide range of infectious diseases caused by microorganisms. Such diseases or ailments include amoebic dysentery, bronchitis, chest complaints, diarrhoea, sexually transmitted infections, sores, syphilis, toothache, tuberculosis, urinary tract infections, venereal diseases and wounds [<xref rid="B7-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>,<xref rid="B8-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>,<xref rid="B13-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>,<xref rid="B16-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>,<xref rid="B21-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>,<xref rid="B24-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>,<xref rid="B25-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>,<xref rid="B29-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>,<xref rid="B30-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>,<xref rid="B38-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>,<xref rid="B39-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>,<xref rid="B41-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>]. Such wide use of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> against bacterial, fungal and viral infections in traditional medicine prompted several researchers to assess antibacterial, antifungal, antimycobacterial and antiviral activities of crude extracts and compounds isolated from the species. The antibacterial, antifungal, antimycobacterial and antiviral investigations reported mixed results as highlighted in the relevant sub-sections below.</p><sec id="sec5dot1dot1-molecules-22-02128"><title>5.1.1. Antibacterial Activity</title><p>Khan and Nkunya [<xref rid="B40-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>] evaluated antibacterial activities of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> root bark extract against <italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic>. The extract was active against <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic> exhibiting 15–20 mm inhibition zone. Khan and Nkunya [<xref rid="B40-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>] evaluated antibacterial activities of the compounds mamegakinone <bold>4</bold>, diospyrin <bold>11</bold> and 7-methyljuglone <bold>12</bold> isolated from <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> roots against <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus anthracis, Bacillus cereus, Clostridium perfringens, Corynebacterium diphtheriae, Escherichia coli, Haemophilus influenzae, Klebsiella aerogenes, Neisseria gonorrhoeae, Pseudomonas aureginosa, Salmonella Heidelberg, Shigella dysenteriae, Shigella flexnerii</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic>.
