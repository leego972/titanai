In the emerging field of minimal invasive surgery, these systems might gain further relevance.</p><p id="Par26">OCT offers unprecedented quality to delineate the microstructural composition of <bold>vessel walls and their pathologies</bold>. In aneurysm surgery, OCT of the neck of the aneurysm could help to aid clip placement in relation to intravascular arteriosclerosis, thrombosis, aneurysm wall thickness, and vessel wall calcifications—characteristics and pathologies which were concealed so far. In case of <bold>bypass surgery</bold> OCT would be an imaging method which could aid to determine optimal site of bypass in correlation to vessel wall pathologies.</p><p id="Par27">Neuroscientific advancements like diffusion-dependent OCT for <bold>functional brain imaging</bold> have not yet found clinical applications. Brain pulsation and vessel artifacts as well as intermodality validation with hemodynamic and electrophysiological measurements still inhibit clinical transfer [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR32">32</xref>].</p><p id="Par28">Spatial resolution of polarization-sensitive OCT lays yet <bold>beyond the scope of manual microsurgery</bold>.
