On the basis of the chemical nature of their active principles, some indication of the possible mechanism of action of these plants may be derived. Thus, the extracts of <italic toggle="yes">Panax ginseng, Withania somnifera, Bryonia alba</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Aralia mandshurica</italic> contain phytosterols, and tetracyclic and pentacyclic triterpenes, that likely exert their effect on the hypothalamus-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis in which cortisone plays a key role during stress. Typically these adaptogens prevent or at least decrease certain hormonal changes, such as the increased level of cortisone, that are characteristic of a stress reaction (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b36-dti-2-2007-039">Panossian et al. 1999b</xref>; <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b17-dti-2-2007-039">Kim et al. 2003a</xref>). Plants such as <italic toggle="yes">R. rosea</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">S. chinensis</italic> accumulate phenolic secondary metabolites such as phenyl- and phenylethyl-propanoids and their dimeric lignans (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b48-dti-2-2007-039">Wagner et al. 1996</xref>; <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b41-dti-2-2007-039">Saratikov and Krasnov 2004</xref>). Such compounds can play an active role in stress response in respect of achieving a state of maximum work capacity as would be required in fight-or-flight situations (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b26-dti-2-2007-039">Lüllmann et al. 2005</xref>). Interestingly, <italic toggle="yes">E. senticosus</italic> contains both types of biologically active, low molecular weight compound and exhibits a very wide range of pharmacological effects (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b53-dti-2-2007-039">World Health Organization, 2002</xref>).</p><p>The pharmacological assessment of adaptogens typically includes evaluation of their stimulating, tonic and stress-protective activities in model systems in which animals are subjected to various stress conditions (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b32-dti-2-2007-039">Panossian and Wikman, 2005</xref>). Despite considerable research effort, however, it still remains somewhat unclear which mediators of stress response are predominantly involved in the mechanisms of action of adaptogens, and which biochemical markers need to be assayed in the evaluation of drug efficacy.</p><p>In order to address this problem further, we have determined blood levels of the potential stress response markers (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b11-dti-2-2007-039">Fink, 2000</xref>) stress-activated protein kinase/Jun N-terminal protein kinase (SAPK/JNK)<italic toggle="yes">,</italic> the phosphorylated kinase p-SAPK<italic toggle="yes">/</italic> p-JNK, NO, cortisol, testosterone, prostaglandin E<sub>2</sub>, leukotriene B<sub>4</sub> and thromboxane B<sub>2</sub> in laboratory rabbits, treated with stress-protectors and adaptogens, both before and after immobilization stress. The most extensively studied stress-protectors, <italic toggle="yes">B. alba</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">P. ginseng</italic>, and the adaptogens, <italic toggle="yes">E. senticosus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">R. rosea</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">S. chinensis</italic> (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b34-dti-2-2007-039">Panossian et al. 1997</xref>; <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b46-dti-2-2007-039">Upton, 1999</xref>; <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b52-dti-2-2007-039">World Health Organization, 1999</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b53-dti-2-2007-039">2002</xref>; <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b41-dti-2-2007-039">Saratikov and Krasnov, 2004</xref>)<italic toggle="yes">,</italic> together with rhodioloside, an active ingredient of <italic toggle="yes">R. rosea</italic> (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b1-dti-2-2007-039">Aksenova et al. 1968</xref>), were employed in this study.</p></sec><sec sec-type="materials|methods"><title>Materials and Methods</title><p>Details of the project were submitted to and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Armenian Drug and Medical Technology Agency of the Ministry of Health of the Republic of Armenia. The principles of laboratory animal care, as delineated in EEC Directive 75/318 (1994), were followed throughout the study.</p><sec sec-type="methods"><title>Study animals</title><p>Male Chinchilla rabbits were obtained from the breeding unit of the Institute of Fine Organic Chemistry of the National Academy of Science, Yerevan, Armenia. All animals were clinically examined upon arrival and any that showed signs of abnormality or disease were excluded.
