Although improvements in gingival inflammation were observed, they were not statistically significant compared to placebo. Future research without alcohol-based controls was recommended [<xref rid="B172-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">172</xref>]. Jung et al. (2024) evaluated a <italic toggle="yes">PMEC</italic> compound for moderate-to-severe gingivitis. Over eight weeks, <italic toggle="yes">PMEC</italic> significantly reduced inflammatory biomarkers in GCF, including IL-1β and PGE2, though clinical improvements in GI and BOP were modest. The authors demonstrated the significant reductions in gingival crevicular fluid biomarkers, by explaining the mechanism of inhibition that the <italic toggle="yes">PMEC</italic> complex explicates on the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β and PGE2) and metalloproteases (MMP-8 and MMP-9) in periodontal tissue cells infected with oral bacteria, including neutrophils, macrophages, and gingival fibroblasts; such inhibition, in fact, reduces the process of osteoclastogenesis and conversely promotes the osteogenetic one, thereby reducing alveolar bone destruction. However, <italic toggle="yes">PMEC</italic> does not prevent the direct invasion of periodontal pathogens into gingival tissue cells or the subsequent release of lipopolysaccharide (LPS) and exotoxins into the gingival capillaries, which is why the study still shows a discrepancy between results of clinical parameters in <italic toggle="yes">PMEC</italic>-treated and placebo-treated groups. Innovative Approaches and Phytocompounds</title><p>Aslan Kh. Sheregov et al. (2021) assessed <italic toggle="yes">standardized fermented papaya</italic> gel (<italic toggle="yes">SFPG</italic>) in moderate chronic PD associated with orthodontic treatment. <italic toggle="yes">SFPG</italic> significantly reduced inflammation and cytokine levels, emphasizing its antioxidant and antimicrobial properties as a topical adjunct to conventional therapy [<xref rid="B174-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">174</xref>]. Young Park et al. (2021) evaluated <italic toggle="yes">PMEC</italic> capsules in early gingivitis and PD. Over two months, <italic toggle="yes">PMEC</italic> reduced GI and IL-6 levels in GCF, indicating anti-inflammatory benefits. However, improvements in clinical parameters like periodontal depth were not statistically significant. The study highlighted the potential for systemic phytocompounds in periodontal therapy [<xref rid="B175-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">175</xref>]. Yu Rin Kim et al. (2022) examined a Sambucus williamsii var. coreana-based mouthwash. Significant reductions in periodontal indices and pathogenic bacteria were observed, showcasing the mouthwash’s immediate and progressive antibacterial effects. The study underscored the potential of natural alternatives to prevent and treat dental diseases [<xref rid="B176-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">176</xref>]. The reviewed studies collectively emphasize the potential of natural and herbal compounds in managing gingivitis and PD. Products like <italic toggle="yes">Camellia sinensis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Polyherbal mouthwashes, GSE</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Punica granatum</italic> demonstrate significant anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and antimicrobial properties. While many natural agents show promise as safe alternatives to conventional treatments like CHX, limitations include small sample sizes, short follow-up periods, and variations in study design. Further large-scale, long-term studies are necessary to validate their clinical efficacy and establish standardized protocols for integration into periodontal care. Nonetheless, these findings pave the way for innovative, plant-based solutions that address oral health challenges while minimizing side effects associated with synthetic agents [<xref rid="B177-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">177</xref>,<xref rid="B178-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">178</xref>,<xref rid="B179-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">179</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec4dot7-nutrients-17-00762"><title>4.7.
