Secondly, some of the extracts or preparations of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds were precipitating upon addition to the inoculated plates.</p></sec></sec><sec sec-type="conclusions" id="sec031"><title>Conclusion</title><p>The characterization of the phytochemical composition and antimicrobial activity of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds was carried out using different extracts. Medicinally active components of interest from <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds were successfully extracted using different solvents (petroleum spirit, ethyl acetate, methanol, water, and hexane), identified, quantified, and characterized using optimum methods or techniques.</p><p>The extraction of the oil from <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds was achieved by Soxhlet extraction method using two extractants namely, absolute ethanol and hexane which are known to be suitable when extracting oil for testing biological activity. The phytochemical screening investigations found most of the major bioactive phytochemicals in different extracts of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds and this ascertained their medicinal value. Regarding the determination of total phytochemical content, higher total phenolic and flavonoids contents were found in methanol extract compared to the aqueous extract of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds while, the total tannin content in the two extracts studied was the same. The TLC study provided a reliable experimental basis for further separation of phytochemicals from the methanol extracts of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds using appropriate solvents and this basis is consistent with a study done by Mengesha Yessuf [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref003" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>]. On the other hand, the FT-IR analysis of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds oil extracted using absolute ethanol revealed different functional groups of antimicrobial value and they can also play prominent roles in other biological activities of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds.</p><p>Based on the reports from literature on the bioactivities and/or medicinal applications of compounds detected by GC-MS in this study, oil extracted using hexane and methanol extract of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds can be exploited to promote the use of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds as natural curative remedies against predation by pathogens that have been linked with causing diseases in human and this was ascertained by the antimicrobial analysis. The GC-MS results also signify the use of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds in many pharmaceutical settings due to their diversity in composition however, there is a need to confirm the presence of these newly identified chemical constituents. The phytoconstituents and/or the complex interactions among other constituents present in the extracts and/or oil of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds may be responsible for their indispensable antimicrobial activity. Therefore, the seeds of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> can be used as the natural source of antimicrobial agents in the pursuit of searching for new antibiotics against human pathogens as it is evident with the present results.</p><sec id="sec032"><title>Statistical analysis</title><p>All experiments were performed in triplicate unless stated otherwise. Statistical analysis of the data was performed using Statistical Software for Social Sciences version (SPSS) version 24. A p value &lt;0.05 was regarded as significant.</p></sec></sec><sec id="sec033" sec-type="supplementary-material"><title>Supporting information</title><supplementary-material id="pone.0272457.s001" position="float" content-type="local-data" orientation="portrait"><label>S1 Fig</label><caption><title>TLC profile of methanol extract of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>.
