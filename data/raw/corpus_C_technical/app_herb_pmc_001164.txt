Most of this biomass is used for energy generation through incinerators and for animal feed [<xref rid="B68-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>]. Olive stones, constituting 10–15% of the weight of the olive, and still produced in the table olive industry, are commonly used in domestic boilers and power plants [<xref rid="B88-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">88</xref>]. Another use is in the creation of a biodegradable packaging called Oliplast, as part of the Spanish project GO-OLIVA [<xref rid="B89-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">89</xref>]. Olive mill wastewater is contingent on oil extraction processes, ranging from 85 to 110 kg for two-phase centrifugation to 1000–1200 kg for three-phase centrifugation [<xref rid="B65-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>]. The dispersion of this biowaste into the environment without prior treatment will cause significant issues due to high concentrations of toxic organic substances (chemical oxygen demand (COD) ranging from 50 to 200 g L<sup>−1</sup> and biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) ranging from 40 to 170 g L<sup>−1</sup>), and a relatively acidic pH, altering the color and odor of water resources, and presenting toxicity to aquatic organisms [<xref rid="B65-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>,<xref rid="B74-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>,<xref rid="B90-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">90</xref>]. Olive pomace production varies between 200 and 400 kg for pressing and 800–950 kg for two-phase centrifugation, featuring a pinkish paste-like appearance and slightly acidic pH (pH ≈ 5); it is composed of husk pieces, pulp, stone fragments and seeds, oil, and water [<xref rid="B42-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>,<xref rid="B65-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>,<xref rid="B91-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">91</xref>]. This biowaste is the most abundant, generating approximately 14.4 million tons in the 2024/25 harvest season [<xref rid="B66-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>,<xref rid="B92-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">92</xref>]. The pomace can be transported to other companies (pomace oil extractors), stored in large containers for spontaneous evaporation, and stored for extended periods until reused or treated [<xref rid="B65-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>,<xref rid="B93-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">93</xref>]. The increase in olive oil production is correlated with increased pomace, posing challenges to the industry, and complicating the continuous production of high-quality olive oil [<xref rid="B94-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>].</p><p><xref rid="molecules-31-00996-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref> shows that the by-products generated in olive oil production contain a wide range of bioactive compounds, with potential for use as biopesticides, as presented in <xref rid="molecules-31-00996-t003" ref-type="table">Table 3</xref>.</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot2-molecules-31-00996"><title>5.2. Potato Processing Waste</title><p>The potato (<italic toggle="yes">Solanum tuberosum</italic> L.) ranks among humanity’s most vital food crops, serving as a staple for approximately 1.3 billion individuals [<xref rid="B99-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>]. Its global production has soared, reaching a record 390 million metric tons in 2024, with Asian nations dominating, accounting for roughly 50%, led by China and India [<xref rid="B100-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">100</xref>]. Financial transactions surrounding this commodity amount to approximately US$111 billion [<xref rid="B100-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">100</xref>]. Boasting around 5000 varieties, potatoes exhibit unparalleled genetic diversity compared to other cultivated species [<xref rid="B99-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>].</p><p>The processing industry associated with this crop ranks among the largest in the global food sector. Approximately one-third of the total production is consumed fresh, while the remainder undergoes processing to meet the demands of convenience and fast-food consumption, including but not limited to: frozen fries, wedges, chips, starch, dehydrated potatoes, mashed potatoes, and frozen foods [<xref rid="B99-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>]. In the EU, as of 2023, processed potatoes hold a market value of around €9.7 billion, constituting roughly 2.1% of the European food industry’s value [<xref rid="B101-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">101</xref>].
