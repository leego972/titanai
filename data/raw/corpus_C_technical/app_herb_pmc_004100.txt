Eleutheroside E acts through G-protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) pathways, modulating cyclic AMP (cAMP), phospholipase C (PLC), and phosphatidylinositol signaling cascades [<xref rid="B4-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>].</p><p>Clinical studies have demonstrated that <italic toggle="yes">Eleutherococcus</italic> improves mental performance, increases adrenal cortical activity, and enhances metabolic intensity under both normal and non-stress conditions [<xref rid="B1-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B43-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>]. Supplementation with <italic toggle="yes">E. senticosus</italic> root extract (300–1200 mg/day) for 4–8 weeks significantly increases physical performance and VO<sub>2</sub>max and reduces fatigue in athletes and individuals with chronic fatigue [<xref rid="B43-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>]. These effects involve enhanced muscular glucose utilization, improved mitochondrial biogenesis, and activation of the AMPK signaling pathway [<xref rid="B46-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>].</p><p>Under stress conditions, <italic toggle="yes">Eleutherococcus</italic> reduces adrenal cortex and sympathetic nervous system activity, increases parasympathetic tone, and moderately enhances central nervous system excitation through modulation of GABAergic and glutamatergic neurotransmission [<xref rid="B1-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B43-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>]. This bidirectional regulatory effect on the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis is characteristic of adaptogens and distinguishes them from conventional stimulants [<xref rid="B1-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>]. Eleutheroside B inhibits the activation of MAP kinases (MAPKs), Akt, and NF-κB, leading to reduced levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α in models of neuroinflammation [<xref rid="B35-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>,<xref rid="B43-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>].</p><p>The neuroprotective activity of <italic toggle="yes">E. senticosus</italic> includes increased expression of brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), which promotes neurogenesis and synaptogenesis in the hippocampus [<xref rid="B43-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>]. Eleutheroside B exhibits strong antioxidant properties through activation of the Nrf2 pathway and upregulation of antioxidant enzymes, including superoxide dismutase (SOD), catalase, and glutathione peroxidase [<xref rid="B43-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>,<xref rid="B47-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>]. The antioxidant mechanism of eleutherosides E1 involves both direct free radical scavenging and stimulation of gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT), which explains their immunomodulatory effects [<xref rid="B47-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">E. senticosus</italic> modulates lipid and glucose metabolism by inhibiting serum superoxide dismutase activity, resulting in increased HDL-C levels and reductions in triglycerides, total cholesterol, and LDL-C in D-galactose-induced aging models [<xref rid="B47-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>]. Eleutheroside B inhibits cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) through hydrogen bonding with amino acid residues SER530, TYR355, and LEU352, as well as hydrophobic interactions, providing a molecular basis for its anti-inflammatory and analgesic effects [<xref rid="B48-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">48</xref>]. In silico studies have shown that eleutheroside B possesses drug-like properties and does not exhibit significant toxicity (toxicity class 5), hepatotoxicity, or cardiotoxicity [<xref rid="B48-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">48</xref>].</p><p>Caffeoylquinic acids, syringin (eleutheroside B), and syringaresinol derivatives are the main constituents responsible for the pharmacological effects of <italic toggle="yes">E. senticosus</italic> [<xref rid="B43-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>]. These compounds act synergistically by activating gene sets that are not affected by individual components alone, a hallmark of multicomponent adaptogenic preparations [<xref rid="B4-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>]. Studies in honeybees have demonstrated that <italic toggle="yes">E. senticosus</italic> root extract (0.4 mg/mL) is one of the most effective adaptogens in enhancing immune resistance and improving survival in nosemosis [<xref rid="B44-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>].</p><p>Clinically, <italic toggle="yes">E. senticosus</italic> reduces symptoms of asthenia by 25–40% compared with placebo, improves quality of life, and lowers serum cortisol levels after 4–8 weeks of supplementation [<xref rid="B43-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>].
