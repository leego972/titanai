This could explain the wide distribution of these compounds among halophytic grasses (<xref ref-type="app" rid="app1-ijms-20-01067">Table S1</xref> and <xref ref-type="fig" rid="ijms-20-01067-f002">Figure 2</xref>). PUFAs are also gaining attention of the scientific community due to their broad pharmacological properties [<xref rid="B73-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>]. An important aspect of these compounds is the direct link between dietary PUFAs’ content and lower blood cholesterol levels [<xref rid="B74-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>], which is observed for instance with linolenic acid (<bold>52</bold>) [<xref rid="B75-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>]. In addition, the antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, anti-osteoporosis, anticarcinogenic, neuroprotective and cardioprotective properties of linolenic acid have also been studied [<xref rid="B56-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>]. At the same time, linoleic acid (<bold>50</bold>), another polyunsaturated fatty acid, is involved in the synthesis of prostaglandins, thromboxanes and leukotriene, a fact recognized over the last century [<xref rid="B76-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>]. Several studies have also reported its antimicrobial [<xref rid="B77-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>] and anti-inflammatory activities [<xref rid="B78-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">78</xref>].</p><p>Regarding acylglycerols, these were only described in the aqueous extract of <italic toggle="yes">A. donax</italic> [<xref rid="B43-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>]; in total, 11 compounds were reported (<xref ref-type="app" rid="app1-ijms-20-01067">Table S1</xref>). Acylglycerols serve as storage of lipids and are of great nutritional value, being a common source of edible oils for alimentation and industrial purposes [<xref rid="B79-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">79</xref>]. Furthermore, monoacylglycerols seem to be overexpressed in salt stress conditions [<xref rid="B80-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>].</p><p>Concluding, all fatty acids have important roles in human physiology [<xref rid="B81-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>]. Halophytic grasses seem to be rich in these compounds, especially <italic toggle="yes">A. donax</italic>, since 21 fatty acids were reported in its extracts. This data supports the use of this species for alimentation purposes as well as a new source of bioactive molecules for the pharmaceutical industry. The requirement of fatty acids for resistance to salt stress could enhance the production of these compounds, making halophytic grasses a remarkable source of them. Nonetheless, more studies need to be conducted in order to fully comprehend the presence of this class of compounds in halophytes.</p></sec><sec id="sec2dot3-ijms-20-01067"><title>2.3.
