</p></sec><sec id="Sec6"><title>Quantitative ethnobotanical analysis</title><p id="Par46">To assess the reliability, depth, and consensus of traditional medicinal plant knowledge in Sheikh District, several established quantitative ethnobotanical indices were applied, including the Relative Frequency of Citation (RFC), Fidelity Level (FL), Cultural Value Index (CV), and Informant Consensus Factor (ICF). These indices provide objective measures of the cultural significance, agreement among informants, and therapeutic relevance of medicinal plant species within the local community.</p><p id="Par47">Participatory ranking methods, such as preference ranking and direct matrix ranking, were also employed to capture local perceptions of plant efficacy and to prioritize species according to their cultural and therapeutic importance. This integrative approach allowed for the identification of culturally valued species, assessment of consensus among informants, and recognition of MPs with potential ecological and pharmacological significance.</p><p id="Par48">The Relative Frequency of Citation (RFC) was calculated for each species to quantify its local prominence and cultural importance [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR30">30</xref>]. RFC represents the proportion of informants who mentioned a particular species for the treatment of one or more human ailments, providing an indicator of how widely recognized and commonly utilized a species is within traditional healthcare practices. These quantitative measures complement qualitative ethnobotanical data, enabling a robust understanding of the relative significance of MPs in Sheikh District. In contrast, values approaching 1 indicate that a species was cited by a large proportion of informants, reflecting widespread knowledge, frequent use, and high cultural relevance within the community.
