In art conservation, OCT has been applied for assessment of the quality and thickness of the varnish layer over paint layers on paintings, ceramics, identification of underdrawings and topography measurements of surfaces (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b13">Hughes, 2010</xref>). In dentistry, it was found useful in the characterisation of gaps between different materials employed in dental constructs, such as polymers, ceramics and metal (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b22">Sinescu <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, 2008</xref>).</p></sec></sec><sec sec-type="conclusions"><title>Conclusions</title><p>Twenty years after the introduction of the OCT (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b11">Huang <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>., 1991</xref>), advancement of OCT technology continues to be an active field of research. In great proportion, the progress in recent years is due to the continuous attention given to the field of optical sources for OCT. This is somehow expected due to the essential dependence of OCT performance to the optical source in terms of depth resolution. In SS-OCT, the source determines not only the depth resolution, but also the scanning protocols and the acquisition speed. Fast SSs, based on a variety of principles such as that of Micro-Electro-Mechanical Systems (MEMS)/Vertical cavity surface emitting lasers (VCSELs) (Jayaraman <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, 2011), ring lasers equipped with a fast tuning filter (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b27">Wieser <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>., 2010</xref>) and dispersive cavity mode-locked lasers (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b16">Lee <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, 2011</xref>) have been reported with tuning rates exceeding hundred of kHz making the SS-OCT method the fastest.</p><p>Novel concepts have been put forward, which straddle the delimitation between the optical source and the optical configuration. For instance, by stretching the supercontinuum and using a dispersive element, such as a dispersion compensating fibre, a SS is obtained. By using Raman amplification with dispersion compensating fibre in both interferometer arms (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b8">Goda <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>., 2008</xref>), the fastest A-scan rate was reported, of 36 MHz.</p><p>Another focus is the combination of OCT with other imaging techniques and combination of OCT with assistive technologies such as adaptive optics and tracking (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b19">Podoleanu &amp; Rosen, 2008</xref>). Any flying spot OCT is assembled around a confocal microscope and therefore, several versions of combined technologies have been reported for dual imaging systems, OCT/confocal microscopy, some providing three channels, with a 3rd channel tuned on fluorescence. A typical example is that of combining OCT with scanning laser ophthalmoscopy (SLO), that resulted in the imaging instrument known as OCT/SLO for imaging the eye. This was extended from TD-<italic toggle="yes">en face</italic> OCT, where both images where C-scans to SD-OCT, where the SLO channel continues to provide a C-scan whereas the OCT channel delivers a B-scan along selected lines from the SLO image.</p><p>Combination of assistive techniques with OCT improves the operation of the OCT channel and enhances the accuracy of the information provided. Techniques of adaptive optics corrected wavefronts have been incorporated into both TD and SD-OCT systems with applications in ophthalmology and microscopy. Tracking proved essential in imaging the eye to take advantage of the high resolution achievable with OCT (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b19">Podoleanu &amp; Rosen 2008</xref>).</p><p>A present trend is in combining OCT with photoacoustics, as a complementary technique (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b29">Zhang <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, 2010</xref>). OCT delivers a structure image, based on discontinuities in the real part of the indices of refraction at interfaces. Photoacoustics is sensitive to absorption changes, therefore providing images sensitive to the imaginary part of the index of refraction, and in this way, adding information on function to that of structure provided by the OCT.</p><p>For its advantage of speed, it is envisaged that SD-OCT will dominate the imaging of moving organs and is more suitable for hand held probes. Where transversal resolution is paramount and the object is stationary, then TD-OCT is superior, due to its compatibility with dynamic focus.
