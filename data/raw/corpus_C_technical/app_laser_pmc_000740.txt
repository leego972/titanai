2022</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><sec id="Sec1"><title>Introduction</title><p id="Par2">Oral leukoplakia (OL), according to the World Health Organization (WHO), is defined as a mainly white patch, plaque, or lesion on the oral mucosa that cannot be certainly classified clinically or histologically as any other ascertainable disorder<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR1">1</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR4">4</xref></sup>. OL is a well-known oral potentially malignant disorder (OPMD) with a potential risk of malignancy. It has been reported that 15.8–48.0% of patients with oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) have a history of OL when diagnosed<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR5">5</xref></sup>. According to a systematic review of 24 retrospective studies, the prevalence rate of OL is 0.13–34%<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR6">6</xref></sup>.</p><p id="Par3">The main goal of the treatment of OL is to prevent malignant transformation and even reduce or eliminate lesions<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR7">7</xref></sup>. There are probably two traditional methods to treat OL, namely, conservative treatment and surgical resection. Medical interventions, such as vitamin A, retinoids, beta-carotene or bleomycin cannot reduce the possibility of oral cancer development<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR8">8</xref></sup> and have low efficiency with a long treatment time<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR1">1</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">9</xref></sup>. Therefore, surgical methods, including conventional excision, cryosurgery therapy, electrocauterization and various forms of laser ablation, are the preferred choice in OL management for many clinicians<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR8">8</xref></sup>.</p><p id="Par4">However, when the diseased area extends to multiple locations or is located in potentially important functional areas, surgical methods may be impractical, as they can cause large tissue defects, therefore influencing function and aesthetics<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR7">7</xref></sup>. Additionally, data have shown that OL has a 13–42% recurrence rate after surgical removal<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR1">1</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">9</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR10">10</xref></sup>. Photodynamic therapy (PDT) is a less invasive method that can successfully be applied in the treatment of premalignant and malignant disorders of the skin, digestive tract, lungs, and genitourinary mucosa with vastly reduced morbidity and disfigurement. Since PDT is a cold photochemical process, there is no tissue heating and a much lower risk of damaging the integrity of the underlying functional structures compared to thermal laser techniques and other invasive approaches.
