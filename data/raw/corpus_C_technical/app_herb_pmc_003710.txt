Deep Eutectic Solvents</title><p>Recently, DESs have become a very popular topic in research and general chemistry. The product consists of a mixture of HBDs, such as sugars, acids, alcohols, carboxylic acids, amines, and HBAs, including quaternary ammonium salts (e.g., betaine or choline chloride), which are combined together through hydrogen bonding interaction [<xref rid="B18-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>]. DESs can be divided into four main groups: halide and quaternary salts (type I), hydrated metal halide and quaternary salt (type II), hydrogen bond donor and quaternary salt (type III) and hydrogen bond donor and metal halide (type IV) [<xref rid="B19-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>]. Choline chloride is used the most frequently as an HBD, in addition to urea, glycerol, and citric acid. As a new approach, organic substances, including glucose and inorganic salts, have been used to prepare deep eutectic solvents. An HBA and HBD form one chemical compound, with non-symmetric, large ions, and low lattice energy, which results in a new characteristic of the composed substance [<xref rid="B18-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>].</p><p>DESs can be prepared with the use of a variety of methods including freeze-drying, grinding, and evaporation. Different types of DES preparation and its characteristics are shown in <xref rid="molecules-29-04767-t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref>.</p><p>There are also different types of deep eutectic solvents. One of the most commonly used types is the natural deep eutectic solvents (NADESs), which are formed from naturally obtained precursors. NADESs can be obtained by mixing together HBDs, such as amino acids, sugars, and carboxylic acids with HBAs, including lactic acids, betaine hydrochloride, and choline chloride. They exhibit valuable properties, e.g., chemical and thermal stability, non-flammability, and non-volatility below atmospheric conditions with lower viscosities [<xref rid="B23-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>].</p><p>Another group of DESs is formed by therapeutic deep eutectic solvents (THEDESs), in which one of the components consists of an active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) and importantly is also liquid at room temperature. THEDESs can be widely used for pharmaceutical procedures such as drug development to improve the permeation, absorption, or solubility of a compound. They are prepared with the use of both synthetic polymers and also biopolymers [<xref rid="B24-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>].</p><p>Polymeric deep eutectic solvents (PODESs) form another important group of DESs and they are mostly used for the delivery of active substances. They exhibit a lot of advantages, such as good biodegradability and low cost; however, they show high viscosity even showing a solid state at room temperatures [<xref rid="B25-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>]. Their general advantages are presented in <xref rid="molecules-29-04767-f002" ref-type="fig">Figure 2</xref>.</p><p>The physicochemical properties of DESs are a very valuable topic when discovering new substances. DESs can exhibit unique characteristics depending on the used precursors for their preparation. Density and viscosity are some of the most important parameters, which can dictate the application route of solution; however, surface tension, melting point, pH polarity, refractive index, and miscibility also play an important role in describing DESs [<xref rid="B26-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>,<xref rid="B27-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>]. Different properties with their descriptions are shown in <xref rid="molecules-29-04767-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>.</p><p>DESs can be used as media for enzymatic and chemical reactions, carriers of active compounds, antimicrobial and antioxidant agents, as well as in the extraction of active compounds from plants including oils [<xref rid="B30-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>,<xref rid="B31-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>,<xref rid="B32-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>]. Many different application routes are shown in <xref rid="molecules-29-04767-f003" ref-type="fig">Figure 3</xref>.
