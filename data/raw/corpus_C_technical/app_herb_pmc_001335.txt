Bioactive Compounds in Amaranthus</title><sec id="sec6dot1-nutrients-17-01749"><title>6.1. Phenolic Compounds</title><p>Amaranth is a versatile plant renowned for its rich content of phenolic compounds, which impart significant antioxidant and health-promoting properties. These bioactive compounds, distributed across the seeds, leaves, and stems of various <italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus</italic> spp., include both phenolic acids and flavonoids, with concentrations that vary depending on species, environmental factors, and extraction methods. A study involving nine commercially available amaranth samples, sourced from Russia, Peru, and India, analyzed their phenolic acid content. Among the identified phenolic compounds caffeic, ferulic, and p-coumaric acids are noted for their ability to neutralize free radicals [<xref rid="B10-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>]. Flavonoids, a subset of phenolic compounds, are also highly abundant in amaranth, with rutin, nicotiflorin, quercetin, and isoquercetin [<xref rid="B88-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">88</xref>,<xref rid="B89-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">89</xref>].</p><p>Different species of amaranth showcase unique phenolic profiles. For instance, the ethanolic extract of <italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus retroflexus</italic> leaves contains substantial polyphenolic (0.228 mg/mL) and flavonoid content (2.1 × 10<sup>−4</sup> mg/mL), surpassing those found in the seeds of other varieties such as “Lera” and “Ultra” [<xref rid="B90-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">90</xref>]. In <italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus tricolor</italic>, the presence of flavonoids like kaempferol and quercetin and phenolic acids like gallic acid contribute to robust biological activities [<xref rid="B91-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">91</xref>]. Similarly, <italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus spinosus</italic> leaves contain an array of phenolic compounds, including flavonoids and tannins, as confirmed using FT-IR analysis [<xref rid="B92-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">92</xref>,<xref rid="B93-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">93</xref>]. In <italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus cruentus</italic>, compounds such as caffeoylsaccharic acid isomer, quercetin 3-O-rhamnosyl-rhamnosyl-glucoside, and kaempferol rutinoside are concentrated in the leaves, with rutin being particularly abundant in the vegetative parts [<xref rid="B94-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>]. <xref rid="nutrients-17-01749-f003" ref-type="fig">Figure 3</xref> focuses on the comparative distribution of these phenolic compounds across different <italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus</italic> spp. and plant tissues, highlighting the diversity and relative abundance of flavonoids and phenolic acids. These variations in phenolic compound distribution and concentration across species and plant parts are visually summarized in <xref rid="nutrients-17-01749-f003" ref-type="fig">Figure 3</xref>. Germinated amaranth protein concentrate from <italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus hypochondriacus</italic> further enhances bioactivity by releasing phenolics like methyl gallate and syringic acid [<xref rid="B95-nutrients-17-01749" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>].</p><p>A recent study analyzed 120 accessions across the leaves of 9 <italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus</italic> species and found substantial diversity in the content of 17 polyphenolic compounds. Rutin emerged as the dominant compound, with levels varying significantly between species and years. Strong positive correlations were observed among certain compounds (e.g., rutin and kaempferol-3-O-β-rutinoside), while gallic acid showed consistent negative correlations.
