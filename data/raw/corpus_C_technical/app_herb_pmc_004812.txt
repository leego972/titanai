Cardiovascular disease</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> was observed to prevent stress-induced cardiac damage [<xref rid="b9-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. The cardioprotective effects of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic>, including a pronounced antiarrhythmic effect [<xref rid="b9-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>], the prevention of reduced coronary flow, and an increase in contractility in the postischemic period, were observed in animals [<xref rid="b89-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">89</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> was ascertained to prevent both stress-induced catecholamine release and elevation of cAMP levels in the myocardium [<xref rid="b9-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. Moreover, it lowered blood pressure [<xref rid="b90-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">90</xref>] and prevented stress-induced cardiac damage, indicating its critical role as a cardioprotective agent in animals [<xref rid="b8-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>].</p><p>The rhizome of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola kirilowii</italic> significantly increased the expression of von Willebrand factor in the infarct border zone and noninfarct zone in the myocardium of rats [<xref rid="b91-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">91</xref>]. The expression of HIF-1α, HIF-1β, and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) mRNAs as well as HIF-1α and VEGF proteins was significantly increased in a <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola kirilowii</italic> group [<xref rid="b91-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">91</xref>].</p><p>Salidroside exhibited activity similar to that of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola kirilowii</italic>, increasing the expression of HIF-1α, HIF-1β, and VEGF during ischemia and hypoxia [<xref rid="b91-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">91</xref>]. In addition, salidroside protected human endothelial cells (EVC-304) from H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub>-induced oxidative damage in a dose-dependent manner [<xref rid="b92-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">92</xref>]. Salidroside inhibited the activation of caspase-3, caspase-9, cleavage of poly(ADP-ribose)polymerase, and Bax induced by endogenous H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub> [<xref rid="b92-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">92</xref>].</p><p>Salidroside and tyrosol dose-dependently inhibited nuclear condensation in H9c2 cells [<xref rid="b93-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">93</xref>]. Furthermore, salidroside and tyrosol, separately and in combination, significantly reduced caspase-3 activity, cytochrome c release, and JNK activation. The antiapoptotic effect of the combination was markedly higher than that of salidroside and tyrosol alone [<xref rid="b93-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">93</xref>]. The inhibition of the JNK-signaling pathway is the key mechanism for the cytoprotective effects of salidroside and tyrosol in ischemia-reperfusion-induced apoptosis in H9c2 cells [<xref rid="b93-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">93</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> plants, salidroside, and tyrosol may facilitate preventing and treating oxidative stress in cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases.</p></sec><sec sec-type="other"><title>13. DM</title><p>DM is associated with increased oxidative stress. Rats with streptozotocin (STZ)-induced DM experienced heart failure caused by increased PPARδ expression. The ethanol extract of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> increased PPARδ expression and cardiac output in STZ-diabetic rats [<xref rid="b94-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>]. Salidroside administered to mice daily (50 mg/kg, 100 mg/kg, and 200 mg/kg for 28 days) was demonstrated to cause hypoglycemic activity and have protective effects against DM-induced oxidative stress, including significantly reduced fasting blood glucose, total cholesterol, triglyceride, and MDA levels [<xref rid="b95-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>]. In addition, it increased serum insulin, SOD, and glutathione peroxidase levels as well as catalase activity in the liver in mice [<xref rid="b95-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>].
