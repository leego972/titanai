Title: Underpricing Effects in ICOs

Abstract:
Underpricing is found in initial public offerings (IPOs) when share price increases after trading first commences. The literature prevailingly holds asymmetric information as the key determinant. The rise of cryptocurrencies has led to the similar phenomenon of initial coin offerings (ICOs). ICO underpricing is evident but the IPO literature has barely been applied. Only Benedetti and Kostovetsky (2018) examine effects of nominal price, presales and information asymmetry. This thesis is the first to investigate ICO-specific underpricing determinants. Sample mean underpricing is 1543.71% on the first day. It is found that reaching a hard cap suggests higher true value and leads to more sustained long-run performance. Proportional sales lead to higher underpricing after reevaluation once price is set, while presales and bonuses have opposite effects with presales signalizing higher true value and bonuses compensating otherwise. The presence of a white paper seems to be insignificant. Lastly, the findings of Benedetti and Kostovetsky (2018) regarding asymmetric information are disputed. The analysis determines that explanations of IPO underpricing, such as those by Welch (1989) and Rock (1986), are fully applicable to ICOs as information asymmetry clearly affects the degree of ICO underpricing.
