Aggressive ulcerative and destructive forms and pigmented BCCs are other variants [<xref rid="B6-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="B8-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>].</p><p>SCC derives from the keratinocytes of the spinous layer of the epidermis (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="ijms-16-25912-f001">Figure 1</xref>). Although it has a lower frequency in relation to BCC, SCC is more aggressive and has a major capacity of metastasis, thus being responsible for most deaths associated with NMSC. It also appears in photoexposed areas such as the head, neck and extremities and its incidence increases with age [<xref rid="B4-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B10-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>,<xref rid="B11-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>,<xref rid="B12-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>,<xref rid="B13-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>]. Most cases of SCC derive from chronic UV-radiation exposure being the premalignant lesion actinic keratosis (AK). Otherwise, Bowen Disease (BD) or <italic toggle="yes">SCC in situ</italic>, could evolve into an invasive SCC (iSCC) in some cases. In contrast to BD, in iSCC atypical keratinocytes are present beyond the basement membrane, in the dermis or deeper, and it could metastasize. On the other hand, SCC is the most common malignant tumor in transplant patients, who present multiple lesions and with more aggressive behavior than in the general population [<xref rid="B14-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>].</p><fig id="ijms-16-25912-f001" position="float" orientation="portrait"><label>Figure 1</label><caption><p>Formation process, clinical and histological appearance of basal cell carcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma.</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="ijms-16-25912-g001.jpg"/></fig><p>AK is a cutaneous lesion and develops mostly in fair skinned patients susceptible to solar damage. Its frequency is directly related to cumulative exposure of UV, and therefore, usually develops on sun-exposed areas (face, forearms, upper back and legs). AK is often multiple and usually found in an area of “cancerization field” that defines the skin around AK, which provides the basis for clonal expansion of genetically altered neoplastic cells [<xref rid="B4-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>]. SCC and AK essentially represent the same disease process but at different stages of evolution [<xref rid="B15-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>,<xref rid="B16-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>]. It is estimated that between 5% and 10% of AK progress to BD or SCC [<xref rid="B6-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="B17-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec2-ijms-16-25912"><title>2. Photodynamic Therapy for Non-Melanoma Skin Cancer</title><p>There are several classical therapies for the different types of NMSC. In general, the most widely extended is surgery but other treatments are: (1) for BCC curettage and electrodessication, radiotherapy, cryotherapy, photodynamic therapy (PDT), laser, topical (imiquimod and 5-fluorouracil), and systemic treatments (vismodegib and sonidegib [<xref rid="B18-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>]; and (2) for SCC cryosurgery, electrosurgery and radiotherapy; topical treatments such as 5-fluorouracil and imiquimod or PDT are only recommended for SCC <italic toggle="yes">in situ</italic>. Regarding AK cryotherapy, PDT, topical 5-fluorouracil (5%, 1%, 0.5%), imiquimod (5%, 3.75%), ingenol mebutate (0.05%, 0.015%), diclofenac, topical retinoids, chemical peels, electrosurgery or lasers are used to treat isolated or multiple lesions [<xref rid="B19-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>].</p><p>Among all the alternative treatments used for non-melanoma skin cancer, we highlight PDT not only because of its high efficacy (strength of recommendation A, quality of evidence I for AK, BD and BCC) but also for its good cosmetic outcome [<xref rid="B1-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B6-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="B20-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>,<xref rid="B21-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>,<xref rid="B22-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>,<xref rid="B23-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>,<xref rid="B24-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>,<xref rid="B25-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>]. As it has those advantages, PDT treatment has been extensively developed as a new modality and an alternative to classical ones such as surgery, radiation or chemotherapy, being particularly suitable for treating multiple lesions and field cancerization [<xref rid="B26-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>].
