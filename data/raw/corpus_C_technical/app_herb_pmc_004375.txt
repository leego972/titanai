Introduction</title><p>Advantages of network pharmacology and the systems biology approach vs. the ligand–receptor-based reductionist concept were discussed recently in several reviews [<xref rid="B1-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B2-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="B3-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B4-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B5-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>]. Pharmacology concerns drug action on physiological systems for therapeutic benefits, focusing on theories, procedures, and mechanisms related to the chemical control of physiological processes. Pharmacology aims to define the molecular events initiating drug effects at the pharmacological targets in therapeutic and other systems. The term “pharmacological target” refers to biomolecules, such as DNA, mRNA, and proteins, including transmembrane and nuclear receptors, ion channels, transport proteins, and numerous enzymes to which a drug binds first in the body to elicit its pharmacologic effect (<xref rid="pharmaceuticals-15-01051-f001" ref-type="fig">Figure 1</xref>).</p><p>The complementary binding of drug molecules to a protein with a physiological purpose in the cell can change physiological response and result in a pharmacologic effect. The efficacy of addressing a drug (ligand) to its specific biomolecule (receptor) depends on the drug’s chemical structure and affinity.</p><p>Historically, the drug development strategy assumed that a single target mechanism of action is the best option to obtain the target-specific therapeutic, which is selective for treating specific conditions and free of adverse events. However, many drugs and natural compounds interact and bind with multiple receptors (multitarget interaction), resulting in polyvalent pharmacological action and pleiotropic therapeutic activity. For example, the polytropic therapeutic activity of ginseng and some natural compounds or “Ginseng-like” plant extracts, e.g., <italic toggle="yes">Eleutherococcus senticosus</italic> (Rupr. and Maxim.) Maxim., <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> L., <italic toggle="yes">Withania somnifera</italic> (L.) Dunal., <italic toggle="yes">Schisandra chinensis</italic> (Turcz.) Baill. <italic toggle="yes">Andrographis paniculata</italic> (Burm. f.) Nees, <italic toggle="yes">Rhaponticum cartamoides</italic> Iljin, and <italic toggle="yes">Bryonia alba</italic> L., collectively known as adaptogens, are associated with their polyvalent modes of action on the neuroendocrine-immune complex (stress system), multitarget effects on adaptive stress response signaling pathways, and molecular networks allied with these pharmacological targets [<xref rid="B5-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>,<xref rid="B6-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="B7-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>,<xref rid="B8-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>,<xref rid="B9-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>].
