</p></sec><sec id="Sec14"><title>Plant parts used for medicinal purposes</title><p id="Par85">Traditional remedies in the Karamara forest patches employed a variety of plant parts, including leaves, roots, bark, latex, seeds, fruits, stems, flowers, and whole plants. Leaves were the most commonly used, followed by roots and latex, reflecting their accessibility, ease of processing, and high concentration of bioactive compounds. Other parts fruits, seeds, stems, whole plants, and bark were used less frequently (Fig. <xref rid="Fig7" ref-type="fig">7</xref>), highlighting a preference for plant tissues that balance therapeutic efficacy with sustainable harvesting practices.</p><p id="Par86">

</p></sec><sec id="Sec15"><title>Methods of medicinal plant Preparation</title><p id="Par87">The local community of the Karamara forest employs a diverse array of preparation techniques tailored to the plant part, therapeutic target, and species characteristics.</p><p id="Par88">The most frequently used method is crushing or homogenizing plant parts with water, applied to leaves, roots, or fruits of species such as <italic toggle="yes">Aloe megalacantha</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Carica papaya</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Cissus quadrangularis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Leucas jamesii</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Moringa stenopetala</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Ricinus communis</italic>. This approach is commonly used to prepare decoctions, infusions, or juices for oral administration, primarily targeting gastrointestinal, respiratory, and febrile ailments.</p><p id="Par89">Topical application of crushed or pounded material is widely practiced for species including <italic toggle="yes">Abutilon fruticosum</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus dubius</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Tagetes minuta</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Cynoglossum amabile</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Verbascum sinaiticum</italic>. The resulting paste is applied directly to wounds, insect bites, or skin infections, underscoring the importance of external treatments in local ethnomedicine.</p><p id="Par90">Direct consumption or chewing of plant parts, such as gums, seeds, fruits, or leaves, is common for species like <italic toggle="yes">Acacia nilotica</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Acacia nubica</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Cordeauxia edulis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Dovyalis abyssinica</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Myrsine africana</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Opuntia ficus-indica</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Psidium guajava</italic>.
