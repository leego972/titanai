However, the lack of standards of some compounds is a limiting factor of HPLC viability. Thus, to improve phytochemical detection and quantification, the combination of HPLC with the highly sensitive method MS (LC–MS) is needed as a rapid, selective and sensitive approach [<xref rid="B35-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>,<xref rid="B115-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">115</xref>]. Yet, due to its insufficient precision and accuracy to identify some GLS peaks (e.g., similar <italic toggle="yes">m</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">z</italic> of both glucoiberin (<italic toggle="yes">m</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">z</italic> 422.0255) and gluconasturtiin (<italic toggle="yes">m</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">z</italic> 422.0585)) and the lack of reference materials, LC–MS use remains limited [<xref rid="B111-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">111</xref>]. LC–tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS) can resolve this issue since fragmentation improves both selectivity and sensitivity [<xref rid="B90-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">90</xref>,<xref rid="B103-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">103</xref>]. Recently, ultra-HPLC–MS/MS (UHPLC–MS/MS) data were used to create a new platform (GLS-Finder) useful for intact GLS identification in 49 popular <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> vegetables [<xref rid="B116-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">116</xref>]. However, certain reviewers stated that the number of GLS/isomer chromatographic peaks was higher than the known GLS number of every extract, which should be verified with the published compositions [<xref rid="B91-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">91</xref>,<xref rid="B117-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">117</xref>].</p><p>Moreover, NMR has been utilized as the most effective technique for
