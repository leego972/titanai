# PMC: ethnobotany medicinal plants Africa Asia — Overview

The individual herder is responsible for the well-being of the livestock and uses plants found in the grazing area in addition to the knowledge passed from their parents to manage animal health conditions [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">9</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR54">54</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR58">58</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR112">112</xref>].</p><p id="Par40">Most of the medicinal plants used for the preparation of remedies were shrubs and trees that are available throughout the year. The shrubs and trees are dominantly used for the preparation of medications in most sub-arid and arid regions since the plants survive and are available in dry seasons. Trees and shrubs are used in the preparation of medications in pastoralists area such as in Borena pastoralists, 56% of remedies are prepared from trees and shrubs, and in Ada’ar District, Afar Reginal state 67.3% of ethnoveterinary plants are shrubs [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">9</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR34">34</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR41">41</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR43">43</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR48">48</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR68">68</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR72">72</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR113">113</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR120">120</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR123">123</xref>]. In Yalow Woreda, the majority of remedies are prepared from fresh leaves; similar to the reports by most of the ethnobotanical studies conducted in Ethiopia and elsewhere [9,34,35 43 53,60,71,74,76] though some societies in Ethiopia prefer to use root [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR46">46</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR47">47</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR69">69</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR72">72</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR89">89</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR114">114</xref>] and Karamoja pastoralist uses bark for the preparation of remedies [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR36">36</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR57">57</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR58">58</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR69">69</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR78">78</xref>]. The uses of the leaf would better protect the plants than roots unless the people considers in using lateral roots than the taproots, which enable the plants to draw water from depth. On the other hand, excessive defoliation could also endanger the regeneration and survival of the plants in semi-arid and arid areas [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR17">17</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR60">60</xref>]. Fresh plant parts and leaf are predominantly used in traditional treatments in Ethiopia. The use of fresh parts lessens the depletion of ingredients that affects the healing potential of the remedies through drying and storage. The people, when using fresh plants, they assume that ingredients in the plant are not lost through drying and curing potential is more than dried once [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR35">35</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR41">41</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR46">46</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR76">76</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR77">77</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR115">115</xref>].</p><p id="Par41">The practice of single plant for remedy preparation is common than a combination treatment in many studies conducted in Ethiopia and elsewhere [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">9</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR30">30</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR58">58</xref>] Nevertheless, in some regions such as in Gindeberet district, Western Ethiopia, 94% of the preparations are made from a mixture of multiple plant species [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR41">41</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR49">49</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR54">54</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR58">58</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR71">71</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR82">82</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR113">113</xref>]. The number of plants in multiple plant treatment ranged from two to ten and administered to disease where single plant preparations effectiveness is low. The people in the area perceived that use of multiple plants in preparation of ethnomedicine adds up the curing potential and confer synergetic effects [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR58">58</xref>]. On the other hand, the use of multiple plants to treat a disease is an indication of the prevalence and severity of illness in the region [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR41">41</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR56">56</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR58">58</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR113">113</xref>]. The most popular plants used in multiple plant preparations in Yalo Woreda and other societies are <italic toggle="yes">A. fruticosa</italic> [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR53">53</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR104">104</xref>], <italic toggle="yes">A. tortilis</italic> [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR20">20</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR54">54</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR64">64</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR81">81</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR115">115</xref>], <italic toggle="yes">B.
