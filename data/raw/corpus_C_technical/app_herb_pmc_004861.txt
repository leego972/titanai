These studies are in line with our hypothesis on the mechanism of ATP-generation by adaptogens and their potential benefits in fatigue and aging-related diseases.</p><p>The brain’s prefrontal cortex cells are known to contain hyperpolarization-activated channels that can open when they are exposed to cAMP in stress. Excessive opening of these channels impairs cognitive function. It has been suggested that cAMP inhibitors can inactivate the channels allowing for normal neuron function. This reconnects the hyperpolarized cells to the neural network, and thus improves working memory, which plays a key role in abstract thinking, planning, organizing, etc. (Wang et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B126">2007</xref>; Arnsten, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B4">2011</xref>). Adaptogens may help in therapies for cognitive deficits associated with normal aging, and for cognitive changes associated with schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, or attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD; Wang et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B125">2011</xref>).</p><p>This hypothesis is in accordance with our findings and publications, where ADAPT-232 and other adaptogens have demonstrated beneficial effects on cognitive function in humans (Bogatova et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B11">1997</xref>; Hartz et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B40">2004</xref>; Narimanian et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B76">2005</xref>; Darbinyan et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B23">2007</xref>; Fintelmann and Gruenwald, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B35">2007</xref>; Weng et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B128">2007</xref>; Olsson et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B80">2009</xref>; Aslanyan et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B6">2010</xref>; Panossian and Wikman, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B93">2010</xref>; Edwards et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B29">2012</xref>). Two clinical pilot studies showed that ADAPT-232 significantly improved attention and increased speed and accuracy during stressful cognitive tasks in comparison to a placebo control (Aslanyan et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B6">2010</xref>). ADAPT-232 significantly decreased the number of mistakes made by test subjects in complicated psychometric tests (Bogatova et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B11">1997</xref>) and improved the quality-of-life and recovery period of patients suffering from acute non-specific pneumonia (Narimanian et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B76">2005</xref>).</p></sec><sec><title>Adaptogen effects on G-protein signaling phosphatidylinositol and phospholipase C pathways</title><p>All tested adaptogens up-regulate the <italic toggle="yes">PLCB1</italic> gene, which encodes phosphoinositide-specific PLC and the <italic toggle="yes">PI3KC2G</italic> gene, which encodes phosphatidylinositol 3-kinases (PI3Ks; Table <xref ref-type="table" rid="T6">6</xref>; Figure <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F3">3</xref>).</p><p>When activated by a G-protein, PLC catalyses hydrolysis of phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate (PIP2) into diacylglycerol (DAG) and inositol-1,4,5-triphosphate (IP<sub>3</sub>; Figure <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F3">3</xref>; Gilman, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B37">1987</xref>). IP<sub>3</sub> is involved in a variety of cellular signaling pathways associated with phenomena as diverse as depression and tumor growth (Stutzmann, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B114">2005</xref>; Sarkisov and Wang, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B104">2008</xref>). DAG activates protein kinase C (PKC), which phosphorylates numerous other proteins (Nishizuka, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B78">1995</xref>) and plays an important role in tumor growth (Yamasaki et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B135">2009</xref>).</p><p>PI3K is a key upstream mediator of signal transduction and plays an important role in the regulation of NF-kB-mediated defense responses as well as apoptosis. PI3K is required for long-term enhancement of signal transmission between neurons, which is associated with potentiating memory and learning (Opazo et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B81">2003</xref>; Karpova et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B53">2006</xref>; Yang et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B136">2008</xref>).</p></sec><sec><title>Common effects on gene expression involved in regulation of cytoplasmatic and nuclear proteins</title><p>ADAPT-232 up-regulated the <italic toggle="yes">SERPINI1</italic> gene (serpin peptidase inhibitor, neuroserpin), which is involved in the development and function of the nervous system (Teesalu et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B117">2004</xref>; Yepes and Lawrence, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B137">2004</xref>).
