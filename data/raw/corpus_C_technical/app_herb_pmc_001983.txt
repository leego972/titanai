Therefore, conclusions based solely on GC-MS data require confirmation by complementary analytical techniques such as NMR or LC-MS to ensure structural accuracy. Despite their minor abundance, these metabolites may contribute substantially to the plant’s biological activity and adaptive responses to environmental stressors.</p><p>Among these newly identified compounds is loliolide (<bold>49</bold>), a norisoprenoid lactone known for its potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anti-aging properties. While loliolide is commonly found in marine algae [<xref rid="B121-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">121</xref>] and was recently confirmed in <italic toggle="yes">Camellia ptilosperma</italic> leaves [<xref rid="B122-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">122</xref>], its detection in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> is uncommon. This suggests possible chemotypic variability or previously unrecognized biosynthetic pathways within the Araliaceae family. Loliolide efficiently scavenges reactive oxygen species (DPPH, H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub>), protects cells against oxidative damage, and exerts cytoprotective effects in both in vitro and in vivo models (including Vero cells and zebrafish embryos) [<xref rid="B123-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">123</xref>]. It also acts as a natural herbivore resistance inducer, functioning independently of the jasmonic acid pathway [<xref rid="B124-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">124</xref>], and enhances dermal papilla cell activity via AKT/β-catenin signaling [<xref rid="B125-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">125</xref>]. Recent studies further highlight its neuroprotective properties against glutamate-induced excitotoxicity, mediated through the MAPK/Nrf2 pathway and improved mitochondrial function [<xref rid="B126-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">126</xref>]. These diverse effects render loliolide a promising multipotent molecule that may contribute to both the ecological and pharmacological profile of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>.</p><p>Actinidiolide (<bold>48</bold>), another norisoprenoid lactone detected in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, is a degradation product of carotenoids and shares structural features with loliolide. While its specific activity has not yet been confirmed in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, studies in other species suggest its involvement in cytoprotective and antioxidant pathways. Its presence further supports the relevance of norisoprenoids in the chemotaxonomic and ecological profile of the species [<xref rid="B10-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>].</p><p>Alismol (<bold>75</bold>), an oxygenated sesquiterpene originally isolated from <italic toggle="yes">Alisma orientale</italic> and later reported in <italic toggle="yes">Vladimiria souliei</italic> [<xref rid="B127-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">127</xref>], has also been identified in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>. This compound displays notable anti-inflammatory activity via activation of the Nrf2 pathway and inhibition of pro-inflammatory mediators, including TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and COX-2 expression [<xref rid="B128-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">128</xref>,<xref rid="B129-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">129</xref>]. In LPS-stimulated microglial cells, alismol suppresses nitric oxide (NO) and prostaglandin E<sub>2</sub> (PGE<sub>2</sub>) production by inhibiting NF-κB signaling while sparing MAPK pathways [<xref rid="B127-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">127</xref>]. Additionally, it exhibits neuroprotective effects by mitigating microglia-mediated neuronal cytotoxicity and alleviating lung injury in LPS-induced inflammatory models [<xref rid="B128-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">128</xref>,<xref rid="B129-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">129</xref>].</p><p>α-bergamotene (<bold>52</bold>), a hydrocarbon sesquiterpene detected in the essential oil of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, has been reported in other plant species such as <italic toggle="yes">Guarea cedrata</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Schisandra perulata</italic> [<xref rid="B130-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">130</xref>,<xref rid="B131-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">131</xref>].
