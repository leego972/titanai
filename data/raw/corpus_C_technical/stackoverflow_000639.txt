Question: <p>I'm mainly a C# developer, but I'm currently working on a project in Python.</p>

<p>How can I represent the equivalent of an Enum in Python?  </p>

Answer: <p>Use the following.</p>

<pre><code>TYPE = {'EAN13':   u'EAN-13',
        'CODE39':  u'Code 39',
        'CODE128': u'Code 128',
        'i25':     u'Interleaved 2 of 5',}

&gt;&gt;&gt; TYPE.items()
[('EAN13', u'EAN-13'), ('i25', u'Interleaved 2 of 5'), ('CODE39', u'Code 39'), ('CODE128', u'Code 128')]
&gt;&gt;&gt; TYPE.keys()
['EAN13', 'i25', 'CODE39', 'CODE128']
&gt;&gt;&gt; TYPE.values()
[u'EAN-13', u'Interleaved 2 of 5', u'Code 39', u'Code 128']
</code></pre>

<p>I used that for <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Django_%28web_framework%29" rel="nofollow">Django</a> model choices, and it looks very pythonic. It is not really an Enum, but it does the job.</p>
