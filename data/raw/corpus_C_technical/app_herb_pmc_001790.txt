XO activity in the liver produces uric acid, which is released into circulation and excreted by the kidney. Hyperuricemia, due to excessive uric acid production in the liver or impaired clearance in the kidney, causes accumulation of uric acid crystals in joints (gout) and in the kidney. Scopoletin shows inhibitory activity in enzymatic assays of XO <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> (<xref rid="B506" ref-type="bibr">506</xref>). Additionally, scopoletin administered either by intraperitoneal injection or by oral gavage of scopoletin-loaded micelles was shown to correct hyperuricemia in mice through two separate mechanisms, namely inhibiting hepatic XO activity and enhancing uric acid excretion by the kidney (<xref rid="B201" ref-type="bibr">201</xref>, <xref rid="B202" ref-type="bibr">202</xref>). Notably, XO activity is associated with obesity-related metabolic dysfunction, and XO inhibitors typically prescribed for gout or hyperuricemia are proving to be effective in mitigating cardiovascular and renal complications of diabetes (<xref rid="B504" ref-type="bibr">504</xref>, <xref rid="B507" ref-type="bibr">507</xref>, <xref rid="B508" ref-type="bibr">508</xref>).</p><p>In addition to antioxidant effects, scopoletin has potent anti-inflammatory activity. Numerous studies have demonstrated the ability of scopoletin to diminish the production of proinflammatory mediators such as cytokines and eicosanoids in many cell types, including macrophages, mast cells, fibroblasts, and platelets (<xref rid="B228" ref-type="bibr">228</xref>–<xref rid="B237" ref-type="bibr">237</xref>). <italic toggle="yes">In vivo</italic> effects of scopoletin in rodent ear or paw edema models and in models of inflammatory conditions such as arthritis, gastro-esophageal disease, gastric ulcers, gout, pleurisy, pancreatitis, as well as nociceptive or analgesic properties, have also been described (<xref rid="B215" ref-type="bibr">215</xref>, <xref rid="B237" ref-type="bibr">237</xref>–<xref rid="B242" ref-type="bibr">242</xref>, <xref rid="B291" ref-type="bibr">291</xref>, <xref rid="B509" ref-type="bibr">509</xref>, <xref rid="B510" ref-type="bibr">510</xref>). Mechanisms involved in scopoletin’s anti-inflammatory effects include negative regulation of inflammatory signaling pathways and inhibition of lipoxygenase and cyclooxygenase enzyme activities (<xref rid="B214" ref-type="bibr">214</xref>, <xref rid="B239" ref-type="bibr">239</xref>, <xref rid="B243" ref-type="bibr">243</xref>, <xref rid="B247" ref-type="bibr">247</xref>, <xref rid="B280" ref-type="bibr">280</xref>, <xref rid="B281" ref-type="bibr">281</xref>). Scopoletin’s anti-inflammatory properties have also been implicated in its effects on various pathologies, particularly in the realm of immunity, as it has been shown to regulate complement pathway activation, mast cell degranulation, as well as several aspects of innate, humoral, and adaptive immune function, suggesting potential roles in allergies, asthma, and autoimmune diseases such as multiple sclerosis and rheumatoid arthritis (<xref rid="B175" ref-type="bibr">175</xref>, <xref rid="B251" ref-type="bibr">251</xref>, <xref rid="B282" ref-type="bibr">282</xref>–<xref rid="B288" ref-type="bibr">288</xref>, <xref rid="B511" ref-type="bibr">511</xref>).</p><p>Scopoletin has been studied in several models of neurological dysfunction and has an array of favorable effects. Some of these are at least partly attributable to its antioxidant or anti-inflammatory properties, like reducing inflammation-induced anxiety in mice or attenuating neural deficits, brain edema, and inflammatory cytokine production in intracerebral hemorrhage in rats (<xref rid="B252" ref-type="bibr">252</xref>, <xref rid="B253" ref-type="bibr">253</xref>). In addition, several groups have demonstrated scopoletin’s ability to inhibit acetyl- and butyryl-cholinesterases <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> (<xref rid="B110" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>, <xref rid="B247" ref-type="bibr">247</xref>, <xref rid="B254" ref-type="bibr">254</xref>–<xref rid="B257" ref-type="bibr">257</xref>, <xref rid="B512" ref-type="bibr">512</xref>) and <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> (<xref rid="B258" ref-type="bibr">258</xref>). Antidepressant, anti-psychotic, and anti-amnesic effects were also observed in behavioral studies in mice (<xref rid="B246" ref-type="bibr">246</xref>, <xref rid="B258" ref-type="bibr">258</xref>–<xref rid="B261" ref-type="bibr">261</xref>).
