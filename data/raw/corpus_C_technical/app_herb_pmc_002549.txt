the probiotic strain. The coating compound preserved the anthocyanins

importance of fruits have already been discussed, as they contribute
to overall health and potentially prevent some kinds of diseases including
cancer. However, large quantities of bioactives are being lost during
the production stages and discarded as a waste material. Melons are
also a type of fruits that suffer from losing their valuable chemicals
as a result of being processed into various kinds of end-products,
mainly juices. <italic toggle="yes">Cucumis melo L.</italic>, also known as melon
or cantaloupe, belongs to the <italic toggle="yes">Cucurbitaceae</italic> family,
which also includes watermelon and honeydew. According to Agricultural
Marketing Resource Center, ∼13 kg of melon per capita is being
consumed every year in America.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref243">243</xref></sup> The
relatively high consumption of these fruits can be attributed to their
high organoleptic properties as well as nutritional attributes.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref244">244</xref></sup> Melon processing generates huge amounts of
byproducts, mainly melon seeds, which are estimated as 738 tons according
to FAOSTAT (2015).<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref2">2</xref></sup> Mallek-Ayadi et al.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref245">245</xref></sup> analyzed the total phenolic content of melon
seeds of the Maazoun cultivar, and they have reported the TPC as 304
mg/100 g, which is particularly important for preventing neurodegenerative
and cardiovascular diseases, as proved by various studies.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref246">246</xref></sup> Among all phenolic compounds present in Maazoun
cultivar seed oil, the most abundant phenolic acid was gallic acid
(7.26 μg/g), followed by caffeic acid (3.13 μg/g), rosmarinic
acid (2.91 μg/g), and protocatechuic acid (0.89 μg/g). Besides, amentoflavone as a flavonoid (32.80 μg/g) and some
lignans were also identified (3.95 μg/g).<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref245">245</xref></sup> Ismail et al.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref247">247</xref></sup> reported the
TPC of cantoloupe seeds as 285 mg GAE/100 g extract, where total flavonoids
content was 162 μg/100 g extract. The highest phenolic content
was found in cantaloupe leaf as 2640 mg GAE/100 g extract, followed
by the stem (1025 mg GAE/100 g extract), skin (470 mg GAE/100 g extract),
seed, and flesh (168 mg GAE/100 g extract).
