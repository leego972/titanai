Detector). The principle behind the GC analysis is separation techniques. In separation techniques, there are two phases-the mobile and the stationary phase. The mobile phase is the carrier gas (Helium, 99.99% purity), while the stationary phase is the column. The model of the column is HP5 MS with length 30 m, internal diameter 0.320 mm, while the thickness is 0.25 µm. The oven temperature program is initial temperature of 80 °C to hold for 1 min. It increases by 10° per minute to the final temperature of 240 °C to hold for 6 min. The injection volume is 1 µl and the heater or detector temperature is 250 °C.</p><p id="Par22">Operation: The sample extracted was put in a vial bottle and the vial bottle was placed in auto injector sample compartment. The automatic injector injects the sample into the liner. The mobile phase pushes the sample from the liner into the column where separation takes place into different components at different retention time. The MS interpret the spectrum MZ (mass to charge ratio) with molar mass and structures.</p></sec></sec><sec id="Sec9"><title>Compound identification</title><p id="Par23">The extract’s bioactive compounds were identified by analyzing their retention times on an HP-5MS column using gas chromatography (GC) and by comparing their mass spectra with the NIST 14 library installed in the MassHunter software (Agilent Technologies), with a match threshold of ≥  80%.</p></sec><sec id="Sec10"><title>FTIR spectroscopy procedure</title><p id="Par24">Fourier-transform infrared (FT-IR) analysis was performed using a Buck Scientific M530 spectrophotometer (USA) equipped with a deuterated triglycine sulfate (DTGS) detector and a potassium bromide (KBr) beam splitter, as described by Dike et al.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR15">15</xref></sup>. A 1.0 g portion of the extract was homogenized with 0.5 mL of Nujol and mounted onto a salt pellet for transmission analysis using the KBr pellet method. Spectra were recorded in the range of 4000 to 600 cm⁻<sup>1</sup> with a resolution of 4 cm⁻<sup>1</sup> and 32 co-added scans. Spectral data were processed using the Gramme A1 software and interpreted in transmittance mode<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR15">15</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR16">16</xref></sup>.</p><p id="Par25">The analysis focused primarily on the functional group region (4000–1000 cm⁻<sup>1</sup>), which includes distinct absorption bands characteristic of major phytochemical groups such as flavonoids, polyphenols, glycosides, and terpenoids. Although peaks below 1000 cm⁻<sup>1</sup> (fingerprint region) were not emphasized due to spectral complexity and overlap, future comparative studies incorporating spectral libraries may improve compound specificity in this region.</p></sec></sec><sec id="Sec11"><title>Results</title><sec id="Sec12"><title>Extract yield</title><p id="Par26">Table <xref rid="Tab1" ref-type="table">1</xref> illustrates the percentage yield, calculated using the formula; Yield (%) = (Mass of Extract Obtained/Mass of Plant Material Used) × 100, based on an initial plant material weight of 600 g.
