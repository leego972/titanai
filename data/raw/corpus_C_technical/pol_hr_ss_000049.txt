Title: Climate Rights and Their Judicial Protection: Commentary on the Legal Positions of the Grand Chamber of the European Court of Human Rights

Abstract:
The topic of climate change litigation is becoming increasingly clear, convincingly influencing not only the disappearance of skepticism about the role of courts in helping to mitigate the effects of climate change, but also considering them as bodies capable of filling gaps in legal regulation and effective action practices at the national and global levels. On April 9, 2024, the European Court of Human Rights issued three decisions related to climate change, each of which raised important new issues arising from climate change. If the complaints in two cases against France and Portugal were declared inadmissible on procedural grounds, then the third — against Switzerland — is of great importance, as the first decision of the International Court of Human Rights on the inadequacy of the State’s actions to take effective measures against the effect of global warming and fulfill its positive obligations to protect the rights established by the European Convention on the Protection of human rights and fundamental freedoms (ECHR). The Court not only recognized climate protection as a human right, but also overcame the actio popularis problem, which could objectively serve as an obstacle, by allowing the filing of lawsuits by associations according to the model of the Aarhus Convention. The Court also confirmed that Article 8 of the ECHR includes the right to effective protection by public authorities from the serious harmful effects of climate change on the life, health, well-being and quality of life of citizens, establishing a direct link between the rights under the Convention and climate change. According to the Court, mitigation of the effects of climate change is a direct responsibility of the State, which it must implement for every person. The Court’s conclusion is based on indisputable scientific evidence of anthropogenic climate change, current and future harm that it entails. With the commented decision, the Court laid down a new paradigm of judicial proceedings in this area, based on the concept of human rights protection.
