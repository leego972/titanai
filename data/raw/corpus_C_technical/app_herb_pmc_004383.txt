Specific and Nonspecific Actions of Adaptogens</title><p>Extensive research of herbal medicines in the past decades has provided more evidence that, as a rule, they exhibit polyvalent nonspecific pharmacological activity affecting many physiological functions and regulatory systems in humans, e.g., <italic toggle="yes">Panax ginseng</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Andrographis paniculata</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Withania somnifera</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Curcuma longa</italic>, etc. [<xref rid="B5-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>,<xref rid="B95-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>,<xref rid="B96-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>,<xref rid="B97-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>,<xref rid="B98-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">98</xref>,<xref rid="B99-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>,<xref rid="B100-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">100</xref>,<xref rid="B101-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">101</xref>,<xref rid="B102-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>,<xref rid="B103-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">103</xref>,<xref rid="B104-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">104</xref>,<xref rid="B105-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">105</xref>]. Some of them, e.g., <italic toggle="yes">Panax ginseng</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Bryonia alba</italic>, etc., were traditionally used as panaceas [<xref rid="B95-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>,<xref rid="B105-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">105</xref>]. A rational explanation for their mysterious pleiotropic actions remains a challenge. </p><p>Depending on the chemical compositions of herbal extracts, the pharmacological effects of certain medicinal plants are specific to some extent, e.g., <italic toggle="yes">Hypericum perforatum</italic> is known mainly as an anti-depressant [<xref rid="B106-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>], but in the meantime, it exhibits a variety of other pharmacological activities, such as antiviral, antitumor, anti-stress, etc. [<xref rid="B106-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>,<xref rid="B107-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">107</xref>,<xref rid="B108-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>,<xref rid="B109-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">109</xref>].</p><p>In the past century, Brekhman and Dardimov suggested that some of the “Ginseng-like” plants can increase the so-called “state of nonspecific resistance” of an organism to stress, resulting in tonic, stress-protective, and adaptogenic activity [<xref rid="B110-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>]. Regretfully, some researchers misinterpret that definition of adaptogens as agents, which does not reveal any specific therapeutic action. Others freely declare some plants [<xref rid="B111-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">111</xref>] or natural substances [<xref rid="B112-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>] as adaptogenic without scientific evidence of their efficacy and safety and lack of knowledge of the mode or mechanisms of action.</p><p>Meanwhile, the modes and mechanisms of action of adaptogens have been studied for seven decades [<xref rid="B5-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>,<xref rid="B9-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>,<xref rid="B113-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>] and elucidated to some extent due to the implementations of methods of molecular biology, network pharmacology, and systems biology concepts [<xref rid="B6-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="B7-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>,<xref rid="B8-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>,<xref rid="B10-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>,<xref rid="B11-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>,<xref rid="B12-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>,<xref rid="B13-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>,<xref rid="B14-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>,<xref rid="B15-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>].</p><p>The similarity of chemical structures of ginsenosides with the stress hormone cortisol (<xref rid="pharmaceuticals-15-01051-f002" ref-type="fig">Figure 2</xref>), suggests that their mechanism of action is associated with glucocorticoid receptors and the mode of action with the hypothalamus–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis—a functional part of the neuroendocrine-immune complex, collectively known as a “stress system” [<xref rid="B114-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>,<xref rid="B115-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">115</xref>], which regulates adaptability, survival, and resilience of organisms in stress and progression of aging-related disorders [<xref rid="B96-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>,<xref rid="B116-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">116</xref>], including neurodegenerative diseases (Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinson’s disease, senile dementia, etc.), atherosclerosis, cardiovascular disease, metabolic diseases (type 2 diabetes, obesity, and hypertension) [<xref rid="B117-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">117</xref>], muscle degeneration (sarcopenia), degenerative joint disease (osteoarthritis), cancer, etc.</p><p>Indeed, in the earlier studies dated 1970–1980, it was demonstrated that ginsenosides act as functional ligands of glucocorticoid receptors [<xref rid="B119-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">119</xref>,<xref rid="B120-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">120</xref>,<xref rid="B121-pharmaceuticals-15-01051" ref-type="bibr">121</xref>].
