The putative SARS-CoV-2 Mpro inhibitors lopinavir and nelfinavir [<xref rid="B57-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>] showed predicted binding energies to Mpro at −8.2 and −8.1, respectively. The predicted binding energies show that at least 3 of 24 phytotochemicals, namely 4,5-di-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-caffeoylquinic acid, rutin, and schaftoside possessed higher binding energies than the inhibitors.</p><p>Incidentally, chlorogenic acid, dicaffeoyl quinic acids, and hispidulin have been reported to inhibit SARS-CoV [<xref rid="B58-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>]; H1N1 (influenza A virus), Coxsackievirus B3, and herpes simplex virus 1 [<xref rid="B59-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>]; and Respiratory Syncytial Virus [<xref rid="B60-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>], respectively. <italic toggle="yes">Desmodium canadense extract</italic> containing isovitexin reportedly showed inhibitory activity against avian infectious bronchitis virus (IBV) [<xref rid="B61-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>]. Other in silico studies have shown the potential of rutin as an inhibitor of SARS-CoV-2 [<xref rid="B62-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>]. The inhibition of a different virus is not tantamount to inhibiting SARS-CoV-2, but, in a predictive sense, it may be considered to have a higher probability of acting against the COVID-19 virus [<xref rid="B63-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>], more so if there is a high degree of sequence homology and other similarities between the two viruses.</p><p>The non-bonding interactions of the <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia herba-alba</italic> phytochemicals (top three compounds with highest binding affinities to Mpro amino acid residues) are shown in <xref rid="molecules-27-00492-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>. These three compounds are rutin, 4,5-di-<italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-caffeoylquinic acid, and schaftoside, with predicted binding energies, respectively, of −8.8, −8.5, and −8.3 kcal/mol. Mpro is 306 amino acids long; it has been reported to contain a catalytic dyad comprising of His41 and Cys145. The protease exists as a dimer, with the monomeric form being essentially inactive [<xref rid="B64-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>,<xref rid="B65-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>]. Each monomer contains three domains; domain 1 comprises amino acid residues 8–101, domain 2 contains amino acid residues 102–184, and domain 3 contains residues 201–306. A loop region (amino acid residues 185–200) connects domain 2 with domain 3. The substrate-binding site location is within a cleft formed between domains 1 and 2 [<xref rid="B66-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>]. The interactions of the three phytochemicals, as shown in <xref rid="molecules-27-00492-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>, demonstrate that all three phytochemicals bind to one or both amino acids of the catalytic dyad and mainly form strong interactions with the amino acid residues of domain 2. The same holds true for other phytochemicals from <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia herba-alba,</italic> which show predicted high binding energies to Mpro (≥7.5 kcal/mol); with the exception of vicenin-2 and isovitexin, several other phytochemicals that were evaluated, such as chlorogenic acid, hispidulin, patuletin-3-glucoside, and patuletin-3-rutinoside, demonstrated interactions with one or both of the catalytic dyad amino acid residues His41 and Cys145 (data not shown). Overall, the results from <xref rid="molecules-27-00492-t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref>, <xref rid="molecules-27-00492-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref> and <xref rid="molecules-27-00492-t003" ref-type="table">Table 3</xref>, as well as those shown <xref rid="molecules-27-00492-f002" ref-type="fig">Figure 2</xref>, <xref rid="molecules-27-00492-f003" ref-type="fig">Figure 3</xref>, <xref rid="molecules-27-00492-f004" ref-type="fig">Figure 4</xref>, <xref rid="molecules-27-00492-f005" ref-type="fig">Figure 5</xref> and <xref rid="molecules-27-00492-f006" ref-type="fig">Figure 6</xref> and other Figures (data not shown) suggest that phytochemicals from <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia herba-alba</italic> have a strong probability of binding to SARS-CoV-2 Mpro, inhibiting the protease with the consequential blockage of viral replication (compare binding with nelfinavir to Mpro shown in <xref rid="molecules-27-00492-f005" ref-type="fig">Figure 5</xref>).
