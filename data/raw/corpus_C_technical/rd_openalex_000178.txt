Title: Capturing Technological Opportunity via Japan's Star Scientists: Evidence from Japanese Firms' Biotech Patents and Products

Abstract:
Using detailed data on biotechnology Japan, we find that identifiable between particular university star scientists and firms have a large positive impact on firms' productivity, increasing the average firm's biotech patents by 34 percent development by 27 percent, and products on the market by 8 percent as of 1989-1990. However there is little evidence of geographically localized knowledge spillovers. In early industry formation, star scientists holding tacit knowledge required practice recombinant DNA (genetic engineering) were of great economic value, leading incentives motivating their participation in technology transfer. In Japan, the legal and institutional context implies that firm scientists work the stars' university laboratories contrast America where the stars are more likely work the firm's labs. As a result, star collaborations Japan are less localized around their research universities so that the universities' local economic development impact is lessened. Stars' scientific productivity is increased less during collaborations with firms Japan as compared to the U.S.
