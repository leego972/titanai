Bapela et al. [<xref rid="B58-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>] assessed effect of nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium fertilizers on accumulation of isodiospyrin <bold>3</bold>, neodiospyrin <bold>10</bold>, diospyrin <bold>11</bold>, 7-methyljuglone <bold>12</bold> and shinanolone <bold>13</bold>. A significantly positive correlation was established between the concentration of isodiospyrin <bold>3</bold>, neodiospyrin <bold>10</bold>, diospyrin <bold>11</bold>, 7-methyljuglone <bold>12</bold> and shinanolone <bold>13</bold> with fertilization from field-grown seedlings [<xref rid="B58-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>]. Joubert et al. [<xref rid="B59-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>] used high performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) to quantify the concentration of diospyrin <bold>11</bold> and 7-methyljuglone <bold>12</bold> in roots of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic>. The concentration of diospyrin <bold>11</bold> was higher (about 2750 mg/kg) than the concentration of 7-methyljuglone <bold>12</bold> which was about 450 mg/kg. Joubert et al. [<xref rid="B59-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>] argued that the observed variation in naphthoquinones concentration could be due to the age of the roots harvested, wound and environmental or other stress factors.
