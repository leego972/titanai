Additionally, AS-OCTA effectively monitors the revascularization process.<sup> <xref rid="bibr31-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">31</xref> </sup></p><p>AS-OCT effectively visualizes structural pathologies in the cornea and anterior chamber (AC) following chemical burns, even in the presence of corneal opacity. These changes include epithelial defects, corneal edema, opacification, neovascularization, stromal disintegration, stromal cyst formation, Descemet’s membrane detachment, bulla formation, AC inflammation, anterior synechiae, and potential angle-closure glaucoma.<sup><xref rid="bibr32-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">32</xref><xref rid="bibr33-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr34-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">34</xref></sup> Consequently, AS-OCT plays a crucial role in staging chemical burns, monitoring wound healing, and guiding both the initial treatment plan and the assessment of treatment success.<sup><xref rid="bibr34-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>,<xref rid="bibr35-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">35</xref></sup></p><p>AS-OCT provides valuable data for monitoring the response to various topical and surgical treatments for ocular burns. It is particularly useful in assessing the postoperative anatomical stability of keratoprostheses, which are occasionally used for managing advanced chemical burns.<sup>

</sup></p></sec><sec id="sec7-03000605251382298"><title>Foreign bodies</title><p>Corneal foreign bodies are the second most common cause of ocular trauma after corneal epithelial defects.<sup>
<xref rid="bibr37-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>
</sup> AS-OCT is a noninvasive and effective tool for identifying, localizing, and monitoring intrastromal foreign bodies.<sup>
<xref rid="bibr38-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>
</sup> In many cases, slit-lamp examination alone cannot accurately determine the type of deep corneal foreign body; however, this information is crucial for planning its removal. AS-OCT has been used to differentiate specific materials based on their characteristics.<sup>
<xref rid="bibr39-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>
</sup> Opaque objects, such as metal, wood, and pencil graphite, appear hyperreflective at the anterior border; however, their posterior border is obscured due to shadowing. A mirroring effect, seen as an inverted chain of signals, is characteristic of metal and pencil graphite. Transparent objects display distinct hyperreflective borders when surrounded by air or fluid but appear differently when embedded in the corneal stroma. However, organic foreign bodies do not exhibit specific AS-OCT features.<sup><xref rid="bibr40-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>,<xref rid="bibr41-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">41</xref></sup></p><p>Determining the depth of an intrastromal foreign body can be challenging using slit-lamp biomicroscopy. If a foreign body has fully penetrated the cornea and remains undetected before removal, an extraction attempt may cause open globe injury.<sup>
<xref rid="bibr42-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>
</sup> However, AS-OCT accurately delineates the borders of an intrastromal foreign body, clearly determining whether it has reached the Descemet’s membrane and entered the AC.<sup><xref rid="bibr42-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">42</xref><xref rid="bibr43-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr44-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr45-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr46-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">46</xref></sup> This makes AS-OCT a valuable tool for clinicians when deciding between surgical and outpatient management of fully embedded corneal foreign bodies.<sup><xref rid="bibr42-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>,<xref rid="bibr43-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>,<xref rid="bibr47-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>,<xref rid="bibr48-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">48</xref></sup> Additionally, AS-OCT can assess corneal wound depth and width following foreign body removal,<sup>
<xref rid="bibr47-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>
</sup> allowing clinicians to predict the corneal scar size.<sup><xref rid="bibr49-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>,<xref rid="bibr50-03000605251382298" ref-type="bibr">50</xref></sup></p><p>AS-OCT is also valuable in the diagnosis and management of post–laser-assisted in situ keratomileusis (LASIK) corneal foreign bodies. In these cases, careful evaluation for flap-related complications is essential.
