Some of these plants and their compounds prove to be very effective against one or more types of cancers. Based on their activities, the following plants are selected for the in vitro and in vivo anticancer activities of their compounds. The rest of the important plants shortlisted for their activities are presented in Table 1 along with their activities.</p><sec id="sec3dot1-biomolecules-10-00047"><title>3.1. Artemisia annua</title><p>The genus Artemisia, widespread in Europe, Asia, North America, and South Africa has approximately 400 species worldwide [<xref rid="B20-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>]. Plants of the genus were used for centuries in classical medicine [<xref rid="B21-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia annua</italic> is an annual short-day plant that belongs to family Asteraceae, having a brownish rigid stem. <italic toggle="yes">A. annua</italic> is known as sweet wormwood (Chinese: qīnghāo) and “dona” in the Urdu language in India and Pakistan [<xref rid="B8-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">A. annua</italic> was used by old Chinese for the preparation of anti-malarial drugs known as artemisinin (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="biomolecules-10-00047-f001">Figure 1</xref>). Having a unique ability of environmental adaptation it consistently resists insects and pathogens [<xref rid="B22-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">A. annua</italic> also synthesize scopoletin and 1,8-cineole compounds. Similarly, semi-synthetic derivatives of artemisinin are also generated such as arteether, artemether, and artesunate. Artesunate has been studied to be a very effective anticancer compound. Efferth [<xref rid="B23-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>] studied the effect of artesunate on 55 different cancer cell lines including leukemia, melanoma, lung cancer, colon cancer, renal cancer, ovarian cancer, and tumors of the central nervous system. They suggested that artesunate was most effective against leukemia and colon cancers. Furthermore, it was observed through these studies that the artesunate was more active than the drugs used for such cancers.</p><p>The stem and leaves <italic toggle="yes">A. annua</italic> were subject to extraction with the help of 80% ethanol and water. Several quantitative phenolic compounds from <italic toggle="yes">A. annua</italic> were identified using high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). The extracts were tested against HeLa and AGS cell lines. The cell growth inhibition activity of stem extracts was lower compared to leaf extracts. The ethanolic extracts of leaves lead to growth inhibitions (57.24% and 67.07%) in HeLa and AGS cells, respectively at a concentration of 500 mg/mL. HPLC analysis showed that the amount of phenolic acids was lower in stem extract than in leaves extract of <italic toggle="yes">A. annua</italic>. It was concluded from the data that the antioxidant and anticancer capacity was the result of phenolic compounds as well as unidentified compounds within <italic toggle="yes">A. annua</italic> [<xref rid="B24-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec3dot2-biomolecules-10-00047"><title>3.2. Coptis chinensis</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Coptis chinensis</italic>, the Chinese goldthread, is a herb used as a traditional medicine in China thus officially enlisted in the Chinese pharmacopeia [<xref rid="B25-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>]. It is widely known for its traditional use against various diseases like diarrhea, dysentery, acute febrile, and supportive infections. The organic extract of <italic toggle="yes">C. chinensis</italic> possesses anti-inflammatory and anti-oxidant properties [<xref rid="B26-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>,<xref rid="B27-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">C. chinensis</italic> extract has wide use in the treatment of cholera, dysentery, diabetes, blood and lung cancer because of its strong antibacterial activity [<xref rid="B28-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Coptis</italic> genus contains the most important and active components, such as an alkaloid i.e., berberine (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="biomolecules-10-00047-f001">Figure 1</xref>). Berberines alkaloids are used frequently as criteria in the quality control of <italic toggle="yes">Rhizoma coptidis</italic> (Huang Lian) products and lead to the apoptosis of human leukemia HL-60 cells by down regulating nucleophosmin/B23 and telomerase activity.
