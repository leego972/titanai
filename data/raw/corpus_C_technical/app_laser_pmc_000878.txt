In the original formulation<xref rid="r38" ref-type="bibr"><sup>38</sup></xref> and subsequent applications<xref rid="r40" ref-type="bibr"><sup>40</sup></xref> of the depth-resolved attenuation coefficient analysis, the CPSF and sensitivity roll-off in depth were not taken into account. Smith et al.<xref rid="r41" ref-type="bibr"><sup>41</sup></xref> introduced these corrections, including for noise. The depth-resolved method was validated on homogeneous and layered phantoms by Vermeer et al.<xref rid="r38" ref-type="bibr"><sup>38</sup></xref> and showed promising results for estimating attenuation coefficients with a higher axial resolution. As pointed out above, though, the method relies on the assumptions of complete extinction in an A-scan and a fixed ratio between <inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math127"><mml:mrow><mml:msub><mml:mrow><mml:mi>μ</mml:mi></mml:mrow><mml:mrow><mml:mi>OCT</mml:mi></mml:mrow></mml:msub></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula> and <inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math128"><mml:mrow><mml:msub><mml:mrow><mml:mi>μ</mml:mi></mml:mrow><mml:mrow><mml:mi>b</mml:mi><mml:mo>,</mml:mo><mml:mi>NA</mml:mi></mml:mrow></mml:msub></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula>. The former becomes more problematic for pixels toward the end of the A-scan and when prominent multiple-scattering background is present, which can be eliminated by carefully choosing a cutoff constant.<xref rid="r40" ref-type="bibr"><sup>40</sup></xref><sup>,</sup><xref rid="r42" ref-type="bibr"><sup>42</sup></xref> The latter assumption does not hold in the case of absorption or for scatterers with a strongly structured angular scattering cross section, such as Mie scatterers. Since <inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math129"><mml:mrow><mml:msub><mml:mrow><mml:mi>μ</mml:mi></mml:mrow><mml:mrow><mml:mi>OCT</mml:mi></mml:mrow></mml:msub></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula> is directly proportional to <inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math130"><mml:mrow><mml:mi>I</mml:mi><mml:mo stretchy="false">[</mml:mo><mml:mi>i</mml:mi><mml:mo stretchy="false">]</mml:mo></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula> in Eq. (6), the analyzed attenuation contains speckle. This artifact may be dealt with by conventional methods such as local averaging or median filtering, which will then lower the resolution. Validation studies on tissue are still scarce, so the impact of the assumptions and necessary postprocessing on the accuracy of the extracted attenuation coefficients, and their utility for tissue classification, is not yet clear.</p><p>Alternatively, Yuan et al.<xref rid="r36" ref-type="bibr"><sup>36</sup></xref> proposed a distinct frequency-domain method, aided by Fourier transformation, to extract the attenuation coefficients.
