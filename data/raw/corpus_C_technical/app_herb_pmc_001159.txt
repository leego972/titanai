Several plant-derived molecules may exert toxic effects on non-target organisms, including beneficial insects, aquatic species, and even mammals, depending on the concentration and exposure conditions. Therefore, although biopesticides obtained from agri-food residues are frequently associated with improved environmental compatibility, their development and practical application must rely on comprehensive toxicological and ecotoxicological evaluation, as well as the assessment of environmental persistence and unintended biological effects. A critical evaluation of both efficacy and potential risks is essential to ensure that these alternatives effectively contribute to sustainable agriculture.</p></sec><sec sec-type="methods" id="sec2-molecules-31-00996"><title>2. Methodology</title><p>This systematic review was conducted by following structured methodological principles inspired by the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines to enhance transparency and reproducibility.</p><sec id="sec2dot1-molecules-31-00996"><title>2.1. Literature Search Strategy</title><p>A comprehensive literature search was performed in the Scopus, Web of Science Core Collection, and PubMed databases between January and March 2025. The search strategy combined controlled vocabulary and free-text terms related to agri-food waste, including “pesticide”, “biocide”, “biopesticide”, “bioherbicide”, “biofungicide”, “bioinsecticide”, “phenolic compounds”, “fatty acids”, “essential oils”, “by-products”, “bio-wastes”, “agri-food industry”, “olive”, “citrus”, “banana”, “circular economy”, and “industry 4.0”. Boolean operators (“AND”, “OR”) were applied to refine search combinations.</p><p>Although the primary focus was on studies published after 2000, selected earlier publications were included due to their unique contributions to the field.</p></sec><sec id="sec2dot2-molecules-31-00996"><title>2.2. Eligibility Criteria</title><p>The inclusion criteria were defined as follows:<list list-type="simple"><list-item><label>(i)</label><p>Peer-reviewed original research articles or review papers;</p></list-item><list-item><label>(ii)</label><p>Studies reporting the extraction, identification, or characterization of bioactive compounds derived from agri-food industry residues;</p></list-item><list-item><label>(iii)</label><p>Investigations evaluating pesticidal, insecticidal, fungicidal, herbicidal, or antimicrobial activities that are relevant to agricultural applications;</p></list-item><list-item><label>(iv)</label><p>Studies providing experimental data, mechanistic insights, or quantitative biological assessment.</p></list-item></list></p><p>The exclusion criteria comprised the following:<list list-type="simple"><list-item><label>(i)</label><p>Studies unrelated to crop protection or pest management;</p></list-item><list-item><label>(ii)</label><p>Articles focusing exclusively on synthetic pesticide development without a connection to agri-food-derived bioactive compounds;</p></list-item><list-item><label>(iii)</label><p>Conference proceedings, editorials, patents, and non-peer-reviewed documents;</p></list-item><list-item><label>(iv)</label><p>Publications lacking sufficient methodological description or reproducible experimental detail.</p></list-item></list></p></sec><sec id="sec2dot3-molecules-31-00996"><title>2.3. Study Selection Process</title><p>All records retrieved from the databases were exported and duplicates were removed prior to screening. Titles and abstracts were independently screened for relevance to the review scope. The full texts of articles meeting the preliminary criteria were subsequently assessed to confirm their eligibility.</p><p>Studies were selected based on their relevance to chemical diversity, mechanism of action, biological performance, and potential contribution to circular economy strategies. When necessary, the reference lists of eligible articles were manually screened to identify additional pertinent studies.</p></sec><sec id="sec2dot4-molecules-31-00996"><title>2.4. Data Extraction and Qualitative Assessment</title><p>Data were extracted focusing on: sources of agri-food residue, types of bioactive compound, extraction approach, reported biological activity, target organisms, and any discussion regarding environmental safety or regulatory considerations.</p><p>Although no quantitative meta-analysis was conducted due to heterogeneity among experimental designs, particular attention was given to methodological robustness, clarity in reporting biological assays, and the consistency of results. This qualitative appraisal aimed to ensure a critical and balanced interpretation of the available evidence.</p></sec></sec><sec id="sec3-molecules-31-00996"><title>3. Literature Search Outcomes and Study Selection</title><p>The structured database search yielded a total of 578 records. After the removal of 118 duplicate entries, 460 articles remained for title and abstract screening.</p><p>During the initial screening phase, 250 records were excluded for not meeting the predefined scope of the review, which was primarily due to the lack of direct relevance to agri-food waste valorization or absence of application in agricultural pest management.</p><p>A total of 210 reports were subsequently sought for full-text retrieval.
