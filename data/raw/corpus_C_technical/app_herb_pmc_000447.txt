It holds the potential to reverse P-gp-mediated multidrug resistance and influence apoptosis through ROS-dependent activation of IRE1α/JNK [<xref rid="B60-molecules-28-06370" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>]. To enhance the bioavailability and antitumor efficiency of gambogenic acid, Yuan et al. developed gambogenic acid nanocrystals (GNA-NSs) with PVP K30 and PEG<sub>2000</sub> as stabilizers, resulting in particles sized at 183.7 nm with a zeta potential of −22.8 mV. The AUC<sub>0–∞</sub> and t<sub>1/2</sub> of GNA-NSs were increased by 2.63-fold and 1.77-fold compared with those of the reference formulation, along with elevated cytotoxicity in HepG2 cells [<xref rid="B61-molecules-28-06370" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>].</p><p>10-Hydroxycamptothecin is an alkaloid extracted from the seeds or roots of <italic toggle="yes">Camptotheca acuminata Decne.</italic> It can selectively inhibit topoisomerase I, interfering with DNA replication [<xref rid="B62-molecules-28-06370" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>], and is widely exploited in clinical practice [<xref rid="B63-molecules-28-06370" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>]. Although it has poor solubility in water, it can easily dissolve in a basic solution and precipitate after adding an acidic solution. Utilizing a modified acid–base microprecipitation technique coupled with high-pressure homogenization, Yang et al. created a 10-hydroxycamptothecin nanocrystal free of organic solvents. With a spherical particle size of around 130 nm, this nanocrystal demonstrated improved tumor accumulation and anticancer efficacy compared to 10-hydroxycamptothecin injection [<xref rid="B29-molecules-28-06370" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>]. Zhou et al. compared the anticancer activities of hydroxycamptothecin nanocrystals with different shapes (nanorods and nanoparticles). They proved that nanorods showed higher and longer-term toxicity than nanoparticles [<xref rid="B64-molecules-28-06370" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>], indicating that particle shape is also an important characteristic for nanocrystals.</p><p>Resveratrol, an extract from grapes, has gained attention as a potential antitumor drug and chemopreventive agent [<xref rid="B65-molecules-28-06370" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>] due to its cancer initiation, promotion, and progression effects [<xref rid="B65-molecules-28-06370" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>]. It was reported that resveratrol could inhibit cell proliferation by blocking PI3k/Akt/mTOR [<xref rid="B66-molecules-28-06370" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>]. It could also downregulate CD8<sup>+</sup>CD122<sup>+</sup> Tregs to inhibit hepatocellular carcinoma. Wang et al. crafted a folate-modified resveratrol nanocrystal, enhancing cytotoxicity against A549 cells and amplifying antitumor efficacy [<xref rid="B67-molecules-28-06370" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>]. Additionally, a recent study revealed that resveratrol nanocrystals, when compared to their non-nanocrystalline forms, demonstrated significant reductions in tumor cell proliferation, fewer blood vessels in the peritoneum, and diminished systemic toxicity (such as hepatocellular necrosis and apoptosis, hepatic fibrosis, and minor fatty changes) in Ehrlich ascites tumor-bearing mice [<xref
