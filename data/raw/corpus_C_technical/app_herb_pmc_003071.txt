While ICF has frequently been used to highlight plants of potential interest for further investigation [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR6">6</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR45">45</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR53">53</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR56">56</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR77">77</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR99">99</xref>], its results should be interpreted cautiously. In the Karamara context, high-consensus categories such as dermatological and gastrointestinal disorders are best understood as culturally validated domains of shared practice, whereas low-consensus categories may warrant further ethnographic exploration rather than immediate pharmacological prioritization [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR80">80</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR89">89</xref>].</p><p id="Par215">Fidelity Level (FL) similarly serves as a quantitative indicator of how consistently a given plant species is associated with a specific ailment within a community. Like ICF, FL has recognized limitations, including its sensitivity to informant number and its inability to distinguish between cultural preference and biomedical efficacy. In this study, FL is therefore applied as an interpretive measure of therapeutic specificity within local knowledge systems, rather than as proof of medicinal effectiveness.</p><p id="Par216">In Karamara, FL values ranged from 0.434 to 1.0, reflecting varying degrees of use specificity. Three species <italic toggle="yes">Euphorbia abyssinica</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Moringa stenopetala</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Cyphostemma adenocaule</italic> achieved maximum FL values, as they were consistently cited for wound healing, hypertension, and malaria, respectively [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR17">17</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR41">41</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR53">53</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR56">56</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR60">60</xref>]. High FL values for these species indicate strong cultural agreement on their primary uses, though such agreement should be interpreted as cultural reliability rather than clinical validation.</p><p id="Par217">Other species, such as <italic toggle="yes">Datura stramonium</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Jatropha aceroides</italic>, also exhibited high FL values for specific conditions such as “evil eye” and cough. Moderate FL values were recorded for <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus spina-christi</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Commiphora myrrha</italic>, which were primarily associated with stomachache and snakebite but also had secondary applications. This pattern reflects the multifunctional use of medicinal plants, a strategy widely reported in ethnobotanical studies from India, Pakistan, and China [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR93">93</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR94">94</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR101">101</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR108">108</xref>].</p><p id="Par218">Lower FL values were observed for <italic toggle="yes">Aloe megalacantha</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Calotropis procera</italic>, reflecting their use across multiple ailment categories. Similar trends have been documented in Europe and the Americas, where culturally prominent or readily available species often display lower FL values due to their broad therapeutic application [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR80">80</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR89">89</xref>]. Such multipurpose use does not diminish their cultural importance but instead highlights adaptive and flexible healthcare strategies within traditional medical systems.</p><p id="Par219">Thus, FL analysis in Karamara reveals a dual pattern: certain species are highly ailment-specific and culturally emphasized, while others serve multiple therapeutic roles. While high-FL species are often considered candidates for further scientific investigation [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR17">17</xref>], this study emphasizes that FL primarily reflects patterns of cultural emphasis and shared practice. Ethiopian ethnobotanical studies have similarly employed FL to explore medicinal plant prioritization while acknowledging its interpretive limitations [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR3">3</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR33">33</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR37">37</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR59">59</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR60">60</xref>].
