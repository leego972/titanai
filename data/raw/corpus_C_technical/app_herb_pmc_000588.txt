Peak <bold>1, 7, 9, 20</bold> was identified to be darendoside A, 8-Arabinosyl-6-glucosyl-2’, 3, 5, 7-dihydroxyflavone and 3’, 5, 5’, 7-Tetrahydroxy-2’, 8-dimethoxyflavone and 5, 7-Dihydroxy-6, 8-dimethoxy flavone-7-O-glucuronide, respectively, based on previous studies [<xref rid="pone.0175751.ref022" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>]. Peaks <bold>2 and 8</bold> exhibited typical UV absorbance of flavanones band II situated at a region of long wavelength (more than 280 nm). On the other hand, band I presents with no signal or is only detected as a low signal. The negative ESI analysis of peak <bold>2</bold> gave the [M−H]<sup>−</sup> ion at m/z 303, with characteristic fragment ions as m/z 217 [M−H−5OH]<sup>−</sup>. Negative ESI analysis of <bold>8</bold> gave the [M−H]<sup>−</sup> ion at m/z 287, with peaks of m/z 161 [C<sub>9</sub>H<sub>6</sub>O<sub>3</sub>−H]<sup>−</sup>, m/z 125 [C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>6</sub>O<sub>3</sub>−H]<sup>−</sup> [<xref rid="pone.0175751.ref023" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>]. <bold>Peak 2, 8</bold> were identified as 2’, 3, 4’, 5, 7-Pentahydroxyflavanone and 4’, 5, 7, 8-Tetrahydroxyflavanone, respectively. Peaks <bold>4</bold> and <bold>6</bold> showed same [M−H]<sup>−</sup> ions at <italic toggle="yes">m</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">z</italic> 547. Data from the UV demonstrated maximum absorption wavelength at 272 and 315 nm. Their AUTO/MS fragments gave ions of [M−H−60]<sup>−</sup> at <italic toggle="yes">m</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">z</italic> 487, [M−H−90]<sup>−</sup> at <italic toggle="yes">m</italic>/z457 and [M−H−120]<sup>−</sup> at <italic toggle="yes">m</italic>/z 427 respectively, comparable to the characteristic fragment ions of peaks 4 identified as C-glycoside [<xref rid="pone.0175751.ref024" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>]. The UV spectrum also showed different retention time by C18 reversed-phase column [<xref rid="pone.0175751.ref023" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>]. Thus, peak <bold>4</bold> and <bold>6</bold> were initially identified as chrysin-6-C-ara-8-C-glu and chrysin-6-C-glu-8-C-ara, respectively. Peak <bold>12</bold> gave [M−H]<sup>−</sup> ion at m/z 447. In Auto/MS mode, it produced an ion at m/z 271 [M−H−176]<sup>−</sup>, inferring it presents with a glucuronic acid. And it gave ions of [M−H−176−18]<sup>−</sup> at m/z 253, due to the loss of H<sub>2</sub>O group, suggesting the presence of two −OH in or the positions. Peak <bold>12</bold> was identified as 5,6-Dihydroxy-7-O-glu acid flavanone[<xref rid="pone.0175751.ref024" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>]. Negative ESI analysis of Peak <bold>13</bold> gave the [M−H]<sup>−</sup> ion at <italic toggle="yes">m</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">z</italic> 445 and the aglycone anion at <italic toggle="yes">m</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">z</italic> 270. Furthermore, the same fragmentation patterns were observed as was for the Auto/MS of the protonated norwogonin, thus identifying peak <bold>13</bold> as a glucuronide of norwogonin. Also, spectrum from the UV showed the maximum absorption wavelength at 231nm and 280 nm and it was similar to that of norwogonin, showing the glycosylation of 7 −OH for <bold>13</bold>.
