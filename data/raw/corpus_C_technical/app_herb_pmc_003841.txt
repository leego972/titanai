During the following 2 weeks, they received each morning a supplement comprising either <italic toggle="yes">R. rosea</italic> HRE (a 5 g/kg solution containing 80% glycerin, i.e., 4 g/kg; test group, <italic toggle="yes">n</italic> = 8) or glycerin alone (4 g/kg; control group, <italic toggle="yes">n</italic> = 8) administered by gavage using a V0105040 feeding probe (ECIMED, Boissy‐Saint‐Léger, France). At the end of this period, the mice were subjected to an acute mild stress protocol and anxiety‐like behavior was evaluated. Blood was drawn from the mandibular vein before initiation of the stress protocol (at t0 min) and then at t30 min and t60 min. Mice were sacrificed at t90 min, and brain structures (hippocampus, hypothalamus, and amygdala) and plasma were excised and frozen at −80°C (Figure <xref rid="fsn31249-fig-0001" ref-type="fig">1</xref>).</p><fig fig-type="Figure" xml:lang="en" id="fsn31249-fig-0001" orientation="portrait" position="float"><label>Figure 1</label><caption><p>Experimental protocol in adult Balb/c mice</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" id="nlm-graphic-3" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="FSN3-7-3827-g001.jpg"/></fig></sec><sec id="fsn31249-sec-0007"><label>2.5</label><title>Induction of acute mild stress</title><p>On the last day of supplement administration, half the mice in each group were subjected to acute mild stress. The stress protocol consisted in subjecting the mice to an open‐field (OF) test for 10 min immediately followed by an elevated plus maze (EPM) test for 5 min (see the following sections for details; Figure <xref rid="fsn31249-fig-0001" ref-type="fig">1</xref>). Experiments were performed in the morning, one hour after gavage, under conditions of dim light and low noise. Both tests induce mild stress in animals by subjecting them to anxiogenic conditions (Treit, Menard, &amp; Royan, <xref rid="fsn31249-bib-0060" ref-type="ref">1993</xref>).</p></sec><sec id="fsn31249-sec-0008"><label>2.6</label><title>Evaluation of anxiety‐like behavior</title><p>Anxiety‐like behavior was evaluated after induction of acute mild stress as previously reported by Dinel et al. (<xref rid="fsn31249-bib-0015" ref-type="ref">2011</xref>). Mouse behavior was videotaped and scored using “Smart” software (Noldus, Wageningen, Netherlands).</p><sec id="fsn31249-sec-0009"><label>2.6.1</label><title>OF test</title><p>Mice were exposed to an unfamiliar square (40 × 40 cm) OF from which escape was prevented by surrounding walls (16 cm high). The apparatus was virtually divided into 4 central squares defined as the central area (anxiogenic) and 12 squares along the walls, defined as the periphery. Each mouse was placed in the central area and allowed to freely explore the OF for 10 min. Parameters recorded to evaluate anxiety‐like behavior comprised the number of entries into the central area and the percentage of time spent in this area (Dinel et al., <xref rid="fsn31249-bib-0015" ref-type="ref">2011</xref>).</p></sec><sec id="fsn31249-sec-0010"><label>2.6.2</label><title>EPM test</title><p>The EPM was a plus‐shaped acryl maze with two opposing open arms (30 × 8 cm) and two opposing closed arms (30 × 8 × 15 cm) connected by a central platform (8 × 8 cm), elevated 120 cm above the floor. Each mouse was placed in the center of the maze facing an open arm, a situation that is highly anxiogenic. The test was performed over a period of 5 min. The number of arm entries and the percent of time spent in open arms were calculated to evaluate the basal level of anxiety. An entry was scored as such only when the mouse placed all its four limbs in any particular arm. A reduction in the percentage time spent in the open arms and the number of entries into these is considered as an index of anxiety‐like behavior, independent of locomotor activity (Dinel et al., <xref rid="fsn31249-bib-0015" ref-type="ref">2011</xref>).</p></sec></sec><sec id="fsn31249-sec-0011"><label>2.7</label><title>Biochemical measurements</title><sec id="fsn31249-sec-0012"><label>2.7.1</label><title>Measurement of corticosterone</title><p>Corticosterone was measured in plasma before and 30, 60, and 90 min after initiation of the stress protocol, using a DetectX corticosterone immunoassay kit (Euromedex, Strasbourg, France) (Dinel, Joffre, et al., <xref rid="fsn31249-bib-0017" ref-type="ref">2014</xref>).</p></sec><sec id="fsn31249-sec-0013"><label>2.7.2</label><title>Assessment of RNA expression using Fluidigm microfluidic arrays</title><p>One microgram of total RNA was obtained from each brain area as described in Dinel et al. (Dinel, Andre, et al., <xref rid="fsn31249-bib-0016" ref-type="ref">2014</xref>) and was reverse‐transcribed with SuperScript III reverse transcriptase (Invitrogen, Cergy‐Pontoise, France).
