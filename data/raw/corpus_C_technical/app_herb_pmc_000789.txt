CYP Inhibition Assay in cDNA-Expressed Recombinant CYP Isozymes</title><p>The results of the initial assessment with human liver microsomes revealed that <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extract significantly inhibited the enzyme activities of all three enzymes. Accordingly, the CYP inhibitory effect of <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extract was further investigated using the cDNA-expressed recombinant CYP isozymes for CYP2C8, CYP2C19, and CYP2D6. As a result, the inhibitory effect of the <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extract on CYP2C8, CYP2C19 and CYP2D6 was concentration-dependent, as shown in the assay with human liver microsomes (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="molecules-22-01241-f003">Figure 3</xref>). The IC<sub>50</sub> values of the extract against CYP2C8, CYP2C19 and CYP2D6 were 0.08 ± 0.01, 0.58 ± 0.03 and 6.72 ± 0.22 mg/mL, respectively, which are lower than the corresponding levels in human liver microsomes. Additionally, the inhibitory effect of HDC against recombinant CYP isozymes was found to be negligible (data not shown); this finding was consistent with the data of human liver microsomes, confirming that the CYP inhibitory effect of <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extract was not attributed to HDC.</p></sec><sec id="sec2dot3-molecules-22-01241"><title>2.3. Time-Dependent Inhibition (TDI) and Mechanism-based Inactivation (MBI) Tests with Recombinant CYP2C8, CYP2C19 and CYP2D6 Isozymes</title><p>To elucidate whether the inhibition of CYP by <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extracts was time-dependent, the IC<sub>50</sub> values derived from the co-incubation and pre-incubation assays were compared. Thus, the <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extract in recombinant CYP2C8, CYP2C19 and CYP2D6 isozymes was pre-incubated for 20 min with NADPH and the IC<sub>50</sub> value shift for co-incubation was calculated. The inhibitory effects of <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extracts on CYP2C8 and CYP2C19 were more potent after pre-incubation (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="molecules-22-01241-f003">Figure 3</xref>), which suggested that the <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extract may act as a time-dependent inhibitor of CYP2C8 and CYP2C19. The IC<sub>50</sub> shift for CYP2C8, CYP2C19 and CYP2D6 from the co-incubation assay to the pre-incubation assay was 3.01 ± 0.02, 1.88 ± 0.01 and 1.21 ± 0.06 mg/mL, respectively. The fold shift was calculated as the ratio of the IC<sub>50</sub> of co-incubation (IC<sub>50-co</sub>) to that of the pre-incubation with NADPH IC<sub>50-pre</sub> as follows:
<disp-formula>IC<sub>50</sub> shift-fold = IC<sub>50-co/</sub>IC<sub>50-pre</sub></disp-formula></p><p>Compounds showing an IC<sub>50</sub> shift of ≥1.5 can be classified as positive for TDI [<xref rid="B17-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>,<xref rid="B18-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>]. Based on this criterion, <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extracts may have a time-dependent inhibitory effect on CYP2C8 and CYP2C19. Another IC<sub>50</sub> shift approach [<xref rid="B19-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>,<xref rid="B20-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>] was performed to identify the potential of MBI. For this, the IC<sub>50</sub> shift from the pre-incubation assay in the absence and presence of NADPH was investigated (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="molecules-22-01241-f003">Figure 3</xref>). As a result, the IC<sub>50</sub> shift for CYP2C8, CYP2C19 and CYP2D6 was 2.77 ± 0.12, 6.31 ± 0.25, and 1.38 ± 0.08 mg/mL, respectively. Compounds showing a ratio of IC<sub>50</sub> shift of ≥4 can be classified as positive for MBI [<xref rid="B19-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>]. Thus, <italic toggle="yes">H. helix</italic> extracts were considered to be a mechanism-based inactivator on CYP2C19.</p></sec></sec><sec id="sec3-molecules-22-01241"><title>3. Discussion</title><p>CYP enzymes are vital to the metabolism of many conventional and herbal medicines. Some of these enzymes can be induced or inhibited by xenobiotics, resulting in clinically significant drug-drug or herb–drug interactions and causing unanticipated adverse reactions and/or therapeutic failure [<xref rid="B17-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>]. There are no standardized methods for detecting in vitro TDI, but they generally have in common a two-step process of pre-incubation with a test compound followed by the quantification of enzyme activity with a probe substrate. The MBI represents the TDI, where the inhibitory effects are not only time-dependent but they also require metabolism by the enzyme that is ultimately inactivated [<xref rid="B20-molecules-22-01241" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>]. Thus, time-dependent enzyme inhibitors display an increasing degree of enzyme inhibition with an increased pre-incubation time with the enzyme.
