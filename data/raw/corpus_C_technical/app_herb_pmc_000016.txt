The extracts showed weak activities with MIC value of 250 µg/mL in comparison to MIC value of 3.1 µg/mL demonstrated by tetracycline, the positive control.</p><p>Otieno et al. The multi-plant extracts of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> mixed with <italic toggle="yes">Carissa spinarum</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Ximenia caffra</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Harrisonia abyssinica</italic> were more active against all tested microbes with inhibition zones ranging from 18 to 22 mm, MIC values ranging 8.3 ± 0.6 µg/mL to 55 ± 2.6 µg/mL and MBC values ranging from 0.02 mg/mL to 0.335 mg/mL [<xref rid="B25-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>]. The single extract of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> showed some activity against <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic> only with inhibition zone of 14 mm and MIC value of 55 ± 4.4 µg/mL. These results support use and preference of <italic toggle="yes">E. natelensis</italic> mixed with other plant species as remedy for stomach complaints in South Africa [<xref rid="B14-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>,<xref rid="B24-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>], amoebic dysentery, opportunistic infections and venereal diseases in Tanzania [<xref rid="B25-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot1dot2-molecules-22-02128"><title>5.1.2. Antimycobacterial Activity</title><p>Lall and Meyer [<xref rid="B39-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>] evaluated antimycobacterial activities of acetone and water root extracts of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> against drug-resistant and drug-sensitive strains of <italic toggle="yes">Mycobacterium tuberculosis</italic> H37Rv using the agar plate method. Acetone and water extracts inhibited the growth of <italic toggle="yes">Mycobacterium tuberculosis</italic> at a concentration of 0.5 mg/mL. Lall and Meyer [<xref rid="B39-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>] evaluated the acetone and water extracts using a rapid radiometric method against <italic toggle="yes">Mycobacterium tuberculosis</italic> and obtained a MIC value of 0.1 mg/mL against the strains. In 2001, Lall and Meyer [<xref rid="B64-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>] evaluated the antimycobacterial activities of diospyrin <bold>11</bold> isolated from <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> against the drug-sensitive and drug-resistant strains of <italic toggle="yes">Mycobacterium tuberculosis</italic> using the radiometric respiratory BACTEC assay.
