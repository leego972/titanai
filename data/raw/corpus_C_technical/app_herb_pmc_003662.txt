According to the data of the National Bureau of Statistics, in 2010, the illiteracy rate of men in the rural areas was 29%, and that of women was 71%. Men who received more education might develop more knowledge about medicinal plants. Moreover, the family collaboration model in the local Chuanqing people’s areas was “men work outside and women do housework” [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR44">44</xref>], so men had more opportunities to identify and collect medicinal plants in the field.</p><p id="Par30">The main families of medicinal plants used by the Chuanqing people were the Orchidaceae and Asparagaceae families. First, as one of the most typical karst areas in the world, Guizhou’s unique geographical location and complex natural environment provided suitable conditions for wild orchids [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR45">45</xref>]. There are 1240 species of orchids in 171 genera in China, of which 343 species of 82 genera were used for medicinal purposes [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR46">46</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR47">47</xref>]. Additionally, most plants in Asparagaceae, such as <italic toggle="yes">Polygonatum kingianum</italic> Collett &amp; Hemsl. and <ext-link xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" ext-link-type="uri" xlink:href="http://www.theplantlist.org/tpl1.1/record/kew-275055"><italic toggle="yes">Asparagus filicinus</italic> Buch.-Ham. ex D.Don</ext-link> were used frequently because their thick root tubers were attractive to herb collectors. Second, Orchidaceae and Asparagaceae plants such as <italic toggle="yes">Bletilla striata</italic> (Thunb.) Rchb.f., <italic toggle="yes">Gastrodia elata</italic> Blume., and <italic toggle="yes">Polygonatum kingianum</italic> Collett &amp; Hemsl. were widely used in TCM. <ext-link xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" ext-link-type="uri" xlink:href="http://www.theplantlist.org/tpl1.1/record/kew-275055">The</ext-link>se plants were traditional Chinese herbal medicines are commonly used by people of Han nationality. Therefore, a large amount of TCM was used by the Chuanqing people as they had long been influenced by the traditional Chinese medical system.</p><p id="Par31">The underground plant parts (the sum of roots, rhizomes, and root tubers) used in the Chuanqing people traditional medicine accounted for 46%. Therefore, why is the proportion of underground parts of medicinal plants used by the Chuanqing people so high? Roots, rhizomes, and tubers were frequently used as medicinal parts in TCM; more than one fourth of the medicinal plants added in over 400 preparations were derived from roots and/or rhizomes [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR48">48</xref>]. The whole plant, which accounted for 36%, was commonly used for medicinal purposes because the whole plant was easy to obtain and convenient to use by local people.</p><p id="Par32">Decoction (44%) was the most common preparation method used by the Chuanqing people. Decoction was also the most commonly used TCM compound dosage form by traditional Chinese doctors, and it was also the longest and most widely used preparation in the history of China [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR49">49</xref>]. Since the decoction method was also the most commonly used preparation method for the Chuanqing people, it can be seen again that their medicine has a long history of being influenced by TCM. Alcohol maceration was the second most common preparation method, accounting for 19%. Alcohol maceration was also a traditional Chinese medicinal preparation, with unique curative effects, a convenient preparation method, and wide application [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR50">50</xref>]; it was thus widely used. The Chuanqing people also had some other special usages, such as making a soup with glutinous rice wine and steaming with eggs.</p><p id="Par33">Most medicinal materials were used to treat diseases of the musculoskeletal system (34 mentions).
