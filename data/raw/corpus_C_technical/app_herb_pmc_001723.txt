[<xref rid="B12-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>] was found to inhibit DPPH with a much weaker activity (IC<sub>50</sub> = 463.71 μg/mL) compared to that reported by Ghribia et al. [<xref rid="B11-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. The HPLC-based analysis of the aqueous extract from the flowers revealed a rich composition of benzoic acids, cinnamic acids, and flavonoids (<xref rid="molecules-28-04396-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>). Among those identified were (reported in % <italic toggle="yes">w</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">w</italic> of crude extract) were quercetin <bold>3</bold> (0.112), kaempferol <bold>22</bold> (0.0445), naringenin <bold>42</bold> (0.145), syringic acid <bold>28</bold> (0.006), <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-hydroxybenzoic acid <bold>31</bold> (0.014), salicylic acid <bold>32</bold> (0.004), caffeic acid <bold>35</bold> (0.003), <italic toggle="yes">o</italic>-coumaric acid <bold>36</bold> (0.042), <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-coumaric acid <bold>37</bold> (0.002), and ferulic acid <bold>38</bold> (0.007). The hydroxybenzoic and hydroxycinnamic acid compositions in this extract were much more than those found in the methanolic extract of flowers collected in Tunisia. Interestingly, quercetin <bold>3</bold> and naringenin <bold>42</bold> were commonly found in both extracts. However, this aqueous extract showed a weaker antioxidant activity than the ethyl acetate extract. This is perhaps due to the bulk content of non-active compounds, such as benzoic acid <bold>24</bold> (0.162) and caffeine <bold>46</bold> (0.1), thereby contributing to the high value of IC<sub>50</sub>.</p><p>Elansary et al. [<xref rid="B13-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>] screened their methanolic leaf extract in a DPPH assay, which resulted in the reported IC<sub>50</sub> value of 17 μg/mL (<xref rid="molecules-28-04396-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>). The HPLC-based analysis of their leaf extract revealed a high content of phytochemicals (reported in % <italic toggle="yes">w</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">w</italic> of crude extract). These were gallic acid <bold>25</bold> (0.136) and <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-coumaric acid <bold>37</bold> (0.035). Other flavonoids such as rutin <bold>6</bold> (1.533), hyperoside <bold>9</bold> (0.633), isoquercetin <bold>8</bold> (0.073), quercetin <bold>3</bold> (0.006), and miquelianin <bold>7</bold> (0.126) were also identified. Isolated compounds such as quercetin <bold>3</bold>, rutin <bold>6</bold>, miquelianin <bold>7</bold>, isoquercetin <bold>8</bold>, hyperoside <bold>9</bold>, apigetrin <bold>17</bold>, gallic acid <bold>25</bold>, and <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-coumaric acid <bold>37</bold> were also tested alongside the crude extract. The results indicated that high-content compounds such as rutin <bold>6</bold>, hyperoside <bold>9</bold>, miquelianin <bold>7</bold>, and gallic acid <bold>25</bold> exerted potent antioxidant activities, with IC<sub>50</sub> values of 15, 4, 3, and 4 μg/mL, respectively. Evidently, these compounds were responsible for the high antioxidant activity exerted by the methanolic leaf extract. </p><p>The work by Salem et al. [<xref rid="B18-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>] with a bark extract was shown to have potent antioxidant activity in a DPPH assay, which yielded an IC<sub>50</sub> of 10.2 μg/mL compared to the positive control of ascorbic acid (IC<sub>50</sub> = 7.66 μg/mL) (<xref rid="molecules-28-04396-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>). Notably, the crude extract contained a higher content of phenolic acids and hydroxycinnamic acids than flavonoids. An HPLC-based analysis of the extract revealed (reported in % <italic toggle="yes">w</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">w</italic> of crude extract) gallic acid <bold>25</bold> (0.0255), benzoic acid <bold>24</bold> (0.0255), caffeine <bold>46</bold> (0.0106), and chlorogenic acid <bold>40</bold> (0.0106), followed by vanillin <bold>29</bold> (0.007), caffeic acid <bold>35</bold> (0.0054), rosmarinic acid <bold>39</bold> (0.005), and ferulic acid <bold>38</bold> (0.0042) as the main compounds. Flavonoids such as quercetin <bold>3</bold> (0.0037), kaempferol <bold>22</bold> (0.0011), and rutin <bold>6</bold> (0.0016) were also detected. </p><p>Four different extracts from individual parts (flowers, leaves, and bark) of Australian <italic toggle="yes">A. saligna</italic> were obtained by our group using a sequential polarity-based extraction (<xref rid="molecules-28-04396-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>).
