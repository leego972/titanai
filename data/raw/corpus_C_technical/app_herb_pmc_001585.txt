Novel Alkaloids</title><p>The main phytochemical class, well known for its wide pharmaceutical spectrum, is the alkaloids [<xref rid="B121-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">121</xref>]. Anticancer alkaloids comprise a large number of chemical structures [<xref rid="B18-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>]. Prominent among anticancer alkaloids are paclitaxel [<xref rid="B122-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">122</xref>], pyrrolizidines [<xref rid="B123-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">123</xref>], indole [<xref rid="B124-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">124</xref>], quinoline [<xref rid="B125-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">125</xref>], tropane [<xref rid="B126-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">126</xref>], and acronycine alkaloids [<xref rid="B127-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">127</xref>]. <xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f007" ref-type="fig">Figure 7</xref> shows the chemical structures of some of the newly identified anticancer alkaloids. There are some new taxane derivatives isolated from the ethanol extract of <italic toggle="yes">Taxus wallichiana</italic>. Compounds <bold>91</bold>–<bold>94</bold> (<xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f007" ref-type="fig">Figure 7</xref>) induced a tubulin effect similar to paclitaxel and had antiproliferative activities at IC<sub>50</sub> values between 0.077 and 7.48 µM against MCF -7, A549, 3-AO, and normal HUVEC cells [<xref rid="B128-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">128</xref>].</p><p>Another phytochemical group of pharmacological importance are the naphthoquinones [<xref rid="B129-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">129</xref>]. Some naphthoquinones have been reported as typical endoplasmic reticulum stressors (ERS) [<xref rid="B130-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">130</xref>,<xref rid="B131-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">131</xref>]. Studies have shown that ERS is involved in the activation of inositol-regulating enzyme 1 (IRE1), a representative of unfolded protein regulators (UPRs), which are responsible for proteostasis [<xref rid="B132-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">132</xref>,<xref rid="B133-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">133</xref>]. Persistent ERS induces IRE1 to activate ASK1 (apoptosis signal-regulating kinase 1) and subsequently, activation of the downstream c-Jun N-terminal kinases (JNK) phosphorylation, which induces cell death [<xref rid="B134-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">134</xref>]. Naphthoquinones are potent anti-invasive agents acting on EMT (endothelial mesothelial transition) of cancer stem cells and STAT3 signaling cascades [<xref rid="B135-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">135</xref>,<xref rid="B136-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">136</xref>,<xref rid="B137-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">137</xref>]. Their modulation of ROS enzymes such as ubiquitin-specific protease-2 (USP2) and NADPH Quinone Oxidoreductase 1 (NQO1) gave them selectivity against cancer cells [<xref rid="B138-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">138</xref>]. Compounds <bold>95</bold>–<bold>98</bold> (<xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f007" ref-type="fig">Figure 7</xref>) isolated from <italic toggle="yes">Alkanna cappadocica</italic> showed intriguing cytotoxic activities against a panel of cancer cells including HT-29, MDA-MB 231, PC-3, AU565, HepG2, LNCaP, MCF7, HeLa, SK-BR-3, DU 145, Saos, and Hep3B at low micromolar concentration. Compound <bold>95</bold> showed significantly higher activity in these cell lines than doxorubicin and etoposide. [<xref rid="B139-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">139</xref>]. Moreover, a new phytochemical from Alkanna tinctoria (Compound <bold>99</bold>, <xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f007" ref-type="fig">Figure 7</xref>) was isolated and evaluated for its cytotoxicity against HCT-116 and SW-480 (IC<sub>50</sub>: 4.4 and 9.6 µM, respectively) [<xref rid="B123-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">123</xref>]. A novel naphthoquinone alkaloid (Compound <bold>100</bold>, <xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f007" ref-type="fig">Figure 7</xref>) isolated from <italic toggle="yes">Goniothalamus lanceolatus</italic> showed selective antiproliferative propery that was more potent than 5-fluorouracil when tested against lung and colon carcinoma cells [<xref rid="B140-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">140</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec9-molecules-27-08307"><title>9.
