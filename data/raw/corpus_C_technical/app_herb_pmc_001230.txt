Methods</title><p>Major scientific literature databases, including PubMed, Scopus, ScienceDirect, SciFinder, Chemical Abstracts, Medicinal and Aromatic Plants Abstracts, Henriette′s Herbal Homepage, Dr. Duke′s Phytochemical and Ethnobotanical Databases, were searched to retrieve articles related to the review subject. General web searches using Google and Google scholar were also utilized. The search period covered articles published between 1980 and the end of October 2021. The search used the keywords and MeSH terms for ‘<italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic>’, AND (‘phytochemical content’, ‘pharmacological properties, or activities, or effects, or roles’, ‘anti-inflammatory’, ‘anti-drought’, ‘anti-thermal’, ‘anthelmintic’, ‘antidiabetic’,’ anticancer’, ‘anticholinesterase’, ‘antimicrobial’, ‘sedative’, ‘antipyretic’, ‘analgesic’, or ‘gastrointestinal’). </p></sec><sec id="sec4-molecules-27-04240"><title>4. Phytoconstituents of <italic toggle="yes">Zizyphus nummularia</italic></title><p><italic toggle="yes">Zizyphus nummularia</italic> is rich in several phytochemical compounds, particularly cyclopeptide alkaloids. One of the earliest studies performed in 1983 reported the isolation of two new cyclopeptide alkaloids, nummularine-M and nummularine-N (<xref rid="molecules-27-04240-t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref> and <xref rid="molecules-27-04240-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>), and the presence of a dozen cyclopeptide alkaloids in the root bark of the plant [<xref rid="B19-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>]. Further research by the same team led to the identification of other cyclopeptide components: nummularogenin in 1984 [<xref rid="B20-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>], nummularine-P in 1987, and nummularine-S in 1988 in the stem bark (<xref rid="molecules-27-04240-t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref> and <xref rid="molecules-27-04240-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>) [<xref rid="B21-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>,<xref rid="B22-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>]. Cyclopeptide alkaloids are natural macrocyclic compounds with fascinating chemical and biological characteristics, widely present in the Rhamnaceae family, and particularly the <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus</italic> genus [<xref rid="B23-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>,<xref rid="B24-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>]. In fact, these compounds have been described as playing a role in the central nervous system due to their sedative, analgesic, and anti-nociceptive effects, in addition to having antimicrobial properties and an antidiabetic potential [<xref rid="B23-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>], This has been drawing the attention of chemists and biologist to elucidate the structure-activity relationships of these compounds, being a promising source of therapeutic agents.</p><p>The use of different solvents in the process of extraction and plant part can lead to the identification of different phytochemicals. For instance, in a study investigating the composition of <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> leaves, the authors showed that 105 constituents were identified in the n-hexane extract consisting mainly of terpenoids, and 56 compounds were isolated from the ethanolic extract, mostly alkaloids and flavonoids with ethyl alpha-d-glucopyranoside as the main constituent (<xref rid="molecules-27-04240-t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref> and <xref rid="molecules-27-04240-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>) [<xref rid="B25-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>]. In another study using <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphus nummularia</italic> fruits, the dichloromethane and petroleum ether extracts showed the highest flavonoid content, compared to methanolic and acetone extractions, with the latter exhibiting a high phenolic content [<xref rid="B26-molecules-27-04240" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>].
