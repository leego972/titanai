# PMC: optical coherence tomography clinical application — Overview

DRIL is defined as the absence of distinguishable boundaries between the ganglion cell–IPL junction, INL, and OPL and is usually reported in eyes with macular edema.<sup><xref rid="bibr113-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>,<xref rid="bibr115-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">115</xref>,<xref rid="bibr116-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">116</xref></sup> Moien and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr117-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">117</xref></sup> have used OCTA with a two-layer segmentation scheme and found enlargement of the FAZ as well as ischemia in the superficial, deep, and full retina in eyes with DRIL. Using PR-OCTA as well as a three-layer segmentation scheme, Onishi and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr30-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">30</xref></sup> were able to further localize these perfusion deficits to the SCP or MCP in addition to underlying DCP ischemia present in areas of DRIL.</p><p>The complexity of perfusion deficits at multiple capillary levels on a 3D basis in these entities emphasizes the importance of OCTA and algorithms such as PR-OCTA in the study of macular ischemia at the capillary level.</p><sec id="section12-2515841419840249"><title>Posterior uveitis</title><p>OCTA has been shown to be a useful research tool and potential clinical adjunct in inflammatory eye disease. FA can provide valuable information about active areas of inflammation in retinal vasculitis by displaying vessel wall staining and leakage, but this leakage may prevent visualization of the adjacent vasculature that may therefore only be assessed with OCTA.<sup><xref rid="bibr118-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">118</xref></sup> In addition, OCTA can help identify complications such as inflammatory CNV, which mostly affects the macula and can lead to severe vision loss.<sup><xref rid="bibr119-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">119</xref></sup> While dye-based imaging is often helpful for identification of these neovascular membranes, there is also overlap with inflammatory lesions.<sup><xref rid="bibr118-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">118</xref>,<xref rid="bibr119-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">119</xref></sup> Additional OCT imaging may be useful as it can show subretinal hyperreflective tissue that is consistent with CNV, but can also be seen with other conditions like fibrosis.<sup><xref rid="bibr118-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">118</xref></sup></p><p>The highly detailed images and depth-resolved quantitative data regarding retinal perfusion contributed by OCTA provides information that may not be possible with other imaging modalities. For example, a case report by Pichi and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr120-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">120</xref></sup> was the first to report NV within a foci of retinochoroiditis secondary to <italic toggle="yes">Bartonella henselae</italic> with OCTA that was not seen on FA. With their cohort of uveitic eyes, Kim and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr121-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">121</xref></sup> used a two-layer segmentation scheme to show that parafoveal vessel density and fractal dimension in the SCP and DCP were lower in uveitic eyes compared to healthy eyes. In the same study, these authors found that uveitic eyes with macular edema had significantly lower DCP vessel densities (with no significant difference in SCP vessel densities) compared to uveitic eyes without edema, thus potentially implicating DCP ischemia in the development of cystoid macular edema.</p><p>OCTA has been helpful in the study of conditions such as birdshot chorioretinopathy (BSCR). de Carlo and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr122-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">122</xref></sup> used OCTA to study eight eyes of patients with BSCR and identified abnormalities such as inner retinal telangiectatic vessels and increased intercapillary space. These areas of decreased SCP perfusion have been found to correlate with central retinal thinning,<sup><xref rid="bibr123-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">123</xref></sup> raising the possibility that ischemia could contribute to ganglion cell damage and the macular thinning that has been observed in BSCR,<sup><xref rid="bibr118-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">118</xref></sup> although prior studies have shown that the majority of thinning is in the outer retina.<sup><xref rid="bibr124-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">124</xref></sup> In a patient with BSCR complicated by retinal NV and vitreous hemorrhage, Sarraf and colleagues found more profoundly impaired vessel densities in the DCP than the SCP compared to age-matched normal eyes, indicating that both ischemia and inflammation may contribute to the development of retinal NV in BSCR.<sup><xref rid="bibr125-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">125</xref></sup></p></sec><sec id="section13-2515841419840249"><title>Value of OCTA beyond the role of FA and ICGA</title><p>Given the ease and noninvasive nature of OCTA imaging in relation to conventional angiography, OCTA can be used to study changes in the retinal vasculature in conditions and procedures where angiographic imaging would not traditionally be indicated.
