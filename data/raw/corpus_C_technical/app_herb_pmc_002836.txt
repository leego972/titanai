The Role of Medicinal Plant Species in Social, Ecological, and Cultural Welfare</title><p>One study reported that some medicinal plants are also used for cultural and social purposes. For instance, <italic toggle="yes">Ficus thonningii</italic> Blume (umuvumu) is used in the fermentation of banana wine, and it was planted at the main gate as a symbol of protection and respect to family belongings. <italic toggle="yes">Erythrina abyssinica</italic> Lam. ex Dc. (umuko) is planted in the compound of the house for social and spiritual protection against devils. <italic toggle="yes">Erythrina abyssinica</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Ficus thonningii</italic> are important for rituals and social ceremonies [<xref rid="B33-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">Prinus africana</italic> was reported to be a threatened species [<xref rid="B38-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>], but was later reported as extinct in Buhanga sacred forest due to the over-exploitation of both its leaves and bark as ingredients, while <italic toggle="yes">Chenopodium ugandae</italic> is on the list of priority species, for which the preservation of stored grains would prevent them from going extinct [<xref rid="B33-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>]. </p><p>The populations of <italic toggle="yes">Euphorbia tirucalli</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Erythrina abyssinica</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Ficus thonningii</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Dracaena afromontana</italic> have heavily declined due to a shift from fencing with plants species to brick and concrete-walled fences [<xref rid="B38-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>].</p></sec></sec><sec sec-type="discussion" id="sec3-antibiotics-10-01231"><title>3. Discussion</title><p>The current review discusses the ethnobotany, ethnopharmacology, and phytochemistry of medicinal plants that are commonly used in treating diarrheal cases in Rwanda. The results showed that the most reported species with anti-diarrheal properties include <italic toggle="yes">Vernonia amygdalina, Clerodendrum myricoides</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Chenopodium ugandae,</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Chenopodium ambrosioides</italic> L. These species have been reported in East Africa [<xref rid="B62-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>,<xref rid="B63-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>,<xref rid="B64-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>], in other parts of Africa [<xref rid="B65-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>], and in Mexico as well as Brazil [<xref rid="B66-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>,<xref rid="B67-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>] for treating diarrhea. <italic toggle="yes">Psidium guajava</italic> L. is known for the treatment of diarrhea in Burundi [<xref rid="B67-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>], Tanzania [<xref rid="B63-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>], and in various tropical countries [<xref rid="B61-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>,<xref rid="B67-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>]. </p><p>Some medicinal plants, including <italic toggle="yes">Mitragyna rubrostipulata</italic> Havil, <italic toggle="yes">Tragia brevipes</italic> Pax, <italic toggle="yes">Euphorbia hirta</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Chenopodium ugandae</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Psidium guajava</italic>, are used to treat diarrhea, dysentery, cholera, or gastroenteritis in general [<xref rid="B33-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>,<xref rid="B42-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>,<xref rid="B44-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>]. This reflects slight differences among various forms of diarrhea or the limited knowledge of these forms by traditional healers [<xref rid="B34-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>,<xref rid="B68-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Psidium guajava</italic> has previously been reported to be used in the treatment of dysentery, gastroenteritis, stomach aches, and indigestion [<xref rid="B9-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. This may be explained by the fact that the same plant may contain various phytochemicals which are active against different diseases [<xref rid="B68-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>].
