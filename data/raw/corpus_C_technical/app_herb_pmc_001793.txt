Numerous flavonoids have been identified in SCOPA, however their relative abundance varies widely among plants and extracts from different sources (<xref rid="B36" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>, <xref rid="B41" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>). Since there exists abundant literature regarding the bioactivities of flavonoids, we will present only a subset of the most commonly reported, most abundant, or most thoroughly investigated flavonoids in SCOPA. As a representative example, cirsmaritin has been shown to have anti-proliferative, anti-metastatic, and anti-carcinogenic effects in cancer cell lines (<xref rid="B529" ref-type="bibr">529</xref>–<xref rid="B534" ref-type="bibr">534</xref>), diabetes- and metabolism-related effects (<xref rid="B535" ref-type="bibr">535</xref>–<xref rid="B537" ref-type="bibr">537</xref>), as well as anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and antimicrobial properties (<xref rid="B538" ref-type="bibr">538</xref>–<xref rid="B543" ref-type="bibr">543</xref>). Studies have also demonstrated that cirsimaritin can modulate neurological, immune, and digestive functions (<xref rid="B544" ref-type="bibr">544</xref>–<xref rid="B548" ref-type="bibr">548</xref>), and attenuate renal injury (<xref rid="B549" ref-type="bibr">549</xref>, <xref rid="B550" ref-type="bibr">550</xref>). Chlorogenic acid and its derivatives are abundant in SCOPA and have been shown to mediate some of SCOPA’s effects (<xref rid="B71" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>, <xref rid="B73" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>, <xref rid="B85" ref-type="bibr">85</xref>).
