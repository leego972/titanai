As discussed in the broader IC<sub>50</sub> section, cytotoxic potency is influenced by biological model and assay conditions. However, within maceration studies, this variability is further amplified by the diffusion dependent and relatively low selectivity nature of the method. Since maceration often yields crude extracts containing broad and variably enriched mixtures of metabolites, reported IC<sub>50</sub> values reflect not only intrinsic bioactivity, but also how solvent choice, plant matrix accessibility, extraction duration, and repeated extraction cycles shape the final chemical composition of the extract. This is particularly important as maceration does not actively intensify extraction through heat or pressure, and therefore recovery depends strongly on passive metabolite release from the plant matrix. Under these conditions, small differences in extraction time, solvent penetration, plant part, or tissue structure can substantially alter the relative abundance of active, less active, or potentially
