# PMC: phytochemical bioactive compound anticancer — Overview

NIR fluorescence imaging showed that the polysaccharides from <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> could protect against acute alcoholic liver injury in vivo by increasing the antioxidant levels, in which the level of GSH was balanced in the liver [<xref rid="B88" ref-type="bibr">88</xref>]. In addition, the alcohol-induced liver injury could be mitigated by the extracts of the <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> flower, which was associated with its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activities. The flower extracts treatment reduced the serum levels of ALT, AST, TC, and TG and increased the activities of the antioxidant enzymes. It was associated with the downregulation of hepatic cytochrome P450 2E1 (CYP2E1) and upregulation of Nrf2, HO-1, and NQO1. Moreover, it inhibited inflammation by downregulating TLR-4 and NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B p65 [<xref rid="B89" ref-type="bibr">89</xref>].</p><p>NAFLD is often caused by excessive lipid accumulation or steatosis due to an unhealthy diet pattern with little or no alcohol consumption. After the high-sucrose and high-fat diet was stopped, the 3-week administration of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> could reduce the hepatic lipid accumulation, regulate the metabolism of fatty acid, and improve the histopathology of the liver in NAFLD mice. It may increase the <italic toggle="yes">β</italic>-oxidation and reduce the synthesis, desaturation, and uptake of fatty acids and alleviate the abnormality of major phospholipids in the liver of mice [<xref rid="B90" ref-type="bibr">90</xref>]. Furthermore, the polysaccharides could also reduce the disturbed hepatic lipid metabolism involved with the fatty acid, glycerolipid, and glycerophospholipid, and it restored the metabolism of ceramide and bile acids in type 2 diabetic rats [<xref rid="B85" ref-type="bibr">85</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec4.6"><title>4.6. Lung Protection</title><p>Due to climate changes and personal unhealthy lifestyles, the risk of chronic respiratory disease and acute lung injury goes high in these decades. Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease increases airway inflammation and leads to respiratory dysfunction. Cigarette smoke is a vital risk factor for the incidence of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and it could induce mucus hypersecretion and viscosity. Both in vitro and in vivo studies found that <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> polysaccharides attenuated the cigarette smoke-induced mucus hypersecretion and viscosity by inhibiting mucus secretory granules and downregulating the expression of mucin-5AC (MUC5AC) [<xref rid="B91" ref-type="bibr">91</xref>]. Moreover, a randomized, double-blind, and placebo-controlled clinical trial was conducted on 40 patients with smoking habits and mild airflow obstruction, and patients randomly received 1.2 g <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> polysaccharides thrice daily. The treatment of polysaccharides could significantly ameliorate lung functions and reduce the serum levels of proinflammatory mediators (IL-6, IL-8, CRP, and TNF-<italic toggle="yes">α</italic>), and the expression of MUC5AC was decreased, and AQP5 was increased [<xref rid="B92" ref-type="bibr">92</xref>]. Additionally, it could decline cigarette smoke-induced oxidative stress in the lung and decrease the number of lymphocytes as well as monocytes in serum, which reduced the infiltration of inflammatory cells in lung tissue and inflammation indicators in serum. These effects might be mediated by inhibiting MAPK and NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B signaling pathways [<xref rid="B93" ref-type="bibr">93</xref>].</p><p>The polysaccharides of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> attenuated the bleomycin-induced pulmonary inflammation and fibrosis in rats by inhibiting the transforming growth factor-beta 1- (TGF-<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>1-) Smad2/3 signaling pathway, and it effectively suppressed the transformation of alveolar epithelial type II cells into myofibroblasts and reduced the expression of Smad2/3 and fibronectin in rats [<xref rid="B94" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>]. Besides, colitis-induced secondary lung injury could be attenuated by the polysaccharides, which reduced inflammation and oxidative stress. The treatment inhibited the protein expression of TLR4 and increased the protein expressions of Nrf2, HO-1, and NQO-1 both in mice and in LPS-stimulated BEAS2B cells, indicating that TLR4 and Nrf2 signaling pathways played an important role in it [<xref rid="B95" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec4.7"><title>4.7. Neuroprotection</title><p>
<italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> plays a crucial role in protecting the nervous system potentially by reducing neurological damage and improving memory as well as cognitive function. The extracts of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> reduced the impaired neurobehaviors and enhanced the antioxidant capacity in neonatal rats with hypoxic-ischemic brain damage (HIBD), and it protected against HIBD by inhibiting neuronal apoptosis and increasing the expression of neurotrophic factors [<xref rid="B96" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>].
