Gastrointestinal Modulation</title><p>In the past two decades, numerous findings have revealed that the gut microbiota and its derived microbial products are key influential factors in the host metabolism, and dysbiosis is tightly linked to a high risk of many metabolic diseases [<xref rid="B72" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>]. The polysaccharides of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> could regulate the composition and abundance of gut microbiota and its metabolites in mice, which increased the beneficial bacterium like <italic toggle="yes">Ruminococcus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Eubacterium</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Clostridium</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Bifidobacterium</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Parabacteroides</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Akkermansia muciniphila</italic> and decreased the harmful bacteria like <italic toggle="yes">Proteobacteria</italic> and further modulated the production of butyrate [<xref rid="B50" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>]. In addition, <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> increased the diversity of intestinal mucosal flora in mice fed with HFD, which enhanced the abundance of <italic toggle="yes">Ochrobactrum</italic> and reduced the abundance of <italic toggle="yes">Bifidobacterium</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Ruminococcus</italic>, and it further influenced the metabolism of carbohydrate, energy, and amino acid as well as gut microbiota to reduce HFD-induced negative effects [<xref rid="B73" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>].</p><p>As the most abundant and common microbial metabolites, short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) play an important role in the gut and metabolic health. Studies found that SCFAs mediated the G-protein coupled receptors (GPCRs), such as GPCR41 and GPCR43, and the regulation of the SCFA-GPCR pathway by <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> could alleviate metabolic disorders [<xref rid="B74" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>, <xref rid="B75" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>]. Moreover, the enzymatic fragments of polysaccharides could protect against dextran sulfate sodium- (DSS-) induced colitis by ameliorating the gut microbiota dysbiosis. The treatment inhibited the proinflammatory cytokines, restored SCFA levels, increased GPCR levels, and regulated the gut microbiota, which increased the abundance of <italic toggle="yes">Bacteroides</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Lactobacillus</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Ruminococcaceae</italic> and reduced the abundance of <italic toggle="yes">Proteobacteria</italic> [<xref rid="B74" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>]. On the other hand, the polysaccharides were found little absorbed and would be degraded into SCFAs in the large intestine after the oral administration, and thus, its modulatory effects on gut microbiota were considered the main contributor to its bioactivities [<xref rid="B76" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>].</p><p>
<italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> could not only alleviate metabolic disorders via the modulation of intestinal microbiota and microbial products but also improve gut health to maintain host homeostasis (<xref rid="fig2" ref-type="fig">Figure 2</xref>). An in vitro fermentation study showed that polysaccharides from <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> increased the levels of SCFAs which mainly contained the acetic, propionic, and butyric acids, and it changed the gut microbiota community and accelerated the metabolic pathways of amino acid and fatty acids. The results suggested that the polysaccharides had probiotic effects improving gastrointestinal health [<xref rid="B77" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>]. On the other hand, the polysaccharides could ameliorate inflammatory bowel disease by increasing miR-433-3p in the intestinal small extracellular vesicle. The increased delivery of miR-433-3p reduced the inflammation from excessive macrophage activity in the intestine by inhibiting the MAPK signaling pathway, which was beneficial for maintaining the intestinal microenvironment [<xref rid="B78" ref-type="bibr">78</xref>]. Besides, the <italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-acetyl-glucomannan extracted from <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> was found to improve the colonic microenvironment and benefit colon health in mice, which increased the content of SCFAs, colonic length, and fecal moisture and reduced the colonic pH and defecation time [<xref rid="B79" ref-type="bibr">79</xref>]. Furthermore, the ethanol-induced gastric mucosal injury could be protected by the polysaccharides from <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> leaves consisting of mannose, galacturonic acid, glucose, galactose, and arabinose, and it could improve antioxidant capacity and reduce the apoptosis in human gastric epithelial cell line GES-1 cells via the AMPK/mTOR signaling pathway [<xref rid="B80" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>].</p><p>The gut-liver axis has attracted great attention in the field of liver diseases since the gut-derived products could be transported directly to the liver via the portal vein, and the liver could give feedback via the bile and antibody secretion to the intestine [<xref rid="B81" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>].
