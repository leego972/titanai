ICF provides insight into the degree to which a community shares common knowledge and preferences for particular plant remedies, and it is widely used as an indicator of culturally important and potentially effective medicinal species.</p><p id="Par57">The ICF for each ailment category was calculated using the following formula:<disp-formula id="Equd"><tex-math id="d33e637">\documentclass[12pt]{minimal}

ICF values range from 0 to 1. Values approaching 1 indicate a high level of agreement among informants, meaning that a limited number of plant species are repeatedly used to treat a specific ailment. Such high consensus suggests strong cultural validation and highlights species that may have higher therapeutic relevance. In contrast, low ICF values indicate low agreement, with informants reporting a wide variety of species for the same ailment, reflecting more individualized or less standardized treatment practices. In this study, ailment categories with high ICF values were interpreted as culturally salient and were used to guide the identification of priority species for further ethnopharmacological evaluation.</p><p id="Par59">The Jaccard Similarity Index (JSI) was used to evaluate the degree of similarity in medicinal plant knowledge between the present study and other informant groups or geographic regions [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR17">17</xref>]. This index quantifies the extent to which different datasets share medicinal plant species and is commonly applied in comparative ethnobotanical studies to identify patterns of knowledge convergence and divergence.</p><p id="Par60">The JSI was calculated using the following formula:<disp-formula id="Eque"><tex-math id="d33e652">\documentclass[12pt]{minimal}

A value close to 0 indicates minimal overlap in medicinal plant use, suggesting distinct ethnobotanical knowledge systems or strong local specialization. In contrast, values approaching 1 indicate a high degree of shared species, reflecting similar cultural practices, ecological conditions, or historical connections between the communities being compared. In this study, JSI was used to interpret cross-regional similarities and differences in medicinal plant knowledge and to contextualize the Karamara ethnomedicinal system within broader national and regional ethnobotanical patterns.</p><p id="Par63">Preference ranking was used to evaluate the relative importance and perceived efficacy of selected MPs for specific ailments. Following the methodology of [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR2">2</xref>], ten key informants ranked five species based on their effectiveness against intestinal parasites, using a scale from 1 (least effective) to 5 (most effective). Cumulative scores established the overall ranking, highlighting species considered most efficacious by the local community [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR1">1</xref>]. This approach provided insight into community treatment priorities and perceptions of plant efficacy.</p><p id="Par64">To assess multipurpose utility beyond medicinal use, direct matrix ranking was conducted. Five commonly cited tree species were evaluated by ten key informants across seven categories: medicinal value, construction material, firewood, charcoal, furniture, agricultural tools, and food.
