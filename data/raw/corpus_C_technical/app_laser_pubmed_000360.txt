Posterior Hyaloid Adhesion as an Optical Coherence Tomography Biomarker of 
Active Toxoplasma Retinochoroiditis.

Chandra K(1), Pappuru RR(1), Nasir S(2), Ireni S(2), Basu S(2), Sadda S(3), Ali 
H(4), Tyagi M(1)(2).

Author information:
(1)Anant Bajaj Retina Institute, LV Prasad Eye Institute, Hyderabad, India.
(2)Saroja A Rao Centre for Uveitis, LV Prasad Eye Institute, Hyderabad, India.
(3)Doheny Eye Institute, University of California - Los Angeles (UCLA), Los 
Angeles, USA.
(4)Ophthalmic Epidemiology and Biostatistics, LV Prasad Eye Institute, 
Hyderabad, India.

PURPOSE: To explore the utility of Posterior Hyaloid Adhesions (PHA) as an 
Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT) biomarker of toxoplasma retinochoroiditi.
METHODS: A retrospective review of OCT scans of infective retinitis cases to 
evaluate the presence of PHA at the time of presentation.
RESULTS: Ninety-seven eyes of active toxoplasma retinochoroiditis and 125 eyes 
of retinitis of other etiologies were included. OCT feature of PHA was present 
in 70 eyes (72%) in the toxoplasmosis group as compared to other retinitis where 
it was present in 32 eyes (26%). Univariate analysis revealed that PHA was 
significantly more prevalent in toxoplasma retinochoroiditis compared to other 
retinitis. Multivariate analysis, adjusted for age and sex, revealed an odds 
ratio of finding PHA was 5.22 in patients with toxoplasma retinochoroiditis as 
compared to other retinitis. (p < 0.001).
CONCLUSION: PHA to a retinitis lesion can serve as a diagnostic OCT biomarker 
for active toxoplasma retinochoroiditis.
