Furthermore, meta-analyses demonstrate that, in patients with type 2 diabetes and periodontitis, SRP combined with diode laser therapy results in more substantial reductions in probing depth and HbA1c levels than SRP alone [<xref rid="B260-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">260</xref>,<xref rid="B261-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">261</xref>]. The photobiomodulation properties of diode lasers also promote tissue healing and reduce postoperative discomfort, which benefits patients with impaired wound healing associated with diabetes [<xref rid="B262-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">262</xref>]. This is particularly important for diabetic patients, who commonly experience delayed healing and an increased risk of infection.</p><p>Er:YAG lasers (wavelength: 2940 nm) are highly effective in ablating both hard and soft tissue while causing minimal thermal damage. When used to treat periodontitis, they allow subgingival calculus and diseased epithelium to be removed precisely while preserving healthy periodontal structures [<xref rid="B263-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">263</xref>,<xref rid="B264-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">264</xref>]. Clinical trials in diabetic patients have shown that Er:YAG-assisted periodontal therapy improves clinical attachment levels and reduces probing depths more effectively than SRP alone [<xref rid="B173-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">173</xref>]. Furthermore, Er:YAG lasers stimulate fibroblast proliferation and collagen synthesis, thereby supporting periodontal regeneration in patients with metabolic compromise [<xref rid="B265-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">265</xref>,<xref rid="B266-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">266</xref>]. Their ability to modulate inflammatory cytokines and enhance tissue oxygenation makes them particularly suitable for managing chronic periodontal inflammation in diabetic patients.</p><p>CO<sub>2</sub> lasers (wavelength: 10,600 nm) provide an effective means of ablating soft tissue and achieving haemostasis. Their high absorption by water-rich tissues enables precise debridement of the gingiva and periodontal pockets [<xref rid="B267-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">267</xref>]. Several clinical reports and trials [<xref rid="B66-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>,<xref rid="B268-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">268</xref>] have shown that, when applied as laser curettage or as an adjunct to subgingival CO<sub>2</sub> debridement alongside SRP in diabetic patients, CO<sub>2</sub> laser therapy reduces gingival inflammation and bleeding on probing and enhances pocket depth reduction, compared to SRP alone. CO<sub>2</sub> treatment also achieves good haemostasis and may reduce recolonisation of the pockets by microbes after surgery, which is important for long-term stability in hyperglycaemic conditions [<xref rid="B269-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">269</xref>]. Although there is less literature on CO<sub>2</sub> lasers than on diode or Er:YAG lasers, randomised controlled clinical studies support their beneficial role as an adjunct in periodontal therapy, also for patients with systemic conditions that impair healing, such as diabetes [<xref rid="B66-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>,<xref rid="B268-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">268</xref>]. Their haemostatic properties are particularly advantageous in patients with vascular fragility or on anticoagulant therapy, both of which are common in diabetic populations.</p><p>Furthermore, regular periodontal maintenance involving laser therapy in diabetic patients has been shown to reduce the risk of disease progression, improve oral hygiene outcomes, and enhance patient comfort and compliance [<xref rid="B254-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">254</xref>,<xref rid="B255-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">255</xref>]. The synergy between laser therapy and glycaemic control emphasises the importance of integrated management strategies that consider periodontal health to be an integral part of systemic disease management [<xref rid="B255-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">255</xref>,<xref rid="B270-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">270</xref>]. Collaboration between diabetologists and dental professionals is vital to ensure coordinated care and optimal patient outcomes.</p><p>Thus, laser technologies—including diode, Er:YAG, and CO<sub>2</sub> lasers—offer considerable advantages in the periodontal treatment of diabetic patients. Their ability to reduce the microbial load, modulate inflammation, and promote tissue regeneration can contribute to improved periodontal health and potentially positively impact glycaemic control. Incorporating laser-assisted therapies into routine periodontal management protocols for diabetic patients is a promising strategy for improving both oral and systemic health. Future research should focus on long-term outcomes, cost-effectiveness, and patient-reported outcomes to support broader clinical implementation.</p></sec></sec><sec><title>11.
