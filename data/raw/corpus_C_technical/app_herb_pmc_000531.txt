Introduction</title><p>In tropical and sub-tropical regions, there are abundant flora and fauna living in good climates and circumstances. Flora in particular can constitute valuable sources of various kinds of beneficial products such as dyes, edible tubers, oil crops, furniture, agricultural implements, ornamental plants, pharmaceutical products, rubbers, timbers, and cosmetics [<xref rid="B1-molecules-26-06922" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>]. Therefore, the preservation of biodiversity is a compulsory task for the protection of local ecosystems from destruction and to promote healthy conditions for organisms to thrive [<xref rid="B2-molecules-26-06922" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>]. There are several ways to promote and to preserve our local biodiversity, including supporting local farms, protecting bees, planting local flowers, fruits, and vegetables, taking shorter showers, respecting local habitats and knowing the sources of products [<xref rid="B3-molecules-26-06922" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>]. </p><p><italic toggle="yes">Carica papaya</italic> (papaw or papaya) is one of the tropical and subtropical trees that is well known for having the entirety of its parts utilized. As a tropical species, this plant grows continuously during winter, although growth slows down and fruit production ceases during the colder months [<xref rid="B4-molecules-26-06922" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>]. In Indonesia, the export demands for papaya come from Germany, Hong Kong, Japan, Malaysia, Singapore, Taiwan, and the USA; despite still being high, the export volume decreased from 2009 to 2020 [<xref rid="B5-molecules-26-06922" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>]. This could indicate the preservation of this crop becoming less intensive, leading to a reduction in its productivity.</p><p>The productivity of papaya has declined, along with the arising of challenges to its fronting, as a result of papaya dieback disease, which could jeopardize its future [<xref rid="B6-molecules-26-06922" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>]. One of the dieback diseases is <italic toggle="yes">Erwinia mallotivora</italic>, a phytopathogen bacteria, which plays significant roles in terms of overcoming and limiting the effect of this vulnerable crop [<xref rid="B7-molecules-26-06922" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>]. It has 4824 Kbp and the G + C content of the genome detected in papaya showed 52–54% homology to that of reference genomes of other Erwinia species [<xref rid="B8-molecules-26-06922" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>]. This information is useful for elucidating the infection mechanism of this disease, based on which the pathway inhibition strategy can be understood. Although all parts of the papaya crop have been widely studied, the fruit and the leaves are two major parts that are used daily for various purposes such as in foods, medicines, pesticides and cosmetics [<xref rid="B9-molecules-26-06922" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>].</p><p>In foods, the fruit has been advantageously used as a nutritional supplement [<xref rid="B10-molecules-26-06922" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>], appetizer [<xref rid="B11-molecules-26-06922" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>], and snack [<xref rid="B12-molecules-26-06922" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>], whereas as an herbal medicine, the leaves have been utilized in antimicrobe [<xref rid="B13-molecules-26-06922" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>], antioxidant [<xref rid="B14-molecules-26-06922" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>], antivirus [<xref rid="B15-molecules-26-06922" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>,<xref rid="B16-molecules-26-06922" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>], haematology disorder treatment [<xref rid="B17-molecules-26-06922" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>], and antitumor applications [<xref rid="B18-molecules-26-06922" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>].
