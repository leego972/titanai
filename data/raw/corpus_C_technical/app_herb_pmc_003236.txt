# PMC: ethnobotany medicinal plants Africa Asia — Overview

since <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia annua</italic> is the source of the current antimalarial drug, artemisinin, we searched for whether <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia annua</italic> has anti-diabetic uses. As such, we next used the search engine to search for &lt;<italic toggle="yes">Artemisia</italic>, diabetes&gt;, which yielded 2300 hits, but the first hit was on anti-diabetic plants used in the Eastern Cape, South Africa, and contained the plant name of <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia afra</italic> Jacq. (Asteraceae) [<xref rid="B21-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>]. Concentrating on the <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia</italic> genera, we hit upon <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia herba-alba</italic>, which reportedly has ethnic usage against diabetes and hypertension in a large section of North African countries [<xref rid="B22-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>].</p><p>We finally selected <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia herba-alba</italic> for two reasons; phytochemical constituents of the plant have been reported, and the fresh leaves of the plant are used in Northern Africa against the fungus <italic toggle="yes">Mucor rouxii</italic>. This fungus is one of the causative agents of mucormycosis, an often-fatal opportunistic fungal disease in immunocompromised patients, such as COVID-19 patients with diabetes [<xref rid="B23-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>,<xref rid="B24-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>], and against which any total cure is not only not available, but where the fungus-affected areas may need excision surgery not only of the affected areas but also large portions of adjoining areas due to the contagiousness and fast-spreading rate of the fungus. The fatality rate in cases of disseminated mucormycosis is 70–90%; amphotericin B and isavuconazole are considered first-line therapies against mucormycosis, with posaconazole being used as salvation therapy, but the treatment efficacy of these agents is not yet clear [<xref rid="B25-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>]. Interestingly, one of the ethnic uses of the plant <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia herba-alba</italic> is against <italic toggle="yes">Mucor rouxii</italic> infections; the bioactive phytochemicals have also been identified as carvone and piperitone [<xref rid="B22-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>]. This is an important issue for the growing number of cases of mucormycosis, particularly in India, as post-comorbidities of mucormycosis in COVID-19 patients necessitates the immediate discovery of suitable measures for patients who are cured of COVID-19 who then die from mucormycosis.</p><p>Literature searches of ethnic uses and pharmacological activities of <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia herba-alba</italic> were carried out using PubMed as the major source. The various search terms (keywords) used included &lt;<italic toggle="yes">Artemisia herba-alba</italic> with ethnomedicine or review or pharmacological activities or phytochemicals or indigenous uses&gt; or a combination of more than two terms in the above section. Boolean operators were used with the search terms [<xref rid="B26-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>]. It should be clarified in this section is that we did not view any ethnomedicinal uses or pharmacological activity reports as the final say but treated them as supporting or non-supporting documents to build on our hypothesis that <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia herba-alba</italic> may be a suitable plant for further analysis regarding the therapeutic potential of its phytochemicals for the treatment of COVID-19, its symptoms, and/or its comorbidities. It is also emphasized that the final paper selections were based on intense scrutinizing and detailed conversations between the authors involving literally several hundreds of papers in PubMed.</p><p>In silico studies (molecular docking) were conducted with various phytochemicals reported to be present in <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia herba-alba</italic> and the 3C-like protease of SARS-CoV-2, also known as the main protease or Mpro [<xref rid="B27-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>]. The pdb file (6LU7) of Mpro of SARS-CoV-2 3C-like protease was known before [<xref rid="B28-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>] and has been used in the present study. The pdb file contained inhibitor N3 bound Mpro; we conducted molecular docking studies with N3 removed from Mpro. A total of 24 phytochemicals present in <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia herba-alba</italic> [<xref rid="B29-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>,<xref rid="B30-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>,<xref rid="B31-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>] were studied for their binding affinity to Mpro. Ligand molecules were downloaded from Pubchem [<xref rid="B32-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>] in sdf format and were optimized with the force field type MMFF94 using Openbable software and were saved as pdbqt format.</p><p>Blind molecular docking using AutoDock Vina was carried out for ligand binding [<xref rid="B33-molecules-27-00492" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>].
