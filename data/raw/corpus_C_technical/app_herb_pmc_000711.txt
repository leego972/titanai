[Ca<sup>2+</sup>] is pumped out of cytosol and returns to a diastolic level mainly by the activation of the sarcolemmal Na<sup>+</sup>/Ca<sup>2+</sup> exchanger, the sarcoplasmic reticulum Ca<sup>2+</sup>-ATPase2a (SERCA2a), and the sarcolemmal Ca<sup>2+</sup>-ATPase [<xref rid="B11" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. In the diabetic state, the decreased activity of SERCA results from the interaction of advanced glycation end products (AGE) with SERCA and the overexpression of SERCA2a inhibitor phospholamban (PLB) and could be reversed by insulin treatment [<xref rid="B11" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. Suppression of SERCA2a ultimately leads to Ca<sup>2+</sup> overload in the cytosol and diastolic dysfunction [<xref rid="B12" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>]. The activity and expression of Na<sup>+</sup>/Ca<sup>2+</sup> exchanger which contribute to the removal of [Ca<sup>2+</sup>] is also decreased in the diabetic state. The abnormal function of RyR induced by AGE/RAGE and oxidative stress contributes to sarcoplasmic reticulum Ca<sup>2+</sup> leak, decreased sarcoplasmic reticulum-stored Ca<sup>2+</sup>, and systolic Ca<sup>2+</sup> transient [<xref rid="B13" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>, <xref rid="B14" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>]. Moreover, the decreased ATP synthesis rates which result from the reduced uptake of Ca<sup>2+</sup> by mitochondria cause impaired contractility ability. Consequently, the disorder of Ca<sup>2+</sup> handling contributes to diastolic/systolic dysfunction and left ventricular hypertrophy.</p></sec><sec id="sec2.5"><title>2.5. Cardiomyocyte Injury</title><p>Cardiomyocyte injury manners include apoptosis, necrosis, and autophagy. Apoptosis results from oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and abnormalities in Ca<sup>2+</sup> handling. Cardiomyocyte necrosis results in interstitial collagen deposition and ultimately leads to myocardial fibrosis [<xref rid="B15" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>].</p><p>Autophagy, a “housekeeping” subcellular process that maintains the cell nutrition homeostasis and self-renewal by degrading damaged proteins and organelles, is an alternate form of programmed cell death. Autophagy is also considered as a process that maintains cell survival in the condition of starvation and other cell stressors, for its regulation in the turnover of long-lived proteins, and protects cells. However, the dysregulated autophagy may result in excessive cell death. The role of autophagy in pathogenesis of DCM remains controversial. Xie et al. [<xref rid="B16" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>, <xref rid="B17" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>] reported that a low constitutive autophagy is essential for protecting cardiomyocytes from hyperglycemic damage, whereas, the defect autophagy in diabetes contributes to the development of DCM. Coincidence with this finding, Zhao et al. showed that enhanced autophagy prevents DCM induced by STZ administration [<xref rid="B18" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>]. However, Hou et al. showed that AGE impairs the cell viability of rat neonate cardiomyocytes in a dose-dependent manner by inducing autophagy [<xref rid="B19" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>]. Collectively, well-regulated autophagy is essential for DCM attenuation.</p></sec></sec><sec id="sec3"><title>3. Molecules and Signaling Pathways</title><p>NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B is a key transcription factor that regulates inflammatory and cardiomyocyte injury processes. NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B consists of five members including p65 (RelA), RelB, c-Rel, NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B1 (p50 and its precursor p105), and NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B2 (p52 and its precursor p100). The most abundant form of the NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B family is the p65/p50 heterodimer. In resting cells, NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B is inactive by binding to I<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B<italic toggle="yes">α</italic> in the cytoplasm. After high-glucose stimulation, the I<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B<italic toggle="yes">α</italic> is phosphorylated by I<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B kinase (IKK) complex, leading to the translocation of NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B to the nucleus and binding to NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B response element (RE) [<xref rid="B20" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>]. AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) suppresses NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B cascade through inhibition of IKK and decreased I<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B<italic toggle="yes">α</italic> degradation. NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B cascade may be induced by phosphorylation of mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK). It has been also showed that signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT) may contribute to activation of NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B and maintenance of NF-<italic toggle="yes">κ</italic>B activity.
