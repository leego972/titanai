These species belong to 140 genera and 60 families. Among them, 111 species (67.68%) were used exclusively for human ailments, while 32 species (19.51%) were used for livestock ailments. The remaining 21 species (12.8%) were utilized for both human and livestock conditions. In terms of species count, the Asteraceae and Fabaceae families each contained 14 species (8.53%), making them the most numerous. They were followed by the Lamiaceae family with 10 species (6.09%) and the Cucurbitaceae and Poaceae families, each with 8 species (4.87%).
