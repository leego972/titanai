rid="bib215" ref-type="bibr">Zhao et al., 2014</xref>).</p></sec><sec id="s0050"><label>4.6</label><title>Changes in the chemical constitution after processing</title><p id="p0090">The chemical constituents in <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> changes after processing, and novel components can be created. The combined anthraquinone content decreases with increased processing. The content of free anthraquinones, such as emodin and physcion, increases with prolonged processing time. The content of <sc>d</sc>-glucose increases gradually, whereas the <sc>d</sc>-fructose and sucrose contents decrease gradually, and the content of polysaccharide slightly increases. The content of stilbene glucoside (index component of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic>) is reduced with an increase in the processed time, and the content exhibited the following order: rice wine mixed with steamed&gt;steamed&gt;rice wine and fried&gt;black bean juice products. After processing, the content of tannin decreased, and the antioxidant activity of the gallic acid content increased; however, catechin was almost undetectable after processing for 16 h. Other studies have found that the content of trace elements in fleece-flower root before and after processing changed only slightly (<xref rid="bib208" ref-type="bibr">Zhang et al., 2009</xref>, <xref rid="bib16" ref-type="bibr">Chen et al., 2012d</xref>, <xref rid="bib38" ref-type="bibr">Fu, 2013</xref>, <xref rid="bib97" ref-type="bibr">Liu et al., 2008b</xref>, <xref rid="bib126" ref-type="bibr">Qiu and Huang, 2006a</xref>, <xref rid="bib127" ref-type="bibr">Qiu and Zeng, 2006b</xref>, <xref rid="bib151" ref-type="bibr">Wang et al., 2004</xref>, <xref rid="bib130" ref-type="bibr">Shi, 2003</xref>, <xref rid="bib103" ref-type="bibr">Liu et al., 2005c</xref>, <xref rid="bib100" ref-type="bibr">Liu et al., 2009b</xref>, <xref rid="bib116" ref-type="bibr">Nie and Liu, 2002</xref>).</p><p id="p0095">According to a previous study, <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> processing resulted in the production of five ingredients: 2,3-di-hydro-3,5-dihydroxy-6-methyl-4(H)-pyran-4-one (<bold>99</bold>), hydroxymaltol (<bold>100</bold>), 5-hydroxym ethyl-furfural (<bold>101</bold>), butanedioic acid (<bold>102</bold>), and 5-dihydroxy-6-methyl-4(H)-pyran-4-one (<xref rid="bib101" ref-type="bibr">Liu et al., 2007b</xref>, <xref rid="bib297" ref-type="bibr">Liu et al., 2008c</xref>, <xref rid="bib102" ref-type="bibr">Liu et al., 2009c</xref>).
