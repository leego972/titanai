Based on comparative phytochemical and biochemical data, a complete triterpenoid saponin biosynthetic pathway (TSBP) has been proposed for the first time in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, encompassing the mevalonate pathway, β-amyrin cyclization, oxidation to oleanolic acid, and subsequent glycosylation and methylation reactions.</p><p>From the authors’ perspective, the integration of structural, pharmacological, and biosynthetic insights offers a robust and coherent foundation for future studies focused on metabolic engineering, quality control of herbal preparations, and the exploration of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> saponins as candidates for pharmaceutical and biotechnological applications.</p></sec></sec><sec id="sec5dot2-molecules-30-03460"><title>5.2. Phenolic Constituents of Polyscias fruticosa: Chemotaxonomic Significance and Pharmacological Perspectives</title><sec id="sec5dot2dot1-molecules-30-03460"><title>5.2.1. Flavonoids and Phenolic Acids in <italic toggle="yes">Polyscias fruticosa</italic></title><p>To date, only one comprehensive review on phenolic compounds in the genus <italic toggle="yes">Polyscias</italic> has been published, authored by Ashmawy et al. [<xref rid="B4-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>]. Identified compounds include quercetin-3-O-glucoside (<italic toggle="yes">P. fulva</italic>), luteolin (<italic toggle="yes">P. nodosa</italic>), lichexanthone (<italic toggle="yes">P. fulva</italic>), tamaraxetin 3,7-di-O-α-L-rhamnopyranoside (<italic toggle="yes">P. balfouriana</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">P. guilfoylei</italic>), and various other phenolic derivatives. The 2020 review by Ashmawy et al. [<xref rid="B4-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>] did not include phenolic compounds from <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, as these constituents were only confirmed in subsequent studies published between 2021 and 2023. This section provides, for the first time, a comprehensive summary and discussion of 20 phenolic compounds, encompassing both simple phenolics (e.g., 2,6-dimethoxyphenol) and more complex lignin-derived eugenol-type structures (e.g., sinapyl alcohol, 1′-hydroxyeugenol, 1,2-bis(4-hydroxy-3-methoxyphenyl)ethylene) (<xref rid="molecules-30-03460-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>).</p><p>Rarison et al. [<xref rid="B10-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>] identified nine phenolic compounds in the dichloromethane fraction (PFLD) of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> leaf extract. After extraction with 95% ethanol and partitioning with n-hexane and dichloromethane, GC-MS analysis revealed several low-polarity phenolics, including 2-methoxy-4-vinylphenol (<bold>36</bold>), 2,6-dimethoxy-4-vinylphenol (<bold>32</bold>), sinapyl alcohol (<bold>39</bold>), 4-allyl-2,6-dimethoxyphenol (<bold>37</bold>), 1′-hydroxyeugenol (31), 2,6-dimethoxyphenol (<bold>33</bold>), and the dimeric 1,2-bis(4-hydroxy-3-methoxyphenyl)ethylene (<bold>38</bold>). Two additional compounds, 4-vinylphenol (<bold>34</bold>) and vanillin (<bold>35</bold>), were also detected; however, their identification remains tentative. These two structures are commonly recognized as potential thermal artifacts or lignin degradation products and are known to occur as contaminants in GC-MS profiles. In the absence of orthogonal validation (e.g., LC–MS or NMR), their biosynthetic origin in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> remains speculative and should be interpreted with caution. Therefore, their inclusion in the phenolic profile of this species should be treated with caution. These findings suggest that the lipophilic leaf fraction of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> may represent an underexplored source of structurally unusual phenolic compounds with potential antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activities, warranting further investigation (<xref rid="molecules-30-03460-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>).</p><p>Selvaraj et al.
