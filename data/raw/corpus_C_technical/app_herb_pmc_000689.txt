Chemical fingerprint analysis which is recognized as a reliable means for the identification and qualification of herbal medicines, has been accepted by Food and Drug Administration (2000) [<xref rid="B63-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>], European Medicines Agency (2001) [<xref rid="B64-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>], and State Food and Drug Administration of China (2000) [<xref rid="B65-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>] and other authorities as a strategy for quality assessment of herbal medicines. A HPLC-diode array detector (DAD) fingerprint was applied to 56 batches of <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> materials from six provinces in China. The results showed that the most relevant factor in secondary metabolites of <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> was collection location, followed by harvesting time [<xref rid="B62-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>]. Moreover, the on-line LC-MS<sup>n</sup> technique, which provides a ready method for elucidation of compounds and more information in the fingerprint by reprocessing the mass spectrometry data using tandem MS, has become a powerful means in the quality control and quantitative analysis of complicated medicinal herbs. A HPLC-PAD-ESI-MS<italic toggle="yes"><sup>n</sup></italic> method was successfully developed to evaluate the quality of <italic toggle="yes">H. japomicum</italic> from different origins by establishing chromatographic fingerprints, in which 20 common peaks of 10 batches of <italic toggle="yes">H. japomicum</italic> were identified [<xref rid="B58-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>]. Headspsce GC-MS (HSGC-MS) was used to establish the fingerprint of the volatile components from 11 batches of <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> materials. The HSGC-MS fingerprint with 12 common peaks was characteristic and useful for quality control of <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> materials [<xref rid="B66-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>].
