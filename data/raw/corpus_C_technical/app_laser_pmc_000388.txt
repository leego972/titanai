As it has those advantages, PDT treatment has been extensively developed as a new modality and an alternative to classical ones such as surgery, radiation or chemotherapy, being particularly suitable for treating multiple lesions and field cancerization [<xref rid="B26-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>]. PDT is more beneficial in those patients in which size, site or number of lesions limit the efficacy and/or acceptability of conventional therapies [<xref rid="B27-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>].</p><p>This treatment is comparatively non-invasive and is based on the interaction of three elements: a photosensitizing compound, light of an adequate wavelength and oxygen. When the photosensitizer (PS) is activated by light, it triggers a photochemical reaction by which singlet oxygen (<sup>1</sup>O<sub>2</sub>) and other reactive oxygen species (ROS) are produced and selectively kill cancer cells [<xref rid="B27-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>,<xref rid="B28-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>,<xref rid="B29-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>,<xref rid="B30-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>]. Anti-tumoral effects of PDT derive from three principal mechanisms: direct cytotoxicity on cancer cells, indirect effects consequence of damage to the tumor vasculature, and the activation of the immune response.</p><p>The principal compounds used in cutaneous oncology are: 5 aminolevulinic acid (ALA, Levulan<sup>®</sup> (Wilmington, DE, USA) and Ameluz<sup>®</sup> (Leverkusen, Germany) and its methylated derivate (MAL, Metvix<sup>®</sup> (Alby sur Cheran, France)) [<xref rid="B27-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>,<xref rid="B31-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>]. MAL is approved in Europe for the treatment of AK, BD, and for superficial and nodular (less than 2 mm of deepness) BCC, whereas ALA is only indicated for AK. ALA is a precursor of intermediate porphyrins of heme group biosynthesis, specifically of the photoactive compound Protoporphyrin IX (PpIX). ALA administration results in the production and selective accumulation of the PS PpIX in cancer cells. There are also some studies about the use of PDT for other skin cancer types such as lymphomas [<xref rid="B26-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>].</p><p>It has been reported that PDT exerts an anti-tumor immunological effect. This sometimes could be associated with an intense inflammatory response, characterized by an increase of cytokines and accumulation of leukocytes in targeted tumor areas, favoring tumor destruction. On the other hand, Evangelou <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> [<xref rid="B32-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>] revealed that MAL-PDT of BCC reduces local epidermal Langerhans cells. Thus, this suppression of the skin immune response might potentially result in a less effective antitumor response.</p><sec id="sec2dot1-ijms-16-25912"><title>2.1. Limitations of Photodynamic Therapy (PDT)</title><p>Among the benefits of MAL-PDT are its high rates of complete response (CR); in BCC, 91% at three months and 76% at five years of follow up, and in BD, 86% to 93% at three months and 68% to 71% at 24 months [<xref rid="B33-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>]. Other advantages include the possibility of combining PDT with other therapies and repeat the process as needed with an excellent cosmetic result and greater satisfaction of the patient. However its limitations are, pain during illumination [<xref rid="B34-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>,<xref rid="B35-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>], the penetration of the photosensitizer and light, and the possibility of tumor resistance, occasionally found in those refractory to treatment [<xref rid="B36-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>,<xref rid="B37-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>]. Next, we describe the factors that may limit the response to PDT.</p><p>Pain is the most common adverse effect in PDT and it is one of the limiting factors in some patients. Such pain seems due to the combination of heat and intense nerve stimulation. While between 60% and 80% of patients consider the pain light to moderate, 20% describe it as intense [<xref rid="B34-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>]. It has been observed that this technique is less painful when using MAL than ALA, being pre-treatment PpIX fluorescence and lesion diameter associated with pain [<xref rid="B36-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>].</p><p>The intensity of pain is related to the area of treatment, the amount of pain receptor nerves at the tumor site (more in head, hands and perineum), male patients, phototype and in the second cycle of PDT treatment [<xref rid="B33-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>,<xref rid="B37-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>].
