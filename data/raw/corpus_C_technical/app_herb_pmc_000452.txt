# PMC: herbal medicine pharmacology mechanism review — Overview

ref-type="table">Table 6</xref>.</p><p>Berberine, an isoquinoline alkaloid found in Berberis aristata, is frequently employed in the treatment of bacterial diarrhea [<xref rid="B115-molecules-28-06370" ref-type="bibr">115</xref>]. Due to its poor water solubility and limited permeability through intestinal epithelial cells, its oral bioavailability is around 5%. To enhance its solubility, Sahibzada et al. developed two berberine nanocrystals using the evaporation precipitation method (EPN) and antisolvent precipitation with a syringe pump method (APSP). Following nanocrystallization, berberine was in a semicrystalline state, with particle sizes of 90–110 nm for APSP and 65–75 nm for EPN. Both formulations exhibited significantly improved dissolution rates compared to crude berberine. Antimicrobial assays demonstrated that berberine nanocrystals had more potent antibacterial activity than unprocessed berberine [<xref rid="B116-molecules-28-06370" ref-type="bibr">116</xref>].</p><p>Herpetrione (HPE), extracted from Herpetospermum caudigerum, has demonstrated potent antiviral properties. Guo et al. developed HPE nanocrystals (HPE NS) using a high-pressure homogenization method. The HPE NS exhibited a substantial increase in dissolution rate. After oral administration in rats, C<sub>max</sub> and AUC<sub>0–t</sub> increased by 2.45-fold and 2.49-fold, respectively, compared to crude drugs. Reduced Tmax and MRT suggested rapid intestinal absorption. Activity assessment of HPE NS indicated enhanced inhibition of HBsAg and HBeAg replication and expression, as well as more significant inhibitory effects on HBV-DNA [<xref rid="B117-molecules-28-06370" ref-type="bibr">117</xref>].</p><p>While improving oral bioavailability is a significant advantage of nanocrystals for herbal medicines, not all drugs experience improved efficacy after conversion into nanocrystals. Thymol, known for its broad-spectrum antibacterial and antifungal activities and used in treating coughs and diarrhea [<xref rid="B118-molecules-28-06370" ref-type="bibr">118</xref>], was examined by Zhou et al. They produced a caseinate-stabilized thymol nanocrystal through a pH-driven approach to enhance water solubility. X-ray diffraction analysis revealed that thymol nanocrystals were in an amorphous state. Unexpectedly, in antibacterial activity studies, the MICs and MBCs of thymol nanocrystals were higher than those of free thymol against four bacterial strains. This outcome contrasted with the conventional understanding and might be attributed to caseinate coating preventing thymol release, thereby reducing its contact with bacteria [<xref
