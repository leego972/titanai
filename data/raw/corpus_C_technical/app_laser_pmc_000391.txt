Actinic Keratosis</title><p><xref ref-type="table" rid="ijms-16-25912-t001">Table 1</xref> summarizes AK studies combining PDT with other treatments. 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) induces cell cycle arrest and apoptosis and is used in the treatment of AK and superficial BCC twice-daily for two or more weeks [<xref rid="B75-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>]. In a study with 15 patients with facial AK applied 5-FU nightly for five days and underwent ALA-PDT on the sixth day. At one month and one year post-treatment, 90% of treated AK had resolved [<xref rid="B76-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>]. Martin reported in 2011 [<xref rid="B77-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>] that the treatment with sequential 5-FU and ALA-PDT in three old men with AK, when compared with ALA-PDT alone, was more effective, minimized the recurrence of areas of field cancerization and improves the appearance of the skin.</p><p>Imiquimod 5% cream stimulates the innate immune response and is applied between twice and five times a week for 6 to 16 weeks, depending on whether it is being used for AK, or superficial BCC [<xref rid="B75-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>]. In a study from 2009 [<xref rid="B78-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">78</xref>] it was demonstrated that ALA-PDT followed by imiquimod (twice a week for 16 weeks) was more effective than PDT alone. At month 12, the median lesion reduction was 89.9% in patients who were treated with combined therapy <italic toggle="yes">vs.</italic> 74.5% of PDT alone. Serra-Guillén <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> [<xref rid="B79-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">79</xref>] also demonstrated that PDT and imiquimod was more efficacious that PDT alone, but more patients were satisfied with PDT than with the other two modalities. In a more recent study, three patients received one session of PDT plus imiquimod three days per week for one month, and those without complete response underwent a second four-week course. Two patients showed complete clinical clearance of AK [<xref rid="B80-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>].</p><p>Diclofenac is a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug that reduces the production of prostaglandins by inhibiting inducible COX-2. The approved treatment regimen for AK consists of twice-daily application for 60 to 90 days [<xref rid="B75-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>]. Akita <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> [<xref rid="B81-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>] showed a considerable expression of COX-2 in AK (15 of 25, 60%) and BD (13 of 17, 76%). In contrast, only one of 33 (3%) BCC was a COX-2 high expresser. Bagazgoitia <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> [<xref rid="B82-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">82</xref>] also observed that COX-2 is overexpressed in AK and BD, and its expression could be correlated with the PDT response. In a study with 10 patients, the pretreatment with diclofenac plus ALA-PDT reduced the number of lesions compared to PDT alone after 12 months [<xref rid="B83-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">83</xref>].</p><p>Ingenol mebutate appears to have multiple mechanisms of action, including direct cell death and a complex inflammatory response [<xref rid="B75-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>]. Berman <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> [<xref rid="B84-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">84</xref>] carried out a study with 24 patients that were divided in two treatments with ALA-PDT, 1 ALA-PDT treatment and one course of ingenol mebutate daily for three consecutive days, or one course of ingenol mebutate alone. Subjects in the two ALA-PDT treatment group had a 97.5% mean reduction from the number of baseline AK; ALA-PDT plus ingenol mebutate gel reached 86.7% mean reduction; while subjects in the ingenol mebutate group had a 91.7% mean reduction.</p><p>Fractional laser produces small columns of thermal injury to the skin, which are known as microthermal zones (MTZs). These MTZs vary from one device to another. Some are nonablative dermal injuries only, whereas others are associated with ablative changes in the skin, causing both epidermal and dermal injury patterns. In this sense, Togsverd-Bo <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> [<xref rid="B85-ijms-16-25912" ref-type="bibr">85</xref>] compared ablative fractional laser (AFL)-assisted daylight photodynamic therapy (AFL-dPDT) with daylight PDT (dPDT), conventional PDT (cPDT) and AFL alone (AFL) in field treatment of AK in organ-transplant recipients.
