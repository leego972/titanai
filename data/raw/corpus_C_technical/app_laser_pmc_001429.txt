In their study, Sellam and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr85-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">85</xref></sup> used OCTA in RVO patients before and after intravitreal antivascular endothelial growth factor injections and found that significant decreases in retinal fluid, capillary disruption, and capillary cysts could be visualized post intervention.</p></sec><sec id="section10-2515841419840249"><title>Glaucoma</title><p>Although the exact pathophysiologic process of glaucoma is unknown, there is convincing evidence that vascular dysfunction contributes to the progression of the disease.<sup><xref rid="bibr86-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">86</xref><xref rid="bibr87-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr88-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr89-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">89</xref></sup> For that reason, OCTA has been a popular tool for the study of the microvasculature in glaucoma, with the majority of these reports examining the peripapillary region. Generally, these studies have found vascular dysfunction in glaucomatous eyes compared to healthy controls.<sup><xref rid="bibr90-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">90</xref><xref rid="bibr91-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr92-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr93-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">93</xref></sup> Optic disk flow index, the average decorrelation signal in OCTA, has previously been shown to have high sensitivity and specificity for glaucoma and to be highly correlated with visual field pattern standard deviation.<sup><xref rid="bibr94-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">94</xref></sup> Peripapillary perfusion deficits have also been shown to be associated with structural changes in glaucoma on OCTA.<sup><xref rid="bibr93-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">93</xref></sup> Those OCTA studies examining vascular changes in the macula have identified similar dysfunction in retinal blood flow and perfusion.<sup><xref rid="bibr95-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">95</xref><xref rid="bibr96-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr97-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr98-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">98</xref></sup></p><p>OCTA reports have suggested that quantitative OCTA parameters such as vessel density could eventually be used for the diagnosis of glaucoma. Takusagawa and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr96-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">96</xref></sup> demonstrated a high area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.961 using superficial macular vessel density for glaucoma, consistent with a similar report by Richter and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr95-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">95</xref></sup> who identified an AUC of 0.83 for the macular microvasculature. However, there has been some controversy as to the area of choice as Rao and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr99-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">99</xref></sup> found that peripapillary and inside the optic disk vessel densities had higher AUC than macular vessel density.</p><p>One important unresolved question in the field relates to whether vascular changes precede structural changes or whether loss of the retinal ganglion cells and their decreased metabolic need leads to the reduced blood flow observed with OCTA.<sup><xref rid="bibr100-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">100</xref></sup> In their longitudinal OCTA study, Shoji and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr101-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">101</xref></sup> demonstrated that the average rate of decline in macular vessel density over the course of at least 1 year was significantly higher in glaucomatous eyes than healthy or glaucoma suspect eyes.
