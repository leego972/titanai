These findings suggest that the lipophilic leaf fraction of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> may represent an underexplored source of structurally unusual phenolic compounds with potential antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activities, warranting further investigation (<xref rid="molecules-30-03460-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>).</p><p>Selvaraj et al. [<xref rid="B21-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>] identified phenolic compounds in the ethyl acetate fraction derived from a 75% ethanol extract of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> roots (EEPF). Chromatographic purification yielded four phenolic compounds, including one novel and three previously known structures. The newly discovered compound, named politoic acid (<bold>28</bold>), was characterized by HRESI-MS and 1D/2D NMR (<sup>1</sup>H, <sup>13</sup>C, HSQC, HMBC) as (E)-2-(4-((E)-2-carboxyvinyl)-2-methoxyphenoxy)-3-(4-hydroxyphenyl)acrylic acid—a unique lignin-derived dimer not previously reported in any <italic toggle="yes">Polyscias</italic> species, thereby extending the chemotaxonomic spectrum of this genus.</p><p>Additionally, two oligomers were isolated: 8-O-4-dehydrodiferulic acid (<bold>29</bold>) and 8-O-4/8-O-4-dehydrotriferulic acid (<bold>30</bold>), representing a ferulic acid dimer and trimer, respectively, both with known antioxidant and neuroprotective effects. These compounds were identified for the first time in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> and the <italic toggle="yes">Polyscias</italic> genus overall, substantially expanding its known phenolic repertoire [<xref rid="B21-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>].</p><p>The same fraction also contained protocatechuic acid (PCA) (<bold>27</bold>), a well-known plant-derived phenolic acid with potent antioxidant activity [<xref rid="B86-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>]. Although structurally familiar, this compound had not previously been reported in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, making its identification a notable contribution to the species’ phytochemistry and potentially linking it to the documented pharmacological properties of other Araliaceae taxa. PCA acts through ROS scavenging, metal chelation, and inhibition of lipid peroxidation. Furthermore, it exhibits antidiabetic properties by inhibiting α-amylase and α-glucosidase, thereby reducing postprandial hyperglycemia. Notably, it inhibited α-glucosidase with an IC<sub>50</sub> of 1.76 µM [<xref rid="B86-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>,<xref rid="B87-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">87</xref>]. PCA has also shown anticancer activity by suppressing proliferation, inducing apoptosis, and modulating signaling pathways (NF-κB, PI3K/AKT, MAPK), and exerts chemopreventive effects by normalizing redox balance and cellular metabolism [<xref rid="B88-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">88</xref>].</p><p>Do et al. [<xref rid="B8-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>] conducted a detailed phytochemical study of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> leaves extracted with 95% ethanol, followed by partitioning with diethyl ether, ethyl acetate, and n-butanol. From the diethyl ether fraction (26 g), obtained from 859 g of dried leaves, nine phenolic compounds were isolated using silica gel and Sephadex LH-20 chromatography.</p><p>Among them were two flavonol glycosides, kaempferol-3-O-rhamnoside (<bold>20</bold>) and quercetin-3-O-rhamnoside (<bold>21</bold>), along with one flavanone (liquiritigenin) (<bold>23</bold>), one coumarin (esculetin) (<bold>22</bold>), and one phenolic acid (caffeic acid) (<bold>24</bold>). Additionally, two cinnamic aldehyde derivatives, 4-hydroxycinnamaldehyde (<bold>25</bold>) and (E)-isoferulaldehyde (<bold>26</bold>), were identified. Despite their structural simplicity, these compounds contribute to the overall phenolic content due to their hydroxylated aromatic systems, which suggests that these compounds may play a relevant role in the antioxidant profile of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>. (<xref rid="molecules-30-03460-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>).</p><p>Structural elucidation was confirmed by <sup>1</sup>H and <sup>13</sup>C NMR and HRESI-MS, supported by a comparison of the available literature. Interestingly, two triterpenoids, 6,15α-epoxy-1β,4β-dihydroxyeudesmane and olean-12-ene-3β,15α-diol, were also isolated for the first time in the <italic toggle="yes">Polyscias</italic> genus. The aldehydic and acidic phenolics identified in this fraction were previously unreported in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>.</p><p>Altogether, these findings significantly expand the known phenolic profile of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, particularly regarding its lipophilic constituents.
