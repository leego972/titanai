Molecular Mechanisms and Comparative Neuroprotective Potential of <italic toggle="yes">Polyscias fruticosa</italic></title><p>The neuroprotective activity of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> has been demonstrated in both in vivo and in vitro models. In a murine model of Parkinson’s disease induced by MPTP, Ly et al. [<xref rid="B20-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>] reported that oral administration of ethanolic leaf extract significantly restored dopaminergic neuronal density in the substantia nigra and increased striatal dopamine levels. These effects were associated with activation of the AKT/CREB/BDNF signaling cascade, which supports neuronal survival and synaptic plasticity. Concurrently, the extract suppressed NF-κB activation and reduced the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α, as shown by Western blot and immunohistochemical analyses [<xref rid="B20-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>,<xref rid="B163-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">163</xref>].</p><p>Supporting in vitro evidence was provided by Selvaraj et al. [<xref rid="B21-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>], who treated murine HT22 hippocampal neurons with a 75% ethanol extract of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> roots (EEPF). At 50 µg·mL<sup>−1</sup>, EEPF markedly reduced glutamate-induced oxidative stress (assessed by DCF-DA fluorescence) and intracellular Ca<sup>2+</sup> influx (measured with Fluo-3-AM). The extract also inhibited apoptosis by preventing AIF nuclear translocation and restored AKT and CREB phosphorylation and BDNF expression. Western blot analysis revealed modulation of MAPK phosphorylation and normalization of the Bax/Bcl-2 ratio, indicating mitochondrial pathway engagement.</p><p>Taken together, available evidence suggests that the neuroprotective effects of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> are mediated by suppression of pro-inflammatory cytokines, ROS neutralization, mitochondrial stabilization, and activation of neurotrophic signaling pathways [<xref rid="B8-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>]. These multimodal mechanisms are relevant to the pathogenesis of disorders such as Parkinson’s and Alzheimer’s disease [<xref rid="B20-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>,<xref rid="B21-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>]. A meaningful comparison can be made with <italic toggle="yes">Panax ginseng</italic>, whose neuroprotective effects are mainly linked to ginsenosides like Rg3 acting via the PI3K/AKT pathway [<xref rid="B20-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>,<xref rid="B164-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">164</xref>]. In contrast, <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> contains phenolic acids with potent antioxidant capacity (e.g., dehydroferulic acid, protocatechuic acid) and uniquely protects against glutamate excitotoxicity [<xref rid="B21-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>].</p><p>In our view, the broader mechanism of action of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, combining anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and mitochondrial-stabilizing effects, may confer advantages over classical neuroprotective plants. Its distinct phytochemical profile supports further investigation as an alternative or complementary agent in neurodegenerative disease therapy.</p></sec><sec id="sec6dot3dot2-molecules-30-03460"><title>6.3.2. Safety and Therapeutic Relevance</title><p>Toxicological assessments confirm the favorable safety profile of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> extracts in preclinical models. Ly et al. [<xref rid="B20-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>] showed that repeated oral administration of ethanolic leaf extract at 100 mg·kg<sup>−1</sup> for 14 days in MPTP-treated mice did not induce histopathological alterations in major organs.
