2026</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><sec id="Sec1"><title>Introduction</title><p id="Par66">Ashwagandha, a well-regarded herb celebrated for its diverse health benefits, derives its name from the Sanskrit term “ashwa,” signifying equine characteristics. There exists a conjecture that the ingestion of ashwagandha root may engender attributes reminiscent of those observed in horses. The word “gandha,” which alludes to the distinctive scent of the plant’s fresh roots and is also known as Indian ginseng or winter cherry, has a second element that signifies “fragrance.” [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR1">1</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR2">2</xref>]. The group of plants known as “adaptogens” includes ashwagandha. An herb with phytonutrients is referred to as an “adaptogen” if it helps the body adapt by (a) restoring system functions to normal, (b) building resistance to future stress, and (c) raising the body’s functioning to a higher level of performance [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR3">3</xref>]. Among the various members of the herb family known as adaptogens are ashwagandha, rhodiola, ginseng, schisandra, and maca [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR3">3</xref>].</p><p id="Par67">

</p><p id="Par68">As an adaptogen, ashwagandha can control metabolic functions, promote homeostasis, and restore the organism. Ashwagandha roots are known for their role in immunomodulation [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR4">4</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR5">5</xref>] stress reduction [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR6">6</xref>], as an antioxidant, cardioprotective function [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR7">7</xref>] cardiorespiratory endurance [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR8">8</xref>], inflammation prevention, anti-tumor activity, neuroprotection, and anti-cancer activity [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">9</xref>] improves memory and cognition [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR10">10</xref>] augments stamina, strength, and muscle recovery [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR11">11</xref>] boosts sexual function in both men and women [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR13">13</xref>] aids in body weight management [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>] and acts as an antiaging agent [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR15">15</xref>] (Fig. <xref rid="Fig1" ref-type="fig">1</xref>).
