</p></sec><sec id="Sec27"><title>Threats and conservation status of medicinal plants in Sheikh district</title><p id="Par200">MPs in Sheikh District are increasingly exposed to both human-driven and environmental pressures, threatening their sustainability and the continuity of traditional healthcare practices. Based on rankings provided by eight key informants, charcoal production emerged as the most severe threat, followed by firewood collection and prolonged drought, while overgrazing and agricultural expansion were perceived as less critical (Table <xref rid="Tab12" ref-type="table">12</xref>). This pattern indicates that human activities linked to energy, construction, and subsistence needs are the primary drivers of MP depletion. Similar trends have been reported across East Africa, including Kenya, Tanzania, and Ethiopia, where overharvesting for fuelwood and construction is a leading cause of decline among culturally important species [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR63">63</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR95">95</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR108">108</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR147">147</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR199">199</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR200">200</xref>]. Comparable pressures have also been documented in South Asia, particularly in Pakistan and India, where unsustainable harvesting for timber, fuel, and medicinal use has led to local depletion of significant plant species [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR5">5</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR6">6</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR53">53</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR97">97</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR116">116</xref>].</p><p id="Par201">The lack of systematic in-situ conservation in Sheikh District further compounds the vulnerability of MPs. Although two <italic toggle="yes">ex-situ</italic> conservation sites exist in the mountainous regions of Gacan-Libaax and Weger, their scope is limited, leaving most species unprotected within their natural habitats. Only a few MPs are safeguarded in home gardens, and while local customary laws protect certain species, they are insufficient to counter large-scale extraction. Notably, some species experiencing intense local pressure, such as <italic toggle="yes">Vachellia bussei</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Balanites aegyptiaca</italic>, are classified as Least Concern (LC) on the IUCN Red List due to their wider distribution. This discrepancy highlights the importance of localized monitoring, as global assessments may fail to capture pressures within specific communities [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR183">183</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR184">184</xref>].</p><p id="Par202">Sheikh District lies within the Somali xeric eco-region, a unique biogeographical zone characterized by high endemism and species adapted to arid environments. Its flora shares biogeographical connections with the Arabian Peninsula, eastern Mediterranean, and more distant regions such as the Canary Islands and Madagascar. Local communities rely heavily on these resources for medicinal, construction, and fuel purposes and many species have experienced noticeable declines over recent decades due to overexploitation, limited natural regeneration, and unsustainable harvesting practices. Informants reported that species under the most significant pressure include <italic toggle="yes">Vachellia bussei</italic>,<italic toggle="yes"> Vachellia tortilis</italic>,<italic toggle="yes"> Vachellia nilotica</italic>,<italic toggle="yes"> Balanites aegyptiaca</italic>,<italic toggle="yes"> Eucalyptus camaldulensis</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Dodonaea angustifolia</italic> (Table <xref rid="Tab13" ref-type="table">13</xref>).
