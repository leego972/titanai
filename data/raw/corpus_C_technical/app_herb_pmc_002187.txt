Moreover, after being degraded into smaller molecules, the fractions of polysaccharides exerted inhibitory effects on the proliferation of human cervical carcinoma HeLa cells and induced apoptosis by upregulating the expression of ERK, Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK), and p38 [<xref rid="B12" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>].</p><p>The regulation of the Wnt signaling pathway is also closely associated with tumorigenesis. PLGC is a major phase in the progression of gastric cancer, which might be a potential target for the treatment of gastric cancer. The polysaccharides prepared from <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> were found to ameliorate the MNNG-PLGC in rats via the Wnt/<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>-catenin pathway, downregulating the expressions of Wnt2<italic toggle="yes">β</italic> and glycogen synthase kinase 3 beta (Gsk3<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>), proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA), and cyclinD1. In addition, the results of serum endogenous metabolites revealed that the change in betaine was the most significant, indicating that betaine may be a key contributor to the anticancer effects of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> polysaccharides [<xref rid="B55" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>].</p><p>Furthermore, mitochondria are considered important in apoptosis, and a high level of ROS generated from mitochondria could induce apoptosis in cancer cells, suggesting that targeting the mitochondrial function or ROS stimulation might be feasible in cancer cell treatment [<xref rid="B56" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>]. An in vitro study with colon cancer cell line CT26 cells showed that the polysaccharides isolated from <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> reduced the proliferation of cells and induced cytotoxic autophagy as well as mitochondrial dysfunction via the ROS-AMP-activated protein kinase- (AMPK-) autophagy pathway [<xref rid="B57" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>].</p><p>On the other hand, <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> could prevent the growth of tumors by improving the host function and responses. For instance, the polysaccharide of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> reduced colon tumorigenesis by preserving the intestinal barrier function and improving immune response to the tumor microenvironments in mice with colorectal cancer. The intestinal barrier function was restored by increasing the expression of zonula occludens-1 (ZO-1) and occludin, and the immune response was increased to exert anticancer effects via the tumor infiltrated CD8(+) cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) and the expression of programmed death-1 (PD-1) on CTLs [<xref rid="B58" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>].</p><p>The potential anticancer activity of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> could potentiate the efficacy of anticancer agents or chemotherapy. Its polysaccharides inhibited the growth of human colorectal cancer HT-29 cells and reduced the metastasis of tumors in the zebrafish model, and the treatment increased the anticancer efficacy of 5-fluorouracil, which induced apoptosis via the mitochondrial-dependent intrinsic apoptotic pathway. These results indicated that <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> is a potential candidate for colorectal cancer therapy alone or in the combination with chemotherapy medication [<xref rid="B59" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>].</p><p>Notably, the molecular weight and structure of compounds in <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> might influence its anticancer activities. A study compared the anticancer properties of carbohydrates in <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> with different molecular weights, including monosaccharides, oligosaccharides, and polysaccharides. It was reported that polysaccharides had better anticancer properties than monosaccharides and oligosaccharides, suggesting that the efficacy of carbohydrate drugs largely depends on the molecular weight of the cancer treatment [<xref rid="B54" ref-type="bibr">54</xref>]. Moreover, the modification and use of vehicles could promote bioavailability and increase the bioactive function of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic>. The gold nanoparticle synthesized from the extracts of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> showed better anticancer effects without increasing toxicity to the host [<xref rid="B60" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>]. In addition to the polysaccharides, there are also new derivates from <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> with significant anticancer activities. A study found that several new phenanthrene and 9,10-dihydrophenanthrene derivative compounds showed cytotoxicity against cancer cell lines, HI-60 and THP-1 cells, and one of the compounds had a most significant effect with IC50 values of 11.96 and 8.92 <italic toggle="yes">μ</italic>M, respectively [<xref rid="B61" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec4.2"><title>4.2.
