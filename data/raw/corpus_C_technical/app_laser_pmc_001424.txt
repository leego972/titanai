These algorithms exploit differences in OCT signal phase, intensity, or a combination of the two in order to generate the angiograms.</p><p>SSADA is the algorithm currently utilized in the OCTA device RTVue XR Avanti manufactured by Optovue, Inc. (Fremont, CA, USA), which detects the differences in OCT signal intensity to represent flow.<sup><xref rid="bibr12-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">12</xref></sup> As with other OCTA techniques, SSADA relies on the comparison of sequential OCT B-scans for the detection of blood flow. Static tissues are highly correlated, whereas moving erythrocytes cause changes in reflectance between consecutive scans and are therefore highly decorrelated. Uniquely, SSADA splits the spectrum of light into multiple spectral bands, calculates the decorrelation of each band separately, and then averages the results. This leads to an increased signal-to-noise ratio and the ability to produce angiograms using only two consecutive OCT B-scans.<sup><xref rid="bibr3-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="bibr13-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">13</xref></sup> As a result, SSADA has an axial resolution that is approximately three times lower than standard OCT (approximately 15 µm),<sup><xref rid="bibr11-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>,<xref rid="bibr14-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">14</xref></sup> but also has a decreased susceptibility to bulk motion.</p><p>OMAG is another algorithm that is implemented in the AngioPlex system by Carl Zeiss Meditec AG (Jena, Germany). This approach analyzes both the amplitude and phase differences in the OCT signal to generate the angiograms. One distinct advantage is the use of the entire spectrum without loss of axial resolution, but it has the disadvantage of requiring more than two sequential scans to generate the angiogram.<sup><xref rid="bibr15-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">15</xref><xref rid="bibr16-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr17-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">17</xref></sup></p><p>Briefly, OCTA ratio analysis (OCTARA) is a separate algorithm developed by Topcon Corp (Tokyo, Japan), which uses a ratio method that is not based on amplitude decorrelation. Notably, this approach also does not require splitting of the full spectrum, resulting in preservation of axial resolution.<sup><xref rid="bibr18-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>,<xref rid="bibr19-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">19</xref></sup> This algorithm is paired with swept-source OCT (SS-OCT) in the Topcon DRI-OCT Triton SS-OCT system.</p><sec id="section3-2515841419840249"><title>Artifact</title><p>An important limitation of OCTA is its unique susceptibility to imaging artifacts, which have been characterized by Spaide and colleagues.<sup><xref rid="bibr20-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">20</xref></sup> These artifacts include motion artifacts, a result of microsaccades and breathing, and projection artifacts, where flow in the superficial retinal vasculature is projected onto the underlying structures. Projection artifacts appear as long flow tails underneath the superficial vessels on cross-sectional OCTA images and can give the appearance of flow in areas where there should be none.<sup><xref rid="bibr21-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">21</xref></sup> These artifacts can often be magnified by hyperreflective material such as hard exudates or pigment migration.<sup><xref SMARTTrack™ (Topcon Corp.), that compensate for eye movement and blinking,<sup><xref rid="bibr19-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>,<xref rid="bibr23-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">23</xref></sup> a concept that was introduced over a decade ago.<sup><xref rid="bibr24-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">24</xref></sup> Optovue, Inc. instead utilizes a two-level approach with their DualTrac™ software, which incorporates eye tracking along with an orthogonal registration algorithm called motion correction technology (MCT).<sup><xref rid="bibr23-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">23</xref></sup> Their use of SSADA also mitigates effects of movement in the axial direction from microsaccades and pulsations during the cardiac cycle due to the lower axial resolution and subsequent higher signal-to-noise ratio.<sup><xref rid="bibr20-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">20</xref></sup></p><p>Projection artifact in commercial OCTA systems has typically been addressed using a slab-subtraction algorithm.<sup><xref rid="bibr19-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>,<xref rid="bibr25-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>,<xref rid="bibr26-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">26</xref></sup> This technique subtracts the superficial vascular pattern from the outer retina, thereby removing projection artifact but often causing an undesirable negative artifact.<sup><xref rid="bibr21-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">21</xref></sup> More recently, a new algorithm called projection-resolved OCTA (PR-OCTA) was introduced by Zhang and colleagues.<sup><xref rid="bibr27-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">27</xref></sup> This algorithm interprets high decorrelation-value peaks as real vessels while removing lower decorrelation-value signal as projection artifact and has successfully been used to study the retinal vasculature in healthy and diseased eyes.<sup><xref
