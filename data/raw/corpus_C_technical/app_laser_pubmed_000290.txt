This study explores the use of optical coherence tomography (OCT) to monitor and 
diagnose multiple sclerosis (MS). The analysis of reduced total macular volume 
and peripapillary retinal nerve fiber layer thinning are shown. The severity of 
these defects increases as MS progresses, reflecting the progressive 
degeneration of nerve fibers and retinal ganglion cells. The OCT parameters are 
noninvasive, sensitive indicators that can be used to assess the progression of 
neurodegeneration and inflammation in MS.
