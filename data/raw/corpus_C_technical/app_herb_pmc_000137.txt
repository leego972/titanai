<italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic>, an herbaceous perennial plant, was originally called Caulis <italic toggle="yes">Polygoni Multiflori</italic> in the book <italic toggle="yes">“He Shou Wu Zhuan”</italic> written during the Tang dynasty (<xref rid="bib80" ref-type="bibr">Li et al., 2003</xref>). Its root is tuberous, hypertrophic, oblong and dark brown in color. Its stems are approximately 2–4 m in length with a twine-like appearance, many branches, longitudinal ribs, glabrous and micro-rough skin and exhibit lignification in the lower parts. The leaves are ovate or broadly elliptic and 3–7×2–5 cm<sup>2</sup> in size with an acuminate apex and a cordate or subcordate base. Both sides of the leaf are coarse and have entire margins; the petioles are 1.5–3 cm in length; and the ochreas are membranous, oblique, glabrous and 3–5 mm in length. The inflorescences are paniculate, terminal or axillary and approximately 10–20 cm in length; the branches are expansive, with longitudinal ridges and small dense protrusions along the ridge. The bracts are triangular and ovate with small protrusions, and the apex is acute. In addition, each inflorescence contains three to four flowers. The pedicels are 2–3 mm in length and slender, and the perianths are white or greenish and five-parted. The perianth segments are oblong and of non-standard size. The male flowers have eight stamens, and the lower parts of the filaments are wide and very short, exhibit three styles, and have stigmas with a capitulum. The achenes are ovate with three ribs, black-brown, shiny, and approximately 2.5–3 mm in length (<xref rid="bib34" ref-type="bibr">Editorial Board of Flora of China, 1998</xref>).</p><p id="p0040">This plant is widely cultivated in many provinces of China, including Gansu, Shanxi, Sichuan, Yunnan, Guizhou, and Henan, and other countries, such as Japan. It grows in valley shrubs, hillside forests, gutter rock crevices and other locations with altitudes of 200–3000 m (<xref rid="bib34" ref-type="bibr">Editorial Board of Flora of China, 1998</xref>, <xref rid="bib222" ref-type="bibr">Zhou, 1993</xref>).</p><p id="p0045">As a widely used traditional Chinese medicine, there are some adulterants of this plant, involving nine species belonging to six families. The original plants are <italic toggle="yes">Pteroxygonum giraldii</italic> Damm et Diels, <italic toggle="yes">P. cillinerve</italic> (Nakai) Ohwi, <italic toggle="yes">P. subertii</italic> L. Henry, <italic toggle="yes">Cynanchum auriculatum</italic> Royle ex Wight, <italic toggle="yes">C. wilfordi</italic> (Maxim) Hemsl., <italic toggle="yes">Stephania cepharantha</italic> Hayata, <italic toggle="yes">Musa basjoo</italic> Sieb. et Zucc, <italic toggle="yes">Dioscorea bulbifera</italic> L. and <italic toggle="yes">Rodgersia aesculifolia</italic> Batal. (<xref rid="bib10" ref-type="bibr">Chen et al., 1999a</xref>, <xref rid="bib25" ref-type="bibr">Cheng and Zhou, 2005a</xref>, <xref rid="bib216" ref-type="bibr">Zhao et al., 1998</xref>, <xref rid="bib167" ref-type="bibr">Xia and Li, 2003</xref>). Until now, several methods have been developed to identify and distinguish them, including experiential identification, morphological identification, ultraviolet spectrophotometry, the TLC method, HPLC, gel electrophoresis, HPLC-ESI/MS and ITS2 rDNA sequencing (<xref rid="bib11" ref-type="bibr">Chen et al., 1998</xref>, <xref rid="bib66" ref-type="bibr">Li et al., 1995</xref>, <xref rid="bib41" ref-type="bibr">Ge et al., 2011</xref>; <xref rid="bib41" ref-type="bibr">Sun et al., 1996a</xref>; <xref rid="bib202" ref-type="bibr">Zhang and Shi, 2007a</xref>, <xref rid="bib218" ref-type="bibr">Zheng et al., 2009</xref>, <xref rid="bib219" ref-type="bibr">Zheng, 2010</xref>).</p><p id="p0050">The morphological identification method is regarded as the most popular method for identifying <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic>. The fracture plane of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> is yellowish-brown or reddish-brown and starchy and exhibits a brocaded pattern (abnormal vascular bundle).
