Among the error structures that converged, the one with the smallest Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) was chosen. All terms up to the level of a 2-way interaction were considered. To assess the ANOVA assumptions, comprehensive residual analyses were performed. The assumption of normality was formally tested by use of Shapiro-Wilk, Kolmogorov-Smirnov, Cramér-von Mises, and Anderson-Darling tests. Residuals were plotted against the predicted values and explanatory variables were used in the models to show outliers, bimodal distributions, the need for data transformations, or other issues that should be addressed.</p></sec><sec id="Sec10"><title>Results</title><sec id="Sec11"><title>Scratch migration assay</title><p>A total of fifteen experiments were included in the study. To effectively meet the ANOVA assumptions of the statistical analysis, a square-root transformation was applied to all data as well as accommodating unequal variance in time (the error structure chosen was arh [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR1">1</xref>]). The results obtained at the 48- and 60-h time points were removed from the analysis as most of the groups tested were closed, except for the vast majority of cells irradiated with a higher dose of 10 J/cm<sup>2</sup>. No apparent outliers were identified in the analysis. There was no significant inter-observer variability observed for each surface area measured; all of the effects involving the observers had a <italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &gt; 0.05.</p><p>The linear mean length of each wounded region of the cell monolayer was compared with control groups and with each other over time. There was no statistical difference (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &gt; 0.05) in the linear mean length of each scratch created in the cell monolayer at the beginning of the study period (Fig. <xref rid="Fig5" ref-type="fig">5</xref>). Twelve hours following LLLT (Fig. <xref rid="Fig6" ref-type="fig">6</xref>), there was a significant reduction in the linear mean length of the control positive (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.0001) and the cells irradiated with 0.1 J/cm<sup>2</sup> (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> = 0.0001), 0.2 J/cm<sup>2</sup> (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> = 0.0003) and 1.2 J/cm<sup>2</sup> (p = 0.026) compared to the cells irradiated with 10 J/cm<sup>2</sup>. A significant diminution of the area was also noted between the control positive (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.0001) and the cells irradiated with the two lower energy doses (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.01) compared to the control negative, and between the control positive and the cells irradiated with 1.2 J/cm<sup>2</sup> (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> = 0.0013). Twenty-four hours (Fig. <xref rid="Fig7" ref-type="fig">7</xref>) and 36 h (Fig. <xref rid="Fig8" ref-type="fig">8</xref>) following LLLT, there was no significant difference observed in the linear mean length of the control positive wounded region compared to the groups irradiated with 0.1, 0.2, and 1.2 J/cm<sup>2</sup> (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &gt; 0.15).
