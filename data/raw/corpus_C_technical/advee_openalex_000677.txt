Title: Evaluation and Design of High Frequency Transformers for On Board Charging Applications

Abstract:
The demand and production of hybrid and full electric vehicles is steadily increasing.\nThis accelerates the development of infrastructure and related systems, such\nas chargers. When connecting the charging port directly to the grid, chargers integrated\ninto the vehicles converts the AC mains to correct battery voltage and regulates\nthe power. In order to achieve the required galvanic isolation of the battery\nfrom the grid, high frequency transformers are normally used. However, designing a\npower transformer is not trivial since its properties are highly complex to determine.\nThis thesis includes an investigation of the main sources of transformer winding and\ncore losses at high frequency operation. The origin of the losses are presented in a\ntheoretical perspective and quantified for a real case by simulations and measurements.\nAdditionally, two identical 5.5 kW transformers for use in a 11 kW isolated\nDC-DC stage are designed using a loss calculation model and an optimization algorithm,\nboth developed in this work. The transformers reach an efficiency of above\n99.63% under normal operating conditions and the losses are limited to 20.8W in\neach transformer throughout the full operating range. Moreover, the power density\nfor the designed transformer is 46 kWdm−3 and the total packaging volume is\n0.119dm3.\nIt is further studied how the loss characteristics depend on a multiple of parameters\nand operating points. Quantification of the losses clearly motivates that the\nharmonic content of the non-sinusoidal waveforms is non-neglectable. The reason is\nthat, in many cases, the majority of the winding losses are caused by harmonics. It\nis also shown how a well constructed winding alignment and litz wire configuration\nsignificantly can reduce losses.
