Statistical Analysis</title><p>For each evaluated parameter mean values ± standard deviations (SDs) were calculated. The data obtained from the semiquantitative evaluation were compared using the nonparametric Kruskal-Wallis test. Significance was accepted at <italic toggle="yes">P</italic> &lt; 0.05.</p><p>For the comparison of the PMNL/TM ratios, an analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey-Kramer's multiple comparison test was used.</p></sec></sec></sec><sec id="sec3"><title>3. Results</title><p>During the post-surgery period, the animals remained healthy, with no clinical evidence of infection. The results of our investigation are summarised in Figures <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig1">1</xref>–<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig5">5</xref>.</p><sec id="sec3.1"><title>3.1. Nondiabetic Group</title><sec id="sec3.1.1"><title>3.1.1. Two Days after Surgery</title><p>PMNL infiltration was found to be significantly lower in the irradiated group with 15 mW/cm<sup>2</sup> of power density (<italic toggle="yes">P</italic> &lt; 0.05) when compared to the control group. The ratio of PMNL/macrophages was significantly higher in the control group and in wounds irradiated by 1 mW/cm<sup>2</sup> compared to wounds irradiated by 15 mW/cm<sup>2</sup>  (<italic toggle="yes">P</italic> &lt; 0.05) (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig1">Figure 1(a)</xref>). The layer of epithelium was observed to be thickened at the edge of the wound after 48 hours in the control group and irradiated groups (1 mW/cm<sup>2</sup> and 5 mW/cm<sup>2</sup>), while the start of keratinocyte migration to the middle part of the wound in the 15 mW/cm<sup>2</sup>group was observed. A significant difference (<italic toggle="yes">P</italic> &lt; 0.05) in the amount of newly formed collagen in the irradiated group (5 mW/cm<sup>2</sup> and 15 mW/cm<sup>2</sup>) was recorded compared to 1 mW/cm<sup>2</sup> irradiation and control wounds (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig1">Figure 1(a)</xref>).</p></sec><sec id="sec3.1.2"><title>3.1.2. Six Days after Surgery</title><p>Acute inflammation phase of wound healing was completely finished; there was only accidental infiltration of a few polymorphonuclear leukocytes in all irradiated and nonirradiated wounds without statistical relevance after six days of treatment. Histologic examination after six days was revealed on the surface of excisions the formation of an almost continual layer in the 15 mW/cm<sup>2</sup> group with a significant difference compared to the control and 1 mW/cm<sup>2</sup> groups. Proliferation, migration of fibroblast, and neoangiogenesis was slightly accelerated in the 1 mW/cm<sup>2</sup> group, but without significant differences compared to the 1 mW/cm<sup>2</sup>, 5 mW/cm<sup>2</sup>, and controls. The synthesis and organisation of collagen fibres were significantly enhanced in the 5 mW/cm<sup>2</sup> and 15 mW/cm<sup>2</sup> groups compared to the others. (Figures <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig1">1(b)</xref> and <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig3">3</xref>).</p></sec><sec id="sec3.1.3"><title>3.1.3. Fourteen Days after Surgery</title><p>The changes in the wound healing of the skin were not any more remarkable in the later phase of maturation; even the effect of LLLT was without a statistical significance (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig1">Figure 1(c)</xref>). Healing remodeling and reorganisation of extracellular matrix (ECM) were characterised.</p></sec></sec><sec id="sec3.2"><title>3.2. Diabetic Group</title><sec id="sec3.2.1"><title>3.2.1. Two Days after Surgery</title><p>Formation of the demarcation line beneath the scab consisting mainly of polymorphonuclear leukocytes was seen mostly in wounds which received a laser beam with a power density of 15 mW/cm<sup>2</sup>, but there was no significant difference between each group. Epithelization, fibroplasia, and neoangiogenesis were only slightly enhanced in all stimulated wounds, without significant differences. The only significant difference was recorded in the ratio PMNL/Ma in wounds stimulated by 5 mW/cm<sup>2</sup> compared to the others (<italic toggle="yes">P</italic> &lt; 0.01) after two days of irradiation (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig2">Figure 2(a)</xref>).</p></sec><sec id="sec3.2.2"><title>3.2.2. Six Days after Surgery</title><p>The wound healing process in diabetic rats was delayed.
