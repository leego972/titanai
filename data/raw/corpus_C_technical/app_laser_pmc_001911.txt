The wound healing rate was calculated by measuring the area of the “wound” that was not covered with cells after irradiation with the Image J software.</p><p id="Par17">As shown in Fig. <xref rid="Fig2" ref-type="fig">2</xref>, 72 h after irradiation, most of the groups presented a greater healing rate than the control one. Multiple comparisons with Bonferroni correction revealed differences between the control group and the most of irradiated ones. More specific, in the groups irradiated with 10 and 15 mW/cm<sup>2</sup>, the observed increase in wound healing rate was statistically significant for all the irradiation times tested (<italic toggle="yes">P</italic> &lt; 0.02 and <italic toggle="yes">P</italic> &lt; 0.04 respectively), while for 3 and 5 mW/cm<sup>2</sup>, only 5 and 8 min revealed a statistically significant wound healing rate (<italic toggle="yes">P</italic> &lt; 0.01 for both cases).</p><p id="Par18">Furthermore, groups were clustered in two categories: the groups that are statistically different from the controls and the groups that are not.
