GLYCERATE Glyc"er*ate, n. (Chem.) Defn: A salt of glyceric acid. GLYCERIC Gly*cer"ic, a. (Chem.) Defn: Pertaining to, or derived from, glycerin. Glyceric acid (Chem.), an organic acid, obtained by the partial oxidation of glycerin, as a thick liquid. It is a hydroxyl derivative of propionic acid, and has both acid and alcoholic properties. GLYCERIDE Glyc"er*ide, n. Etym: [See Glycerin.] (Chem.) Defn: A compound ether (formed from glycerin). Some glycerides exist ready formed as natural fats, others are produced artificially. GLYCERIN; GLYCERINE Glyc"er*in, Glyc"er*ine (, n. Etym: [F. glycérine, fr. Gr. glykero`s, glyky`s, sweet. Cf. Glucose, Licorice.] (Chem.) Defn: An oily, viscous liquid, C3H5(OH)3, colorless and odorless, and with a hot, sweetish taste, existing in the natural fats and oils as the base, combined with various acids, as oleic, margaric, stearic, and palmitic. It is a triatomic alcohol, and hence is also called glycerol. See Note under Gelatin. Note: It is obtained from fats by saponification, or, on a large scale, by the action of superheated steam. It is used as an ointment, as a solvent and vehicle for medicines, and as an adulterant in wine, beer, etc. GLYCERITE Glyc"er*ite, n. (Med.)
