databases. The literature search was restricted to studies published in English and
Portuguese in the period of 1992-2012. The keywords used were "low level laser
therapy", "laser", "light emitting diode", "LED", "phototherapy", "wound healing",
"fibroblast", "collagen" and "angyogenesis" combined with each other.</p></sec><sec sec-type="results"><title>RESULTS</title><p>Sixty-eight studies were analyzed, including 48 on LASER light, 14 related to LED
light and 6 for both types of light (<xref ref-type="table" rid="t01">Tables
1</xref> to <xref ref-type="table" rid="t03">3</xref>). According to data
presented on <xref ref-type="table" rid="t01">table 1</xref>, 16 of the 48 studies
on the effects of LASER light were <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> and 32 were performed
in animals.<sup><xref rid="r09" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>-<xref rid="r56" ref-type="bibr">56</xref></sup> The use of different wavelengths (532-1064 nm) was
verified, with the most utilized spectral range being between 632.8 and 830 nm. Doses ranging from 0.09 to 90 J/cm<sup>2</sup> were used, predominating the values
from 1 to 5 J/cm<sup>2</sup> . One study did not cite the dose value used.<sup><xref rid="r48" ref-type="bibr">48</xref></sup> The biological effects promoted
were reduction of inflammatory cells, increased proliferation of fibroblasts,
stimulation of collagen synthesis, angiogenesis inducement and granulation tissue
formation. It was noted in a study that the dose of 4 J/cm2 was more effective than
8 J/cm<sup>2</sup> .<sup><xref rid="r14" ref-type="bibr">14</xref></sup>
Furthermore, doses of 10 and 16 J/cm<sup>2</sup> promoted inhibitory
