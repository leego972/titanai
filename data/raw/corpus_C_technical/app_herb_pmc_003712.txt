They increase physical endurance, cardiovascular protection, and radioprotective activity [<xref rid="B16-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>].</p><p>There are a large number of plant-based adaptogens, including <italic toggle="yes">Eleutherococcus senticosus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Panax ginseng</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Withania somnifera</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Schisandra chinensis</italic> as well as <italic toggle="yes">Valeriana officinalis</italic> (see <xref rid="molecules-29-04767-f004" ref-type="fig">Figure 4</xref>). The main active compounds which determine the therapeutic effect and can be isolated from plants are ginsenosides, torvanol, salidroside, rutin, a variety of polysaccharides, flavonoids, alkaloids, saponin, phytosteroids, and phytosterols [<xref rid="B53-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>,<xref rid="B54-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">54</xref>].</p><p>Ginsenosides are the main active compound in Ginseng; moreover, there are also saponins, peptides, alkaloids, and lignans in its structure. Ginseng shows great anticancer, anti-inflammatory, and anti-fatigue activities. It helps to improve and promote mental health and improve the memory. Bai et al. co-fermented <italic toggle="yes">Panax ginseng</italic> with multi-enzyme-coupling probiotics and prepared an extract with probiotics and high levels of polysaccharides and ginsenosides. The obtained product was tested on mice and it was observed that their intestinal flora was stabilized, and their immune system was improved significantly [<xref rid="B55-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>]. In another study, An and co-workers combined Korean red ginseng with probiotics to treat diabetic wounds, which were exposed to diesel exhaust particles. It was confirmed that the prepared mixture treated wounds, especially diabetic ones, with high efficiency by controlling the enzymes and accelerating the healing time [<xref rid="B56-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>]. Lee et al. used Korean red ginseng as a reducing agent for the synthesis of gold bimetallic nanoparticles. The prepared particles showed great anticancer ability during chemo–PTT combination therapy and can be used as a drug delivery system for other substances [<xref rid="B57-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>]. Interestingly, the Trametes versicolor endophytic fungus was isolated from wild ginseng. The obtained substance was employed to produce saponins using in vitro fermentation and the total amount of substance was around 2.365 mg/mL, which was more than three times higher than with standard procedure [<xref rid="B58-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>]. Ginseng is a valuable plant, but it might be harder to consume because of its bitter taste. Han and co-workers loaded chitosan and gelatin particles with red ginseng extract. It was confirmed that capturing adaptogenic extract into biopolymeric particles reduced the bitter taste of the active substance and improved its acidic and thermal stability; therefore, it can be used as a food ingredient [<xref rid="B59-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>]. It was also shown that the rhythm of body temperature and reduction of inflammation can be improved with the supplementation of ginseng extract. Moreover, the active substance regulates the genes, which are related to inflammation, gut microbiota, and the circadian clock [<xref rid="B60-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>].</p><p>Another widely used plant is <italic toggle="yes">Acanthopanax senticosus</italic> (Siberian ginseng), which has the ability to prevent diseases. It consists mostly of saponins and polyphenols which enable the anti-inflammatory and antibacterial properties of the plant. The polysaccharide was extracted from <italic toggle="yes">A. senticosus</italic> and its properties of immune support and antitumor activities were examined with the use of tumor cell lines, such as Crocker sarcoma S180, hepatic carcinoma H22, and uterine cervical carcinoma U14, which were implanted in mice. It was observed that the polysaccharide showed an inhibitory effect on tumor cell lines [<xref rid="B61-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>]. Innovative hydrogels were prepared with the addition of <italic toggle="yes">A. senticosus</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Osmundastrum cinnamomeum</italic>. Prepared material enhanced the homeostasis and healing of wounds and supported coagulation, which might be linked to the targeting of relevant factors by active compounds during the healing process [<xref rid="B62-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>]. Therefore, eleutheroside B, the most active compound in <italic toggle="yes">A. senticosus</italic>, exhibits cardioprotective effects and has the ability to suppress atrial fibrillation [<xref rid="B63-molecules-29-04767" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>].
