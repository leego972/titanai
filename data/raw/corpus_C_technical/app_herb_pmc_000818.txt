The effectiveness of berberine was enhanced when it was used in combination with other agents. Coptisine, another alkaloid of <italic toggle="yes">Coptidis rhizoma</italic> is proved to have anticancer effects when used in concentrations of 150 mg/kg against BALB/c nude mice by suppressing tumor growth and reducing cancer metastasis. The inhibition of the RAS-ERK pathway was suggested as the mechanism for this activity [<xref rid="B160-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">160</xref>]. Another study was also performed on the nude mice on the HepG2 cells by applying the aqueous extract of <italic toggle="yes">H. diffusa</italic> which inhibits proliferation of cells in a dose-dependent manner, also delay S phase and arrest cells in G<sub>0</sub>/G<sub>1</sub> phase [<xref rid="B161-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">161</xref>].</p><p>Similarly, a high anticancer activity of SBT-A was found in transplanted tumor nude mice. Yang et al. [<xref rid="B162-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">162</xref>] reported the anticancer activity of the polysaccharides isolated from <italic toggle="yes">S. barbata</italic> by 95-D Xenograft model. The results showed that polysaccharides give strong anti-proliferative activities against a 95-D cell line. It also lowered the expression of phospho-c-Met and other signaling elements like phospho-Erk and phospho-Akt. In vivo study also gave maximum antitumor activity by using a 95-D subcutaneous xenograft model. After one daily intraperitoneal injection for 3 weeks, the tumor growth was significantly decreased (47.72 % and 13.6%) at 100 and 200 mg/kg treatments. The ex vivo studies also showed that polysaccharides of <italic toggle="yes">S. barbata</italic> inhibit the phosphorylation of c-Met signaling pathway.</p><p>Furthermore, Li et al. [<xref rid="B163-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">163</xref>] isolated a steroidal saponin from <italic toggle="yes">P. polyphylla</italic> which inhibited tumor growth in Lewis bearing-C57BL/6 mice and induced apoptosis in A549 cells. Results showed that steroidal saponin in concentration of 2.5, 5.0, and 7.5 mg/kg showed significant inhibition rate of 26.49 ± 17.30%, 40.32 ± 18.91%, and 54.94 ± 16.48%, remarkably increased thymus and sleep indices, decreased inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-8, and IL-10). This in turn inhibited the tumor growth in C57BL/6 mice by reduced volume and weight of tumor. Nuclear changes, DNA condensation, chromatin fragmentation, and apoptosis are induced in A549 cells with a concentration of 0.25, 0.50, and 0.75 mg/mL steroidal saponin. Tumor growth inhibited by steroidal saponin was associated with decreased ROS, inflammatory response, and induction of apoptosis.</p><p>Furthermore, Wanga et al. [<xref rid="B164-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">164</xref>] reported the effect of isoegomaketone from <italic toggle="yes">P. frutescens</italic> on Huh-7 hepatoma cell carcinoma and tumor-xenograft nude mice. Results showed that isoegomaketone inhibited cells and decreased tumor weight and volume. Isoegomaketone in the concentration of 10 nM/L decreased pAkt without affecting Akt. Hepatoma cell carcinoma tumor growth was suppressed by isoegomaketone from <italic toggle="yes">P. frutescens</italic> through PI3K/Akt signaling pathway blocking. <italic toggle="yes">R. coptidis</italic> is also showed anticancer activity in rats as suggested by the inhibition of cyclooxygenase 2 activity. The number of aberrant crypt foci in the rat colon was decreased by 54% after the administration of <italic toggle="yes">R. coptidis</italic> extracts [<xref rid="B165-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">165</xref>].</p><p>Manjamalai and Grace [<xref rid="B166-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">166</xref>] reported the apoptosis along with lowering angiogenesis and lung metastasis activities of the essential oils of <italic toggle="yes">W. chinensis</italic> by using B16F-10 melanoma cell line in C57BL/6 mice. The mice were injected with B16F-10 melanoma cells through the tail vein and treated with different doses of essential oil. A 50-µg essential oil concentration showed maximum cytotoxic activities with 65.17% lethality within 24 h. The numbers of apoptotic cells increased many times in experimental samples as compared to the control group. They also recorded high levels of important proteins like p53 and caspase-3 in essential oil-treated samples compared to other non-treated samples. They recommended this plant for the treatment and control of cancer.</p><p>In vivo activities of oridonin from <italic toggle="yes">R. rubescens</italic> showed a potent anticancer potential in the gallbladder [<xref rid="B113-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>]. When injected intra-peritoneally with a concentration of 5, 10, 15 mg/kg for 3 weeks to athymic nude mice, oridonin significantly inhibited NOZ xenografts growth.
