Based on current evidence, the herbal extract of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> may have clinical effect on erectile function, but needs further clinical evidence of efficacy trials to make any firm recommendation [<xref rid="B131-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">131</xref>].</p><p>In a pilot study, Henkel <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic> investigated the ergogenic effect of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> in elderly people and found that it is a potential herbal supplement for physically active aged male and female (age 57–72 years). Antimalarial Effect</title><p>The WHO estimates that in 2013, there were 207 million annual cases of malaria, resulting in 627,000 deaths, from <italic toggle="yes">Plasmodium falciparum</italic> [<xref rid="B133-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">133</xref>,<xref rid="B134-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">134</xref>]. There are about 10,000 malaria cases per year in Western Europe, and 1300–1500 in the United States [<xref rid="B135-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">135</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> extract is traditionally used for malarial fevers and has good anti-malarial effect against <italic toggle="yes">P. falciparum.</italic></p><p>Chan <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, tested the extracts of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> for antiplasmodial activity against a multi-drug resistant Thailand’s strain (K-1) of <italic toggle="yes">P. falciparum</italic> under <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> conditions. They isolated 10-hydroxycanthin-6-one, eurycomalactone, eurycomanone and eurycomanol from the plant, which showed antimalarial activities [<xref rid="B60-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>].</p><p>According to Kardono <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, two compounds, eurycomanone and 7-methoxy-β-carboline-1-propionic acid showed significant antimalarial activity against <italic toggle="yes">P. falciparum</italic> strains [<xref rid="B61-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>]. Low <italic toggle="yes">et al.</italic>, concluded that the administration of the bioactive standardized extract Fr2 (200 mg/kg) showed a good antimalarial effect. 13α(21)-epoxyeurycomanone and eurycomanone may be the only quassinoids contributing to the overall antimalarial activity of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> [<xref rid="B62-molecules-21-00331" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>].</p><p>In study, conducted during 2008 in Mae Sot, Tailand, a standardized extract of <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> containing three major quassinoids, eurycomanone (<bold>1</bold>), 13,21-dihydroeurycomanone (<bold>2</bold>) and 13α(21)-epoxyeurycomanone (<bold>3</bold>) was evaluated for antiplasmodial activity against <italic toggle="yes">Plasmodium falciparum</italic>. Activity was compared with that of artemisinin, using thirty-eight fresh parasite isolates and assessment of inhibition of schizont maturation. The IC<sub>50</sub>, IC<sub>90</sub> and IC<sub>99</sub> values for artemisinin were 4.30, 45.48 and 310.97 μg/L, and those for the root extract from <italic toggle="yes">E. longifolia</italic> 14.72, 139.65 and 874.15 μg/L respectively.
