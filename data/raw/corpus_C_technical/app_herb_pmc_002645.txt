The anticancer properties of EA-AS have been linked to several distinct mechanisms of action. Previous studies have demonstrated that Anabasis extracts, which are rich in BAC like alkaloids, flavonoids, and terpenoids, can induce apoptosis (programmed cell death) in a variety of cancer cell lines [<xref rid="pone.0310298.ref031" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>,<xref rid="pone.0310298.ref033" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>]. These EA-AS have been observed to activate caspase enzymes, key regulators of the apoptotic pathway, leading to the fragmentation of DNA and ultimately the death of cancer cells [<xref rid="pone.0310298.ref036" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>]. Additionally, plant extracts have demonstrated the ability to inhibit cell proliferation by disrupting the cell cycle and arresting cancer cells in specific phases, thereby preventing their uncontrolled division [<xref rid="pone.0310298.ref037" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>]. Furthermore, the extracts have been found to modulate cellular signaling pathways involved in cancer progression, such as the PI3K/Akt and MAPK pathways, effectively suppressing tumor growth and metastasis in in vivo animal models [<xref rid="pone.0310298.ref034" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>,<xref rid="pone.0310298.ref038" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>]. These multifaceted anticancer mechanisms highlight the therapeutic potential of Anabasis plant-derived compounds and warrant further investigation for the development of novel cancer treatments.</p></sec><sec id="sec017"><title>Antioxidant activity</title><p>The assessment of antioxidant activity is a critical component in the comprehensive evaluation of plant extracts and their potential therapeutic applications [<xref rid="pone.0310298.ref039" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>]. Many plant-derived compounds, such as polyphenols, carotenoids, and terpenoids, possess potent free radical-scavenging and reactive oxygen species (ROS)-neutralizing capabilities, which can have significant implications for their use in the prevention and management of various diseases [<xref rid="pone.0310298.ref040" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>,<xref rid="pone.0310298.ref041" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>]. In this study, EA-AS was assessed for antioxidant activity as shown in <xref rid="pone.0310298.t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref> and S2 Fig in <xref rid="pone.0310298.s001" ref-type="supplementary-material">S1 File</xref>. Results revealed that EA extract of AS showed antioxidant activity where EC<sub>50</sub> (concentration required to obtain a 50% antioxidant effect) was 30.6 μg/mL. In compared to positive control (ascorbic acid), EA-AS had antioxidant activity less than AA were EC<sub>50</sub> of AA was 5.1 μg/mL. Senhaji et al. [<xref rid="pone.0310298.ref042" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>] reported that, <italic toggle="yes">Anabasis aretioïdes</italic> extract has antioxidant activity with EC<sub>50</sub> = 52.91 μg/ml. Also, <italic toggle="yes">Anabasis articulata</italic> (Forssk.) Moq crude extract exhibited antioxidant activity with EC<sub>50</sub> with 90 μg/mL[<xref rid="pone.0310298.ref043" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>]. Abdulsahib et al. [<xref rid="pone.0310298.ref044" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>] evaluated <italic toggle="yes">Anabasis articulata</italic> Stem Extract for antioxidant activity, where results showed potent activity with EC<sub>50</sub> 94.7 μg/mL. Furthermore, leaves of <italic toggle="yes">Anabasis Articulata</italic> from Algerian habitat showed antioxidant activity using DPPH with EC<sub>50</sub> ranged from 3.200 ± 0.088 to 4.900 ± 0.130 μg/mL [<xref rid="pone.0310298.ref045" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>]. The antioxidant activity of the extract is associated with the presence of compounds with high antioxidant activity, as proven by GC mass analysis.
