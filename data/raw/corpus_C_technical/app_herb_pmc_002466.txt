[<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref011" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>] also came up with same observation although their reported TFC was lower than in the present study.</p><p>It is well established that methanol (an organic solvent) can extract most active components such as polyphenol or flavonoid from medicinal plant parts than water despite its lower polarity than water [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref027" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>]. As stated earlier, slight variations were observed in qualitative phytochemical screening results and in the quantitative phytochemical compositions (TTC, TPC and TFC) of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds. These variations can be attributed to the differences associated with the climatic conditions of the country of origin in which the sample seeds were grown, stage of maturity and due to the analytical techniques and the types of extractants employed [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref001" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0272457.ref014" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec027"><title>Thin Layer Chromatographic (TLC) profiling of methanol extract of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds</title><p>TLC studies were performed on methanol extract using two mobile phases which are known to have a good resolution and where more spots were detected [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref003" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>]. Mobile phase I contained petroleum spirit (PS): ethyl acetate (EA): methanol (ME) in the ratio 2:3:5 and Mobile phase II consisted of Petroleum spirit: ethyl acetate: methanol (6:2:2). The methanol extract was preferred for TLC profiling since it is considered the best solvent for extracting secondary metabolites or phytochemicals than other extractants (petroleum spirit, ethyl acetate and water) according to Sivanandham and Institutions [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref027" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>] and also as consistently evidenced by both qualitative and quantitative analysis done in the present study.</p><p>The TLC profiling of the methanol extract of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds yielded positive results directing toward the presence of several phytochemicals (<xref rid="pone.0272457.s001" ref-type="supplementary-material">S1 Fig</xref> and <xref rid="pone.0272457.s002" ref-type="supplementary-material">S1 Table</xref>). Rf values of 0.03, 0.28, 0.81, 0.91 and 0.14, 0.53, 0.93 obtained with mobile phase I and II, respectively are in agreement with those presented by Mengesha Yessuf [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref003" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>]. The variations in Rf values of phytochemicals gives crucial ideas in understanding their polarity. They are also known to aid when selecting an appropriate solvent for the separation of these phytochemicals (by using the chromatography and appropriate spectroscopic techniques) from medicinal plant extracts [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref003" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0272457.ref036" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0272457.ref037" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>]. In this study, more spots were observed in the more polar mobile phase I (<xref rid="pone.0272457.s001" ref-type="supplementary-material">S1 Fig</xref> and <xref rid="pone.0272457.s002" ref-type="supplementary-material">S1 Table</xref>) which implies that the separation of phytochemicals from the methanol extract of NSS can be best achieved by using solvents of increasing polarity.</p></sec><sec id="sec028"><title>FT-IR analysis of NSSO</title><p>The FT-IR spectrum of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds oil (NSSO) extracted using absolute ethanol was scanned at the mid infrared region of 400 to 4000 cm<sup>-1</sup> and a resolution of 4 cm<sup>-1</sup> was used in order to enhance the sharpness of the peaks (<xref rid="pone.0272457.g003" ref-type="fig">Fig 3</xref>). A broad absorption peak at 3350.20 cm<sup>-1</sup> can be attributed to an O-H functional group which is associated with alcohols and phenols while the weak absorption at 3006.4 cm<sup>-1</sup> was related to cis = C-H stretching of the vinyl group. In addition, two consecutive intense peaks at 2923.31 cm<sup>-1</sup> and 2853.82 cm<sup>-1</sup> were assigned to C-H stretching of an aliphatic group, and it implies the presence of methyl and isopropyl components. Another strong C = O stretching band is present at 1743.04 cm<sup>-1</sup> which was assigned to oxygen-containing groups such as esters in general.
