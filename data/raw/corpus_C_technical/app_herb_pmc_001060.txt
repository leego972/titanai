Spasmolytic and Antidiarrheal Activities</title><p>Diseases related to the gastrointestinal system, specifically diarrhea and constipation, affect 70% of the population worldwide, with particular emphasis in developing countries [<xref rid="B174-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">174</xref>]. Usually, medicinal plants are preferred to treat these disorders over synthetic formulations due to their multiple constituents, which can enhance action and/or neutralize side effects [<xref rid="B175-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">175</xref>]. Three halophytic grasses were already studied regarding their spasmolytic and antidiarrheal activities.</p><p><italic toggle="yes">C. ciliaris</italic> has been traditionally used to treat gastrointestinal disorders [<xref rid="B176-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">176</xref>] and this practice has been confirmed by pharmacological studies proving their spasmolytic and antidiarrheal activities. Its ethanol extract showed dose-dependent protective effects against diarrhea and gastrointestinal motility (100 and 200 mg/kg) [<xref rid="B45-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>], which may be due to blockage of Ca<sup>2+</sup> channels. In addition, this plant extract showed antiemetic activity (75, 100 and 150 mg/kg), which might be related to the presence of flavonoids, tannins, and alkaloids [<xref rid="B45-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic>’s ethanol extract displayed similar results; however, the active principles are still unknown [<xref rid="B177-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">177</xref>]. Moreover, its hydroalcoholic extract, at doses of 200 mg/kg and 400 mg/kg, showed laxative and diuretic effects. The acute toxicity (LD<sub>50</sub>, median lethal dose) of <italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic>’s ethanol and hydroalcoholic extracts was assessed as 2000 mg/kg [<xref rid="B177-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">177</xref>]. Several flavonoids known for their anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activities have been described in these species; nonetheless, the mechanisms of action, as well as the active principles, are still unknown [<xref rid="B44-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>].</p><p>Thespasmolytic effect (0.3–3.0 mg/mL) of the <italic toggle="yes">D. aegyptium</italic>’s ethanol extract was also hypothesized to be due to Ca<sup>2+</sup> blocking components which ultimately causes relaxation of gastrointestinal smooth muscle, combating diarrhea [<xref rid="B178-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">178</xref>]. Similarly to <italic toggle="yes">C. ciliaris</italic>, this species is also used traditionally to cope with these gastrointestinal disorders [<xref rid="B178-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">178</xref>]. Once again, its chemical composition is still unknown.</p></sec><sec id="sec3dot3-ijms-20-01067"><title>3.3.
