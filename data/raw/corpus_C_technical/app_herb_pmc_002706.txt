L.).</p></sec><sec><title>Interviews</title><p>Information on the number of species and number of uses and common names were obtained from 110 residents interviewed from March-July 2012 (50 interviews) and July-December 2012 (60 interviews). Ethnobotanical information was collected through semi-structured interviews [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B24">24</xref>], and according to the efficiency decreasing law [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B12">12</xref>]. Several questions were asked, a) what is it used for (medicinal, food, forage, wood, charcoal, construction, ceremonial, etc.), b) how is the plant used (depending of its use, raw, boiled, macerated, toasted), c) what parts of the plant are used. Moreover, the printed catalog was shown to the interviewed residents in order to inquire the use of each species. Additionally, we made several field trips with some men and women living in the area for <italic toggle="yes">in situ</italic> identification of some uncommon plants when we had questions regarding their identification. The age of the residents being interviewed ranged from 20–92 years old, 59% of them are 56 years old or older, and 41% of them are 55 years old or younger. According our experience in previous works in this State [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B11">11</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B12">12</xref>]; we skewed our interviews number, and we interviewed mainly people older than 32 years old or older instead of younger people, since we have seen that older people know more about plants and their uses. All interviews were carried out in the scrublands area, since all people live there and none of the respondents live in the temperate woods. All the people that were interviewed were born in this area or had been residents of this area for at least 32 years. All the interviews were performed in Spanish, since none of informants speaks a different dialect or language; none of the respondents belong to a different ethnic group. All information concerning plants names and their uses were recorded in notes and photographs.</p></sec><sec><title>Data analysis</title><p>Considering gender as independent group, we compare each variable (number of species and number of uses) between men and women by using the <italic toggle="yes">t</italic> Test [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B25">25</xref>]. For question 2, age of individuals was correlated with the number of species and the number of uses by means of the Pearson Correlation Coefficient [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B25">25</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B26">26</xref>].</p></sec></sec><sec sec-type="results"><title>Results</title><sec><title>Knowledge of plants and uses</title><p>As in southern Nuevo León [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B12">12</xref>], women from Rayones know more plants and uses than men. In average, women mentioned 50 plants and 36 uses while men mentioned 36.5 plants and 24.5 uses. The women know greater number of wild medicinal plants and their uses than men, but men, know more wild timber species and their uses than women. Men and women know almost the same number of cultivated and wild food plants. In average, women mentioned 19 food plants, while men mentioned 16 species. The age was not correlated with the knowledge of species and their uses, however, it is interesting to note that the youngest (20–31 years old) and the oldest (80 &gt; years old) individuals of both genders know fewer number of plants and uses than those of middle age (32–65 years old), this agrees with the results found in other countries of [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B9">9</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B27">27</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B28">28</xref>]. Test of Kolmogorov-Smironov for both, women (number of plants: <italic toggle="yes">K-S</italic> = 0.55, <italic toggle="yes">d.f</italic> = 55, <italic toggle="yes">P</italic> &gt; 0.20; uses: <italic toggle="yes">K-S</italic> = 0.098, <italic toggle="yes">d.f.</italic> = 55, <italic toggle="yes">P</italic> = 0.200) and men (number of plants: <italic toggle="yes">K-S</italic> = 0.104, <italic toggle="yes">d.f.</italic> = 55, <italic toggle="yes">P &gt;</italic> 0.20; uses: <italic toggle="yes">K-S</italic> = 0.106, <italic toggle="yes">d.f.</italic> = 55, <italic toggle="yes">P &gt;</italic> 0.185) and Levene test (number of plants: <italic toggle="yes">F</italic> = 1.049; number of uses; <italic toggle="yes">F</italic> = 1.391) shown normality and homogeneity of variance respectively. The <italic toggle="yes">t</italic> Test showed that women know more plants (<italic toggle="yes">t =</italic> 2.93<italic toggle="yes">, d.f. =</italic> 108<italic toggle="yes">, p &gt;</italic> 0.007) and more uses (<italic toggle="yes">t =</italic> 2.703<italic toggle="yes">, d.f. =</italic> 108<italic toggle="yes">, p &gt;</italic> 0.008) than men in Rayones.
