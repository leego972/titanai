Indeed, there is a growing trend to isolate proteins from <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds, e.g., from canola seed meal to be used in human food [<xref rid="B52-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>] and as a coproduct of oil recovery [<xref rid="B53-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>]. An acyl carrier protein [<xref rid="B54-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">54</xref>] and a ~60 kDa aminopeptidase enzyme, with maximum activity at pH 6.5 and temperature of 40 °C for Phe-pNA as a substrate, were characterized and purified from maturing seeds of <italic toggle="yes">B. napus</italic> [<xref rid="B55-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>]. Furthermore, from <italic toggle="yes">B. napus</italic> var. <italic toggle="yes">oleifera</italic> seeds, a new low-molar-mass trypsin serine proteinase iso-inhibitor, 5-oxoPro1-Gly62-RTI-III (6767.8 Da), was isolated [<xref rid="B56-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>]. Mustard seeds are very rich in protein of low value due to the presence of napin seed storage proteins, which are difficult to digest and have been identified as major allergens in humans [<xref rid="B57-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>,<xref rid="B58-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>]. In seed meal, four candidate napin genes in R-o-18 and 10 in Chiifu-401 were identified with high sequence similarity to <italic toggle="yes">Arabidopsis thaliana</italic> 2S albumin-like napin genes, which might be responsible for a high prevalence of food allergies [<xref rid="B59-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>]. From <italic toggle="yes">B. rapa</italic> seeds, 7S globulin-like <italic toggle="yes">vicilin</italic> SSPs were identified as the dominant seed storage proteins in the mature seed along with 2S albumin-like napin seed storage proteins (SSPs) and 11/12S globulin-like <italic toggle="yes">cruciferin SSPs</italic> [<xref rid="B60-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>]. In addition to napin seed storage proteins, three allergens were found in <italic toggle="yes">S. alba</italic>: Sin a2 (cruciferin), Sin a3 (non-specific lipid transfer protein), and Sin a4 (profilin) [<xref rid="B61-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>].</p><p>Chemical investigation of seeds has also revealed the presence of phytonutrients such as trace minerals like phosphorus [<xref rid="B39-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>], vitamin E [<xref rid="B62-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>], carotenoids, and tocopherols [<xref rid="B63-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>] (<xref rid="molecules-27-06008-t003" ref-type="table">Table 3</xref>). </p><p>Moreover, the aqueous and organic extracts of <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> plant seeds have been found to contain considerable amounts of bioactive phytochemical compounds such as volatile oil, glycosides, reducing sugar, polyphenols, phenolic acids, flavonoids, alkaloids, saponins, terpenoids, tannins, and glucosinolates. The major chemical constituents in <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds as reported in the relevant literature are quantitatively mentioned in <xref rid="molecules-27-06008-t003" ref-type="table">Table 3</xref>. It is clear that the specific molecules and their content in seeds vary greatly within species, varieties, and the extracting solvent. Thus, for example, as demonstrated in <xref rid="molecules-27-06008-t003" ref-type="table">Table 3</xref>, alkaloids, saponins, and tannins are present only in <italic toggle="yes">B. nigra</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">B. juncea</italic> seeds [<xref rid="B64-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>,<xref rid="B65-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>,<xref rid="B66-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>]. In addition, studies have found that <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds are a valuable source of polyphenols. Vanillin, catechin, and quercetin have also been detected in <italic toggle="yes">B. juncea</italic> L. Czern seed extract with a high content of catechin, followed by vanillin [<xref rid="B62-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>].</p><p>Within the polyphenol richness, <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds are characterized by the presence of glucosinolates, especially in <italic toggle="yes">B. campestris</italic> [<xref rid="B67-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>] and <italic toggle="yes">B. napus</italic> [<xref rid="B39-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>], along with a high value in the seed meal of <italic toggle="yes">B.
