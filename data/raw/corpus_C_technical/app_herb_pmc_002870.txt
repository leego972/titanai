2026</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><sec id="Sec1"><title>Introduction</title><p id="Par5">Ethnobiology and ethnobotany are increasingly acknowledged as vital disciplines for conserving traditional knowledge systems, which face imminent erosion from global environmental change, urbanization, and language shift [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR1">1</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR2">2</xref>]. Beyond their archival value, these disciplines provide the empirical basis for understanding how communities adapt health practices to local environments, preserving a biocultural heritage that might otherwise be eroded by modernization [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR3">3</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR4">4</xref>]. Simultaneously, ethnobotanical knowledge has long underpinned drug discovery and phytomedicine development. Historical analyses consistently demonstrate that a substantial proportion of approved drugs originate from natural products initially identified through traditional medical systems [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR5">5</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR7">7</xref>]. By leveraging ethnobotanical data, ethnopharmacology prioritizes species for mechanistic investigation, thereby narrowing the search space and improving the efficiency of bioprospecting [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR8">8</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">9</xref>]. Ethnobotanical research is also central to conservation planning because it identifies which species and habitats are most salient to local health systems and livelihoods [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR10">10</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR11">11</xref>].</p><p id="Par6">Indonesia, as a megadiverse country, offers a unique landscape for examining these biocultural dynamics. The archipelago harbours exceptionally high plant diversity, home to an estimated 30,000 to 40,000 species of flowering plants [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>]. This biological richness coincides with profound ethnographic diversity. The nation comprises approximately 713 ethnic groups and 360–719 language groups across the archipelago, with Bahasa Indonesia serving as the official unifying language [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR13">13</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>]. This intersection of nature and culture has fostered a vibrant traditional medicinal heritage that spans preventive, curative, and ritual domains. Case studies ranging from the Batak Karo in North Sumatra to Balinese <italic toggle="yes">usada</italic> traditions demonstrate that plant-based therapies remain deeply embedded in everyday health-seeking behavior [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR15">15</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR19">19</xref>].
