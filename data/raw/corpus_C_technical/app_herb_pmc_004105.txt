Bacopa monnieri (L.) Westtst.</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Bacopa monnieri</italic> (L.), also known as brahmi, jalabrahmi, or water hyssop, is one of the most important nootropic plants in Ayurvedic medicine. It has been traditionally used for more than 3000 years as a <italic toggle="yes">medhya rasayana</italic> (cognitive rejuvenating tonic) to enhance memory, learning, and concentration, and to treat anxiety, epilepsy, and neurological disorders (<xref rid="nutrients-18-00931-f007" ref-type="fig">Figure 7</xref>) [<xref rid="B72-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>,<xref rid="B73-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">B. monnieri</italic> contains bacosides A and B as its principal bioactive constituents responsible for neuroprotective and cognitive effects. These compounds are mixtures of dammarane-type triterpenoid saponins containing sugar moieties linked to a steroidal aglycone backbone [<xref rid="B74-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>,<xref rid="B75-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>]. Bacoside A consists of bacoside A3, bacopasaponin C, bacopasides II and X, and jujubogenin derivatives [<xref rid="B74-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>,<xref rid="B75-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>].</p><p>The mechanism of action of <italic toggle="yes">B. monnieri</italic> involves enhancement of acetylcholine synthesis through activation of choline acetyltransferase (ChAT), the key enzyme catalyzing the biosynthesis of acetylcholine from acetyl-CoA and choline in cholinergic neurons. This leads to improved cholinergic neurotransmission in the brain, particularly in the hippocampus and cerebral cortex [<xref rid="B72-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>,<xref rid="B73-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>]. In addition, <italic toggle="yes">Bacopa</italic> inhibits acetylcholinesterase (AChE), the enzyme responsible for acetylcholine degradation in the synaptic cleft, thereby prolonging acetylcholine action and strengthening cholinergic signaling [<xref rid="B72-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>,<xref rid="B76-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>]. In silico and <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> studies have shown that aglycones of bacoside A (jujubogenin and pseudojujubogenin) bind to the active site of AChE (IC<sub>50</sub> = 4.2–12.8 μM), exhibiting stronger inhibitory activity than bacoside A itself, suggesting <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> conversion to more active forms [<xref rid="B74-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>,<xref rid="B76-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">B. monnieri</italic> exhibits strong antioxidant properties by increasing the activity of superoxide dismutase (SOD), catalase, and glutathione peroxidase (GPx) in the hippocampus, cerebral cortex, and cerebellum, thereby protecting neurons against oxidative stress-induced damage [<xref rid="B72-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>,<xref rid="B73-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>]. Its antioxidant mechanism involves activation of the transcription factor Nrf2 (nuclear factor erythroid 2–related factor 2), which induces the expression of genes encoding antioxidant enzymes and phase II detoxification proteins [<xref rid="B72-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>]. Administration of <italic toggle="yes">B. monnieri</italic> extract (40–80 mg/kg body weight for 30–60 days) reduces oxidative stress markers, including malondialdehyde (MDA), protein carbonyls (PC), and reactive oxygen species (ROS), while increasing reduced glutathione (GSH) levels by 30–50% in the brains of rats exposed to oxidative stress [<xref rid="B72-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>,<xref rid="B76-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>].</p><p>Bacoside A modulates levels of neurotransmitters, including serotonin (5-HT), dopamine (DA), noradrenaline (NA), acetylcholine (ACh), and GABA in limbic brain structures, contributing to its anxiolytic and antidepressant effects [<xref rid="B72-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>,<xref rid="B73-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">B. monnieri</italic> increases serotonin levels in the hippocampus, prefrontal cortex, and striatum through inhibition of monoamine oxidase (MAO) and upregulation of serotonergic receptors 5-HT<sub>1</sub>A and 5-HT<sub>2</sub>A, thereby modulating neuronal plasticity underlying memory formation [<xref rid="B73-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>].
