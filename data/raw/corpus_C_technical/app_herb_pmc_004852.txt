Effect of adaptogens on CNS system, particularly neuroprotective activity has been demonstrated in many animal and human studies (Panossian and Wikman, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B93">2010</xref>; Panossian et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B96">2011</xref>). Clinical efficacy of adaptogens in behavioral and mental disorders such as depression, anxiety, bipolar disorder, chronic, and stress-induced fatigue has been recently reviewed (Panossian and Wikman, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B92">2009</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B93">2010</xref>).</p><p>ADAPT-232 (Chisan<sup>®</sup>) is a traditional herbal medicinal product consisting of a fixed combination of extracts from <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> root, <italic toggle="yes">Schisandra chinensis</italic> berry, and <italic toggle="yes">Eleutherococcus senticosus</italic> root. It is taken for decreased body performance such as fatigue and weakness (Bogatova et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B11">1997</xref>; Narimanian et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B76">2005</xref>; Aslanyan et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B6">2010</xref>). In general, herbal mixtures exert their bioactivities through synergistic interactions of single components. This synergism can be attributed to the fact that medicinal herbs contain many different phytochemicals, which may mutually influence each other’s activity. More than 140 compounds have been identified in <italic toggle="yes">R. rosea</italic> roots (Panossian and Wikman, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B93">2010</xref>), 100 compounds in <italic toggle="yes">E. senticosus</italic> roots (Huang et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B47">2011</xref>), and about 200 compounds in <italic toggle="yes">S. chinensis</italic> berries (Panossian and Wikman, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B91">2008</xref>). Many of them were shown to be active in pharmacological <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> experiments, and likely contribute to the activity of the total extracts (Wagner et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B122">1994</xref>; Panossian, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B85">2003</xref>; Panossian and Wagner, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B89">2005</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B90">2011</xref>; Panossian and Wikman, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B91">2008</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B93">2010</xref>; Panossian et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B88">2008</xref>; Huang et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B47">2011</xref>). It has been shown that the adaptogenic activity of ADAPT-232 is associated with key mediators of stress response, e.g., heat-shock-proteins (Hsp70) and NPY, involved in the regulation of homeostasis, oxidative stress, energy metabolism, cognitive function, and activation of the immune system during fatigue and exhaustion (Prodius et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B100">1997</xref>; Panossian et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B94">2009</xref>). However, the cellular and molecular modes of action are not well understood due to the fact that herbal remedies in general have multiple targets and several mechanisms rather than a single mechanism may account for their pharmacological effects.</p><p>Any pharmacological effect represents an integrated response of an organism to the drug. The response can be associated with interactions between the drug and the cell at various levels of regulation:
<list list-type="bullet"><list-item><p>The level of a small physiologically active molecules, e.g., cAMP plays an important role in the integrative response of the organism. This is the so-called metabolomic level, because ATP is a precursor of cAMP and AMP is a metabolite.</p></list-item><list-item><p>The level of proteins involved in the synthesis or degradation of ligands or ligand receptors. This is the so-called proteomics level of regulation.</p></list-item><list-item><p>Genes encoding proteins involved in synthesis, degradation, signal reception, and regulation. This is the so-called genomic and transcriptional level of regulation.</p></list-item></list></p><p>Activation or suppression of gene expression results in activation or inhibition of the biosynthesis of encoded proteins (enzymes, cofactors, or receptors), which in turn affects the production and function of active small molecules. Gene expression profiles aid in tracing molecular interactions and signal transduction pathways and to predict the pharmacological effect a drug will have on various cellular functions.</p><p>Recently, the so-called “-omics” technologies have been established for the analysis of pharmacological effects (Ulrich-Merzenich et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B119">2007</xref>; Sarris et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B106">2012</xref>).
