The concentrations of the free ICG and DOX examined in the cytotoxicity experiment corresponded to the doses that can be provided from the HIDPPNPs. From the haemocytometer analyses (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="f8">Fig. 8-II</xref>), our data show that the viability of the cells treated with NIR laser irradiation in the absence of an agent (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="f8">Fig. 8-I</xref>; X2) was 96.5%, indicating that the slight temperature increase due to the NIR laser irradiation (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="f6">Fig. 6</xref>) was nontoxic to the cells. In contrast, a dose-dependent increase in cytotoxicity was observed in the agent-treated cells (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="f8">Fig. 8-I</xref>; rows A–E), in which the cells treated with HIDPPNPs and NIR laser irradiation (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="f8">Fig. 8-I,E1–E6</xref>) underwent higher levels of cell death as compared to the cells treated with HIDPPNPs without light illumination (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="f8">Fig. 8-I,A1–A6</xref>), those treated with DOX alone (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="f8">Fig. 8-I,B1–B6</xref>), and those treated with free ICG + NIR laser irradiation (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="f8">Fig. 8-I,C1–C6</xref>), whereas the level of cell death was similar with that obtained from the cells with free ICG/DOX-mediated photochemotherapy (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="f8">Fig. 8-I,D1–D6</xref>). Based on the finding that the viability of the cells treated with HIDPPNPs and NIR laser irradiation was lower than that of the cells without NIR exposure throughout the dose range, the HIDPPNPs were characterized as slightly toxic to the cells in the absence of light illumination. Moreover, we found that the mortality of the cells treated with HIDPPNPs and NIR laser irradiation increased exponentially when the concentrations of ICG and DOX provided by the HIDPPNPs were ≥ 1 and 0.8 μM, respectively (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="f8">Fig. 8-II</xref>). As shown in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="f8">Fig. 8-II</xref>, viabilities of 64%, 40%, and 12% were obtained for the cells with 1, 2, and 4 μM ICG in the HIDPPNPs, respectively, and those results were 1.2 (<italic toggle="yes">P</italic> = 0.0372)-, 1.6 (<italic toggle="yes">P</italic> = 0.0162)-, and 4.4 (<italic toggle="yes">P</italic> = 0.0173)-fold lower than the viability values obtained for the groups with equal concentrations of free ICG. Furthermore, those results were 1.3 (<italic toggle="yes">P</italic> = 0.0011)-, 2.1 (<italic toggle="yes">P</italic> = 0.0004)-, and 5.8 (<italic toggle="yes">P</italic> = 0.0022)-fold lower than the viability values obtained for the groups with equal doses of DOX alone (0.8, 1.5, and 3 μM DOX), respectively. Based on the results shown in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="f6">Fig. 6</xref>, we reason that the significant cytotoxicity of HIDPPNPs with concentrations ≥1 μM ICG equivalent was achieved due to the relatively high dose of DOX and the combination of the photodynamic and photothermal effects, while the HIDPPNPs with concentrations ≤0.5 μM ICG equivalent only provided a mild photodynamic effect and a relatively low dose of DOX and thus resulted in a moderate efficacy of cytotoxicity.</p><p>In this study, the HIDPPNP-mediated cancer cell killing was performed by 5 min of phototherapy after 4-h uptake of the photosensitizers (i.e., HIDPPNPs) followed by 24 h of chemotherapy. To minimize the chemotherapy-induced side effects, the HIDPPNPs were used with up to 3 μM of DOX, which is lower than the clinical dose of DOX (typically ≥ 10 μM)<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b37">37</xref>. However, robust cytotoxicity of the HIDPPNPs upon NIR exposure was still obtained in each setting, and the resulting mortality was even higher than that caused by using twice amount of encapsulated DOX alone (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="f8">Fig. 8-II</xref>), indicating that the phototherapy played a crucial role in the HIDPPNP-mediated anticancer treatment. The importance of phototherapy in such combined therapeutics can also be identified through comparing the viability of cells treated by free DOX alone (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="f8">Fig. 8-I</xref>; Row B) to that treated with both free ICG and DOX (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="f8">Fig. 8-I</xref>; Row D). In this study, although the effect of photochemotherapy generated from the HIDPPNPs <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> can be achieved by using free ICG and DOX individually (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="f8">Fig. 8-I</xref>; Row D vs. E), it is predictable that the latter may not be an appropriate approach for use in the clinic due to defects of applicability such as lack of target-ability, insufficient stability, and rapid plasma clearance for the free ICG molecule as described above.
