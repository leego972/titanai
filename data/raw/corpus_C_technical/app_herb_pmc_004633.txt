# PMC: adaptogen stress response HPA axis — Overview

ref-type="bibr">114</xref>,<xref rid="B115-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">115</xref>,<xref rid="B118-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">118</xref>].</p><p>Various self-assessments and diverse types of questionnaires were developed to detect and assess mental fatigue, including the following:<list list-type="bullet"><list-item><p>Occupation fatigue exhaustion recovery (OFER) scale;</p></list-item><list-item><p>Visual analog scale to evaluate fatigue severity (VAS-F);</p></list-item><list-item><p>Fatigue assessment scale (FAS);</p></list-item><list-item><p>Multidimensional fatigue inventory (MFI);</p></list-item><list-item><p>Fatigue severity inventory (FSI);</p></list-item><list-item><p>Mental fatigue scale (MFS).</p></list-item></list></p><p>Overall, the pros of mental fatigue detection methods include multiple brief questionnaires, which are reliable, easy to administer, and accessible. Cons are related to the inability to provide moment-to-moment fluctuations of mental fatigue, subjective and not immune to response biases, and may not accurately reflect humans’ cognitive state at that particular moment. The validity of these questionnaires is uncertain, as they are highly subjective measurement tools and are not immune to response bias detection techniques [<xref rid="B2-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="B113-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>,<xref rid="B114-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>].</p><p>Another method to detect mental fatigue is the Attention Network Test, which is related to three areas: alerting, orienting, and executive control [<xref rid="B119-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">119</xref>], particularly the following:<list list-type="bullet"><list-item><p>The changes in reaction times insinuated by a warning signal (alerting).</p></list-item><list-item><p>The changes in reaction times result from cues indicating where the target will occur (orienting).</p></list-item><list-item><p>The subject examined the efficiency of the executive network in responding by pressing a key indicating the direction of a central arrow surrounded by congruent, incongruent, or neutral flankers.</p></list-item></list></p><p>The pros of mental fatigue detection methods cover three fundamental areas of attention: alertness, orientation, and executive control. The cons are related to a long testing period (25–30 min), practice effects, mainly cognitive and for inferring purposes, and an attention network too complex to be assessed using a single task [<xref rid="B2-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>].</p><p>Mental fatigue was also measured using EEG spectral changes in the theta and alpha waves in the brain’s frontal and occipital cortical regions, where slow wave activity increases in mentally fatigued individuals [<xref rid="B120-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">120</xref>,<xref rid="B121-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">121</xref>,<xref rid="B122-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">122</xref>], suggesting that as mental load increases, the brain loses its capacity to cope with it, thus slowing down brain activity. Also, individuals who were mentally fatigued from performing cognitive tasks produced decreased α-frequency band power in the brain area, which is responsible for language and semantic memory processing, visual perception, and multimodal sensory integration [<xref rid="B123-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">123</xref>].</p><p>Overall, the theta wave was a reliable primary biomarker for mental fatigue, while the alpha wave was a reliable secondary biomarker. These methods require a laboratory setting to reduce signal noise, and they cannot detect mental fatigue while performing cognitively demanding tasks accurately [<xref rid="B2-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>].</p><p>Heart rate variability measurement is easily integrated into smart devices; however, there are too many confounding variables, and it is of low reliability.</p><p>Cortisol level was used alongside health monitoring technologies; however, there is a lack of evidence to support a link between salivary cortisol and mental fatigue; furthermore, saliva samples are easily contaminated.</p><p>The testosterone/cortisol ratio, the anabolic index, was used as a biological marker of exercise that indicates chronic overload and overtraining of athletes, suggesting that it reflects the anabolic and catabolic hormonal balance [<xref rid="B124-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">124</xref>,<xref rid="B125-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">125</xref>,<xref rid="B126-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">126</xref>,<xref rid="B127-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">127</xref>]. A prolonged increase in catabolism and a decrease in anabolism may reduce athletic performance and result in overtraining [<xref rid="B128-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">128</xref>,<xref rid="B129-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">129</xref>].</p><p>The primary symptoms and signs of chronic overload and overtraining syndrome were classified into four categories [<xref rid="B129-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">129</xref>,<xref rid="B130-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">130</xref>] as follows:<list list-type="bullet"><list-item><p>Psychological: feelings of depression, general apathy, difficulty concentrating, emotional instability, fear of competition, excitation, and restlessness.</p></list-item><list-item><p>Immunological: increased susceptibility to infection, increased severity of minor ailments, decreased functional activity of neutrophils, decreased total lymphocyte counts, reduced response.</p></list-item><list-item><p>Biochemical: decreased hemoglobin, increased lactate, elevated cortisol levels, decreased free testosterone levels, and a decrease of more than 30% in the ratio of free testosterone to cortisol.</p></list-item><list-item><p>Physiological: decreased performance, body fat, decreased muscular strength, increased CO<sub>2</sub> at submaximal loads, muscle soreness, changes in the heart rate, prolonged recovery periods, loss of appetite, and chronic fatigue.</p></list-item></list></p><p>Among these symptoms and signs, the testosterone-to-cortisol ratio (T/C) is a reliable marker for monitoring an athlete’s training and possibly diagnosing overtraining syndrome [<xref rid="B124-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">124</xref>,<xref rid="B125-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">125</xref>,<xref rid="B126-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">126</xref>,<xref rid="B127-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">127</xref>,<xref rid="B131-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">131</xref>] and assessing the efficacy of adaptogens used for recovery of athletes after exercising and prevention of the symptoms of overtraining [<xref rid="B21-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>].
