While computational methods have shown remarkable potential in accelerating drug discovery efforts, their success relies heavily on two primary factors: the quality and relevance of input data sets and the algorithms’ efficacy and precision [<xref rid="B113-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>,<xref rid="B114-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>,<xref rid="B115-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">115</xref>].</p><p>Over the past years, several studies have explored the properties of phytochemicals as either adjuvants or inhibitors to enhance the potency of existing antibiotics, showing promising results for future medical applications. By employing computational techniques like virtual screening, molecular docking, QSAR modelling, and network pharmacology, scientists can quickly and more efficiently discover and enhance natural compounds with activity against drug-resistant targets (<xref rid="ijms-25-08792-f009" ref-type="fig">Figure 9</xref>) [<xref rid="B1-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B29-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>,<xref rid="B116-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">116</xref>,<xref rid="B117-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">117</xref>]. For example, Epigallocatechin Gallate (EGCG) found in green tea targets β-lactamases enzymes, which ae responsible for anti-biotic resistance in bacteria and efflux pumps [<xref rid="B118-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">118</xref>,<xref rid="B119-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">119</xref>]. Allicin is another natural compound found in garlic that targets bacterial efflux pumps and prevents biofilm formation [<xref rid="B120-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">120</xref>].</p><p>Modern drug discovery methods provide insight into the mechanisms underlying phytochemical action towards drug resistance, thus adding to our understanding of such diseases. Advanced analytical methods that help isolate, identify, and characterise potential compounds have recently been applied. Furthermore, combining separation and detection methods through hyphenated approaches such as LC-MS and LC-NMR are efficient in streamlining compound identification (<xref rid="ijms-25-08792-f009" ref-type="fig">Figure 9</xref>) [<xref rid="B121-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">121</xref>,<xref rid="B122-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">122</xref>,<xref rid="B123-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">123</xref>].</p></sec></sec></sec><sec id="sec4-ijms-25-08792"><title>4. Computational Approaches to Identifying Potential Phytochemical Drugs</title><p>Computational approaches have emerged as an effective means of identifying and optimising phytochemical therapeutics. For instance, machine learning, virtual screening, molecular dynamics simulations, and molecular docking have previously been used to identify and modify the biological activity of phytochemicals (<xref rid="ijms-25-08792-f009" ref-type="fig">Figure 9</xref>) [<xref rid="B124-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">124</xref>].</p><p>Virtual screening is a popular computational technique in drug discovery that can rapidly evaluate and prioritise compounds for experimental testing against a specific target or disease model [<xref rid="B125-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">125</xref>,<xref rid="B126-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">126</xref>,<xref rid="B127-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">127</xref>]. Several approaches can be used, e.g., molecular descriptors and fingerprint-based similarity searching to ligand-based pharmacophore models or structure-based techniques. [<xref rid="B128-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">128</xref>,<xref rid="B129-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">129</xref>,<xref rid="B130-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">130</xref>]. Virtual screening methods can be applied to large databases containing known phytochemicals or in-silico-generated libraries mimicking natural products [<xref rid="B131-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">131</xref>]. This efficient technique manages large datasets and can reduce the number of compounds evaluated in biological assays [<xref rid="B132-ijms-25-08792" ref-type="bibr">132</xref>].</p><sec id="sec4dot1-ijms-25-08792"><title>4.1.
