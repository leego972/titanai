Protection against neuron and central neuron system disorders</title><p>A titolated extract from <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> and salidroside protected human cortical cells (HCN 1-A) from oxidative stressors such as H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub> and glutamate-induced cell apoptosis [<xref rid="b40-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>]. Pre-treatment with <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> extract significantly increased cell survival and prevented plasma membrane damage and morphological disruption caused by glutamate or H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub>, indicating that <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> extract protects neurons from oxidative-stress-induced disorders [<xref rid="b40-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>]. In addition, <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> extract significantly reduced oxidative-stress-induced elevation of intracellular free Ca<sup>2+</sup> concentrations [<xref rid="b40-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>].</p><p>Significant improvement in long-term memory was observed after 10 days of treatment with 0.1 mL of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> extract [<xref rid="b6-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>]. However, in another study, <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> exhibited cytotoxic effects at 100 μg/mL in cultured primary cortical neurons [<xref rid="b80-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> extracts exhibited anti-oxidant capacity but did not exhibit neuroprotective effects in primary cortical neurons [<xref rid="b80-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>].</p><p>Salidroside suppressed the LPS-induced expression of iNOS and cytokines in BV2 cells in a concentration-dependent manner [<xref rid="b81-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>]. Orally administering <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> crude extract (500 mg/kg) suppressed the expression of the proin-flammatory factors iNOS, IL-1β, and TNF-α in the kidney and prefrontal cortex of the brain in mice [<xref rid="b81-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>]. L-Glutamate treatment increased the levels of phosphorylated MAPK, <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-JNK, and <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-p38 [<xref rid="b81-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>]. These results indicate that <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> may have therapeutic potential for treating inflammation and neurodegenerative disease [<xref rid="b81-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>].</p><p>Oxidative stress plays a crucial role in Parkinson's disease and other neurodegenerative disorders. Salidroside has a neuroprotective effect in cortical neurons because of its antioxidant activity and ability to stabilize cellular Ca<sup>2+</sup> ho-meostasis [<xref rid="b40-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>]. Incubating PC12 cells with salidroside prior to MPP<sup>+</sup> exposure significantly reduced cell apoptosis and attenuated the collapse of the mitochondrial membrane potential [<xref rid="b82-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">82</xref>]. Furthermore, salidroside inhibited MPP<sup>+</sup>-induced NO increases, the overexpression of nNOS and iNOS, and the accumulation of ROS and intracellular free Ca<sup>2+</sup>[<xref rid="b82-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">82</xref>]. Salidroside inhibited ROS and NO production, protecting PC12 cells from oxidative stress. The protective effects of salidroside on PC12 cells are mediated by the inhibition of ROS generation and the NO pathway [<xref rid="b82-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">82</xref>].
