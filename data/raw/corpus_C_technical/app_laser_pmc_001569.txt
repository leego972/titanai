# PMC: optical coherence tomography clinical application — Overview

style="fixed-case" toggle="no">OCTA</styled-content> Findings in Congenital and Hereditary Aetiology</title><sec id="ceo14544-sec-0012"><label>4.1</label><title>Hereditary Optic Neuropathies</title><p>Autosomal Dominant Optic Atrophy (ADOA) is an inherited mitochondrial optic neuropathy, with prevalence in the population of 1 in 25 000 [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0086" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>]. Mitochondrial membrane dysfunction, most commonly because of OPA1 gene mutation, induces apoptosis in retinal ganglion cells, with preferential involvement of the papillomacular bundle [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0087" ref-type="bibr">87</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0088" ref-type="bibr">88</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0089" ref-type="bibr">89</xref>]. There is heterogeneity in the manifestation of ADOA due to genotype‐related variation; however, most typically it manifests as symmetrical reduced visual acuity, centro‐caecal scotoma, and blue‐yellow dyschromatopsia with temporal pallor of the optic disc. Visual symptoms have their onset in childhood, progressing slowly, and reaching a plateau in early adulthood [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0090" ref-type="bibr">90</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0091" ref-type="bibr">91</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0092" ref-type="bibr">92</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0093" ref-type="bibr">93</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0094" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>].</p><p>The OCTA analysis demonstrates global reduced vessel density in the macular and RPC network, with the strongest indicator being the superficial perifoveal capillary density and less so the inferior and temporal RPC density when differentiating those with ADOA from healthy subjects; the pattern of capillary dropout correlates strongly with visual acuity, RNFL thickness and visual field loss on ancillary testing [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0095" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0096" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>]. These analyses were all performed on individuals with genetically confirmed ADOA. It is theorised that the capillary dropout occurs secondary to RGC loss [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0096" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>]. Investigators are yet to show evidence that microvascular changes occur prior to clinical manifestation of disease to refute this theory. However, vascular changes on OCTA may be used to predict the rate of disease progression given the phenotypic heterogeneity of ADOA [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0095" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0097" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>].</p><p>Leber's Hereditary Optic Neuropathy (LHON) is another mitochondrial optic neuropathy with prevalence in the population of 1 in 30 000 [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0098" ref-type="bibr">98</xref>]. Mitochondrial dysfunction occurs as a result of gene mutations in the complex I protein [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0099" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>]. The clinical features include that of a normal disc or a hyperaemic pseudo‐oedematous disc with peripapillary telangiectasia [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0100" ref-type="bibr">100</xref>]. A lack of leakage on FFA is typically used to differentiate this pseudo‐papilloedema from other disc swelling caused by optic neuritis or genuine papilloedema, which is a shortcoming of OCTA [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0099" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>]. OCTA investigators have shown evidence that microvascular changes occur prior to clinical manifestation of disease, suggesting that the retinal microvasculature plays a pathogenic role in retinal ganglion cell loss [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0101" ref-type="bibr">101</xref>]. Early diagnosis may serve useful in light of evolving therapeutic options available for LHON [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0102" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0103" ref-type="bibr">103</xref>]. In the acute phase of disease, OCTA demonstrates a pseudo‐oedematous disc with telangiectatic vessels at the optic nerve head, but reduced vascular density in the temporal RPC, and thickening of the RNFL on OCT cross‐section. In the subacute phase, reduced vascular density in the temporal RPC persists and is accompanied by thinning of the temporal RNFL (Figure <xref rid="ceo14544-fig-0006" ref-type="fig">6</xref>). This is later followed by capillary dropout and RNFL thinning of the other quadrants in the chronic phase [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0100" ref-type="bibr">100</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0104" ref-type="bibr">104</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0105" ref-type="bibr">105</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0106" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0107" ref-type="bibr">107</xref>]. Furthermore, retinal capillary density loss positively correlates to disease severity [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0108" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>].</p><fig position="float" fig-type="FIGURE" id="ceo14544-fig-0006" orientation="portrait"><label>FIGURE 6</label><caption><p>12 mm × 12 mm widefield OCTA findings of a representative patient with LHON.
