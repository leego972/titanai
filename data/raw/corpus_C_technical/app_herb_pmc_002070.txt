Biological Activities of Almond By-Products</title><p>Phytochemicals are compounds known for their biological effects including anticancer, antiviral, and anti-inflammatory activities, among others, demonstrated upon a number of experimental procedures conducted with in vitro and in vivo (in animals and humans) models [<xref rid="B69-molecules-22-01774" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>,<xref rid="B70-molecules-22-01774" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>,<xref rid="B71-molecules-22-01774" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>].</p><p>Almond skin, hulls, and blanch water have been shown to have antimicrobial and antibacterial potential [<xref rid="B56-molecules-22-01774" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>,<xref rid="B72-molecules-22-01774" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>] as well as anti-inflammatory, photoprotective, and prebiotic effects [<xref rid="B27-molecules-22-01774" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>,<xref rid="B43-molecules-22-01774" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>,<xref rid="B73-molecules-22-01774" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>,<xref rid="B74-molecules-22-01774" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>,<xref rid="B75-molecules-22-01774" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>], antitumor properties [<xref rid="B39-molecules-22-01774" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>], and antiviral activity [<xref rid="B76-molecules-22-01774" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>]. Based on these biological properties, by-products from the almond industry have been promoted as useful functional ingredients useful for the control of oxidative process due to their strong antioxidant/antiradical activity [<xref rid="B30-molecules-22-01774" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>,<xref rid="B41-molecules-22-01774" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>] and more specifically, enhancing effect on the resistance of human LDL to oxidation [<xref rid="B29-molecules-22-01774" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>].</p><p>Almond skin polyphenols are bioavailable in the upper gastrointestinal tract and potentially available for absorption during digestion [<xref rid="B66-molecules-22-01774" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>,<xref rid="B77-molecules-22-01774" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>]. Hence, blanched skin obtained during the industrial process developed to obtain marketable almond kernels constitutes a potential source of bioactive compounds. Furthermore, the antimicrobial potential of almond skin refers to this by-product as a potential source of natural antimicrobials. Mandalari et al. (2010) evaluated the antimicrobial properties of fractions derived from almond skin, against Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria, and the yeast [<xref rid="B72-molecules-22-01774" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>].
