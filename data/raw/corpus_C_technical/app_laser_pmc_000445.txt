The primary objective was to compare the efficacy of BF‐200 ALA to placebo for the field‐directed treatment of mild to moderate AKs with PDT when using the BF‐RhodoLED® lamp.</p></sec><sec id="jdv20452-sec-0015"><title>Participants</title><p>Seven centres in Germany collected clinical and follow‐up data between October 2013 and April 2015. The study including follow‐up was approved by the responsible ethics committees and the competent authority (BfArM, Germany) prior to the start of the study and performed according to the German drug law, the guidelines of Good Clinical Practice and the Declaration of Helsinki (EudraCT‐no.: 2013‐002510‐12; NCT‐no.: <ext-link xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" ext-link-type="pmc:clinical-trial" xlink:href="NCT01966120">NCT01966120</ext-link>).</p><p>Details on participant inclusion and exclusion criteria, study medication, randomization and treatment protocol were published previously along with the results of the treatment phase.<xref rid="jdv20452-bib-0020" ref-type="bibr">
<sup>20</sup>
</xref>
</p></sec><sec id="jdv20452-sec-0016"><title>Interventions</title><p>In the treatment phase, participants received one or two field‐directed PDTs with either BF‐200 ALA (Ameluz®) or placebo (vehicle of BF‐200 ALA). After lesion preparation, 2 g of BF‐200 ALA or placebo was applied to a treatment field of approximately 20 cm<sup>2</sup> with 4–8 mild to moderate AK lesions (target lesions) on the face and/or scalp. After 3 h incubation under light‐tight occlusive dressing, treatment fields were illuminated with narrow‐spectrum red light (BF‐RhodoLED®, 635 nm, 37 J/cm<sup>2</sup>). If any lesions remained 12 weeks after first PDT, the participant received a second field‐directed PDT.</p></sec><sec id="jdv20452-sec-0017"><title>Outcomes</title><sec id="jdv20452-sec-0018"><title>Assessment of recurrences</title><p>Assessments of participant‐ and lesion‐based recurrences were performed 6 and 12 months after the last PDT.</p><p>For assessment of participant‐based recurrences, only complete responders 12 weeks after the last PDT were considered (i.e. participants who had complete remission of all target lesions (=Olsen grade 0)). A target lesion was defined as a distinct, measurable lesion (Olsen grade I or II) with a diameter of 0.5–1.5 cm at baseline.</p><p>A participant is regarded as recurrent if at least one target lesion recurred during follow‐up (as assessed clinically via visual inspection). For assessment of lesion‐based recurrences, only lesions were considered which were clinically cleared 12 weeks after the last PDT. Participants and lesions that were recurrent at the 6‐month assessment were also classified as recurrent at 12 months.</p><p>Participant‐ and lesion‐based recurrence rates were analysed overall and by subgroups ((maximum) severity of AK lesions at baseline, age, gender, skin type group (I–III, IV–VI), treatment area (face and/or scalp), number of AK lesions at baseline and total AK lesion area).</p></sec><sec id="jdv20452-sec-0019"><title>Life table estimates</title><p>The life table approach is based on the following values:
