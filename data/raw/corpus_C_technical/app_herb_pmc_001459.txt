Antidiabetic Activity</title><p>Diabetes is one of the major diseases causing morbidity and mortality worldwide. Several research studies have demonstrated the potential hypoglycemic effect of seeds of the <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> genus. <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seed extracts have been tested in vitro and in animals. The <italic toggle="yes">B. juncea</italic> seed diet (10%, 15%) showed potent antihyperglycemic effects in alloxan diabetic rats, whereas daily oral feeding of 10% powder of seeds of <italic toggle="yes">B. juncea</italic> for 60 days showed weak antihyperglycemic activity in a severe hyperglycemic state in streptozotocin (STZ) diabetic rats [<xref rid="B175-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">175</xref>,<xref rid="B176-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">176</xref>]. Another in vivo study of aqueous seed extract of <italic toggle="yes">B. juncea</italic> (250, 350, 450 mg/kg) proved that the significant hypoglycemic activity on STZ-induced diabetic male albino rats was related to the presence of isothiocyanate glycoside sinigrin, protein, and fixed oil [<xref rid="B177-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">177</xref>]. Moreover, the administration of oil purified from <italic toggle="yes">B. nigra</italic> seeds at a dose of 500 and 1000 mg/kg body weight to STZ diabetic rats caused a reduction in blood glucose level of STZ diabetic rats from 335 to 280 mg/dL and from 330 to 265 mg/dL at 4 h, respectively, as compared with the diabetic control, along with a significant increase in body weight, liver glycogen content, and plasma insulin level, as well as a decrease in glycosylated hemoglobin, a decrease in malondialdehyde (MDA), and an increase in reduced glutathione (GSH) in test groups as compared to the control group. The obtained results indicated that the <italic toggle="yes">B. nigra</italic> seed oil at both doses remarkably induced an antihyperglycemic effect [<xref rid="B178-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">178</xref>].</p><p>The consumption of padding enriched with soluble dietary fiber (DF) from mustard seed mucilage significantly attenuated the blood glucose and insulin levels at specified post-meal intervals in adults with high type 2 diabetes risk [<xref rid="B179-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">179</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec4dot5-molecules-27-06008"><title>4.5. Anti-Inflammatory Activity</title><p>In addition to the properties already mentioned, it has been reported that <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds, particularly broccoli seeds, showed a stronger anti-inflammatory effect higher than carrot and cucumber seeds [<xref rid="B136-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">136</xref>]. Furthermore, <italic toggle="yes">Sinapis alba</italic> seeds exhibited a significant inhibition of the mouse prostatic hyperplasia induced by testosterone propionate and the serum acid phosphatase activity by sinalbin and beta-sitosterol (16.0 and 8.0 mg/kg/day).
