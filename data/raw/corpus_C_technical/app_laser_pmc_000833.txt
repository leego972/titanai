Another version of TD-OCT is <italic toggle="yes">en face</italic> OCT, based on one-dimensional reflectivity profiles (T-scans), collected by flying the spot transversally, while maintaining the axial coordinate fixed (<italic toggle="yes">Reference mirror</italic> at rest). The operation of the SB-OCT is based on the optical spectrum output demodulation of the low coherence interferometer. The spectrum exhibits peaks and troughs (channelled spectrum) and the period of such a modulation is proportional to the OPD in the interferometer (<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="b25">Taplin <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>., 1993</xref>). The larger the OPD, the larger the number of peaks in the spectrum, as shown in <xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig06">Figure 6(a)</xref> when using a mirror as <italic toggle="yes">Object</italic>, for an OPD of ∼3 cl (left) and ∼6 cl (right). The linear camera needs pixels of sufficient small size, δλ (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig06">Fig. 6b</xref>) to be able to sample the succession of peaks and troughs in the channelled spectrum. By downloading its charge content, the linear camera in the spectrometer transforms the optical spectrum into an electrical signal in time (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig06">Fig. 6c</xref>). If multilayered objects are imaged, such as retina or skin, each layer imprints its own spectrum modulation periodicity, depending on its depth. A fast Fourier transform of the linear camera signal translates the periodicity of the channelled spectrum (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig06">Fig. 6a</xref>) into peaks of different frequency, related to the OPD (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig06">Fig. 6c</xref>).
