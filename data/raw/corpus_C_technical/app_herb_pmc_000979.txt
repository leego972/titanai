2026</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><sec id="Sec1"><title>Background</title><p id="Par27">Herbal medicine (HM), rooted in indigenous knowledge and cultural beliefs, has served as the cornerstone of human healthcare for centuries. Today, it remains the primary source of treatment for nearly 80% of the population in developing countries, particularly in rural communities with limited access to conventional medical services [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR1">1</xref>]. In recent decades, many developed countries have also embraced the use of HM to promote healthier living [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR2">2</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR4">4</xref>]. Despite its widespread use and perception of fewer side effects, HM is usually self-administered and largely unregulated, posing huge safety risks for consumers. To address these concerns and harness their value for achieving universal health coverage, the World Health Organization (WHO) has developed technical guidelines and standards to ensure the safety, efficacy and quality control of HM[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR5">5</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR8">8</xref>].</p><p id="Par28">In Ghana, HM is a significant aspect of cultural heritage, with its development well documented [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">9</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR13">13</xref>]. Since 2012, government approval has enabled its integration into the mainstream healthcare system[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>], resulting in a wide range of HM products for various conditions. The Ghana Food and Drugs Authority (FDA) oversees their registration and approval based on efficacy, safety, and clinical data. Scientific validation of efficacy claims has supported the rational use of many of these products. However, many FDA-approved products target common ailments such as malaria, anaemia, cough, and asthma, unlike herbal remedies for neglected tropical diseases (NTDs), largely due to lack of access to suitable efficacy testing platforms [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>].
