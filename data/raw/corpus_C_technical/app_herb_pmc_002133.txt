BY-NC</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><sec sec-type="intro" id="s0001"><title>Introduction</title><p>The <italic toggle="yes">Poikilospermum</italic> genus, belonging to the Urticaceae family,<xref rid="cit0001" ref-type="bibr">1</xref> is primarily located in tropical areas of Southeast Asia,<xref rid="cit0002" ref-type="bibr">2</xref> especially Malaysia and Indonesia.<xref rid="cit0003" ref-type="bibr">3–5</xref> Numerous species of this genus have been used in traditional medicine for the past several decades. Ethnobotanical records indicate that various components of these plants, including the leaves, roots, bark, and flowers, are utilized to treat ailments such as gastrointestinal disorders, inflammation, fever, and dermatological illnesses.<xref rid="cit0006" ref-type="bibr">6–8</xref> Despite its extensive application in traditional medicine, the pharmacological characteristics and phytochemical makeup of <italic toggle="yes">Poikilospermum</italic> have not been adequately investigated, particularly the potential for therapeutic advancement.</p><p>Phytochemical studies have indicated that <italic toggle="yes">Poikilospermum</italic> species possess many bioactive chemicals including flavonoids, tannins, alkaloids, and terpenoids,<xref rid="cit0009" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>,<xref rid="cit0010" ref-type="bibr">10</xref> which are associated with notable pharmacological effects. These chemicals exhibit antibacterial,<xref rid="cit0003" ref-type="bibr">3</xref> anti-inflammatory,<xref rid="cit0011" ref-type="bibr">11</xref> antioxidant, and analgesic activities,<xref rid="cit0003" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="cit0012" ref-type="bibr">12</xref> indicating that species within this genus have significant potential for medicinal use. Nevertheless, extensive evaluations of these discoveries are scarce, and most investigations thus far have concentrated on discrete pharmacological effects without integrating overarching conclusions regarding the genus in its entirety.</p><p>In this review, we focus on <italic toggle="yes">Poikilospermum suaveolens</italic> (Blume) Merr. the most extensively studied species within the genus, and emphasize its bioactive components and medicinal qualities.</p><p>P. <italic toggle="yes">suaveolens</italic>, a climbing shrub belonging to the family Urticaceae, is widely distributed across the tropical forests of Southeast Asia, particularly Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, and Vietnam. This species thrives in humid, shaded environments and is commonly found in secondary forests, riverbanks, and lowland areas. Traditionally, <italic toggle="yes">P. suaveolens</italic> has been used for various ethnomedicinal applications. In Indonesian and Malaysian folk medicine, its leaves and roots are employed in traditional medicine for gastrointestinal ailments such as diarrhea and dysentery, as well as inflammatory conditions, skin infections, and fever.
