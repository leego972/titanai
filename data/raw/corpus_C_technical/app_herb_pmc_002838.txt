However, decoction was the preferred preparation method, while the oral route was the most reported administration route. It is known that boiling allows for the extraction and preservation of herbal medicines for a longer time when compared to cold extraction [<xref rid="B29-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>]. The addition of water, salt, milk, or banana highlights the importance of restoring minerals, water, and other nutrients lost due to diarrhea, and improve the bitter taste of some extracts [<xref rid="B29-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>,<xref rid="B40-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>,<xref rid="B61-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>,<xref rid="B64-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>]. Some extracts are used in a mixture, which makes it difficult to know the contribution of each to the treatment [<xref rid="B46-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>]. Therefore, the phytochemicals of each medicinal plant need to be isolated and chemically characterized.</p><p>Although medicinal plants are used in treating diarrhea, a limited number of them have undergone validation through clinical trials, highlighting the risk of using them [<xref rid="B9-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. The knowledge of using medicinal plants has been transferred from generation to generation through verbal communication [<xref rid="B30-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>,<xref rid="B32-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>]. There is a possibility of the original formula having been altered due to the lack of a written document. Additionally, the limited number of studies on the toxicity of medicinal plants underlines the steps to be covered before the integration of traditional medicine into conventional medicine [<xref rid="B74-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>]. For instance, some medicinal plants may possess mutagenic and carcinogenic compounds, which may show their effects over long periods [<xref rid="B65-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>]. Lastly, preparation methods are not standardized, which may influence the effectiveness of the medicinal extracts [<xref rid="B23-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>,<xref rid="B36-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>]. Therefore, the use of medicinal plants is important in healthcare systems, but their negative effects need to be well-known and addressed. Short-term and long-term health complications may arise from the improper use of medicinal plants, putting the lives of patient at risk [<xref rid="B36-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>].</p><p>It is important to check the antimicrobial activities of plants known to treat diarrhea using diarrhea-causing pathogens. This review found that two studies did not report any activity against the Gram-negative bacteria used [<xref rid="B48-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">48</xref>,<xref rid="B51-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>]. It is known that the source of plants and extraction methods may affect the screening results [<xref rid="B75-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>]. The use of agar-based methods instead of broth microdilution in screening for antimicrobial activities is not suitable, as some bioactive compounds are non-polar and do not diffuse well in agar media [<xref rid="B19-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>,<xref rid="B76-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>]. Phytochemical characterization studies are limited in Rwanda, which is somewhat problematic as they are crucial to the discovery of new drugs from medicinal plants [<xref rid="B34-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>]. This study highlighted the studied phytochemicals with anti-diarrhea activities. Flavonoids are reported to have anti-diarrheal activity by promoting water and ion reabsorption in the colon [<xref rid="B61-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>], while 8(14),15-sandaracopimaradiene-7α,18-diol proved to be anti-helminthic [<xref rid="B59-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>]. This shows that new drugs can be made from those plants which would help in curbing the threat of antimicrobial resistance. However, further investigations need to be carried out to elucidate the in vivo efficacy, safety, mechanisms of action, and clinical trials [<xref rid="B74-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>,<xref rid="B77-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>]. This would pave the way for the integration of traditional medicine into national healthcare systems and reduce the high price of existing antimicrobials. </p><p>Efforts to preserve endangered species such as <italic toggle="yes">Prinus africana,</italic>
<italic toggle="yes">Euphorbia tirucalli</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Erythrina abyssinica</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Ficus thonningii</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Dracaena afromontana</italic> are important based on their cultural, social, ecological, and medicinal values [<xref rid="B8-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>,<xref rid="B38-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>,<xref rid="B78-antibiotics-10-01231" ref-type="bibr">78</xref>].
