The mTHPC Stability Properties and Reactive Oxygen Species’ (ROS) Generation Disturbs Mitochondrial Membrane Potential (<sup>∆</sup>ψ<sub>m</sub>)</title><p>Photostability is a central characteristic when tracking photosensitizers in vitro and in vivo. Furthermore, photostability is a crucial parameter for developing fluorescent photosensitizers because high photostability allows the phototherapy process to endure high-intensity laser scanning while lasting a long period with reduced photobleaching [<xref rid="B61-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>]. Continuous high-intensity bleaching by laser scanning microscopy was used to quantitatively measure the photostability of both the free mTHPC and AuNP–mTHPC complex.</p><p>As shown in <xref rid="ijms-23-02286-f004" ref-type="fig">Figure 4</xref>A, the signal loss was &lt;20% for the AuNP–mTHPC complex during the initial 15 bleaches upon excitation at 561 nm. In contrast, more than 40% fluorescence signal loss was observed after the 15th bleaching iteration for the free mTHPC. Finally, by 105 bleaching iterations the signal intensity was more than double for the AuNP–mTHPC complex (25%) in comparison to the free mTHPC (10%). These results showed improved photostability for the AuNP–mTHPC complex relative to the free photosensitizer.</p><p>The intracellular ROS generation by AuNP–mTHPC in SH-SY5Y cells upon light irradiation was then assessed using 2′,7′-dichlorofuorescin diacetate (DCFDA) as an ROS indicator. Following incubation with AuNP–mTHPC and irradiation with a red light (6 mW/cm<sup>2</sup>) for 4 min, SH-SY5Y cells were examined using microscopy. As shown in <xref rid="ijms-23-02286-f004" ref-type="fig">Figure 4</xref>B, significant green fluorescence was found in SH-SY5Y cells upon irradiation. This was due to the DCFDA oxidation by the ROS produced from the AuNP–mTHPC, in comparison to particularly weak green fluorescence in cells without light irradiation. All the above results suggested that the AuNP–mTHPC is photostable in living SH-SY5Y cells and can be used for PDT because of excessive ROS productivity. As we know, singlet oxygen is highly reactive and damaging to membranes, particularly in the mitochondria [<xref rid="B62-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>,<xref rid="B63-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>,<xref rid="B64-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>]. Likewise, our AuNP–mTHPC complex concentrates in the cytoplasm and the mitochondria. Thus, we postulated that the increased production of singlet oxygen in mitochondria will cause damage to its membranes. To test this hypothesis, we measured the mitochondria membrane potential (<sup>∆</sup>ψ<sub>m</sub>) by studying the effect of the probe accumulation in mitochondria on its membrane potential using tetramethylrhodamine ethyl ester (TMRE) as an indicator [<xref rid="B65-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>].</p><p>TMRE is a fluorescent lipophilic cationic dye, which stains polarized mitochondria with high potential [<xref rid="B62-ijms-23-02286" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>]. It is important to note that, although both AuNP–mTHPC and TMRE have red fluorescence, there was no interference signal obtained from the probe upon excitation at 560 nm.</p><p>In these experiments, SH-SY5Y cells were initially incubated with AuNP–mTHPC followed by PDT treatment. This was followed by co-staining with TMRE, and the confocal images are shown in <xref rid="ijms-23-02286-f004" ref-type="fig">Figure 4</xref>C. PDT-treated SH-SY5Y cells showed a decrease in mitochondrial membrane potential as measured by a tetramethylrhodamine ethyl ester (TMRE) fluorescent probe after 3 h. This suggested a disruption of mitochondrial function, whereas cyan fluorescence signal was observed for cells without light irradiation. These results indicated that irradiating the AuNP–mTHPC can depolarize the mitochondrial membrane potential and exert applicable cell death.</p></sec><sec id="sec2dot5-ijms-23-02286"><title>2.5.
