Novel Xanthones</title><p>Xanthones are polyphenols that belong to the flavone subgroup. Many potent anticancer agents have been reported from this group (as shown in <xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f005" ref-type="fig">Figure 5</xref>A,B). The genus Garciniaea produces many anticancer xanthones [<xref rid="B77-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>,<xref rid="B78-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">78</xref>,<xref rid="B79-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">79</xref>,<xref rid="B80-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>]. Compounds <bold>24</bold>, <bold>25</bold>, and <bold>26</bold> (<xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f005" ref-type="fig">Figure 5</xref>A) are new xanthones that have not received due attention, though they have potent anticancer activities. These compounds were purified from <italic toggle="yes">Garcinia hanburyi</italic> and were reported to show cytotoxicity on cervical carcinoma cells with IC50 values of 3.82, 2.11, and 1.73 µM, respectively [<xref rid="B79-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">79</xref>]. A caged-prenylxanthone (compound <bold>27</bold>) isolated from <italic toggle="yes">Garcinia bracteata</italic> showed cytotoxic effects against a panel of cancer cell lines including A549, MCF7, SMMC-7721, SW480, and HL-60 cells at IC50 values ranging between 2.02–3.25 µM [<xref rid="B81-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>]. Compound <bold>27</bold> has been shown to induce growth inhibition via apoptosis and inhibited autophagy flux in A549 and HeLa cells [<xref rid="B80-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>]. Compound <bold>27</bold> also inhibited the growth of A549 and HeLa xenograft in mice a little bit higher than the taxol-positive control via the upregulation of muscleblind-like 2 (MBNL2) and CELF 6 RNA-binding proteins [<xref rid="B82-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">82</xref>,<xref rid="B83-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">83</xref>].</p><p>Some new xanthones from <italic toggle="yes">Garcinia oligantha</italic> were reported to be cytotoxic against many cancer cell lines [<xref rid="B84-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">84</xref>,<xref rid="B85-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">85</xref>,<xref rid="B86-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>,<xref rid="B87-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">87</xref>]. Compounds <bold>28</bold>–<bold>38</bold> (<xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f005" ref-type="fig">Figure 5</xref>A,B) had IC<sub>50</sub> values ranging from 1.52–&lt;20 µM against A549, HepG2, HT-29, PC, HL-7702, and HeLa cells [<xref rid="B86-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>]. Compound <bold>35</bold> (<xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f005" ref-type="fig">Figure 5</xref>A) showed cytotoxic activities against quiescent prostate cancer cells (LNCaP) that are insensitive to Taxol at 20 µM [<xref rid="B84-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">84</xref>]. Xanthones from other plants source include a novel diprenylated xanthone. Compound <bold>39</bold> (<xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f005" ref-type="fig">Figure 5</xref>B) from <italic toggle="yes">Calophyllum soulattri</italic> was reported to have moderately higher antiproliferative effects across nine different cancer cell lines than the positive controls quercetin and kaempferol (IC<sub>50</sub> values from 9.2–22.10 µM, see <xref rid="app1-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="app">Supplementary Table S1</xref>) [<xref rid="B88-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">88</xref>].
