Low-Level Laser Therapy (LLLT)</title><p>LLLT is considered a non-specific therapeutic factor that aims to photochemically activate biochemical processes within biological tissues. This modulation occurs without inducing thermal damage or other structural changes. Essentially, it acts as an optical catalyst for cellular biochemical activity, thereby enhancing the physiological functions of the entire organism, including the neuroendocrine, endocrine, immune, and vascular systems [<xref rid="B3-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B128-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">128</xref>]. This modulation occurs without inducing thermal damage and other structure changes, which is remarkable and distinguishes LLLT from high-intensity laser applications used in surgical contexts. Consequently, LLLT has a wide range of applications in rehabilitative medicine. As the research into this method progresses and the experience in treating various diseases increases, the use of LLLT is expanding.</p><p>LLLT has shown significant potential in treating chronic pain [<xref rid="B95-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>,<xref rid="B129-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">129</xref>], promoting tissue repair [<xref rid="B3-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B130-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">130</xref>], and modulating inflammation [<xref rid="B131-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">131</xref>]. It is a treatment in which the light energy absorbed and scattered by the body does not increase its temperature by more than 1 °C. Exposure to LLLT (monochromatic, polarised, coherent light with a radiant power of several milliwatts concentrated within a few microns) results in optical effects typically observed when light passes through an inhomogeneous medium in a biological environment [<xref rid="B132-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">132</xref>]. These optical interactions include scattering, refraction, and selective absorption, influencing the depth and distribution of photobiological effects.</p><p>LLLT uses low-intensity laser light, typically ranging from one to one thousand milliwatts. It is commonly used for its tissue-healing, pain-relieving, and anti-inflammatory properties [<xref rid="B133-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">133</xref>,<xref rid="B134-ijms-27-02078" ref-type="bibr">134</xref>].
