Hu and colleagues [<xref rid="B31" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>] also found an increase in cytochrome c oxidase activity and concluded there was a cascade of reactions which altered cellular homeostasis. The effect of laser irradiation on the mitochondria at a transcriptional level was also investigated, and there is evidence that that there is an upregulation of genes involved in complexes I, IV, and V [<xref rid="B33" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>] (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig2">Figure 2</xref>).</p><p>A second possibility is the localized transient heating of the photoacceptor which may cause structural changes and trigger mechanisms such as activation or inhibition of enzymes [<xref rid="B29" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>]. Another theory is the release of nitric oxide (NO) from reduced cytochrome c oxidase which reverses the signalling consequences of excessive NO binding [<xref rid="B29" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>, <xref rid="B34" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>, <xref rid="B35" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>], as NO in very low concentrations inhibits cytochrome c oxidase by competing with oxygen [<xref rid="B34" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>, <xref rid="B35" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>].</p><sec id="sec2.1"><title>2.1. <italic toggle="yes">In Vitro</italic> Effects of Photoirradiation</title><p>A number of studies, on various cell types, have shown positive effects of photoirradiation. Studies have been conducted on stem cells [<xref rid="B36" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>–<xref rid="B39" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>], keratinocytes [<xref rid="B40" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>, <xref rid="B41" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>], mast cells [<xref rid="B42" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>, <xref rid="B43" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>], fibroblasts [<xref rid="B44" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>–<xref rid="B49" ref-type="bibr">48</xref>], smooth muscle cells [<xref rid="B50" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>], osteoblasts [<xref rid="B51" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>, <xref rid="B52" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>], and schwann cells [<xref rid="B53" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>] to name but a few.</p><p>Impaired diabetic wound healing has been associated with impaired cellular function, and there is a decrease in cellular migration, proliferation, NO synthesis, growth factors, and collagen synthesis. There is also an increase in proteinases that degrade the extracellular matrix and collagen (MMPs) and cells appear to be stuck in the inflammatory phase of wound healing. The increase in oxidative stress also leads to increased cell death. Laser irradiation <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> has shown that these cells respond in a favourable fashion, even irradiation of diabetic cells (<xref ref-type="table" rid="tab1">Table 1</xref>). There is an increase in cellular migration [<xref rid="B46" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>, <xref rid="B47" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>], proliferation [<xref rid="B45" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>, <xref rid="B47" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>, <xref rid="B54" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>–<xref rid="B57" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>], viability [<xref rid="B45" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>, <xref rid="B47" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>], collagen production [<xref rid="B46" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>, <xref rid="B50" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>, <xref rid="B58" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>–<xref rid="B60" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>], ATP [<xref rid="B32" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>], mitochondria concentration [<xref rid="B32" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>], cytochrome c oxidase activity [<xref rid="B32" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>], NO [<xref rid="B61" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>], growth factors [<xref rid="B58" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>, <xref rid="B59" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>, <xref rid="B62" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>, <xref rid="B63" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>], and gene regulation [<xref rid="B33" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>]. There is also a decrease in MMPs [<xref rid="B50" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>], apoptosis [<xref rid="B47" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>, <xref rid="B56" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>, <xref rid="B64" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>] and proinflammatory cytokines [<xref rid="B47" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>].</p><p>Irradiation of hypoxic cells has also shown favourable effects, with an increase in ATP and cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) [<xref rid="B65" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>], proliferation [<xref rid="B47" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>, <xref rid="B52" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>], viability [<xref rid="B47" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>], transforming growth factor-<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>1 (TGF-<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>1) [<xref rid="B52" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>], intracellular Ca<sup>2+</sup> [<xref rid="B65" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>] and mitochondrial membrane potential [<xref rid="B65" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>], and a decrease in apoptosis and the pro-inflammatory cytokine tumour necrosis factor alpha (TNF-<italic toggle="yes">α</italic>) [<xref rid="B47" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>].
