Methoxylated flavones present in this species’ extracts (<xref ref-type="app" rid="app1-ijms-20-01067">Table S1</xref>) possess noteworthy cytotoxic activity, with IC<sub>50</sub> values lower than the ones of the standard compound used [<xref rid="B90-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">90</xref>]. Similarly, the butanol and hexane extract of <italic toggle="yes">E. indica</italic> inhibited the growth of human lung cancer (A549) and cervical cancer (HeLa) cells. Its hexane extract also induced apoptosis of A549 cells, indicating cytotoxic effects with IC<sub>50</sub> values between 202 and 845 µg/mL, in both cases [<xref rid="B207-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">207</xref>]. Similarly, the methanol extract of <italic toggle="yes">I. cylindrica</italic>’s leaves showed anticancer activity on oral cancer cell lines, inducing apoptosis of human tongue squamous cell carcinoma cells (SCC-9) [<xref rid="B208-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">208</xref>]. This extract also showed cytotoxic activity against leukemia cell line CCRF-CEM in a dose-dependent manner [<xref rid="B209-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">209</xref>]. These properties could be related to the cinnamic acids with anticarcinogenic activity [<xref rid="B98-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">98</xref>,<xref rid="B210-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">210</xref>] present in its extracts such as caffeic [<xref rid="B86-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>] and ferulic acids [<xref rid="B87-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">87</xref>].</p><p>Overall, five halophytic grasses were tested concerning its anticancer properties against a large array of cancer cell lines and promising results were achieved in all of them. Hepatoprotective Activity</title><p>The liver is of extreme importance since it plays essential roles in the regulation of homeostasis and is frequently a target of numerous toxicants [<xref rid="B211-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">211</xref>]. Although great advances have been made, in the field of hepatology, liver issues are still rising. In addition, only a few drugs with severe side effects are available for the treatment of liver disorders [<xref rid="B211-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">211</xref>]. To cope with side effects, there is a growing interest in the study of medicinal plants, and halophytic grasses may be the answer to these issues.</p><p><italic toggle="yes">D. bipinnata</italic> has proven to be hepatoprotective since its polyphenolic fraction was able to combat hepatoxicity in rats. This fraction is thought to protect the liver by free radical scavenging activity, ultimately leading to lipid peroxidation prevention at a dose of 100 mg/kg/day and 200 mg/kg/day [<xref rid="B212-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">212</xref>]. Once again, the active principles are not known and the components of this polyphenolic fraction were not identified [<xref rid="B212-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">212</xref>]. The methanol extract of <italic toggle="yes">E. colona</italic> also showed dose-dependent hepaprotective activity until 200 µg/mL in liver hepatocellular carcinoma (HepG2) cells [<xref rid="B213-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">213</xref>]. Similarly, the aqueous extract of <italic toggle="yes">E. indica</italic> showed hepatoprotective effects against hepatoxicity in rats with IC<sub>50</sub> of 2350 µg/mL [<xref rid="B214-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">214</xref>]. Rehman and co-workers (2017) demonstrated the hepatoprotective activity of methanol extract of <italic toggle="yes">I. cylindrical</italic>, which was confirmed by the normalization of plasma markers after treatment with this extract [<xref rid="B215-ijms-20-01067" ref-type="bibr">215</xref>]. Little information is known about the polar chemical composition of this species and therefore, no correlation between the phytoconstituents and the activity can be performed. </p><p>Hepatoprotective activity has been observed with methanolic and aqueous extracts, as well as polyphenolic rich fractions of these halophytic grasses. In general, this activity appears to be linked with free radical scavenging abilities of the extracts. Nonetheless, more in vitro and in vitro studies are required to achieve a clear conclusion regarding the active principles and mechanisms of action.</p></sec><sec id="sec3dot7-ijms-20-01067"><title>3.7.
