Because of their easy availability in the field and because of the perennial habit of the species, the Cacatceae (<italic toggle="yes">Echinocactus, Opunita, Marginatocerus</italic>) and Agavaceae (<italic toggle="yes">Agava</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Yucca</italic>) are the most conspicuous plants in most gardens, not only for ornamental purposes, but also, for living fences, food, sap, medicinal uses, etc. Almost 30% of the total flora recorded is given an ornamental use. Most gardens in the study area cultivate herbaceous, shrubs and trees, and are commonly used as food, condiments and medicinal purposes such as <italic toggle="yes">Bougainvillea spectabilis, Chilopsis linearis, Echinocereus platyacanthus, Justicia spicigera, Marginatocereus marginatus, Mentha piperita, Pelargonium hortorum,</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Washingtonia filifera</italic>, among others. Several different cosmetics, firming skin lotions and tonics and health hair products uses recorded in Rayones have not been reported for other areas in the state of Nuevo León, especially those species used for make shampoo or used as shampoo such as <italic toggle="yes">Equisetum leavigatum, Rosmarinus officinalis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Jatropha dioica, Loeselia mexicana, Simmondsia chinensis, Agave lecheguilla, Juglans major</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Cucurbita foetidissima</italic>. As far as we know [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B11">11</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B12">12</xref>], no other areas in the State of Nuevo Leon use wild autochthonous plants such as <italic toggle="yes">Chenopodium ambrosioides, Hechtia scariosa,</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Litsea glauscecens,</italic> and also one introduced species (<italic toggle="yes">Azadirachta indica</italic>) to make shampoo for lice control. Rayones is the one of the few municipalities where products manufactured from wild and cultivated plants are marketed on a small scale.</p></sec><sec><title>Uses in Rayones and other areas of Nuevo León and north of Mexico</title><p>One of the first plant collections of useful plant in northeastern Mexico (Coahuila, San Luis Potosí, and Tamaulipas) was carried out by Palmer [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B45">45</xref>], this set of plants is currently stored at the Peabody Museum (Harvard University), and several of the plants mentioned are used in Rayones in similar way. As in other parts of Nuevo León and north of Mexico, the peyote and its psychoactive compounds are used to heal arthritis, but also for its hallucinogenic properties [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B46">46</xref>,<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B47">47</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Echinocactus platyacanthus</italic> is commonly used to feed goats and to make candies, the populations of this species and the ones of <italic toggle="yes">Ferocactus histrix</italic> are severely impacted for these uses [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B48">48</xref>], currently, and both of them are endangered species in México. The quelites (<italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus</italic>) are widely used in Mexico as food [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B49">49</xref>], the quelites are a common food in the diet of the Rayones residents. The lechuguilla production [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B50">50</xref>], wood use [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B51">51</xref>], amole root used as shampoo[<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B52">52</xref>], ixtle fiber extraction [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B53">53</xref>], consumption of wild beans [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B54">54</xref>], laurel oils uses [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B55">55</xref>] recorded in the north, central and south of Mexico are also common practices of routine use in Rayones; all of them are similarly used in Rayones for same purposes.
