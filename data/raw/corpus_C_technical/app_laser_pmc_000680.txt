Gain- and loss-of-function experiments demonstrated that miR-193 levels regulate the proliferation of MSCs of both humans and rats; in particular, blockade of miR-193 repressed the MSCs proliferation induced by LLLT. However, this miRNA apparently does not affect apoptosis or differentiation. In addition, Wang <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>. found that miR-193 regulated expression of cyclin-dependent kinase 2 (CDK2). Bioinformatic analyses and luciferase reporter assays revealed that inhibitor of growth family, member 5 (ING5), was the most likely target of miR-193 to functionally regulate proliferation and CDK2 expression; indeed, the mRNA and protein levels of ING5 are regulated by miR-193. Furthermore, inhibition of ING5 by small interfering RNA (siRNA) upregulated both MSC proliferation and the expression of CDK2. Another miRNA, miR-335, has been shown by others to regulate the proliferation and migration of MSCs [<xref rid="b58-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>], so it is likely to play an important role in MSC proliferation after LLLT. Moreover, several studies have shown that LLLT also stimulates cell differentiation [<xref rid="b53-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>–<xref rid="b55-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>,<xref rid="b59-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>–<xref rid="b75-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>], and future work should reveal miRNAs specifically involved in mediating this effect.</p><p>Although some literature reported that tumor or apoptosis related miRNAs were induced by UV irradiation to cells [<xref rid="b76-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>–<xref rid="b81-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>], Gu <italic toggle="yes">et al</italic>. reported UV-phototherapy and its effect on miRNA expression [<xref rid="b57-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>]. They showed the effect of narrow-band ultraviolet B (NB-UVB) irradiation on miR-21 and -125b expression in psoriatic epidermis. Psoriasis is an inflammatory skin disease in which dysregulation of p63, a member of the p53 family that is crucial for skin development and maintenance, has been demonstrated [<xref rid="b82-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">82</xref>–<xref rid="b84-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">84</xref>]. Involvement of miR-203, miR-21 and miR-125b were implicated in the regulation of p63 or p53 in the pathogenesis of psoriasis. Skin biopsies from 12 psoriasis patients were collected before, during and after NB-UVB therapy. The p63 expression was not significantly affected, whereas NB-UVB phototherapy significantly decreased expression of miR-21and increased miR-125b levels. Since NB-UVB phototherapy is commonly used in the treatment of psoriasis [<xref rid="b85-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">85</xref>–<xref rid="b87-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">87</xref>], those results indicate a complex mechanism of p63 regulation, which merits further investigation in order to achieve better long-term clinical improvement.</p></sec><sec><title>2. Photodynamic Therapy and Its Effects on miRNA Expression</title><p>Photodynamic therapy (PDT), a class of laser therapy, is a photochemical modality approved for the treatment of various cancers and diseases in which neovascularization occurs [<xref rid="b88-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">88</xref>,<xref rid="b89-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">89</xref>]. The PDT process consists of injecting a photosensitizer, which selectively accumulates at the lesion site, followed by local irradiation of the tumor with light of an appropriate wavelength to activate a specific drug [<xref rid="b90-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">90</xref>]. Irradiation leads to the generation of singlet oxygen and other reactive oxygen species (ROS) [<xref rid="b91-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">91</xref>]. PDT is being considered not only as palliative therapy, but also as a treatment option for early-stage skin, lung, cervical and esophageal cancers, as well as basal-cell carcinomas. Currently, PDT has been approved for localized diseases and precancerous lesions, such as bladder cancers, pituitary tumors and glioblastomas [<xref rid="b92-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">92</xref>,<xref rid="b93-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">93</xref>]. Furthermore, numerous ongoing clinical studies have been designed to optimize the conditions for PDT; subsequently, PDT has been approved in several countries.</p><p>Upon absorption of one or more photons, the excited photosensitizer undergoes one of two possible reactions (type I or/and II) with a neighboring oxygen molecule, yielding ROS [<xref rid="b94-ijms-14-13542" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>]. These ROS oxidize various cellular substrates, affecting cellular functions and resulting in cell death.
