This study used a range of molecular methods, including sequencing of the rbcL gene and chloroplast DNA, to resolve infrafamilial and tribal-level phylogenies.<xref rid="cit0018" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>,<xref rid="cit0019" ref-type="bibr">19</xref> The application of next-generation sequencing (NGS) technologies, combined with analyses of nuclear and plastid DNA, has revealed previously unrecognized clades and novel character states, prompting the reclassification of several genera within the family.<xref rid="cit0025" ref-type="bibr">25</xref></p><p>Focused phylogenomic research on <italic toggle="yes">Poikilospermum</italic> such as comprehensive chloroplast genome sequencing of <italic toggle="yes">P. lanceolatum</italic> has generated critical genomic data for comparative analyses, improving our understanding of the placement of this genus within the Urticeae tribe.<xref rid="cit0026" ref-type="bibr">26</xref> Additionally, Sanger sequencing and target enrichment markers have enhanced taxonomic clarity within the Urera clade by addressing inconsistencies in earlier classifications.<xref rid="cit0018" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>,<xref rid="cit0021" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>,<xref rid="cit0027" ref-type="bibr">27</xref> Morphological analyses, particularly carpological research on seed and fruit structures, have complemented molecular evidence and contributed to refining the genus classification.<xref rid="cit0022" ref-type="bibr">22</xref></p><p>The integration of genetic, genomic, and morphological datasets has significantly advanced phylogenetic interpretations and taxonomic revisions of <italic toggle="yes">Poikilospermum</italic>. These results redefine generic boundaries within Urticaceae and lay a foundation for future analyses exploring species diversity, adaptive evolution, and the potential pharmacological relevance of the genus.<xref rid="cit0023" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>

The leaves contain high concentrations of flavonoids, including quercetin and kaempferol, which are primarily responsible for their antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties (Jumania et al, 2020). Additionally, tannins, such as catechin and ellagic acid, along with saponins, including oleanolic acid, contribute to the astringent and antimicrobial activities of the leaves. Phenolic compounds (gallic acid and caffeic acid) and glycosides (beta-sitosterol glycoside) further enrich the therapeutic potential of the leaves. Moreover, essential oils such as lupan-3-yl acetate, phytol, and vitamin E (tocopherol) have been identified and show significant antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory activities.<xref rid="cit0011" ref-type="bibr">11</xref></p><p>The stems of <italic toggle="yes">Poikilospermum</italic> species contain alkaloids and glycosides that are associated with analgesic, antispasmodic, cardiovascular, and antidiabetic effects.<xref rid="cit0017" ref-type="bibr">17</xref> High concentrations of tannins and saponins have been observed in the roots, contributing to their anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, immune-enhancing, and antidiabetic properties.<xref rid="cit0017" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>,<xref rid="cit0031" ref-type="bibr">31</xref> The essential oils and alkaloids present in the roots further augment their therapeutic potential and offer analgesic and antimicrobial benefits.<xref rid="cit0031" ref-type="bibr">31</xref></p><p>Similarly, bark is rich in flavonoids and tannins, which have antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, antidiabetic, and wound-healing activities.<xref rid="cit0032" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>,<xref rid="cit0033" ref-type="bibr">33</xref> Essential oils extracted from the bark have been reported to have additional antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory effects.<xref rid="cit0034" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>,<xref rid="cit0035" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>
