Polyphenolic compounds, such as flavonoids and tannins, donate hydrogen atoms to neutralize ROS such as superoxide anions (O<sub>2</sub><sup>−</sup>•) and hydroxyl radicals.<xref rid="cit0106" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>,<xref rid="cit0107" ref-type="bibr">107</xref>,<xref rid="cit0112" ref-type="bibr">112</xref> These actions are potentiated by the upregulation of antioxidant enzymes such as SOD, CAT, and glutathione peroxidase. Quercetin activates the Nrf2 pathway and stimulates the transcription of antioxidant response elements (ARE).<xref rid="cit0113" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>,<xref rid="cit0114" ref-type="bibr">114</xref> This dual mechanism of action reinforces the role of <italic toggle="yes">P. suaveolens</italic> in combating oxidative stress due to associated conditions such as cardiovascular disease, neurodegeneration, and carcinogenesis.</p></sec><sec id="s0008-s2004"><title>Anticancer Activity</title><p>Research on <italic toggle="yes">P. suaveolens</italic> shown promising anticancer activity, which is attributed to its rich phytochemical profile, including flavonoids (quercetin and kaempferol), terpenoids, and alkaloids. These compounds synergistically enhanced the antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and cytotoxic capacities of, making <italic toggle="yes">P. suaveolens</italic> them viable candidates for advanced anticancer interventions. The methanol fraction reported strong cytotoxic potential in the brine shrimp lethality test (BSLT), with LC<sub>5</sub><sub>0</sub> values ranging from 4.87 to 85.54 µg/mL, showing potent bioactivity.<xref rid="cit0001" ref-type="bibr">1</xref> Fetalvero et al reported an IC<sub>5</sub><sub>0</sub> of 26.58 µg/mL against A549 non-small cell lung carcinoma cells, confirming dose-dependent cytotoxicity accompanied by morphological signs of apoptosis.<xref rid="cit0115" ref-type="bibr">115</xref> Mechanistically, <italic toggle="yes">P. suaveolens</italic> induced cancer cell death through multiple pathways. Flavonoids neutralize ROS, minimize DNA damage, and halt tumor progression.<xref rid="cit0116" ref-type="bibr">116</xref>,<xref rid="cit0117" ref-type="bibr">117</xref> This is achieved through direct radical scavenging and upregulation of endogenous antioxidant enzymes such as SOD. Additionally, flavonoids donate electrons to stabilize free radicals (R•), forming less-reactive compounds. In parallel, bioactive compounds from <italic toggle="yes">P. suaveolens</italic> inhibit the NF-κB signaling cascade, a critical regulator of inflammation and cell survival.<xref rid="cit0118" ref-type="bibr">118</xref>,<xref rid="cit0119" ref-type="bibr">119</xref> This suppression decreases the transcription of anti-apoptotic genes, promoting apoptosis and arresting the cell cycle at the G<sub>0</sub>/G<sub>1</sub> phase.<xref rid="cit0120" ref-type="bibr">120</xref>,<xref rid="cit0121" ref-type="bibr">121</xref> ROS neutralization, NF-κB inhibition, and apoptosis induction <italic toggle="yes">P. suaveolens</italic> show substantial anticancer potential through these mechanisms.</p><p>Despite these results, the safety profile of this compound must be assessed. BSLT-based toxicity evaluations of the methanol stem extract obtained LC<sub>5</sub><sub>0</sub> values between 4.87 and 114.49 µg/mL, with fractions A, B, D, E, and G considered highly toxic (LC<sub>5</sub><sub>0</sub> &lt; 100 µg/mL).<xref rid="cit0001" ref-type="bibr">1</xref> These results necessitate cautious interpretation, because high in vitro cytotoxicity does not inherently imply selectivity for malignant cells. To ensure safe therapeutic application, further research must include in vivo toxicity profiling in mammalian systems, including histopathological and organ-specific evaluations as well as determination of the therapeutic index. These integrated pharmacological and toxicological assessments are important for translating the anticancer potential of <italic toggle="yes">P. suaveolens</italic> into clinically viable phytomedicine.</p></sec></sec><sec id="s0009"><title>Comparisons Within Urticaceae</title><p>Beyond Southeast Asia, several Urticaceae genera show pharmacological themes that mirror those reported for <italic toggle="yes">Poikilospermum</italic>. Across these taxa, flavonoids (eg, flavonols, flavones) and phenolic acids (eg, caffeic/ferulic derivatives) recur as dominant classes, with triterpenoids and lignans appearing in several species.
