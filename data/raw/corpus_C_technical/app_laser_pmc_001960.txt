2023</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><sec id="Sec1"><title>Introduction</title><p id="Par28">Medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw (MRONJ) is a severe adverse drug reaction associated with antiresorptive or antiangiogenic medication used to manage osteoporosis, lung, breast cancers, multiple myeloma, and cancer-related bone metastasis [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR1">1</xref>]. Patients are considered to harbor MRONJ if the following characteristics are present: (1) exposed bone in the maxillofacial region persisted for &gt; 8 weeks; (2) current or previous treatment with antiresorptive or antiangiogenic agents; (3) no history of radiation therapy to the jaws or obvious metastatic disease to the jaws [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR1">1</xref>]. This condition is usually challenging to treat and can cause substantial pain and reduce the quality of life. Also, the pathogenic mechanisms of MRONJ remain elusive. Currently, the proposed pathophysiology hypostasis of MRONJ includes bone remodeling inhibition, infection, angiogenesis inhibition, immune dysfunction, gene factors, and so on [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR1">1</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR2">2</xref>]. Most reported cases of MRONJ were caused by dental operations, like tooth extractions, intraoral surgical intervention, and mucosal trauma [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR3">3</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR4">4</xref>].</p><p id="Par29">Recent studies have suggested bisphosphonates (BPs)-induced impaired soft tissue healing as a potential mechanism of MRONJ. In 2017, Hasegawa et al<italic toggle="yes">.</italic> recommended the removal of any bone edges and mucosal wound closure as standard procedures in patients receiving bisphosphonates [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR5">5</xref>]. In 2019, we applied adipose-derived stem cells to prevent the onset of bisphosphonate-related osteonecrosis of the jaw in rabbits through transforming growth factor-beta-1 (TGF-β1)-mediated gingival wound healing [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR6">6</xref>]. It has been reported that surgery in the early stages can ensure favorable outcomes in terms of mucosal integrity and lesion downstaging [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR7">7</xref>]. Moreover, a few other studies suggested promoting primary soft tissue healing or closure after surgical operation to effectively reduce the occurrence of MRONJ [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR8">8</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR10">10</xref>].
