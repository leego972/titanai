Inhibition of α-Glucosidase Enzyme</title><p>Although many research groups have reported the potent antioxidant properties of <italic toggle="yes">A. saligna</italic>, the plant’s antidiabetic property is under investigation. Diabetic disorders such as type 2 diabetes (T2D) are driven by metabolic abnormalities connected to obesity and impaired insulin response, together with the rise of hepatic glucose production, thus ultimately disrupting glucose homeostasis [<xref rid="B98-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">98</xref>,<xref rid="B99-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>]. Previous studies have shown that compounds with antioxidant activity can improve glycaemic control in animal models of T2D [<xref rid="B100-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">100</xref>]. Furthermore, one known therapy for T2D is the inhibition of key digestive enzymes related to diabetes, namely, human <italic toggle="yes">α</italic>-amylase and <italic toggle="yes">α</italic>-glucosidase and their inhibitors, such as acarbose, voglibose, and miglitol. These synthetic inhibitors have been known to have adverse side effects, namely, hepatotoxicity and gastrointestinal symptoms [<xref rid="B101-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">101</xref>]. It would be beneficial to find safer and more affordable alternatives. Studies have revealed that the inhibition of <italic toggle="yes">α</italic>-glucosidase enzyme and antioxidant activity could be achieved using natural products such as flavonoids [<xref rid="B102-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>].</p><p>Buttner et al.
