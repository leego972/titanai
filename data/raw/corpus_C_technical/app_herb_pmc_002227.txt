# PMC: phytochemical bioactive compound anticancer — Overview

ref-type="bibr">59</xref>,<xref rid="B60-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>,<xref rid="B61-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>,<xref rid="B62-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>,<xref rid="B63-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>,<xref rid="B64-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>,<xref rid="B65-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>,<xref rid="B66-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>,<xref rid="B67-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>,<xref rid="B68-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>,<xref rid="B69-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>,<xref rid="B70-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>].</p><p>Across the literature, polar solvents such as methanol, ethanol, and water generally favored the recovery of hydrophilic metabolites, including phenolics, flavonoids, glycosides, alkaloids, and related phenolic derivatives, whereas less polar solvents more often enriched lipophilic constituents such as terpenoids, sterols, and other nonpolar metabolites. Intermediate polarity systems, particularly hydro-alcoholic mixtures, broaden the extraction window by recovering both polar and moderately lipophilic compounds [<xref rid="B6-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="B12-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>,<xref rid="B43-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>,<xref rid="B45-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>,<xref rid="B66-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">66</xref>,<xref rid="B69-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>]. However, broader recovery does not consistently correspond to stronger anticancer activity. Instead, the reviewed studies indicate that chemically broader extracts often improve metabolite coverage without necessarily improving selective enrichment of the constituents most responsible for cytotoxic or antiproliferative effects (<xref rid="molecules-31-01202-t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref>) [<xref rid="B3-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B5-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>], [<xref rid="B21-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>,<xref rid="B31-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>,<xref rid="B51-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>,<xref rid="B52-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>,<xref rid="B60-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>,<xref rid="B68-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>]. In this context, extraction breadth should not be interpreted as equivalent to biological potency.</p><p>A similar pattern is evident in multi-solvent and sequential extraction workflows [<xref rid="B3-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B5-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>,<xref rid="B10-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>,<xref rid="B21-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>,<xref rid="B31-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>,<xref rid="B32-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>,<xref rid="B51-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>,<xref rid="B52-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>,<xref rid="B59-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>,<xref rid="B60-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>,<xref rid="B63-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>,<xref rid="B64-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>,<xref rid="B65-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>,<xref rid="B68-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>]. This distinction is important as it shows that maximizing extraction yield and achieving selective enrichment are related but fundamentally different objectives. Accordingly, variability in anticancer response across extracts may reflect differences in chemical resolution rather than simple differences in recovery efficiency. Plant part selection introduces an equally important and often greater source of variability. Different plant organs accumulate distinct metabolite profiles according to their physiological roles, with aerial tissues more frequently associated with soluble phenolics and flavonoids, and roots, bark, rhizomes, and seeds more often associated with structurally bound or relatively lipophilic constituents [<xref rid="B1-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B12-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>,<xref rid="B42-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>,<xref rid="B45-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>,<xref rid="B65-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>]. As a result, extracts obtained under similar solvent conditions can still differ markedly in composition and bioactivity when derived from different plant parts. This source of variability is further amplified by developmental stage, geographic origin, climate, harvest timing, and post-harvest handling, all of which influence metabolite abundance before extraction begins. When considered collectively, the reviewed literature supports a hierarchical interpretation of extraction variability.
