Insecticide and Antiparasitic Effects</title><p>In 1995, Talakal et al. reported that EEXL possess anti-plasmodial activity against <italic toggle="yes">Trypanosoma evansi</italic> both in vitro and in vivo. The EEXL exhibited trypanocidal activity at all the four tested doses at 5, 50, 500 and 1000 µg/mL in vitro, and it can significantly prolong the survival period of the <italic toggle="yes">T. evansi</italic> infected mice at concentrations of 100, 300 and 1000 mg/kg [<xref rid="B99-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>]. In 2011, xanthatin was demonstrated to be the dominating insecticidal active compound against <italic toggle="yes">Trypanosoma brucei brucei</italic> with an IC<sub>50</sub> value of 2.63mg/mL and a selectivity index of 20 [<xref rid="B84-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">84</xref>]. In addition, Go¨kce et al. showed that MEX exhibited both ingestion toxicity and ovicidal activity to <italic toggle="yes">Paralobesia viteana</italic> with an LC<sub>50</sub> of 11.02% (<italic toggle="yes">w</italic>/<italic toggle="yes">w</italic>) [<xref rid="B100-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">100</xref>]. In 2012, by using schizont inhibition assay, the anti-plasmodial activity of EEXL against <italic toggle="yes">Plasmodium berghei</italic> was assessed, and it showed significant activity (IC<sub>50</sub> = 4 µg/mL) and high selectivity index in vitro [<xref rid="B101-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">101</xref>]. Later, in 2014, Roy et al. found that WEXL had distinct insecticidal properties against <italic toggle="yes">Callosobruchus chinensis</italic> with strong toxicity, repellent properties, inhibited fecundity and adult emergence of the insects at 1%, 2% and 4% concentrations [<xref rid="B102-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>]. Moreover, it is reported that EEX revealed anti-nematode activity against <italic toggle="yes">Meloidogyne javanica</italic> in inhibiting egg hatching and inducing mortality among second stage juveniles (J2s) [<xref rid="B103-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">103</xref>]. Furthermore, the effect of MEX on the mortality rates of <italic toggle="yes">Aedes caspius</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Culex pipiens</italic> were investigated, and the results revealed that the LC<sub>50</sub> values of MEX were found to be 531.07 and 502.32 μg/mL against <italic toggle="yes">A. caspius</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">C. pipiens</italic>, respectively [<xref rid="B80-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot5-molecules-24-00359"><title>5.5. Antioxidant Effect</title><p>In 2010, it was reported that CEXR and MEXR showed significant free radical scavenging activity by 1,1-diphenyl-2-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) method with LC<sub>50</sub> values of 10.28 and 40.40 µg/mL, respectively [<xref rid="B86-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>]. After administration of PEEXW (250 and 500 mg/kg, p.o., for 20 days), the contents of superoxide dismutase, glutathione peroxidase, glutathione reductase and catalase significantly increased in rats’ brain [<xref rid="B104-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">104</xref>]. Later, in 2011, Huang et al. found that WEX exhibited 70.6% to 76.4% and 35.2% to 79.1% scavenging activity on 2,2’-Azinobis-(3-ethylbenzthiazoline-6-sulphonate) (ABTS) radicals and DPPH radical scavenging in the concentration of 0.05–0.2 mg/mL; simultaneously, the reducing activity of WEX increased and liposome protection effect enhanced in a concentration-dependent manner with the same doses [<xref rid="B95-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>]. In the treatment with the MEXS (100 and 200 mg/kg, p.o. for 10 days), the contents of SOD, CAT, GSH and GPx were obviously increased in the diabetic rats’ tissues [<xref rid="B105-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">105</xref>]. Moreover, in 2011, Sridharamurthy et al. evaluated the antioxidant effect of EEXR and CEXR by the scavenging activity of free radicals such as DPPH, super oxide, nitric oxide, and hydrogen peroxide [<xref rid="B106-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>]. Results showed that the IC<sub>50</sub> values of EEXR were 29.81, 495.30, 395.20 and 10.18 µg/mL, respectively, and the IC<sub>50</sub> values of CEXR were 24.85, 418.30, 415.18 and 9.23 µg/mL, respectively. In addition, Kamboj et al. demonstrated that EEXL possessed strong scavenging capacity against DPPH, nitric oxide and hydrogen peroxide with IC<sub>50</sub> values of 85, 72 and 62 µg/mL.
