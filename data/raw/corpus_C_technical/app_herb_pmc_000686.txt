When three flavonoids isolated from <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic>, namely quercitrin, isoquercitrin and quercetin-7-O-α-<sc>l</sc>-rhamnoside (0.25, 0.5 and 1.0 mg/kg), were used separately to treat the liver injury in rats induced by CCl<sub>4</sub> and D-aminogalactose, the levels of AST, ALT and TBIL were significantly reduced at the three dose levels as compared to the model group [<xref rid="B32-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>]. Anti-Cancerous Activity</title><p>The anti-cancerous effect is a noticeable bioactivity for <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> reported in the last decade. <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> extract significantly inhibited the proliferation of human tongue cancer cell line TSCCa <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> in a dose-dependent manner by damaging the mitochondria and rough endoplasmic reticulum [<xref rid="B34-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>]. The extract of <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> significantly inhibited the proliferation of CNE-2 cells and HepG2 cells <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> by inducing cell cycle arrest, showing a dose-dependent response at the low concentrations of 25 mg/mL and 5 mg/mL, respectively [<xref rid="B35-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>,<xref rid="B36-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>,<xref rid="B37-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>]. Meanwhile, the aqueous extract of <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> displayed a synergistic tumor-inhibiting effect with 5-FU in mice at 3, 6 and 12 g/kg/day, administered 24 h after the tumor inoculation, once daily for 10 days, indicting its usefulness in antitumor therapy [<xref rid="B38-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>]. Using a serum pharmacology method, BEL-7404 liver cancer cells were co-cultured with the collected serum containing four fraction of <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> (ethanol extract, ethyl acetate extract, <italic toggle="yes">n</italic>-butanol extract and aqueous extract) at different dosage. All extracts showed inhibitory activity on the growth of BEL-7404 cell with inhibitory rates of 29.74%, 53.80%, 40.79% and 54.24%, respectively [<xref rid="B39-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>].</p></sec><sec><title>3.4. Antibacterial Activity</title><p>Antibacterial activity for aqueous extract of <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> was studied by the disc diffusion method. The results demonstrated that both Gram positive and Gram negative bacteria, including <italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Alcaligens faecalis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus subtilis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">E. aerogenes</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Klebsiella pneumonia</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Shigella flexneri</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Salmonella enterica</italic> ser. Typhi, <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus epidermidis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Streptococcus pyogenes</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Xanthomonas vesicatoria</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">X. oryzae</italic> pv. oryzae and <italic toggle="yes">X. malvacearum</italic> were inhibited by the extract, except for <italic toggle="yes">P. aeruginosa</italic>, and the minimum inhibitory concentration (MICs) of the extract was 1 mg/mL against all the test cultures used, except <italic toggle="yes">E. aerogenes</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">P. aeruginosa</italic>. This indicated the extract had a broad spectrum antibacterial activity [<xref rid="B28-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>].</p><p>Isojacareubin, the xanthone from the aerial parts of <italic toggle="yes">H. japonicum</italic> had an effect on methicillin-resistant <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic> (MRSA) with MICs/MBCs ranging from 4/16 to 16/64 μg/mL. When it was used together with some conventional antibacterial agents, namely ceftazidime, levofloxacin and ampicillin, the values of 50% of the fractional inhibitory concentration indices (FICI<sub>50</sub>) were 0.25, 0.37 and 0.37, respectively, indicating good anti-MRSA activities [<xref rid="B40-molecules-19-10733" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>].</p></sec><sec><title>3.5.
