making.<xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0119" ref-type="ref">119</xref>, <xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0120" ref-type="ref">120</xref>, <xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0121" ref-type="ref">121</xref> The lamina cribrosa has also been hypothesised to play a role in the pathogenesis of the disease.<xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0122" ref-type="ref">122</xref> New, high‐resolution OCT has been able to visualise the lamina cribrosa, describing parameters such as pore size and deformity.<xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0123" ref-type="ref">123</xref> The significance of both of these parameters is still not fully known, but provides an exciting area for future study.</p><p>Aside from the optic nerve head, OCT is also used for assessment of the anterior segment.<xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0124" ref-type="ref">124</xref>, <xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0125" ref-type="ref">125</xref> Anterior segment OCT has shown promise in identifying abnormalities in key features within the anterior chamber angle by highlighting eyes that can develop angle closure and glaucoma, particularly in high‐risk populations (Figure <xref rid="cxo12847-fig-0007" ref-type="fig">7</xref>B).<xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0126" ref-type="ref">126</xref>, <xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0127" ref-type="ref">127</xref>, <xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0128" ref-type="ref">128</xref> However, there remains a significant number of limitations in anterior segment OCT, including resolution, feature identification, obstructing structures and static viewing, for example, in comparison to methods such as ultrasound biomicroscopy and gonioscopy.<xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0129" ref-type="ref">129</xref>, <xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0130" ref-type="ref">130</xref>, <xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0131" ref-type="ref">131</xref>, <xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0132" ref-type="ref">132</xref> Therefore, similar to examination of the posterior segment, anterior segment OCT remains an adjunct to gonioscopy, the current gold standard of anterior chamber angle assessment.<xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0133" ref-type="ref">133</xref>
</p><p>OCT therefore appears to be an attractive option for visualising structural changes in a rapid manner, reinforced by recent studies that have provided precise visualisation of structural loss preceding significant visual field defects.<xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0134" ref-type="ref">134</xref>, <xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0135" ref-type="ref">135</xref>, <xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0136" ref-type="ref">136</xref> It is not surprising that OCT has become indispensible for clinical glaucoma assessment, especially when its speed, precision and relative objectivity contrasts so heavily with the arduous, subjective and variable task of automated perimetry.<xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0137" ref-type="ref">137</xref>, <xref rid="cxo12847-bib-0138" ref-type="ref">138</xref>
