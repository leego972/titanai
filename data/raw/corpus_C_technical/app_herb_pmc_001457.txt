Furthermore, the cytotoxic activity of the ethalonic extract of <italic toggle="yes">B. nigra</italic> seeds was exerted via the reduction in viability and clonogenic survival of A-549 and H-1299 cells, the induction of cellular apoptosis in a time- and concentration-dependent manner, and the increase in caspase-3 activity [<xref rid="B161-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">161</xref>]. Canolol at 100 μM concentration, isolated from rapeseed oil, reduced human colon cancer cell line (SW480) apoptotic death, and it was also found to be an effective anticarcinogenic chemical in the <italic toggle="yes">Salmonella typhimurium</italic> modified Ames assay [<xref rid="B162-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">162</xref>]. In addition, mustard seed emulsion was found to be able to attenuate 1,2-dimethylhydrazine (DMH) and azoxymethane (AOM)-induced mice colon carcinogenesis, by reducing growth and activating the apoptotic death of (SW480) [<xref rid="B138-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">138</xref>,<xref rid="B163-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">163</xref>]. Antimicrobial Activity</title><p>In addition to their agricultural and nutritional importance, numerous studies have highlighted the appreciable antimicrobial and fungicidal potential of <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds against various important pathogens, as they are a predominant source of proteins, polyphenols, glucosinolates, and essential oils [<xref rid="B6-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>]. RCN seed meal of <italic toggle="yes">B. rapa</italic> showed a large antimicrobial spectrum, mainly against food-borne pathogens, Gram-positive bacteria (<italic toggle="yes">Bacillus cereus</italic> (minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC)=32 µg/mL), <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic> (MIC=32 µg/mL), <italic toggle="yes">Enterococcus faecalis</italic> (MIC=16 µg/mL), and <italic toggle="yes">Listeria monocytogenes</italic> (MIC=128 µg/mL)), Gram-negative bacteria (<italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic> (MIC=256 µg/mL), <italic toggle="yes">Proteus mirabilis</italic> (MIC=128 µg/mL), <italic toggle="yes">Proteus vulgaris</italic> (MIC=128 µg/mL), <italic toggle="yes">Pseudomonas aeruginosa</italic> (MIC=256 µg/mL), <italic toggle="yes">Salmonella typhi</italic> (MIC=128 µg/mL), <italic toggle="yes">Enterobacter cloaceae</italic> (MIC=64 µg/mL), <italic toggle="yes">Yersinia enterocolitica</italic> (MIC=64 µg/mL), and <italic toggle="yes">Klebsiella pneumoniae</italic> (MIC=128 µg/mL)), yeasts (<italic toggle="yes">Candida albicans 10231</italic> (MIC=64 µg/mL), and <italic toggle="yes">Candida albicans 90028</italic> (MIC=64 µg/mL)), and molds (<italic toggle="yes">Fusarium oxysporum</italic> (MIC=128 µg/mL), <italic toggle="yes">Cladosporium herbarum</italic> (MIC=128 µg/mL), <italic toggle="yes">Botrytis cinerea</italic> (MIC=64 µg/mL), and <italic toggle="yes">Aspergillus flavus</italic> (MIC=128 µg/mL)). Individually, the best antimicrobial activity of RCN seed meal extract was toward <italic toggle="yes">Enterococcus faecalis</italic> (MIC=16 µg/mL) and <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic> (MIC=32 µg/mL) [<xref rid="B51-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>]. However, with <italic toggle="yes">B. rapa</italic> seeds, positive activity was found only against <italic toggle="yes">Plasmodium berghei</italic> infection in mice and <italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Klebsiella pneumonia</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Salmonella para-typhi</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Pseudomonas aeuriginosa</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic>, with the inhibition zone diameters ranging between 7 and 23 mm [<xref rid="B64-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>,<xref rid="B165-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">165</xref>].
