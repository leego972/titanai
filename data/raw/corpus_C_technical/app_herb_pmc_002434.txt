The exploration of anti-carcinogenic activities of <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> not only underscores its significance in traditional medicine but also presents an avenue for the development of novel therapeutic interventions in the field of oncology. Further research is warranted to elucidate specific molecular pathways involved and optimize the utilization of <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> in potential anti-cancer treatments. Due to the abundant phenolic content found in <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic>, the ethyl acetate extract from this plant has been noted for its ability to exhibit anti-carcinogenic activity against human malignant cell lines, particularly those associated with breast adenocarcinoma and cervix squamous cancer. This was substantiated by a significant decrease in cell viability (<xref rid="ref-65" ref-type="bibr">Valadez Vega et al., 2019</xref>). Additionally, the aerial portion of <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> is employed in the preparation of infusions, that helps regulate vaginal fluids, disinfect genital areas, and serve as a supplementary element in the treatment of uterine cancer.</p></sec><sec><title>Antifungal and antimicrobial activity</title><p>In Mexico, the hydroalcoholic extract of <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> has also been noted for its ability to impede the growth of key fungi (isolates 501 and 498 of <italic toggle="yes">Candida albicans</italic>, isolates 434 and 514 of <italic toggle="yes">Aspergillus fumigatus</italic>, isolates 1526 and 1591 of <italic toggle="yes">Histoplasma capsulatum</italic>, and isolates 168 and 167 of <italic toggle="yes">Coccidioides immitis</italic>) associated with pulmonary mycoses, a fungal disease characterized by lung tissue necrosis and the development of vascular thrombosis (<xref rid="ref-6" ref-type="bibr">Alanís-Garza et al., 2007</xref>).</p><p>Additionally, <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> exhibits antifungal activity against <italic toggle="yes">Curvularia lunata</italic>, a causal fungus of corn leaf spot disease, where it causes a significant growth inhibition (inhibition by 80%) when exposed to the extract of this plant (<xref rid="ref-27" ref-type="bibr">Hernandez-Rodriguez et al., 2018</xref>).</p></sec><sec><title>Herbicidal/phytotoxic activity</title><p><italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> demonstrates considerable potential in controlling common weeds that can adversely impact the growth of economically important plants. Extracts from various parts of <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> have been found to inhibit the seed germination and development of several common weeds like <italic toggle="yes">Poa annua</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus retroflexus</italic>, and a common vegetable, <italic toggle="yes">Solanum lycopersicum</italic> (<xref rid="ref-47" ref-type="bibr">Ping, Zhu &amp; Zhang, 2012</xref>; <xref rid="ref-53" ref-type="bibr">Shao, 2015</xref>; <xref rid="ref-80" ref-type="bibr">Zhou et al., 2021</xref>). The soil surrounding <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> harbors various fungi, including <italic toggle="yes">Aspergillus flavus</italic>, which exhibits allelopathic behavior toward <italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus retroflexus, Thlaspi arvense</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Poa annua</italic> by producing the mycotoxin, kojic acid, that affects the normal functioning of these plants (<xref rid="ref-56" ref-type="bibr">Shao et al., 2017</xref>). Furthermore, essential oils extracted from <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> have demonstrated efficacy in inhibiting the growth and development of <italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus retroflexus</italic>, impacting shoot and root length growth as well as seed germination. <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> also exerts phytotoxic effects on <italic toggle="yes">Arabidopsis thaliana</italic>, a model organism, by inhibiting root elongation and seed germination (<xref rid="ref-33" ref-type="bibr">Liu et al., 2023</xref>). Additionally, secondary metabolites such as thymine, adenosine, and cerevisterol, isolated from an endophytic fungus <italic toggle="yes">Purpureocillium</italic> sp. found in <italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic>, have been reported to exhibit inhibitory activity against the growth of <italic toggle="yes">Amaranthus retroflexus</italic>, through the inhibition of seed sprouting and a reduction in root length (<xref rid="ref-32" ref-type="bibr">Kuchkarova et al., 2020</xref>).</p></sec><sec><title>Insecticidal and pesticidal activities</title><p><italic toggle="yes">S. rostratum</italic> has demonstrated growth-inhibitory effects against <italic toggle="yes">Helicoverpa armigera</italic>, a pest that primarily feeds on Fabaceae, Malvaceae, Poaceae, and Solanaceae plants (<xref rid="ref-17" ref-type="bibr">Cunningham &amp; Zalucki, 2014</xref>), where it has also shown to cause an inhibition in the feeding process of this pest thus positioning it as a viable pesticide (<xref rid="ref-35" ref-type="bibr">Liu et al., 2019</xref>).
