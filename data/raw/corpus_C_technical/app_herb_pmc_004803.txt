In recent years, the root extracts of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> have been used as drinks, food additives, and in commercial pharmaceutical preparations offered worldwide [<xref rid="b10-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> plants contain polyphenols such as flavonoids, proanthocyanidines, tyrosol, and cinnamyl alcohol, as well as glycosides, organic acids, essential oils, sugars, fats, alcohols, and proteins [<xref rid="b5-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>]. The polyphenol content of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> is approximately 41.4 ± 3.41% [<xref rid="b11-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. Rosavin (such as rosavin, rosarin, rhodionin, rhodiosin, and rosin), cinnamyl alcohol, salidroside (<xref rid="f1-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="fig">Fig. 1B</xref>), and tyrosol (<xref rid="f1-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="fig">Fig. 1A</xref>) are the major components of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> plants [<xref rid="b12-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>–<xref rid="b18-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>]. Salidroside and its aglycone, tyrosol, are the major compounds of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic>, and the content of the two compounds are often used as a criterion in evaluating the quality of crude drugs of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> [<xref rid="b19-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>].</p><p>This review article presents an overview of the bioactivities of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> plants and their major components.</p></sec><sec sec-type="other"><title>2. Adaptogenic and antifatigue activity</title><p>Adaptogens are substances that enable the normalization of physiologic responses to various stressors, enhances work performance, and increases the stress tolerance of the body [<xref rid="b20-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>,<xref rid="b21-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>]. Researchers have categorized <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> as an adaptogen because of its observed ability to increase resistance to various chemical, biological, and physical stressors [<xref rid="b4-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="b6-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="b22-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>,<xref rid="b23-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>] and its performance-enhancing effect in humans [<xref rid="b24-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>]. A study reported that repeated administration of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> extract, SHR-5, exerts an antifatigue effect that increases mental performance, particularly the ability to concentrate, and reduces cortisol response to awakening stress in patients with burnout and fatigue syndrome [<xref rid="b25-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>]. The adaptogenic and central nervous system activities of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> may be attributable to its influence on the levels and activity of monoamines and opioid peptides such as beta-endorphins [<xref rid="b3-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>].</p><p>Administering 100 mg/kg of an aqueous extract of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola imbricata</italic> provided maximum resistance to cold-hypoxia-restraint stress-induced hypothermia and quickened recovery from the stressor [<xref rid="b26-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>]. In addition, the aqueous extract of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola imbricata</italic> exhibited dose-dependent adaptogenic activity [<xref rid="b27-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>]. Research indicated that <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> extracts have great utility in treating asthenic conditions which develop after intense physical or intellectual strain, including a decline in work performance, sleep difficulties, poor appetite, irritability, hypertension, headaches, and fatigue [<xref rid="b27-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>].</p><p>Intense exercise increases oxygen consumption and causes oxidative stress as a result of increased reactive oxygen species (ROS) production [<xref rid="b28-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>]. Exogenous antioxidants may prevent oxidative damage because they scavenge ROS generated during exercise [<xref rid="b29-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>–<xref rid="b32-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>]. Salidroside was reported to elevate exercise tolerance and increase the liver glycogen levels of rats after exhaustive exercise such as swimming [<xref rid="b29-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>]. In addition, salidroside reduced malondialdehyde (MDA) levels and enhanced the activity of antioxidant enzymes [such as catalase, superoxide dismutase (SOD), and glutathione peroxide] in the liver tissue of Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats [<xref rid="b29-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>].</p><p>The above mentioned studies suggested that <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> plants elevate work performance and resistance to stress, and that salidroside is effective in preventing oxidative stress following exhaustive exercise.</p></sec><sec sec-type="other"><title>3.
