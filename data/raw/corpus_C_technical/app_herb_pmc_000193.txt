In terms of selectivity, treating rosemary leaves with US (19.5 kHz,140 W) showed remarkably high (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.05) recovery of carnosic acid and rosmarinic acid contents (13%, 6.8% of the dried extract) compared to MW (100 °C, 20 bar) [<xref rid="b0150" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>]. However, MW showed tendency of higher (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &gt; 0.1) total terpenoid (28 mg/gDL) compared to US with 27.5 mg/g DL [<xref rid="b0150" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>].</p><p id="p0215">In a recent study, Nguyen, Gavahian and Tsai [<xref rid="b0325" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>] compared the effects of US (150 W, 20 min) with conventional (CON), high voltage electric field (HVEF, 4000 kV m<sup>−1</sup> min), HPP (300 MPa, 3 min) and their combinations treatments on Gac (<italic toggle="yes">Momordica cochinchinensis</italic>) leaves. The authors found that US resulted in the highest (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.05) total chlorophyll content (TCC) recovery with 13.67 mg/g DL followed by HPP (12.65 mg/g DL), and then CON (12.58 mg/g DL). Similar results were observed for Stevia (<italic toggle="yes">Stevia rebaudiana</italic>) leaves, in which, the highest TCC was obtained using US and HVEF treatments (20.7 and 20.4 mg/g, respectively) [<xref rid="b0565" ref-type="bibr">115</xref>].</p><p id="p0220">On the other hand, combined emerging technologies (e.g., US and HPP) are considered superior to individual methods alone. For example, the combination of US and HPP increased (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.05) the total flavonoids extraction from Gac leaves to 623 mg QE/100 g DL compared to US and HPP with 592 and 582 mg QE/100 g DL. Furthermore, the inhibitory effect of α-amylase of the combined technique was increased up to 37% compared to US and HPP with 34% and 29% [<xref rid="b0325" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>]. The better outcome achieved by the combined technologies can be explained by the more efficient disruption of plant cell walls by US and the improved diffusion caused by HPP that enhances the release of bioactive compounds compared to their individual potentials [<xref rid="b0405" ref-type="bibr">[83]</xref>, <xref rid="b0410" ref-type="bibr">[84]</xref>, <xref rid="b0570" ref-type="bibr">[116]</xref>]. A method combined US with MW confirmed that they could enhance the oil extraction from tea (<italic toggle="yes">Camelia sinensis</italic>) seeds. In that study, an oil yield of 31.52% was obtained under optimum extraction conditions of MW (440 W), US (550 W), at 70 °C for 38 min compared to MW and US methods (27.45% and 25.13%, respectively) [<xref rid="b0375" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>]. Similarly, Tzima, Brunton, Lyng, Frontuto and Rai [<xref rid="b0575" ref-type="bibr">117</xref>] found that PEF (5 kW, 1.1 kV cm<sup>−1</sup>, 30 μs), as a pre-treatment step, enhanced (<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.05) the phenolic extraction and their antioxidant potential of fresh rosemary and thyme herbs by US (200 W, 13 min). The reason could be due to the rearrangement of the phenolic molecules electric ion by PEF and PEF ability to electroporate the cell envelopes, which facilitate the improved recoveries of the US extracted bioactive compounds [<xref rid="b0575" ref-type="bibr">117</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="s0100"><label>6</label><title>New technologies combined US with herbs and their compounds</title><sec id="s0105"><label>6.1</label><title>Ultrasound with herbal phytochemicals in nanotechnology</title><p id="p0225">The application of US to incorporate herbal phytochemicals in nanotechnology showed promising potential to stabilize bioactives, improve functionality and bioavailability, and delivery. High-intensity US and clove essential oil were used for producing nanofiber hydrogel using cellulose nanofiber. In this study, US (20 kHz, 4 °C) strongly increased the clove essential oil entrapment efficiency by 34%, cell viability rates by 74–101% to human gingival fibroblast cells, water retention, and color characteristics of the prepared hydrogel [<xref rid="b0190" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Citrus limon</italic> leaves extract played effectively their role as reducing, capping, and stabilizing agent for forming silver and cadmium oxide Ag:CdO photocatalyst nanotubes and nanospheres [<xref rid="b0220" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>]. The phytocomponents of <italic toggle="yes">Citrus limon</italic> played an important role against radicals formed by US during nanocomposite formation [<xref rid="b0220" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>]. Meanwhile, Sathya, Saravanathamizhan and Baskar [<xref rid="b0225" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>] demonstrated that coriander leaves extract can be used as a reducing agent in iron oxide nanoparticle formation by US assisted technique. The authors reported that the formed nanocomposite had substantial antimicrobial activity against pathogenic bacteria such as <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Micrococcus luteus</italic> that was higher than iron oxide nanoparticle synthesized by the traditional method.</p><p id="p0230">Taha, Modwi, Elamin, Arasheed, Al-Fahad, Albutairi, Arasheed, Alfaify, Anojaidi, Algethami and Bagabas [<xref rid="b0100" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>] used US for synthesizing zinc oxide (ZnO) nanostructures by using <italic toggle="yes">Hibiscus sabdariffa</italic> extract as a reducing and a stabilizing agent.
