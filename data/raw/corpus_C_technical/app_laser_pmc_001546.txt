id="sec005"><title>Introduction</title><p>Despite continual advancements in medical imaging technology, several problems make noninvasive direct visualization of the spinal epidural space (ES) without radiation exposure difficult to attain. Complex bony structures with small access spaces limit the application of surface ultrasonography, although many studies have proposed solutions and various approaches have been evaluated to resolve these issues. [<xref rid="pone.0172149.ref001" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="pone.0172149.ref002" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>]. There is a high demand for a fast bedside, intraoperative, real-time imaging modality for use in clinical anesthesia and in the injection of blood patches for postdural puncture headache and so on.</p><p>Optical coherence tomography (OCT) [<xref rid="pone.0172149.ref003" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>], which does not involve any radiation, can provide high-quality real-time images with a high discriminative ability (15 μm or better). With OCT, it is possible to visualize the surface morphology and underlying tissue microstructures [<xref rid="pone.0172149.ref004" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="pone.0172149.ref005" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>]. Optical coherence tomography is clinically effective in many fields and in clinical scenarios such as heart imaging for coronary stent insertion [<xref rid="pone.0172149.ref006" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="pone.0172149.ref007" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>] and retinal examination. In our previous study, we examined the feasibility of using OCT to evaluate the ES and demonstrated that this method had very high sensitivity and specificity [<xref rid="pone.0172149.ref008" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>]. Other authors have also used OCT for detailed anatomical imaging [<xref rid="pone.0172149.ref008" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>–<xref rid="pone.0172149.ref010" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>]. However, no report exists concerning real-time <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> OCT visualization of the ES. In addition, no study has used OCT to demonstrate the blood patch process in the ES.</p><p>In this preliminary experimental series conducted on experimental piglets, we aimed to produce intraoperative real-time two-dimensional (2D) and reconstructed three-dimensional (3D) OCT images of the ES.
