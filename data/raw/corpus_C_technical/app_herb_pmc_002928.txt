In the Bible “<italic toggle="yes">Ohalot</italic>” and “<italic toggle="yes">Ohalim</italic>” are mentioned four times—three of them in relation to perfumes (Proverbs 7:17; Psalms 45: 9 and Song of Songs 4: 14). Amar [8: 156-7] concludes that the old Jewish interpreters agree only on the identification of “<italic toggle="yes">Ohalim</italic>” cited in Songs of Songs as <italic toggle="yes">Aquilaria agalocha</italic> Roxb. (= <italic toggle="yes">A. malaccensis</italic> Lam.). According to Amar [8:156], this identification is on a level of “high probability but not certain.” Felix [6:255] considers all the citations related to “<italic toggle="yes">Aquilaria agallocha.</italic>” But Zohary [7:204] considers only the Psalms citations, as well as John (19:309-40), as related to <italic toggle="yes">Aquilaria agallocha</italic> /Aloe vera. In the Talmud (Gittin 69b), “<italic toggle="yes">Illava</italic>” is mentioned as a medicinal plant. It is held to be Aloe vera by most of the old Jewish commentaries [4, I:150].</p><p id="Par36">“<italic toggle="yes">Oren</italic>”—<italic toggle="yes">Pinus</italic> or <italic toggle="yes">Laurus nobilis</italic>?
