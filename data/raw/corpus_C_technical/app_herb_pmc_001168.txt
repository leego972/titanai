Grape skin contains fibers, proteins, sugars, anthocyanins, flavonols, and tannins, with red grape skin potentially containing stilbenes (resveratrol), triterpenes, and derivatives of hydroxybenzoic acids [<xref rid="B150-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">150</xref>,<xref rid="B154-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">154</xref>]. Lastly, seeds, representing 2–5% of the weight, contain 40% fiber, 10% proteins, 10–20% lipids (mix of saturated and unsaturated fatty acids) and a wide range of phenolic compounds, sugars, and minerals [<xref rid="B150-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">150</xref>,<xref rid="B154-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">154</xref>,<xref rid="B155-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">155</xref>].</p><p>Grape processing residues contain a high number of phytochemical compounds of significant interest to various sectors. Waste from wine production contains various phenolic compounds, with higher concentrations of anthocyanins, hydroxybenzoic and hydroxycinnamic acids, flavan-3-ols, flavonols, and stilbenes [<xref rid="B169-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">169</xref>]. Montagner et al. 2022 [<xref rid="B170-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">170</xref>] conducted hydroalcoholic extractions of crushed Merlot grape seeds, yielding high concentrations of bioactive compounds, with TPC ranging from 418.30 to 1473.86 μg GAE mL<sup>−1</sup> of extract, flavonoids from 387.08 to 1000.63 μg catechin equivalent (CE) mL<sup>−1</sup>, and catechins from 0.14 to 0.59%. Ferreira and Santos, 2022 [<xref rid="B152-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">152</xref>], investigated solid–liquid extractions with grape pomace and seeds, obtaining phenolic compound yields of 18.40% and 17.40%, respectively, and lipid yields of 13.30% and 14.50%, respectively. Phenolic extracts exhibited higher antioxidant capacity and DPPH<sup>●</sup> scavenging assay than oils, with values of 90.80 and 87.50, respectively [<xref rid="B152-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">152</xref>]. Citrus Waste</title><p>Citrus fruits, belonging to the Rutaceae family, encompass a total of 27 different species, with the most important species being orange (<italic toggle="yes">Citrus sinensis</italic>), tangerine/mandarin (<italic toggle="yes">Citrus reticulate</italic>), lemon (<italic toggle="yes">Citrus limon</italic>) and lime (<italic toggle="yes">Citrus aurantiifolia</italic>) [<xref rid="B176-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">176</xref>,<xref rid="B177-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">177</xref>]. The cultivation origin of these fruits is uncertain, with one widely accepted theory suggesting that they have been cultivated for at least 4000 years in tropical and subtropical regions of Asia [<xref rid="B178-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">178</xref>]. However, research by Rouseff et al. 2009 [<xref rid="B179-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">179</xref>] has demonstrated that sweet orange originated in India, while trifoliate orange, along with tangerine, originated in China, and various other fruits have their origins in Malaysia.</p><p>Citrus fruits had a production of 170 million tons in the 2024 season, according to the World Citrus Organisation (WCO), with oranges (69 million tons), mandarins/tangerines (52 million tons), and lemons and limes (23 million tons) being the most prominent [<xref rid="B180-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">180</xref>]. China leads citrus production, accounting for approximately 27.3%, followed by Brazil and India with 12.5% and 8.6%, respectively [<xref rid="B180-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">180</xref>]. In the 2024 harvest, these fruits held significant commercial value, estimated at US$145–148 billion [<xref rid="B180-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">180</xref>]. Besides fresh consumption, citrus fruits are found in juices, preserves, jams, marmalades, kitchen seasonings, and essential oils, among other products. Approximately 40% of the global production is utilized by the processing industry, primarily for juice production [<xref rid="B181-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">181</xref>].</p><p>Overall, the reviewed studies suggest the processing industry of these foods generates a significant amount of solid waste, with approximately 50–65% of the fresh mass turning into residue obtained from fruit squeezing [<xref rid="B176-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">176</xref>,<xref rid="B182-molecules-31-00996" ref-type="bibr">182</xref>].
