Introduction</title><p>Skin wound healing is a tightly regulated and dynamic biological process comprising overlapping phases: hemostasis, inflammation, proliferation, migration, and extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling [<xref rid="B1-ijms-26-06232" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B2-ijms-26-06232" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>]. In the inflammatory phase, neutrophils and monocytes are recruited to the wound site, where monocytes differentiate into macrophages that clear cellular debris and pathogens [<xref rid="B3-ijms-26-06232" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>]. Migration of fibroblasts, keratinocytes, endothelial cells, and immune cells—alongside cytokine and growth factor signaling—plays a central role in orchestrating tissue repair [<xref rid="B4-ijms-26-06232" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B5-ijms-26-06232" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>,<xref rid="B6-ijms-26-06232" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>]. The proliferative phase is marked by cellular expansion, angiogenesis, and ECM deposition to restore tissue architecture [<xref rid="B2-ijms-26-06232" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>]. Transforming growth factor-beta 1 (TGF-β1) has been implicated as a critical regulator of macrophage-mediated repair and ECM remodeling during skin regeneration [<xref rid="B7-ijms-26-06232" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>].</p><p>Wound healing is profoundly impaired in patients with diabetes mellitus, a highly prevalent metabolic disorder [<xref rid="B8-ijms-26-06232" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>,<xref rid="B9-ijms-26-06232" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. Approximately 15–25% of individuals with diabetes develop diabetic foot ulcers—non-healing, chronic wounds that dramatically increase the risk of infection and amputation [<xref rid="B10-ijms-26-06232" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>,<xref rid="B11-ijms-26-06232" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. The pathophysiology of diabetic wound healing involves dysregulation at multiple stages of the repair cascade, often exacerbated by hyperglycemia, peripheral neuropathy, ischemia, oxidative stress, and chronic inflammation [<xref rid="B12-ijms-26-06232" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>,<xref rid="B13-ijms-26-06232" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>,<xref rid="B14-ijms-26-06232" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>]. These multifactorial impairments result in delayed re-epithelialization, persistent macrophage infiltration, reduced ECM production, and abnormal fibrosis.
