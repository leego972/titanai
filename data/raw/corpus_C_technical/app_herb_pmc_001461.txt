Toxicological Effects of <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> Seeds</title><p>Currently, special emphasis has been placed on the use of <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds in the food and beverage industry, as well as in nonfood uses as they represent a rich source of biologically bioactive compounds, strongly associated with broadly recognized nutritional and functional properties. Among these compounds, erucic acid, glucosinolates, and their degradation products, as well as allergens, have been characterized by undesirable effects on human and animal health, mainly when consumed in high doses and in concentrated or isolated form [<xref rid="B188-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">188</xref>].</p><p>Erucic acid or cis-13-docosenoic acid (C22:1) is the predominant monounsaturated fatty acid in <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds. The intake of high amounts of erucic acid damages the liver, kidneys, muscles, and testes. In animal trials, the heart is the most negatively affected by erucic acid’s harmful effect after either brief or prolonged exposure due to the accumulation of triacylglycerol (TAG), known as myocardial lipidosis. In 2016, the EFSA (European Food Safety Authority) set an acceptable daily intake (ADI) of 7 mg/kg body weight [<xref rid="B189-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">189</xref>], whereas, according to the Food Standards Code of Australia and New Zealand (FSANZ) and the Commission Regulation (EU) 2019/1870, a maximum amount of 20 g/kg (2%) in edible oils is imposed [<xref rid="B190-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">190</xref>,<xref rid="B191-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">191</xref>]. To ensure the safety of oils for both human and livestock consumption, antinutritional components of the seed have to be removed using the conventional breeding process through the development of canola (1970). Canola edible oil seed, with low erucic acid (less than 2%) and low aliphatic glucosinolate (less than 25 µmol/g), is considered one of the most popular healthy cooking oils for its low saturated fatty acid content, high monounsaturated fatty acids, and balanced content of omega-3 fatty acids. Additionally, to raise the nutritional quality and quantity of oil from <italic toggle="yes">B. napus</italic> L., different modifications involving biotechnology, agronomy, genetic interventions, and bioengineering processes have been applied [<xref rid="B192-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">192</xref>]. Despite the toxic effects of erucic acid intake on human and animal health, it is used in a diverse range of nonfood applications, including cosmetics, medicines, plasticizers, and detergents. <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> oils with high erucic acid levels exceeding 55% still receive interest for application in industry processes [<xref rid="B193-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">193</xref>]. In particular, to produce seeds with high oil for industrial uses, genetic studies of candidate genes and their regulatory mechanisms are being developed [<xref rid="B194-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">194</xref>,<xref rid="B195-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">195</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds have abundant proteins with a predominance of napin and cruciferin seed storage proteins, identified as major allergens in humans [<xref rid="B59-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>]. Despite the positive nutritional and antimicrobial effects of the proteins isolated from <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds, it has been proven in numerous clinical trials and case reports that the intake of mustard seeds or products made from them, even in small amounts, immediately causes severe systemic reactions, including anaphylaxis [<xref rid="B196-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">196</xref>]. With the lack of effective preventive treatment for mustard allergy, informative labeling remains the only solution for allergy prevention in children and consumers allergic to mustard protein.</p><p>Glucosinolates, mustard oil glucosides, are one of the most relevant biomolecules of <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> vegetables, responsible for their bitter and pungent taste.
