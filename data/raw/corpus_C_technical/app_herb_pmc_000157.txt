Some of these effects are discussed briefly below.</p><p id="p0230">Torachrysone-8-O-β-<sc>d</sc>-glucoside, a recently discovered compound in <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic>, induced a strong increase in the proliferation of dermal papilla cells and increased the hair-fiber length significantly at doses of 10 and 20 μM (<xref rid="bib142" ref-type="bibr">Sun et al., 2013</xref>). TSG (10 and 20 mg/kg/day, p.o., for 56 days) ameliorated diabetic nephropathy in rats. The therapeutic mechanisms of TSG on diabetic nephropathy involved in inhibiting oxidative stress, inflammation, and the expression of TGF-β1, which partly mediated by activation of SIRT1. These effects occur in part via the activation of SIRT1 (<xref rid="bib64" ref-type="bibr">Li et al., 2010a</xref>). TSG has been shown to attenuate reactive oxygen/nitrogen species (ROS/RNS) formation and to protect against ischemia/reperfusion injury. The neuroprotective effect of TSG is caused by multifunctional cytoprotective pathways (<xref rid="bib155" ref-type="bibr">Wang et al., 2009b</xref>). TSG (30, 60, 120 mg/kg/day) was administered to rats for 30 days in a cardiac remodeling study. In this context, TSG prevented cardiac remodeling induced by pressure overload in rats. The underlying mechanisms may be related to a decrease in the angiotensin II level, an antioxidant effect of the tested compound, the suppression of transforming growth factor-β1 expression, and the inhibition of extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2 and p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase activation (<xref rid="bib176" ref-type="bibr">Xu et al., 2014</xref>). Another study indicated that TSG exerted potent anti-platelet activity against collagen-induced aggregation. TSG is likely to exert protective effects in platelet-associated thromboembolic disorders by modulating human platelets (<xref rid="bib169" ref-type="bibr">Xiang et al., 2014</xref>).</p><p id="p0235">Importantly, emodin (IC<sub>50</sub> was 200 μM) seriously blocks both the binding of SARS-CoV S protein to angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2) and the infectivity of S protein-pseudotyped retrovirus to Vero E6 cells. Therefore, emodin may be considered a potential lead therapeutic to treat SARS (<xref rid="bib45" ref-type="bibr">Ho et al., 2007</xref>). Another study revealed that co-treatment with emodin (1×10<sup>−5</sup> and 1×10<sup>−4</sup>
 μg/ml) significantly prevented ethanol-induced developmental anomalies in cultured mouse fetuses by modulating hypoxia and antioxidant enzymes and attenuating the enhanced levels of TNF-α and caspase 3 in cultured embryos (<xref rid="bib192" ref-type="bibr">Yon et al., 2013</xref>). Emodin (1 μM to 10 μM) also inhibited tonic tension in a concentration- and time-dependent manner by suppressing the PKCδ-mediated inhibition of myosin phosphatase in isolated rat thoracic aortas (<xref rid="bib83" ref-type="bibr">Lim et al., 2014</xref>).</p><p id="p0240">A study that investigated the cerebrovascular protective effects of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> against ischemic brain injury used an <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> photothrombotic mouse model. Hexane, ethyl acetate and methanol extracts of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> (100 mg/kg) were administered i.p. 30 min prior to ischemic insult. The result showed that the hexane extracts induced a significant reduction in infarct volume and subsequent neurological deficits compared with the other extracts. This cerebroprotective effect is primarily mediated <italic toggle="yes">vi</italic>a an eNOS-dependent mechanism (<xref rid="bib62" ref-type="bibr">Lee et al., 2014</xref>).</p></sec><sec id="s0125"><label>5.8</label><title>Clinical application</title><p id="p0245">
<italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> is not usually used as a single herb for clinical application but shows better therapeutic effects when used in combination with other herbs. A previous study investigated the effect of Buyanghuanw Pill Jiajian Fang (BPJF, composed of <italic toggle="yes">Radix astragali</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Angelica sinensis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Ligusticum wallichii</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Radix Paeoniae alba</italic>, peach kernel, safflower, <italic toggle="yes">Lumbricus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Fructus Psoraleae</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Cistanche salsa</italic>) on patients suffering from PD. Sixty patients with PD were randomly divided into the treatment group (BPJF+Madopar group, <italic toggle="yes">n</italic>=30) or the control group (Madopar group, <italic toggle="yes">n</italic>=30) and treated for 3 months. The total effective rate of the treatment group was 87.5%, whereas that of the control group was 77.5%, and this difference was significant (<xref rid="bib201" ref-type="bibr">Zhang, 2008</xref>).
