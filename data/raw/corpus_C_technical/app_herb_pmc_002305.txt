Minor Compounds</title><p>In addition to polyphenols, terpenoids, and pigments, <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> are recognized as a natural source of various other compounds (<xref rid="nutrients-17-03382-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref>). These substances are typically present in much lower concentrations within the species, as exemplified by vitamins, minerals, biosugars, and other biological active compounds. The main biosugars present in <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> are hexoses and pentoses such as fructose, glucose, and sucrose [<xref rid="B53-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>]. These sugars are key components in cellular structures, such as cell walls, where they are associated with cellulose, hemicellulose, and pectin [<xref rid="B67-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>]. Furthermore, the concentration of these sugars has been shown to vary significantly between cultivars, as well as with growing conditions, suggesting that these compounds have an adaptive function in the face of different types of stress [<xref rid="B68-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>,<xref rid="B69-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">69</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> is also a source of fatty acids, mainly saturated fatty acids such as palmitic acid, tridecanoic acid, myristic acid, pentadecanoic acid, heptadecanoic acid, and stearic acid [<xref rid="B18-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>]. This makes <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> a matrix with low applicability for obtaining fatty acids, especially when compared to other parts of the plant, such as seeds or flowers, which have been shown to have a fatty acid profile with health benefits [<xref rid="B70-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">70</xref>].</p><p>Other minor compounds present in <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> include vitamin E, which has been shown to exhibit anticancer and anti-inflammatory effects [<xref rid="B50-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>,<xref rid="B52-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> are also rich in minerals, with significant contents of phosphorus, calcium, potassium, sodium, iron, manganese, zinc, aluminum, and copper [<xref rid="B53-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>,<xref rid="B54-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">54</xref>]. The concentration of these compounds is higher than that reported in other conventional vegetables (i.e., rye, barley, beans) [<xref rid="B71-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">71</xref>]. Moreover, its concentration can be increased by delaying the harvesting or by supplementing the plant with an aluminum solution [<xref rid="B53-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>,<xref rid="B54-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">54</xref>]. Regarding amino acids content, the most abundant amino acids in <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> are aspartic acid, glutamic acid, histidine, and alanine, whose concentration declines as the harvesting time is delayed [<xref rid="B53-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>].</p><p>Despite the relatively low concentrations of these compound groups in <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic>, they play a crucial role in the plant’s overall biochemical diversity and have attracted significant interest due to their potential antioxidant, antimicrobial, and anti-inflammatory properties. Although their levels are lower than those of primary metabolites, their bioactivity indicates a possible contribution to the plant’s defense mechanisms and metabolic flexibility. Further investigations are required to elucidate the interactions and synergistic effects of these minor compounds, as well as to explore their potential applications in pharmaceuticals and nutraceuticals.</p></sec><sec id="sec3dot5-nutrients-17-03382"><title>3.5.
