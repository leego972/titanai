Dose-dependent proliferative effects of IGF1 on cultured hippocampal neural progenitors require activation of the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway or G-protein activation through nuclear translocation of phosphorylated extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK)-1. Downstream signaling through the phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)-AKT (also known as protein kinase B) pathway, IGF1 signaling mediates an increase in survival of newly born neurons via the promotion of anabolic activity and glucose utilization, and the inhibition of glycogen synthase kinase 3b (GSK3b).</p></list-item><list-item><p>In the hippocampus, brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) activates synaptic plasticity and related processes, including learning and memory. BDNF signaling facilitates the proliferation and stimulation of dendritic development through its cognate receptor TrkB, which activates the MAPK/ERK and the PI3K pathways. Glutamatergic effects on neurogenesis depend critically on the expression of receptor subtypes during the various stages of neurogenesis and play a critical role in cell survival and synaptic development, which could be partly mediated through a glutamatergic-mediated enhancement of BDNF.</p></list-item><list-item><p>Wingless-type MMTV integration site-3 (Wnt3) is expressed in the hippocampus and regulates proliferation and neuronal differentiation. The canonical Wnt pathway involves inhibition of GSK3b, though noncanonical signaling pathways directly activate Jun-N-terminal kinase (JNK), or protein kinase C (PKC) and calcium-calmodulin-dependent protein kinase (CaMK) II, via a rise in intracellular calcium.</p></list-item><list-item><p>The transcription factor, cAMP response element-binding protein (CREB), is a downstream effector of depolarizing a classical inhibitory neurotransmitter γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA) that depolarizes newly born neurons, which is opposed to its hyperpolarizing role in mature neurons [<xref rid="B14-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>].</p></list-item></list></p><p>The local environment highly influences developing neurons, and many exogenous factors influence the rate of neurogenesis and the survival and integration of newly born neurons. Exercise, sleep, sex, environmental enrichment, and learning are all conducive to neurogenesis. On the contrary, stress, drug abuse, aging, and depression decrease neurogenesis in the adult brain [<xref rid="B14-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>].</p><p>In addition, age remains a prominent factor affecting neurogenesis. The rate of neurogenesis steadily declines with age, with stroke increasing the sharpness of that decline [<xref rid="B9-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>,<xref rid="B340-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">340</xref>]. An age-associated loss in neural stem cell number and/or activity could cause this decline in brain function, so interventions that reverse aging in stem cells might increase the human cognitive health span [<xref rid="B16-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>]. Dysregulated neurogenesis contributes to neurodegenerative diseases, including Alzheimer’s disease (AD), Parkinson’s disease (PD), Huntington’s disease (HD), and others [<xref rid="B353-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">353</xref>].</p><p>The functional relevance of newborn hippocampal neurons has been implicated in many processes, such as resilience to and remission from stress, pattern separation, memory formation, and learning, as well as in neurological disorders such as AD and PD [<xref rid="B16-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>,<xref rid="B354-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">354</xref>,<xref rid="B355-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">355</xref>]. Stress and exposure to stress hormones (glucocorticoids) decrease the generation of hippocampal neurons and increase cell death in rodents [<xref rid="B356-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">356</xref>]. In animal studies, stress negatively affects hippocampal neurogenesis [<xref rid="B357-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">357</xref>], learning, and memory [<xref rid="B358-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">358</xref>,<xref rid="B359-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">359</xref>], increasing cognitive impairments [<xref rid="B360-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">360</xref>,<xref rid="B361-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">361</xref>]. Stress attenuates the inhibitory hippocampal regulation of the HPA axis [<xref rid="B362-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">362</xref>,<xref rid="B363-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">363</xref>], and neurogenesis is required to maintain this regulation [<xref rid="B364-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">364</xref>,<xref rid="B365-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">365</xref>], which is associated with stress reactivity and mood disorders such as major depressive disorder.</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot2-pharmaceuticals-18-00261"><title>5.2.
