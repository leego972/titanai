# PMC: adaptogen stress response HPA axis — Overview

[<xref rid="B76-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>]. In children with ADHD, <italic toggle="yes">B. monnieri</italic> (225 mg/day for 6 months) reduces symptoms of inattention, impulsivity, and anxiety by 30–50% compared with placebo [<xref rid="B76-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>]. In elderly individuals with mild cognitive impairment (MCI), <italic toggle="yes">B. monnieri</italic> supplementation (300 mg/day for 12 weeks) improves Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) and Alzheimer’s Disease Assessment Scale–Cognitive Subscale (ADAS-Cog) scores by 15–25% [<xref rid="B76-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">B. monnieri</italic> exhibits anxiolytic effects comparable to lorazepam (0.5 mg) in models of generalized anxiety, reducing anxiety symptoms by 40–55% on the Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale (HAM-A) without sedative effects [<xref rid="B72-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>,<xref rid="B76-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>]. The anxiolytic mechanism involves modulation of GABA<sub>A</sub> receptors through bacoside binding to allosteric sites, enhancing GABAergic activity and chloride ion conductance [<xref rid="B72-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">B. monnieri</italic> also demonstrates antidepressant effects in the forced swimming test, reducing immobility time by 35–50% through increased brain monoamine levels (5-HT, DA, NA) [<xref rid="B73-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>].</p><p>The antiepileptic activity of <italic toggle="yes">B. monnieri</italic> involves inhibition of L- and T-type calcium channels and modulation of glutamatergic NMDA receptors, thereby reducing neuronal excitability and protecting against seizures induced by maximal electroshock (MES), pentylenetetrazole (PTZ), and picrotoxin [<xref rid="B72-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>,<xref rid="B76-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">B. monnieri</italic> also protects against heavy metal-induced neurotoxicity (lead, mercury, arsenic, aluminum) through metal ion chelation, increased expression of heat shock proteins (Hsp70, Hsp90), and activation of autophagy pathways [<xref rid="B72-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>,<xref rid="B75-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>]. Collectively, these multitarget mechanisms confirm the adaptogenic and nootropic properties of <italic toggle="yes">Bacopa monnieri</italic> in the prevention and treatment of neurodegenerative diseases [<xref rid="B75-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>,<xref rid="B76-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>].</p><p>Comparative analysis indicates that all described adaptogens modulate key signaling pathways involved in the regulation of oxidative stress and cellular stress responses, particularly Nrf2/NF-κB and PI3K/Akt [<xref rid="B3-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B4-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B8-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>,<xref rid="B29-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>,<xref rid="B72-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>]. Despite their shared pleiotropic mode of action, they differ in their predominant molecular targets. <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Schisandra chinensis</italic> demonstrate pronounced mitoprotective effects, including protection of mitochondrial complexes and induction of Hsp70 expression [<xref rid="B8-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>,<xref rid="B14-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>,<xref rid="B16-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Withania somnifera</italic> primarily affects transcriptional and enzymatic regulation through modulation of PDE4D and transcription factors such as NF-κB and Nrf2 [<xref rid="B38-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>,<xref rid="B41-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Panax ginseng</italic> influences glucocorticoid receptor sensitivity by inhibiting FKBP51 and directly modulating GR activity [<xref rid="B54-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">54</xref>,<xref rid="B55-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>]. In contrast, <italic toggle="yes">Bacopa monnieri</italic> exerts a predominant effect on cholinergic neurotransmission via activation of choline acetyltransferase (ChAT) and inhibition of acetylcholinesterase (AChE), resulting in improved cognitive function [<xref rid="B72-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>,<xref rid="B76-nutrients-18-00931" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>].</p><p>To facilitate comparison of the dominant molecular mechanisms of the analyzed adaptogens, the key bioactive compounds, molecular targets and signaling pathways described in this section are summarized in <xref rid="nutrients-18-00931-t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref>.</p></sec></sec><sec sec-type="discussion" id="sec4-nutrients-18-00931"><title>4.
