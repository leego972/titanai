Title: Characterization of Micro/Nano Electro‐Mechanical Systems (MEMS/NEMS)

Abstract:
For future applications of Micro/Nano Electro-Mechanical Systems (MEMS/NEMS) devices, most components require their structure to be designed on the nano-scale in order to provide the required physical and mechanical properties. The elemental size for MEMS/NEMS devices is considered to be 1/1000 to 1/10 000 that of traditional structures. This down sizing can cause many difficulties, known as ‘size effects’, which result from, Preparation process (layered production of films), Handling and fixing methods (too small for traditional machining and assembling methods), Accuracy of dimensions (nano-metre accuracy required), Accuracy of measurement method (limit of measurement accuracy is 1/1000 that of the component size), Surface effect (high surface/volume ratio), Materials strengthening methods (new methods required for micro-sized materials) and Local mechanical properties (must be homogeneous, as in nano-crystalline or glassy materials). These difficulties are mainly caused by the physical limits of both measurement and manufacturing methods as well as the tremendous chemical activities of the surfaces involved. In order to understand MEMS and these size effects, and also to help to overcome the difficulties associated with them, this special issue has been planned to include a wide range of MEMS characterization techniques. These include the measurement of basic physical properties of thin films (anisotropic elastic constants of thin films), mechanical properties (tensile, fracture toughness and fatigue) and the characterization of basic materials (thin films and wires of single crystal silicon, nickel, TiAl, amorphous Mo-N, Mo-Si-N, Ni-P and polymer). All the authors of the above subjects are active researchers in the field. It is hoped that the production of these devices will allow the comparison of the properties of materials of different origins and the processing methods used to produce them. These methods can then be used to provide new materials and strengthening methods to further advance the capabilities of MEMS devices.
