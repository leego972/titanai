Related skin pathologies, such as actinic keratosis (AK) on the chest, may be particularly problematic because of the potential for worsened esthetic outcomes, including hypopigmentation, uneven texture, and scars, with traditional aggressive treatments like cryotherapy or other destructive modalities <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0003" ref-type="ref">3</xref>. Novel treatments that resolve precancerous lesions while also improving skin appearance are therefore in great demand, but exact treatment protocols need to be further delineated.</p><p>Current light‐based strategies typically include a series of short treatments with either fractional laser therapy (FLT) or photodynamic therapy (PDT) <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0001" ref-type="ref">1</xref>, <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0004" ref-type="ref">4</xref>, <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0005" ref-type="ref">5</xref>, <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0006" ref-type="ref">6</xref>, <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0007" ref-type="ref">7</xref>. Nonablative fractional lasers are generally better tolerated and require shorter healing times than more intense ablative fractional lasers <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0001" ref-type="ref">1</xref>, <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0004" ref-type="ref">4</xref>, <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0008" ref-type="ref">8</xref>. FLT using a low‐energy, 1,927‐nm, nonablative thulium laser has been shown to improve the appearance of photodamage on the face, including the clearance of AKs <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0004" ref-type="ref">4</xref>. PDT usually includes the use of a photosensitizing agent such as aminolevulinic acid HCl (ALA), a combination that has also been shown to improve skin appearance and clear AKs from the face and chest <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0002" ref-type="ref">2</xref>, <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0006" ref-type="ref">6</xref>, <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0009" ref-type="ref">9</xref>.</p><p>PDT using a topical solution of ALA 20% (Levulan® Kerastick®; DUSA Pharmaceuticals, Inc., Wilmington, MA) and activation by 10 J/cm<sup>2</sup> of blue light (Blue Light Photodynamic Therapy Illuminator, BLU‐U®; DUSA Pharmaceuticals, Inc.) is Food and Drug Administration–approved for spot treatment of minimally to moderately thick AKs of the face and scalp or AKs of the upper extremities <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0010" ref-type="ref">10</xref>. This form of PDT has also attracted considerable attention for its use in skin rejuvenation of the face and other areas exposed to chronic sun damage, like the neck, décolleté, and the backs of the hands <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0006" ref-type="ref">6</xref>.</p><p>Although PDT was initially developed for the treatment of the face, it has been recognized that, because of anatomical differences in the target skin, specific adaptations to the treatment procedure may help to ensure efficacy is maintained when treating other anatomical areas <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0006" ref-type="ref">6</xref>. Most notably, chest skin has a thicker stratum corneum than the face, which may reduce the absorption rate of topical photosensitizers in the lower layers of the epidermis and dermis <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0011" ref-type="ref">11</xref>. Additionally, chest skin contains fewer sebaceous glands and other appendages than facial skin, which may result in slower re‐epithelialization and prolonged erythema after treatment <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0006" ref-type="ref">6</xref>.</p><p>These histological differences from the face suggest pretreatment with an ablative or nonablative fractional laser, which improves the penetration of topical medication by creating small channels that can act as conduits for topically applied treatment, may be a useful complement to PDT on chest skin <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0006" ref-type="ref">6</xref>, <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0012" ref-type="ref">12</xref>, <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0013" ref-type="ref">13</xref>. Fractional laser‐assisted drug delivery is increasingly used to enhance topical drug uptake in the treatment of a wide range of skin conditions, including photodamage, neoplastic lesions, scars, and infections <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0008" ref-type="ref">8</xref>. This approach may be advantageous given that resurfacing of facial skin with a nonablative fractional laser can itself improve the appearance of photodamage and AKs <xref rid="lsm23178-bib-0004" ref-type="ref">4</xref>.
