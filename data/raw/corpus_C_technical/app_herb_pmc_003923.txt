Chronic stress affects the functions of the nervous system; immune system; cardiovascular system; gastrointestinal system; and endocrine system [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref027" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>]. Cortisol is a stress hormone secreted by the human body in response to stress; homeostatic regulatory mechanisms maintain cortisol levels within a narrow range; the hypothalamus-pituitary-adrenal axis (HPA) regulates the release of cortisol. During stressful situations, the HPA axis gets activated and releases the corticotrophin-releasing hormone (CRH) from the hypothalamus. The CRH helps in the release of adrenocorticotrophic hormone (ACTH) by binding with the corticotrophin-releasing hormone receptor 1 (CRF1) of the anterior pituitary gland, and the ACTH stimulates the adrenal glands to secrete cortisol helping the body to combat stress [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref028" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0285012.ref029" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>]. When cortisol level in the blood reaches a particular threshold, it inhibits the release of CRH from the hypothalamus and is called the negative feedback mechanism. Cortisol provides more energy to the body by increasing the blood glucose level and is used to combat stress. Cortisol increases blood glucose levels by breaking down the protein and fat stores. Thus, the HPA axis protects the body during stressful situations and is a normal physiological function. However, long-term repeated activation of the HPA-axis results in the dysfunction of the negative feedback mechanism leading to increased cortisol levels in the blood, and a higher level of blood cortisol for a prolonged duration in people with chronic stress may lead to obesity [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref030" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>], poor immunity [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref031" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>], diabetes [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref030" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>], cardiovascular disease [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref032" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>–<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref034" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>], osteoporosis [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref035" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>], poor memory [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref036" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>], and depression [<xref rid="pone.0285012.ref036" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>].
