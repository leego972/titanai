Antischistosomal and Molluscicidal Activities</title><p>Sparg et al. [<xref rid="B75-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>] evaluated antischistosomal properties of crude extracts of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> against the schistosomula of <italic toggle="yes">Schistosoma haematobium</italic> with praziquantel and a culture medium blank as controls. The schistosomula were placed into a culture medium to which the plant extracts were added. <italic toggle="yes">Euclea natalensis</italic> was active, killing 66.7% of the schistosomula worms at a concentration of 3.13 mg/mL. The schistosomula worms that were placed in the culture medium blank survived between 12 and 24 h of exposure while the praziquantel MIC value was 1 µg/mL [<xref rid="B75-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>]. Ojewole [<xref rid="B76-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>] evaluated molluscicidal activities of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> by exposing adult <italic toggle="yes">Bulinus africanus</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Biomphalaria pfeifferi</italic> to sublethal and lethal doses of crude and aqueous bark, leaf and twig extracts of the species for a period of 24 h using niclosamide (Bayluscide<sup>®</sup>) (Coating Place Inc., Washington DC, WA, US) as reference molluscicide for comparison. The extracts demonstrated moderate to strong molluscicidal activity with lethal dose 90% (LD<sub>90</sub>) value of 50–100 ppm compared to the positive control, niclosamide (Bayluscide<sup>®</sup>) which killed all the snails at a dose of 1 ppm [<xref rid="B76-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>]. These pharmacological evaluations are of importance in the traditional use of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> as remedy for schistosomiasis in South Africa [<xref rid="B8-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>,<xref rid="B13-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>] and remedy for intestinal worms in East Africa [<xref rid="B5-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>,<xref rid="B16-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>] and Malawi [<xref rid="B28-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>] and South Africa [<xref rid="B13-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>,<xref rid="B14-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec5dot6-molecules-22-02128"><title>5.6. Dental Health</title><p>Sales-Peres et al. [<xref rid="B77-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>] evaluated the effect of an experimental gel containing <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> extract on dentin permeability. The study assessed the in vitro variations in hydraulic conductance of dentin after treatment with <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> gel and acidified fluorophosphate gel. The acidified fluorophosphate gel was worse for preventing dentin permeability (90.8%), followed by the control gel (77.1%), and the <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> extract was the most effective (66.0%). Therefore, <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> presented the most effective action to reduce dentin permeability. Sales-Peres et al. [<xref rid="B77-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>] revealed that <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> gel not only reduced dentin permeability, but also resisted posttreatment citric acid challenge without changing its permeability. This effect can be attributed to the naphthoquinone compounds present in twigs and roots of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> as the compounds result in the dentin tubule obliteration due to the formation of a protective layer on the teeth [<xref rid="B77-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>]. Similarly, Sales-Peres et al. [<xref rid="B78-molecules-22-02128" ref-type="bibr">78</xref>] evaluated the effect of <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> gel on the reduction of erosive wear with or without abrasion, in enamel and dentin. The authors carried out five-day experimental crossover phases with volunteers wearing palatal devices containing human enamel and dentin blocks. The <italic toggle="yes">E. natalensis</italic> gel was applied in a thin layer in the experimental group and was not applied in the control group.
