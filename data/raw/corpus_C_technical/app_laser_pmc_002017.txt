In our previous works looking at the influence of different power densities of LLLT at 635 nm and 670 nm on wound healing in normal and corticosteroid-treated rats we demonstrated that LLLT at 635 nm and 670 nm effectively stimulates wound healing by using higher power densities [<xref rid="B14" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>, <xref rid="B19" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>]. Histological analysis correlated with significantly increased wound tensile strength at 635 nm in a power density-dependent manner, whereas LLLT at 670 nm increased wound tensile strength by using a lower power density [<xref rid="B33" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>]. According to our previous study, LLLT has a protective effect against early inflammatory tissue response after ischemic reperfusion injury of muscle, with a high power density of 40 mW/cm<sup>2</sup> [<xref rid="B40" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>].</p><p>In our study we used a daily dose of laser irradiation of 5 J/cm<sup>2</sup> as a comparison of the effects of laser therapy in other studies with different doses and wavelengths, where a median dosage of 4.2 J/cm<sup>2</sup> was recorded [<xref rid="B12" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>]. It has been well documented that the He-Ne laser, using a dose of 4 J/cm<sup>2</sup>or 5 J/cm<sup>2</sup>, accelerates wound closure, increases collagen deposition, and has a stimulatory influence on wounded fibroblasts [<xref rid="B24" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>, <xref rid="B27" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>, <xref rid="B28" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>]. Yasukawa and coworkers showed that LLLT with He-Ne laser applied as a daily dose of 4.21 J/cm<sup>2</sup> significantly increased the tensile strength and inhibited inflammation and increased formation of collagen fibres and recovery in the continuity of tissues, compared to a daily dose 2.09 J/cm<sup>2</sup> [<xref rid="B29" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>]. A similar finding was reported in the work of Stadler et al. who investigated the effect of laser irradiation at 830 nm and a daily dose of 5 J/cm<sup>2</sup> on incisional wound healing in diabetic mice. LLLT with these parameters significantly enhances cutaneous wound tensile strength in this study. Stadler et al. used an equal daily dose as in our current study [<xref rid="B32" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>]. Yu et al. have shown that treatment with a 630 nm Argon dye laser at a fluence of 5 J/cm<sup>2</sup> enhanced the percentage of wound closure over time as compared to the negative control group in genetically diabetic mice [<xref rid="B31" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>]. In our previous studies we confirmed a positive effect of He-Ne, GaAlAs, and AlGaInP laser irradiation at a dose of 3 J/cm<sup>2</sup> and 5 J/cm<sup>2</sup> on primary and secondary wound healing [<xref rid="B14" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>, <xref rid="B19" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>, <xref rid="B33" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>, <xref rid="B32" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>, <xref rid="B34" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>]. In contrast, inhibition of the wound contraction under He-Ne and Argon laser exposure to 20 J/cm<sup>2</sup> was recorded [<xref rid="B24" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>], a smaller expression of collagen and elastic fibres with a dosage of 8 J/cm² compared with 4 J/cm<sup>2</sup>[<xref rid="B39" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>], and a decrease in cell viability and cell proliferation of wounded fibroblast exposed to 10 and 16 J/cm<sup>2</sup> compared with 5 J/cm<sup>2</sup> [<xref rid="B28" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>].</p><p>According to the present study, LLLT accelerated the inflammatory phase in nondiabetic animals with more significant difference by 15 mW/cm<sup>2</sup> power density. The anti-inflammatory effect of laser radiation in diabetic animals was observed by the power density-dependent decrease in PMNL/TM ratio with the best results using power density 5 mW/cm<sup>2</sup>. Many researchers confirmed the anti-inflammatory effect of laser radiation [<xref rid="B5" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>, <xref rid="B6" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>, <xref rid="B23" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>, <xref rid="B32" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>, <xref rid="B36" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>, <xref rid="B37" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>]. By contrast, in the group of diabetic rats an increased wound infiltration by inflammatory cells with less effectivity of LLLT was observed. Deceleration of the inflammatory phase is a negative consequence of diabetes on wound healing. The most probable explanation for this observation is that diabetes strongly decelerates the inflammatory phase compared to nondiabetic rats, while the effect of laser irradiation is not able to sufficiently improve the metabolic demand and affected nutrition in the first days of application. We have no data at our disposal to support this.</p><p>We observed that LLLT positively influences the proliferative phase of wound healing with significant acceleration of epithelization and collagen synthesis in nondiabetic rats and neoangiogenesis in diabetic rats.
