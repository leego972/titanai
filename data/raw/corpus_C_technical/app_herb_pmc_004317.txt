For example, Leman observed a stimulatory effect of a <italic toggle="yes">S.</italic>
<italic toggle="yes">chinensis</italic> preparation on the CNS after 16–40 days of treatment in 40 patients with asthenia and depressions of psychogenic or somatic origin [<xref rid="B164-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">164</xref>]. There was also an improvement in dark vision found in almost all patients. Twenty two patients felt pleasantly warm, became more energetic and physically active, whilst hunger and fatigue disappeared. In addition, their mood improved and night sleep was normalised. The authors concluded that a therapy with <italic toggle="yes">Schisandra</italic>
<italic toggle="yes">chinensis</italic> can be indicated in asthenia and depression of psychogenic etiology, so called “exogenous” (stress induced) depression, or states related to excessive fatigue, somatic and nervous exhaustion. However, they also reported that the therapy can only relieve symptoms in “endogenous” depression (organic etiology), asthenia, narcoleptic and amnestic syndromes. An advantage of treatment with <italic toggle="yes">S.</italic>
<italic toggle="yes">chinensis</italic> is the absence of serious side effects. Therefore, the tolerance to <italic toggle="yes">S.</italic>
<italic toggle="yes">chinensis</italic> extract is several times higher than the tolerance to caffeine or phenamine, and the effect is not reduced following a long treatment period.</p><p>Another study reported total recovery of psychosis following treatment with <italic toggle="yes">S.</italic>
<italic toggle="yes">chinensis</italic> extract [<xref rid="B182-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">182</xref>]. The authors treated 36 patients (19 with schizophrenia; six with reactive psychosis; four with alcoholic psychosis; three with depression; and four with psychopathy) with ADS three times daily with <italic toggle="yes">S.</italic>
<italic toggle="yes">chinensis</italic> preparations. All in all, total recovery was observed in patients suffering from psychosis, while no effects were seen in patients with diagnosed psychopathy.</p><p>Furthermore, a tincture of <italic toggle="yes">S.</italic>
<italic toggle="yes">chinensis</italic> was used in treatment of schizophrenia (79 patients) and chronic alcoholism (118 patients and 41 healthy subjects) in an open label observational trials) [<xref rid="B184-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">184</xref>]. The author measured pupillary and vascular (alterations of palm vessel volume) tentative reactions, and refectory reactions in order to evaluate the effects on the CNS. These reactions were comparatively suppressed in schizophrenia and chronic alcoholism. However, it was shown that treatment with <italic toggle="yes">S.</italic>
<italic toggle="yes">chinensis</italic> tincture normalised the reactions in the patients under study and activated reactions in healthy subjects. As a result of the treatment, patients became calm, sociable, and active, free of emotional tension and anxiety, and willing to work. Indeed, they showed excellent well-being, which was associated with a good mood. The author reported an optimal dose of 15–25 drops in simple and hallucinogenic-paranoidal schizophrenia, 5–15 drops in paranoidal schizophrenia, and 5 drops in catatonic schizophrenia [<xref rid="B184-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">184</xref>].</p><p>Furthermore, two open, uncotroled studies [<xref rid="B184-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">184</xref>,<xref rid="B185-pharmaceuticals-03-00188" ref-type="bibr">185</xref>] reported a somewhat unexpected finding in that there was a stimulatory effect in schizophrenic patients and chronic alcoholics. This was due to the ability of the <italic toggle="yes">S.</italic>
<italic toggle="yes">chinensis</italic> extract to increase the reactivity to insulin, sulphadiazine and apomorphine. Thus, administration of a <italic toggle="yes">S.</italic>
<italic toggle="yes">chinensis</italic> preparation, which was used in conjunction with apomorphine, frequently eliminated or decreased addiction to apomorphine.
