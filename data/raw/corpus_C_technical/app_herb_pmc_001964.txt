Elicitors such as jasmonic acid (JA), yeast extract (YE), or mannitol activate cellular defense mechanisms and stimulate the accumulation of bioactive compounds, providing a promising approach for the optimized production of high-value phytochemicals [<xref rid="B50-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>,<xref rid="B53-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>,<xref rid="B54-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">54</xref>,<xref rid="B55-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>].</p><p>In the case of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, this approach is particularly relevant, as roots are the primary site of biosynthesis and storage of triterpenoid saponins a class of compounds known for their anti-inflammatory, cytotoxic, and antihyperglycemic properties [<xref rid="B56-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>,<xref rid="B57-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>]. However, their natural concentration is relatively low, and the plant’s cultivation cycle spans three to five years, rendering conventional harvesting economically inefficient [<xref rid="B22-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>,<xref rid="B58-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>,<xref rid="B59-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>].</p><p>In the study by Lộc et al. [<xref rid="B60-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>], adventitious roots were induced from leaf explants of three-year-old greenhouse-grown <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> plants. The explants were cultured on MS medium supplemented with 2,4-D at concentrations ranging from 0 to 4.0 mg·L<sup>−1</sup>. The highest callus induction rate (100%) was observed at 2.0 mg·L<sup>−1</sup> 2,4-D. After four weeks, the resulting calli were transferred to MS medium containing 1.0 mg·L<sup>−1</sup> IBA and 0.1 mg·L<sup>−1</sup> NAA, which promoted robust root formation. The induction and development of roots followed a two-step protocol involving auxin-specific stimulation for morphogenic differentiation. Qualitative analysis confirmed the presence of saponins in the in vitro-derived roots, with a dry weight content of 1.67%, corresponding to 83.5% of the concentration found in field-grown roots (1.99%). The identification of saponins was conducted using phytochemical tests and gravimetric analysis.</p><p>Subsequently, Le et al. [<xref rid="B12-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>] evaluated the effects of three elicitors—JA, YE, and mannitol—on secondary metabolite production in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> adventitious root cultures. Roots obtained through NAA-induced rhizogenesis (2.0 mg·L<sup>−1</sup>) were transferred to media supplemented with varying concentrations of elicitors: JA (0.5, 1.0, and 2.5 mM), YE (100, 300, and 500 mg·L<sup>−1</sup>), and mannitol (50, 100, and 150 mM). The highest total saponin content (167.19 ± 3.29 mg·L<sup>−1</sup> extract, expressed as aescin equivalents) was recorded in cultures treated with 2.5 mM JA, representing more than a threefold increase compared to the control (54.08 mg·L<sup>−1</sup>) and surpassing the levels found in wild-type roots (137.37 mg·L<sup>−1</sup>). Saponins were extracted using ethanol-based solvent systems and quantified spectrophotometrically via vanillin–sulfuric acid assay.</p><p>These results demonstrate that elicitation of <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> root cultures significantly enhances the biosynthesis of triterpenoid saponins, offering an efficient and reproducible system for the scaled production of pharmacologically valuable compounds.</p><p>This approach ensures metabolic stability of the root system in vitro, reduces production time, and may serve as an ecologically and economically viable alternative to field cultivation.</p></sec><sec id="sec4dot2dot3-molecules-30-03460"><title>4.2.3. Cell Suspension Cultures of <italic toggle="yes">Polyscias fruticosa</italic>: Optimization, Bioreactor Scaling, and Production of Bioactive Triterpenoids</title><p>Plant cell suspension cultures offer a controlled and scalable system for the production of high-value secondary metabolites. Typically initiated from friable callus derived from explants, these cultures are maintained in agitated liquid MS-based media under regulated temperature and photoperiod conditions. Agitation and aeration ensure uniform cell proliferation and support metabolite biosynthesis, independent of environmental fluctuations [<xref rid="B61-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>]. This approach has been validated in numerous medicinal plants, including <italic toggle="yes">Taxus</italic> spp., <italic toggle="yes">Panax</italic> spp., and <italic toggle="yes">Catharanthus roseus</italic>, for the production of taxanes, ginsenosides, and alkaloids, respectively [<xref rid="B62-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>,<xref rid="B63-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>].
