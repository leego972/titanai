It was observed in the current study that most extracts and/or oil with exception to the methanol extract had large zones of inhibition against gram-positive strains as opposed to gram-negative and thus, they were more potent towards the gram-positive bacteria. These results concurred with findings of similar studies by Mohammed et al. and Saleh et al. [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref011" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0272457.ref013" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>] which reported that gram-negative bacterial strains have an outer cell membrane inside the cell wall which can act as a permeability barrier. This can minimize the uptake of antimicrobial compounds such as polyphenols by the bacterial cell and hence, resulting into the extracts being less effective on gram-negative bacteria compared to their counterpart.</p><p>Evaluation of the antimicrobial activity of undiluted extracts and oil of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa seeds</italic> was supplemented by an additional disc diffusion test on nutrient agar plates using the same extracts and/or oil but diluted 1:1 in ethylene glycol. The diluted extracts and oil were also active against the tested bacterial strains with different efficacy as exhibited by their corresponding undiluted extracts of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa seeds</italic>, but it was noted that most of them had lower antimicrobial activity than that of the undiluted ones (<xref rid="pone.0272457.g006" ref-type="fig">Fig 6</xref>). On the contrary, <italic toggle="yes">E</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">coli</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">S</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">aureus</italic> remained unaffected by the diluted oil extracted with absolute ethanol and diluted methanol extract, respectively. In this study, it is speculated that these diluted extracts may be immiscible in the aqueous microbial growth medium resulting in lower or no inhibitory activity as opposed to the activity of undiluted extracts. Taken together these findings support the notion that <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds and their extracts possess significant antibacterial activity and they are consistent with those in a similar study done by Saleh et al. [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref011" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>], although with different efficacy. In this supplementary disc diffusion assay, ethylene glycol was used to serve as a mean to deliver or control the release of antibacterial agents (bioactive compounds) from the extracts of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds to leverage its benefits and provide an effective therapeutic treatment [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref058" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>]. The use of ethylene glycol to control the release of antibacterial agents from <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> extracts allowed the preparations to be directly pipetted onto their respective 5 mm discs without spreading all over the plates and this would reduce the chances for the zones of inhibitions to overlap.</p><p>The antimicrobial activity induced by various extracts and/or oil of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds against pathogenic bacteria was linked to the antimicrobial phytoconstituents present in them as well as to the complex interaction between those components and other components present in the extracts and/or oil of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref011" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0272457.ref013" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>]. As evidenced by earlier phytochemical investigations done in this study, methanol and aqueous extracts of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds contained therapeutically active components such as alkaloids, tannins, phenols and flavonoids or other phytochemicals which have been recognized as antimicrobial compounds and thus, their synergism mechanism against predation by pathogenic microorganisms can serve as a defense [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref016" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0272457.ref033" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0272457.ref059" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>]. In addition, the antimicrobial activity of the methanol extract may be due to o-xylene as its component identified by GC-MS analysis which has been reported to have good antibacterial activity [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref041" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>]. In studies of this kind, influences from the existence of some minor unidentified constituents with antimicrobial properties in the extracts cannot be neglected [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref060" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>].
