Title: Pääomarakenteen ja kontekstuaalisten tekijöiden merkitys eurooppalaisten yksisarvisten kasvussa ja arvostuksessa

Abstract:
This thesis investigates how different financing structures, particularly equity and debt, affect the emergence of unicorn firms in Europe. A unicorn is defined as a privately held company with a valuation exceeding one billion U.S. dollars, a rare and highly sought-after status that signifies exceptional growth potential. Despite the growing number of unicorns in Europe, there is limited research on how various financing sources, such as venture capital, angel investment, and debt financing, affect the trajectory of startups towards achieving this valuation. The study examines the key factors that contribute to reaching unicorn status, with a specific focus on how financing types influence both the time to unicorn emergence and the valuation level at unicorn status. It also explores whether industry and regional differences within Europe moderate these relationships. Using quantitative methods, the research analyses data on European unicorns that emerged between 2015 and 2025, sourced from PitchBook. The findings show that equity ratio and total funding received are the strongest determinants of unicorn valuation, while more diversified financing structures slightly accelerate unicorn emergence. Once capital structure is accounted for, regional and industry differences become statistically insignificant, emphasizing the dominant role of financing strategy.
