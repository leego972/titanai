Surprisingly, the results of our study are not in line with this anticipation because adaptogens down-regulate ERα gene expression. How to interpret these contradictory observations – neuroprotection accompanied by down-regulation of ERα gene expression? This case is similar to another, when it was expected that stress-protective adaptogens have to reduce cortisol, because normally cortisol is increasing in stress. Indeed, adaptogens reduce cortisol in stress (Panossian et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B98">1999b</xref>). However, adaptogens can increase secretion of cortisol/corticosterone when added to isolated adrenocortical cells (Panossian et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B98">1999b</xref>; Panossian et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B86">2007</xref>). Cell response (secretion of cortisol/corticosterone) to adaptogens and stress is similar – an activation, mild to adaptogens and much stronger in stress. However, stress response (secretion of cortisol/corticosterone) was significantly low in adaptogen pre-treated cells than in control cells (Panossian et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B98">1999b</xref>; Panossian et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B86">2007</xref>). Pretreatment with adaptogen, so-called “adaptive stress response” or “preconditioning” or “hormesis” (Mattson et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B71">2007</xref>), acting as a mild stress or “stress vaccine,” adapts the cell to stress (Panossian et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B98">1999b</xref>; Wiegant et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B131">2008</xref>; Boon-Niermeijer et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B12">2012</xref>). Similarly, it can be speculated that down-regulation of ERα gene expression by adaptogens is a signal for glia cell to initiate a feedback regulation of ERα. In a broader sense this concept is related to the inflammation, which is a defense response (“switch on” defense system) to cope the infection. In order to prevent an overreaction, a feedback mechanism of regulation of inflammation is activated, e.g., cortisol and anti-inflammatory cytokines are increasing (“switch off” defense system). Mild stress, in general, is a defense response in order activate innate immunity. In this context, adaptogens are natural substances initiating activation of the innate defense systems, including ERα as one of the component of stress system.</p><p>In addition to “classical” model of steroid receptor function, when the ligand-bound estrogen receptor regulates transcription of target genes in the nucleus by binding to estrogen response element regulatory sequences in target genes, estrogen activated membrane-bound CRα initiates rapid signaling PI3K/PLC pathways in astrocytes to indirectly modulate neuronal function and survival (Deroo and Korach, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B27">2006</xref>; Mhyre et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B73">2006</xref>). Estrogen treatment attenuates “non-classical” transcription at estrogen response elements sites in glioma cells (Mhyre et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B73">2006</xref>). Adaptogens up-regulate <italic toggle="yes">PLCB1</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">PI3KC2G</italic>, and cAMP relate genes, modulate NO and SAPK. These similarities of estrogens and adaptogens allow to suggest that their mechanisms of actions someway interfere. Are they competing as mimetic or antagonists is still unclear albeit both are neuroprotective. Further study on elucidation of this mechanism of action of adaptogens is required.</p><p>ERs are over-expressed in around 70% of breast cancers (Deroo and Korach, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B27">2006</xref>). Pharmacological down-regulation of <italic toggle="yes">ESR1</italic> may be effective in treatment and prevention of breast cancer, ovarian cancer, colon cancer, prostate cancer, and endometrial cancer. (Fabian and Kimler, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B32">2005</xref>; Ascenzi et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B5">2006</xref>).</p><p>Moreover, some of tested samples down-regulated the <italic toggle="yes">OLFM4</italic> gene, which was recently reported to inhibit apoptotic pathways and to promote tumor cell proliferation. This suggests that OLFM4 could serve as a diagnostic marker for human cancers (Koshida et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B58">2007</xref>; Oh et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B79">2011</xref>).
