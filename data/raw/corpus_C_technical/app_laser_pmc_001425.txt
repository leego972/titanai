More recently, studies using segmentation schemes to separate the inner retinal circulation into three layers have become commonplace (<xref ref-type="fig" rid="fig2-2515841419840249">Figure 2</xref>). This approach is based on histologic reports that have demonstrated the existence of four distinct capillary planes in the perifoveal retina<sup><xref rid="bibr32-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">32</xref></sup> that form three capillary plexuses: the superficial capillary plexus (SCP), middle capillary plexus (MCP), and deep capillary plexus (DCP). The SCP is found in the nerve fiber and ganglion cell layers, the MCP is located between the inner plexiform layer (IPL) and inner nuclear layer (INL), and the DCP is present between the INL and outer plexiform layer (OPL). OCTA has successfully and consistently been used to visualize these three capillary layers,<sup><xref rid="bibr8-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>,<xref rid="bibr21-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>,<xref rid="bibr31-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>,<xref rid="bibr33-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>,<xref rid="bibr34-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">34</xref></sup> leading to deeper insights into their structure and connectivity. For example, while there has been debate regarding previously proposed theories of parallel or serial connections between the layers,<sup><xref rid="bibr28-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>,<xref rid="bibr35-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">35</xref></sup> Nesper and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr36-2515841419840249" ref-type="bibr">36</xref></sup> have found evidence supporting a hybrid model of connectivity using OCTA.</p><fig id="fig2-2515841419840249" orientation="portrait" position="float"><label>Figure 2.</label><caption><p>Inner retinal segmentation schemes in a healthy eye using the Optovue OCTA system.
