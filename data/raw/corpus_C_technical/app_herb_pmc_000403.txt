In addition, our findings revealed that <italic toggle="yes">Zingiber officinale</italic> was the most frequently used species followed by <italic toggle="yes">Glycyrrhiza</italic> spp. and <italic toggle="yes">Ocimum sanctum</italic> (Fig. <xref rid="Fig3" ref-type="fig">3</xref>).<fig id="Fig2" position="float" orientation="portrait"><label>Fig. 2</label><caption><p>Frequently used families of medicinal herbs for prevention and treatment of COVID-19</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" id="MO3" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="11418_2021_1575_Fig2_HTML.jpg"/></fig><fig id="Fig3" position="float" orientation="portrait"><label>Fig. 3</label><caption><p>Frequently used species in herbal medicines against COVID-19</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" id="MO4" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="11418_2021_1575_Fig3_HTML.jpg"/></fig></p><p id="Par9"><italic toggle="yes">Z. officinale</italic> (ginger) has been shown to be the most frequently used species in herbal medicine in communities across the countries studied. The crude extract of this herb reportedly exhibited antiviral activity against SARS-CoV-2 in a Vero E6 cell model, with IC<sub>50</sub> of 29.19 µM. The main phenolic compound in ginger, 6-gingerol, only showed an IC<sub>50</sub> of ≤ 100 µM [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR26">26</xref>], which suggests that the efficacy of <italic toggle="yes">Z. officinale</italic> may depend on mechanisms that do not involve 6-gingerol for suppressing COVID-19 symptoms, do not directly attack the virus, or that is synergistic with other herbs to achieve a better therapeutic effect. <italic toggle="yes">Z. officinale</italic> is a spice in which gingerols and shogaol are the active compounds thought to provide health benefits. This herb possesses a range of medicinal benefits, including antiviral activity against feline calicivirus, human respiratory syncytial virus, influenza A, and H9N2, and its activity for stimulating tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNF-α) is postulated as a first-line defense for virus infection [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR27">27</xref>].</p><p id="Par10">Along with ginger, <italic toggle="yes">Glycyrrhiza</italic> spp. is a frequently used medicinal plant for COVID-19 treatment. This herb is mostly included in various formulas in TCM for COVID-19 treatment, such as Ma Xing Shi Gan, Da Yuan Yin, and Lianhua–Qingwen. The Lianhua–Qingwen formula contains various herbs, including <italic toggle="yes">Glycyrrhiza uralensis</italic>, and reportedly exhibits inhibitory activity against SARS-CoV-2 (Table <xref rid="Tab2" ref-type="table">2</xref>). On the other hand, the aqueous extract of <italic toggle="yes">Glycyrrhiza glabra</italic> root reportedly exhibits inhibitory activity against SARS-CoV-2 infected Vero E6 cells. The extract has shown antiviral effects at 2 mg/ml, which is lower than its toxic concentration. Glycyrrhizin, as a triterpenoid glycoside, has also shown viral blocking ability at 0.5 mg/ml and 1 mg/ml in the pre-entry and post-entry conditions, respectively. In addition, no cytotoxic effect has been found at ≤ 4 mg/ml. The half-maximal effective concentration (EC<sub>50</sub>) of this compound reportedly is 0.44 mg/ml. Glycyrrhizin has also been shown to significantly reduce the SARS-CoV-2 RNA level [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR28">28</xref>]. An in silico study showed that this compound binds to the ACE2 receptor, which possibly blocks viral entry [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>].</p><sec id="Sec3"><title>Molecular drug targets for COVID-19</title><p id="Par11">SARS-CoV-2 is a novel coronavirus that belongs to the Coronavirinae family. Coronaviruses are RNA viruses that have a spherically shaped envelope 100–160 nm in diameter. The genome size is 27–32 kb. The 3′ end of the genome encodes structural proteins, such as envelope (E), spike glycoprotein (S), nucleocapsid (N), and membrane glycoprotein (M), and the 5′ end encodes polyproteins involved in replication and transcription [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR20">20</xref>]. SARS-CoV-2 can enter the host cell via various receptors, such as ACE2, aminopeptidase N (APN), and dipeptidyl peptidase 4 (DPP4).
