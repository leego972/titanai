Question: <p>Is there a way to access a list(or tuple, or other iterable)'s next, or previous element while looping through with for loop?</p>

<pre><code>l=[1,2,3]
for item in l:
    if item==2:
        get_previous(l,item)
</code></pre>

Answer: <p>Expressed as a generator function:</p>

<pre><code>def neighborhood(iterable):
    iterator = iter(iterable)
    prev = None
    item = iterator.next()  # throws StopIteration if empty.
    for next in iterator:
        yield (prev,item,next)
        prev = item
        item = next
    yield (prev,item,None)
</code></pre>

<p>Usage:</p>

<pre><code>for prev,item,next in neighborhood(l):
    print prev, item, next
</code></pre>
