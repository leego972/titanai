# PMC: phytochemical bioactive compound anticancer — Overview

Relevance</title><p>Polyacetylenes are a class of unsaturated aliphatic compounds characterized by the presence of one or more carbon–carbon triple bonds. In <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, these metabolites constitute a pharmacologically significant group of secondary metabolites, exhibiting a broad range of biological activities, including anti-inflammatory, cytotoxic, antimicrobial, and immunomodulatory effects (<xref rid="molecules-30-03460-t006" ref-type="table">Table 6</xref>) [<xref rid="B11-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>,<xref rid="B132-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">132</xref>,<xref rid="B133-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">133</xref>,<xref rid="B140-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">140</xref>].</p><p>The presence of polyacetylenes in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> was initially suggested by earlier studies referenced by Do et al. [<xref rid="B7-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>], which reported their occurrence in the roots and associated them with the plant’s traditional use in treating inflammatory conditions. Subsequent phytochemical investigations confirmed the presence of four principal polyacetylenes, namely falcarinol (<bold>92</bold>) (also known as panaxynol), falcarindiol, heptadeca-1,8-diene-4,6-diyne-3,10-diol (<bold>93</bold>), and heptadeca-1,8-diene-4,6-diyne-3-ol-10-one (<bold>94</bold>), in both root and leaf tissues. Rarison et al. [<xref rid="B10-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>] further expanded the understanding of polyacetylene distribution in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic> by identifying falcarinol (<bold>92</bold>) and hexadeca-5,7,9,11-tetrayne-1,16-diol (<bold>95</bold>) in different solvent fractions of the leaf extract. Falcarinol was predominantly present in the n-hexane fraction (PFLH), consistent with its high lipophilicity, while hexadeca-5,7,9,11-tetrayne-1,16-diol was selectively detected in the dichloromethane fraction (PFLD) at a concentration of 0.94 ± 0.01%. These findings indicate solvent-dependent extraction efficiency and suggest differential tissue distribution and solubility of polyacetylenes within the species. Such solvent-specific activity supports the importance of fractionation strategies in metabolite profiling and bioactivity correlation.</p><p>Falcarinol (<bold>92</bold>), a monoacetylene alcohol, has been extensively studied for its pharmacological potential. It exhibits strong anti-inflammatory activity by downregulating inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS) and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2), suppressing nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB) activation, and reducing the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α and IL-6 [<xref rid="B141-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">141</xref>]. In vitro cytotoxicity has also been reported across diverse cancer cell lines, primarily through oxidative stress and apoptosis-related pathways. However, despite promising preclinical outcomes, its translation to clinical models remains unexplored.</p><p>The antimicrobial activity of falcarinol has also been well documented. Lutomski et al. [<xref rid="B11-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>] reported its activity against Gram-positive bacteria (<italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Streptococcus pyogenes</italic>) and fungal strains (<italic toggle="yes">Candida albicans</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Microsporum gypseum</italic>), suggesting its relevance in the treatment of infections. Additionally, Rarison et al. [<xref rid="B10-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>] demonstrated that falcarinol modulates immune responses by downregulating costimulatory molecules on antigen-presenting cells and suppressing T-cell proliferation in inflammatory environments, supporting its immunomodulatory role.</p><p>Mechanistic studies by Alfurayhi et al. [<xref rid="B140-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">140</xref>] have further elucidated the multi-targeted pharmacological profile of falcarinol-type polyacetylenes. These compounds were shown to inhibit iNOS and COX-2 expression, suppress pro-inflammatory cytokine production, activate the Nrf2-mediated antioxidant defense system, and modulate key signaling pathways such as PI3K/Akt and MAPK. This pleiotropic mode of action may underlie their efficacy in both inflammatory and tumorigenic settings.</p><p>Neuroprotective effects—though preliminary—have been linked to calcium channel inhibition and mitochondrial preservation under oxidative stress [<xref rid="B132-molecules-30-03460" ref-type="bibr">132</xref>]. However, further studies are needed to establish their pharmacodynamic profile and therapeutic index.</p><p>Polyacetylenes, especially falcarinol, emerge as promising multifunctional agents in <italic toggle="yes">P. fruticosa</italic>, with reported anti-inflammatory, cytotoxic, antimicrobial, immunomodulatory, and neuroprotective activities. However, compared to triterpenoid saponins or polyphenols, this class remains underexplored.
