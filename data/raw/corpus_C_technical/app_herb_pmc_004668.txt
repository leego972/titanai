IPA performs different calculations based on the Ingenuity Knowledge Base, a large gathering of observations with approximately 5 million findings manually curated from the biomedical literature or integrated from third-party databases [<xref rid="B67-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">67</xref>].</p><p>Adaptra<sup>®</sup> deregulated 22 genes involved in activating neuronal development (<xref rid="pharmaceuticals-18-00261-f016" ref-type="fig">Figure 16</xref>). It means that these two ingredients of BHP Adaptra<sup>®</sup> act synergistically in combination. In other words, BHP Adaptra<sup>®</sup> is superior to Rhodiola or Withania in activating neurogenesis and has the potential effects mentioned above.</p><p>The roles, functions, and consequences of the downregulation of expressions of the PRKCZ gene encoding the protein kinases PKCζ and PKMζ, which regulate glucose transport and long-term memories, and the GRIN3A gene encoding the glutamate ionotropic receptor gene subunit Grin3a of the glutamate ionotropic receptor NMDAR with central roles in brain plasticity, development, learning, and memory in the brain are summarized in <xref rid="pharmaceuticals-18-00261-t007" ref-type="table">Table 7</xref> below. Both genes are critical in maintaining brain health, plasticity, and systemic glucose homeostasis. Their downregulation can contribute to neurological diseases and metabolic dysfunctions, but such changes could confer adaptive benefits in specific pathological contexts. Depending on the context, the downregulation of the PRKCZ and GRIN3A genes could have negative and positive consequences, including health conditions, brain function, and adaptation to specific environments.</p><p>Downregulation of <italic toggle="yes">PRKCZ</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">GRIN3A</italic> can have both positive and negative consequences in long-lasting post-stroke brain fatigue. Post-stroke brain fatigue involves a complex interplay of neuroinflammation, energy deficits, disrupted neuroplasticity, and impaired cognitive recovery, all of which may intersect with the functions of these genes. The predicted beneficial effects of Adaptra<sup>®</sup> due to the downregulation of <italic toggle="yes">PRKCZ</italic> include lowering excitotoxic risk and energy conservation by reducing the overactivation of memory pathways. Downregulation of <italic toggle="yes">GRIN3</italic> suggests enhanced LTP for recovery and improved synaptic transmission efficiency.</p><p>The upregulation of <italic toggle="yes">ADGRL1</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">CDK5R1/CDKL3</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">CHRNB2</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">ROR2</italic> gene expression in the context of long-lasting post-stroke brain fatigue can have a mix of positive and negative effects, depending on how these proteins influence brain function, recovery, and energy regulation. <xref rid="pharmaceuticals-18-00261-t007" ref-type="table">Table 7</xref> shows a summary of predicted beneficial impacts on post-stroke brain fatigue.</p><p>Upregulation of <italic toggle="yes">ADGRL1</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">CDK5R1/CDKL3</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">CHRNB2</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">ROR2</italic> genes in post-stroke long-lasting brain fatigue generally supports neuroplasticity, cognitive recovery, and neurogenesis, which are beneficial for rehabilitation.
