Hedyotis diffusa </title><p><italic toggle="yes">Hedyotis diffusa</italic> (Chinese: sheshecao) is a member of the family Rubiaceae. It is spread over the northeast regions of Asia. <italic toggle="yes">H. diffusa</italic> has been commonly used to cure inflammatory diseases i.e., urethritis, bronchitis, and appendicitis [<xref rid="B53-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>,<xref rid="B54-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">54</xref>]. </p><p>Because of the recent advances in pharmacological practices, this herb received importance for having antitumor properties and showed effective results in treating cancers of the liver, colon, lungs, brain, and pancreas [<xref rid="B55-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">H. diffusa</italic> contains important bioactive derivatives of polysaccharides, triterpenes, and anthraquinones [<xref rid="B56-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>,<xref rid="B57-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>]. </p><p>Methyl anthraquinonesare, one of the bioactive compounds in <italic toggle="yes">H. diffusa</italic>, is responsible for apoptosis of many cancers. It shows apoptosis and inhibitory effect on the MCF-7 cell line of breast cancer via activation of the caspase-4/Ca<sup>2+</sup>/calpain pathway when applied in a concentration of 18.62 µM for 24 h. It was observed that the S phase of the cell cycle and the percentage of the apoptotic cells were markedly increased when methyl anthraquinone was applied to MCF-7 cells [<xref rid="B58-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">58</xref>]. Similarly, a concentrated extract of <italic toggle="yes">H. diffusa</italic> cause an inhibitory effect on the cervical cancer proliferation and induces apoptosis of Hela cells. Studies on the effect of <italic toggle="yes">H. diffusa</italic> ethanolic extracts on anti-colorectal cancer showed that these extracts cause an inhibitory effect on the Ct-26 cells by applying different concentrations (0.06 mg/mL, 0.08 mg/mL, 0.10 mg/mL and 0.12 mg/mL) with the rate of 35.46% to 71.84% [<xref rid="B59-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">59</xref>]. </p></sec><sec id="sec3dot8-biomolecules-10-00047"><title>3.8. Loranthus parasiticus and Scurrulus parasitica</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Loranthus parasiticus</italic>, also known as Sang Ji Sheng (in Chinese), is a member of the Loranthaceae family and is widely distributed in the Southwestern regions of China. <italic toggle="yes">L. parasiticus</italic> is a semiparasitic plant, historically used as traditional folk medicine in China and Japan [<xref rid="B60-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>]. Since they are parasitic in nature, their biological activities including phytoconstituents are highly dependent on the host trees [<xref rid="B61-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">61</xref>,<xref rid="B62-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">62</xref>]. In fact, certain studies indicated the reverse effects of <italic toggle="yes">L. parasiticus</italic> on a host tree in decreasing sugar and chlorophyll content [<xref rid="B63-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">63</xref>]. In vitro and in vivo studies have been conducted to evaluate the possible anticancer and antitumor potential of <italic toggle="yes">L. parasiticus</italic>. Cytotoxicity analysis of aqueous extracts of <italic toggle="yes">L. parasiticus</italic> has shown positive activity against ovarian cancer cell lines; SKOV3, CAOV3, and OVCAR-3 [<xref rid="B64-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">64</xref>]. </p><p>A comparative study conducted by Xiao et al. [<xref rid="B65-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">65</xref>] on flavonoids extracted in 80% ethanol from <italic toggle="yes">Scurrulus parasitica</italic> harvested from different hosts showed a good anticancer potential on acute myeloid leukemia cell line HL-60. Flavonoids from <italic toggle="yes">S. parasitica</italic> on <italic toggle="yes">N. indicum</italic> induced apoptosis and inhibited cell proliferation with an IC<sub>50</sub> value of 0.60 mg/L on HL-60 cells and arrested cell cycle at G<sub>0</sub>–G<sub>1</sub> phase. <italic toggle="yes">S. parasitica</italic> parasitizing on <italic toggle="yes">Morus alba</italic> also showed an IC<sub>50</sub> value of 2.49 mg/L.
