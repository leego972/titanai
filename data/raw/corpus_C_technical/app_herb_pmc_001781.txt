# PMC: phytochemical bioactive compound anticancer — Overview

Its uses are extensively described in the canon of TCM literature (<xref rid="B41" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>–<xref rid="B44" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>) and cited in the current Chinese Pharmacopoeia (<xref rid="B35" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>). “Artemisia scopariae herba” (ASH), or “Yinchen”, refers to the dried aerial parts of SCOPA or of its close relative, <italic toggle="yes">Artemisia capillaris</italic>, with the two plants being used interchangeably for its preparation. A decoction of Yinchen is the principal constituent of many TCM formulae, in which ASH is combined with other herbal products. Importantly, a distinction is made between ASH from small Spring seedlings (Mian Yin Chen) and that from flowering plants in late Summer (Yen Chin Hao), each preferable for treating distinct sets of ailments (<xref rid="B41" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>, <xref rid="B45" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>). Chemical analyses of ASH have confirmed that its constituents vary greatly depending on the time of harvest (<xref rid="B36" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>, <xref rid="B46" ref-type="bibr">46</xref>). While TCM preparations are derived from the aerial parts of SCOPA, the roots of the plant are used in Pakistan (<xref rid="B27" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>) and the use of its flowers has been reported in Iran (<xref rid="B16" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>).</p></sec><sec id="s3"><title>Effects of <italic toggle="yes">A. scoparia</italic> on Determinants of Metabolic Health</title><sec id="s3_1"><title>Metabolically Favorable Effects of <italic toggle="yes">A. scoparia</italic> in Experimental Models of Obesity and Diabetes</title><p>As mentioned above, the use of SCOPA in folk medicine for diabetes and hyperglycemia has been amply documented. A 2016 study found that administration of SCOPA during the second trimester of pregnancy improved insulin sensitivity, fasting plasma glucose levels, and circulating adiponectin levels in patients with gestational diabetes (<xref rid="B47" ref-type="bibr">47</xref>). With the exception of that study, SCOPA has not been evaluated in humans for effects on measures of metabolic health such as insulin sensitivity, glycemic control, or cardiovascular risk factors.
