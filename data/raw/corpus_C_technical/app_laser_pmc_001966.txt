To detect the effects of Zol on HaCaT cells, we treated HaCaT cells with different concentrations of zoledronate, 1 μM, 5 μM, 10 μM, 25 μM, 50 μM, and found that 10 μM was the lowest concentration, at which HaCaT cells proliferation was inhibited at 24 h (P &lt; 0.05, Fig. <xref rid="Fig4" ref-type="fig">4</xref>A). Thus, we chose 10 μM Zol for the migration assay. The migration assay demonstrated that Zol slows down the HaCaT cells’ migration rate, whereas LLLT significantly accelerates the migration rate at 24 h, 48 h, and 72 h (P &lt; 0.05, Fig. <xref rid="Fig4" ref-type="fig">4</xref>B and 4C).<fig id="Fig4" position="float" orientation="portrait"><label>Fig. 4</label><caption><p>LLLT promotes Zol-treated HaCaT cell migration. Scale bar = 200 μm. (<bold>C</bold>) Quantification of the migration rate at 24 h, 48 h, and 72 h. (*<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.05, **<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.01, ***<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.001, ****<italic toggle="yes">p</italic> &lt; 0.0001)</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" id="MO4" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="12903_2022_2678_Fig4_HTML.jpg"/></fig></p></sec></sec><sec id="Sec20"><title>Discussion</title><p id="Par53">Since the first report on MRONJ was published in 2003 [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR24">24</xref>], different methods have been developed to prevent or treat MRONJ. Previous studies have suggested that promoting primary gingival healing or closure might be an effective method to prevent MRONJ [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR6">6</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR8">8</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR10">10</xref>]. Methods to promote MRONJ soft tissue healing include platelet-rich fibrin injections, MSCs therapy, and so on [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR6">6</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR25">25</xref>]. In this study, we found that LLLT might improve BPs-treated gingival wound healing via IL-1RA-mediated inflammation suppression. The primary wound closure provides a favorable environment for underlying bone regeneration of the tooth sockets and prevents the occurrence of MRONJ. Our results may help to clarify the mechanism of MRONJ and early clinical prevention of MRONJ by LLLT.</p><p id="Par54">In MRONJ lesions, BPs-induced soft tissue toxicity impairs the functions of keratinocytes and fibroblasts and leads to delayed wound healing, which is a potential mechanism of MRONJ [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">9</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR26">26</xref>]. Furthermore, periodontal ligament stem cells (PDLSCs) have remarkable regenerative potential and contribute to periodontal tissue regeneration [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR27">27</xref>]. Zoledronate might impair PDLSC’s viability, proliferation, and induce apoptosis, thus affecting gingival tissue healing [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR28">28</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>]. Oral mucosa acts as a defense against pathogenic bacteria or fungi for underlying bones. It also provides vital cytokines like bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs), fibroblast growth factor (FGF), nuclear factor kappa-B ligand (RANKL), and TGF-β1 for bone metabolism [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR6">6</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">9</xref>]. Loss of protection of intact mucosal coverage allows bacteria to invade the bone tissue, causing an inflammatory response, which, in turn, results in the development of osteonecrosis [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR30">30</xref>].</p><p id="Par55">Primary gingival wound healing after tooth extraction can effectively reduce the onset of MRONJ [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR10">10</xref>]. Our previous studies suggested that TGF-β1-mediate early wound closure could promote the underlying bone repair process and prevent the occurrence of MRONJ [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR6">6</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR31">31</xref>]. Moreover, other studies reported that LLLT could effectively promote wound healing, particularly in resistant wounds such as diabetic wounds [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>], and inflammation-related wounds [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR32">32</xref>].
