DAMP expression induced thanks to PDT treatment can be a powerful immune response responsible for tumor ICD (<xref rid="B6" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>).</p><p>DAMPs are necessary for tumor-associated antigens presentation to antigen-presenting cells, which helps to stimulate an immune response against cancerous cells. Dead/dying PDT-treated cells are reported to release PDT-related DAMPs such as ATP and HMGB1 (<xref rid="B43" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>, <xref rid="B84" ref-type="bibr">84</xref>, <xref rid="B85" ref-type="bibr">85</xref>). Photosens and photodithazine have recently been reported to induce ICD which, was associated with the emission of HMGB1 and ATP (<xref rid="B43" ref-type="bibr">43</xref>). HMGB1 can trigger a response to innate immunity by interacting with 2 and 4 toll-like receptors and potentially with recognized receptors on antigen-presenting cells. ATP, on the other hand, encourages antigen-presenting cell recruitment by attaching to purinergic receptors, and this is read by antigen-presenting cells as a signal to ‘find me.’ ATP may be released actively from the cell, which is controlled by a particular signaling pathway, or released passively due to the loss of the integrity of the plasma membrane (<xref rid="B86" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>). Similar to the induced CRT mechanism, the exact mechanism stimulating ATP release following PDT appears to present unique attributes. For instance, hypericin-mediated PDT stimulated ATP secretion in a way that is autophagy-independent, which is contradictory to ICD stimulated by chemotherapy (<xref rid="B87" ref-type="bibr">87</xref>, <xref rid="B88" ref-type="bibr">88</xref>).</p></sec><sec id="s2_5"><label>2.5</label><title>Photodynamic therapy advantages and disadvantages</title><p>PDT has several advantages over conventional cancer therapeutic techniques. PDT has no long-term negative effect when correctly administered, yet first-generation PSs can induce modest, temporary photosensitivity in some areas, such as the eyes and skin (<xref rid="B89" ref-type="bibr">89</xref>, <xref rid="B90" ref-type="bibr">90</xref>). When comparing PDT to surgical procedures, its adverse effects are frequently not severe, and the duration is not prolonged as in radiotherapy or chemotherapy. Also, PDT is typically administered as an outpatient procedure. Tumor mortality is substantially caused by the destruction of the tumor’s associated vasculature (<xref rid="B91" ref-type="bibr">91</xref>, <xref rid="B92" ref-type="bibr">92</xref>). PDT’s dual selectivity allows it to be directly applied and accurately target a precise tissue (<xref rid="B90" ref-type="bibr">90</xref>). PDT can be administered repeatedly in the same treatment area if necessary, as opposed to being dispersed like in radiotherapy. Scarring after the PDT healing process is generally minimal. Moreover, PDT treatment typically costs less than the majority of cancer treatments (<xref rid="B90" ref-type="bibr">90</xref>, <xref rid="B93" ref-type="bibr">93</xref>). PDT can also alter the microenvironments of the stroma and blood vessels, increasing their vulnerability to further treatments like IOT and chemotherapy (<xref rid="B15" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>).</p><p>Cancer tumors of the digestive tract, lung, lung lining (malignant pleural mesothelioma), head and neck, bladder, skin (basocellular carcinoma), and cervix have been treated using PDT (<xref rid="B94" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>). However, some disadvantages are linked with PDT. Tumor cells are documented to be resistant to PDT (<xref rid="B95" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>, <xref rid="B96" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>). Also, resistance to PDT photosensitizers may develop via similar mechanisms as those with conventional drugs, such as decreased uptake, elevated inactivation of the drug, drug efflux, and alterations in intracellular trafficking. A combination treatment strategy with at least two distinct treatment plans is one potential tactic for conquering tumor resistance (<xref rid="B94" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>).
