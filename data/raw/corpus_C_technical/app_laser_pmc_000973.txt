# PMC: optical coherence tomography clinical application — Overview

<xref rid="Fig1" ref-type="fig">1</xref>) [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR13">13</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR37">37</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR41">41</xref>].<fig id="Fig1" position="float" orientation="portrait"><label>Fig. 1</label><caption><p><bold>Recent technical developments in OCT</bold>. (1) Speckle-modulated OCT. Speckle artifacts limit the spatial to noise ratio in OCT imaging. These exemplary speckle-modulated OCT scans of the mouse cornea and retina show the increase of resolution in contrast to conventional OCT imaging. (<bold>1A</bold>) Conventional OCT scan of mouse cornea. (<bold>1B</bold>) Speckle modulated OCT scan of same mouse cornea, notice enhanced sectioning of histological layers. (<bold>1C, D</bold>) Enlarged excerpts (<bold>1D</bold>) notice enhanced delineation of histological structures like lamellae and enhanced delineation of the endothelium in speckle-modulated OCT. (<bold>1E</bold>) Histological section of cornea. (<bold>1F</bold>) Conventional OCT scan of mouse retina. (<bold>1G</bold>) Speckle-modulated OCT scan of mouse retina. (<bold>1H, I</bold>) Enlarged excerpts (<bold>1E</bold>) notice enhanced segregation of single retinal layers; see Yecies et al. [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR41">41</xref>]. (2) Polarization-sensitive OCT (ps-OCT). Through a set of hardware and software components, polarization-sensitive OCT (ps-OCT) is able to measure and correct the birefringence (“bi-refraction” of light) of local regions of tissue, leading to enhanced imaging of tissue with different optical densities and refraction indices. (<bold>2A</bold>) ps-OCT of a block of human cerebellar lobule. The folded cerebellar cortex is shown on orthogonal viewing planes (<bold>xy</bold> coronal; <bold>xz</bold> axial; <bold>yz</bold> sagittal). Note the ability to delineate the Purkinje cell layer. Volume rendering of segmented (<bold>2B</bold>) molecular layer, (<bold>2C</bold>) granual layer, and (<bold>2D</bold>) white matter (see Wang et al. [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR37">37</xref>]. (3) Doppler OCT. In vivo delineation of mouse cortical vasculature with Doppler OCT. (<bold>3A</bold>) Multi-photon laser scanning microscopy (MPM) of cerebral vasculature (<bold>3B</bold>) three-dimensional reconstruction of flow demonstrating the vasculature of the mouse cortex. (<bold>3C</bold>) Doppler OCT velocity projection map (see Gagnon et al. [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR13">13</xref>]. (4) Sensitivity contrast-enhanced OCT<bold>.</bold> Imaging of lymph vessels in ears pinnae in living mice. Injection of large gold nanorods LGNR is used for functional imaging. (<bold>4a</bold>) Delineation of blood vessels (red) by flow detection in OCT prior to LGNR injection. (<bold>4b</bold>) Injection of 815 nm LGNRs (green) and 925 nm LGNRS (cyan). (<bold>4c</bold>) Drainage of LGNRs and delineation of lymphatic vessels. (<bold>4d</bold>) Same imaging technique in a different mouse after injection of LGNRs (<bold>4e</bold>) enlarged excerpt displaying the relationship of blood and lymphatic vessels (<bold>4f)</bold> same area as in (<bold>4e</bold>) after injection of 925 nm LGNRs displaying the (arrow) junction of lymph vessels and mono directional flow (see Liba (2016))</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" id="MO1" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="10143_2021_1599_Fig1_HTML.jpg"/></fig></p><p id="Par6">OCT has the ability to perform an “optic biopsy”. Not only white and gray matter but also healthy and diffusely invaded brain tissue could be distinguished in glioma surgery [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR2">2</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR24">24</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR31">31</xref>].</p><p id="Par7">A part from structural imaging, functional brain imaging is possible. Adaptations of perfusion-dependent OCT offer the possibility of functional cortical mapping after peripheral stimulation and furthermore the delineation of epileptic foci [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR32">32</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR33">33</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR35">35</xref>].</p><p id="Par8">These versatile strengths shed light on OCTs potential for microneurosurgical guidance. This critical literature review focuses on clinical “near” future applications to further enhance neurosurgical excellence.</p></sec><sec id="Sec2"><title>Materials and methods</title><p id="Par9">“Pub Med”, “Cochrane Library”, “Crossref Metadata Search”, and “IEEE Xplore” databases as well as the search engine “Google Scholar” were screened for “optical coherence tomography + neurosurgery”, “optical coherence tomography + intraoperative imaging + neurosurgery”, and “microscope integrated optical coherence tomography + neurosurgery”.</p></sec><sec id="Sec3"><title>Results</title><p id="Par10">Detailed evaluation of the results revealed <italic toggle="yes">n</italic> = 51 articles related to the use of OCT as an imaging technique in neurosurgery or in the field of neurosurgical research.
