These plant remedies were typically homemade and prepared as teas and taken daily, either alone or with other plants, for the purpose of maintaining reproductive health and preventing disease. For example, participants make a tea from the leaves of <italic toggle="yes">Momordica charantia</italic> L. (<italic toggle="yes">asosi</italic>, bitter melon) or <italic toggle="yes">Mentha spicata</italic> (<italic toggle="yes">ti bonm,</italic> spearmint) and drink a cup daily. Among the nine commonly mentioned preventatives for women’s health, five were identified as food plants. These were either brewed as tea or incorporated into daily consumption with the intention of promoting reproductive health.</p></sec><sec id="Sec10"><title>Medicinal plant use for women’s health in NYC Haitian and Dominican communities</title><p id="Par30">Compared to NYC Dominicans, among the 73 plant species independently mentioned by at least three Haitian women in NYC, 52 were exclusive to the Haitian community (Additional file <xref rid="MOESM2" ref-type="media">2</xref>). For one popularly used Haitian ethnotaxon, a tree bark with the local name <italic toggle="yes">kòs bwa</italic>, we were unable to obtain a voucher specimen and thus it remains unidentified. NYC Haitians shared 21 overlapping species with NYC Dominicans (JSI = 0.16) that are used for women’s health (Fig. <xref rid="Fig4" ref-type="fig">4</xref>). Of the 21 overlapping species, 38% are food plants, including <italic toggle="yes">Allium sativum</italic> L. (<italic toggle="yes">lay,</italic> garlic), <italic toggle="yes">Beta vulgaris</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Cajanus cajan</italic> (<italic toggle="yes">pwa kongo,</italic> pigeon pea), <italic toggle="yes">Cinnamomum</italic> spp. (<italic toggle="yes">kanél</italic>, cinnamon), <italic toggle="yes">Cocos nucifera</italic> L.
