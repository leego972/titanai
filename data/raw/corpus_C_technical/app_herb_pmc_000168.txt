Moreover, the adverse effects of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> can be observed not only in the liver and kidney but also in the lung. An ethanol extract of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> was found to exert toxic effects in the rat lung after repeated intragastric administration for 28 days at a dosage of 40 g/kg (<xref rid="bib71" ref-type="bibr">Li et al., 2013b</xref>). Emodin and rhein also showed mutagenicity in TK gene mutation analysis at the concentrations of 80 and 120 μg/mL (<xref rid="bib226" ref-type="bibr">Zhu et al., 2011</xref>). In addition, a previous study compared the acute toxicity of different components of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> on mice. The acute toxicity of dual extraction components was stronger than that of the ethanol- and/or water-extracted components, and the maximum tolerated doses (MTD) were 20.0, 98.4 and 78.0 g/kg, respectively (<xref rid="bib53" ref-type="bibr">Huang et al., 2010</xref>).</p></sec></sec><sec id="s0155"><label>7</label><title>Pharmacokinetics</title><p id="p0280">In pharmacokinetic experiments, rats were administered <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> extracts. After the tissues were extracted with methanol, the metabolites were identified by LC-MS/MS. TSG, the main component of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic>, was rapidly absorbed into the body fluids, widely distributed throughout the body and quickly eliminated. TSG was mainly distributed in the liver and lung but hardly penetrated the blood-brain and blood-testicle barriers (<xref rid="bib107" ref-type="bibr">Lv et al., 2011</xref>). Emodin was found predominantly in the liver and brain (<xref rid="bib87" ref-type="bibr">Lin et al., 2012</xref>). Another study evaluated the pharmacokinetic behaviors of typical constituents of different types of processed <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> after oral administration in rats by LC-MS/MS. The results showed that the bioavailability of gallic acid improved, and the absorption of TSG, polydatin and emodin from the processed product in rats was reduced compared with that obtained with the raw product (<xref rid="bib205" ref-type="bibr">Zhang et al., 2013</xref>). Some studies have found that stilbene glucoside and emodin were rapidly converted to glucuronide conjugates in rats after oral administration (<xref rid="bib136" ref-type="bibr">Sun, 2004</xref>, <xref rid="bib95" ref-type="bibr">Liu et al., 2011</xref>). Further pharmacokinetic studies indicated that the AUC, <italic toggle="yes">C</italic>
<sub>max</sub> and <italic toggle="yes">T</italic>
<sub>1/2</sub> of emodin were increased after stilbene glucoside treatment, and the glucuronidation of the emodin levels was significantly inhibited. This phenomenon may be attributed to the inhibition of UGT1A8 mRNA expression by stilbene glucoside (<xref rid="bib111" ref-type="bibr">Ma et al., 2013</xref>).</p></sec><sec id="s0160"><label>8</label><title>Future perspectives and conclusions</title><p id="p0285">
<italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> is one of the most important and frequently used traditional Chinese herbal medicines because it exhibits anti-aging, immunologic, anti-hyperlipidaemia, neuroprotective, anti-cancer, and anti-inflammatory effects, among others. Quinones and stilbenes are considered to be the major constituents with pharmacological effects. However, some aspects need to be further investigated.</p><p id="p0290">Many recent studies have investigated the effective constituents of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> from different geographical areas. HPLC-fingerprint chromatography is usually used to compare the differences in <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> from different geographical areas (<xref rid="bib166" ref-type="bibr">Wu et al., 2006</xref>, <xref rid="bib106" ref-type="bibr">Luo et al., 2008</xref>, <xref rid="bib98" ref-type="bibr">Liu et al., 2007a</xref>, <xref rid="bib5" ref-type="bibr">Cai et al., 2011</xref>, <xref rid="bib179" ref-type="bibr">Xue et al., 2004</xref>, <xref rid="bib209" ref-type="bibr">Zhang et al., 2003</xref>). These studies have only focused on the differences in the contents of common components but have ignored differences in the more minor components in these plants, which is crucially important for <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> not only in terms of quality but also clinical use. The combined anthraquinones are used as indicator compounds to characterize the quality of this plant, which should have a minimum content of 0.10%, as delineated in the Pharmacopoeia of the People׳s Republic of China; this percentage is calculated based on the amounts of emodin and physcion. However, chrysophanol-8-O-β-<sc>d</sc>-glucopyranoside and other combined anthraquinones (<xref rid="bib185" ref-type="bibr">Yang et al., 1998</xref>) are also found in <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic>.
