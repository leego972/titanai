# PMC: low level laser therapy healing review — Overview

id="data-availability"><meta-name>Data Availability</meta-name><meta-value>All relevant data are within the paper and its <xref ref-type="sec" rid="sec034">Supporting information</xref> files.</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta><notes><title>Data Availability</title><p>All relevant data are within the paper and its <xref ref-type="sec" rid="sec034">Supporting information</xref> files.</p></notes></front><body><sec sec-type="intro" id="sec005"><title>Introduction</title><p>Damage to the dermis, epidermis, muscle, and even the bone at various parts of the body especially in the bony areas caused by prolonged pressure and tissue ischaemia is referred as bedsores or pressure ulcers (PU). Due to deprivation of oxygen supply and nutrition aforementioned PU terminates in tissue necrosis and sometimes bring about fatal consequences by local and systemic infections [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref001" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>–<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref003" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>]. PUs are frequent complications in long-term care known as the third most costly disorder following cancer and cardiovascular diseases [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref004" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>]. Moreover, proper inhibitory interventions in order to decrease the prevalence of avoidable PUs have been reported [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref005" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>]. According to a systematic review and meta-analysis study, global prevalence of PU in adult patients was reported around 12.8% and the incidence rate was 5.4 per 10,000 patient-day [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref006" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>]. Bedsores have imposed high cost on patients and health services in countries every year (in the United States about $26.8 billion was estimated) [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref007" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>].</p><p>Common treatments for PU including changing the patient’s position, taking antibiotics, using ointments and even debridement and surgeries are costly and laborious representing a need for effective wound treatments without complexities [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref008" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0247098.ref009" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>]. Novel treatments such as cell therapy, gene therapy, therapeutic ultrasound and electromagnetic therapies have been introduced as an alternative or adjunctive treatment in wound healing; but not fully responsive to healing especially in higher grade wounds. Recently, applications of photomedicine utilizing low level laser therapy (LLLT) as a nonsurgical technique have gained great attention for accelerating the wound healing procedure [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref010" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>]. It has been shown that LLLT may stimulate cell proliferation, maintaining DNA integrity and the repair of damaged DNA whilst as low energy laser has negligible thermal effects also [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref010" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>–<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref012" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>]. Blue light (wavelength range from 400 nm to 480 nm) which is part of the visible light spectrum is UV-free irradiation having fewer harmful side effects on mammalian cells compared with ultraviolet (UV) irradiation; since irradiation with blue light only reveals toxic effects at high dosages [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref013" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>]. Blue light has attracted increasing attention in wound healing not only due to its intrinsic antimicrobial effect and inactivation of important wound pathogenic bacteria, including staphylococcus aureus and pseudomonas aeruginosa [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref014" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>], but also owing to its favourable role in the treatment of inflammatory skin conditions [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref015" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>] and the stimulation of wound closure [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref016" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>].</p><p>Curcumin is a polyphenol extracted from the rhizome of Curcuma longa and well known as a multifunctional drug with anti-inflammatory, antioxidative, anti-infective and anti-carcinogenic activities owing to its varied influence on multiple signaling pathways [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref017" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0247098.ref018" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>]. Forbye curcumin has a wider absorption range from 300–500 nm with maximum light absorption at 420 nm [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref013" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>]. Besides having pharmacological safeties, mounting studies illustrated that this herbal medicine has promising beneficial therapeutic properties in many diseases, including wound healing. However, the inherent limitations of the compound itself, such as hydrophobicity, instability, poor absorption and rapid systemic elimination, imposes a hurdle for translation to wider clinical applications [<xref rid="pone.0247098.ref018" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0247098.ref019" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>].
