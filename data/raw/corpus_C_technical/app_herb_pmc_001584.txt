Available MDR modulator drugs such as verapamil [<xref rid="B105-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">105</xref>], cyclosporine [<xref rid="B106-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>], dexverapamil [<xref rid="B107-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">107</xref>], tariquidar [<xref rid="B108-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>], and zosuquidar [<xref rid="B109-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">109</xref>] show high toxicity and pharmacokinetic interactions with anticancer drugs [<xref rid="B110-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>].</p><p>Reports from various experiments have shown promising phytochemicals that are more potent than the currently available MDR modulators. Recently, some new jatrophane diterpenoids (compounds <bold>66</bold>–<bold>70</bold>, <xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f006" ref-type="fig">Figure 6</xref>B) were isolated from <italic toggle="yes">Euphorbia welwitschia</italic> [<xref rid="B111-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">111</xref>]. These compounds were reported to be stronger MDR modulators than verapamil [<xref rid="B111-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">111</xref>]. At 2 µM, compounds <bold>68</bold>, <bold>69</bold>, and <bold>70</bold> showed distinct MDR phenotype reversal [<xref rid="B112-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>]. Compounds <bold>69</bold> and <bold>70</bold> (<xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f006" ref-type="fig">Figure 6</xref>B) led to selective MDR reversal in EPG85-257RDB-, EPP85-181RNOV-, and EPP85-181RDB-resistant cell lines. Compound <bold>70</bold> showed only minor activity against EPG85-180RDB [<xref rid="B111-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">111</xref>,<xref rid="B112-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>].</p><p>Some new diterpenoids from <italic toggle="yes">Euphorbia dendroides</italic> (compounds <bold>71</bold>–<bold>77</bold>, <xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f006" ref-type="fig">Figure 6</xref>C) inhibited the growth of a resistant NCI-H460/R lung carcinoma cell line [<xref rid="B113-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>] Compounds <bold>71</bold> and <bold>72</bold> both showed strong anti-MDR activities, possessed strong synergy with paxlitatel and doxorubicin, and reversed the resistance to paclitaxel in the NCI-H460/R cell line [<xref rid="B114-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>]. Some potent MDR reversal compounds were also isolated from Euphorbia sororia (compounds <bold>78</bold>–<bold>84</bold>, <xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f006" ref-type="fig">Figure 6</xref>C,D) [<xref rid="B115-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">115</xref>,<xref rid="B116-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">116</xref>,<xref rid="B117-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">117</xref>]. Compounds <bold>78</bold>–<bold>80</bold> showed low cytotoxicity at 10 µM against a sensitive MCF-7 cancer cell line but had higher modulation of P-glycoprotein (P-gp) transporter in doxorubicin-resistant MCF-7/ADR cells than verapamil [<xref rid="B113-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>].</p><p>A novel mitotic arrest inducer belonging to ent-kaurene diterpenoids (compound <bold>85</bold>, <xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f006" ref-type="fig">Figure 6</xref>D) was purified from <italic toggle="yes">Isodon xerophilous</italic>. It was suggested that the compound’s antimitotic activity was via abnormal activation of the mitotic spindle checkpoint protein BubR1 [<xref rid="B117-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">117</xref>]. More importantly, the compound also induced mitotic arrest in paclitaxel-resistant Jurkat and U2OS cancer cell lines [<xref rid="B117-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">117</xref>]. Novel anticancer triterpenes include the three rarely found triterpene derivatives of C-27-carboxylated-lupine (compounds <bold>86</bold>–<bold>88</bold>, <xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f006" ref-type="fig">Figure 6</xref>D) isolated from Potentilla discolor. These compounds demonstrate a higher antiproliferative effect than matrine (a known anticancer agent) against HepG2, MCF7, and T-84 cell lines. In contrast, they seem to be nontoxic against the HL-7702 noncancerous liver cell line at 35 µM [<xref rid="B118-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">118</xref>]. A pentacyclic triterpene (compound <bold>89</bold>, <xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f006" ref-type="fig">Figure 6</xref>D) isolated from <italic toggle="yes">Glechoma longituba</italic> induced apoptosis and cell cycle arrest in NCI-H460 lung carcinoma by targeting the NF-κB/AP-1 signaling pathway [<xref rid="B119-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">119</xref>]. Compound <bold>90</bold> (<xref rid="molecules-27-08307-f006" ref-type="fig">Figure 6</xref>D), a newly isolated betulin derivative from the stem of <italic toggle="yes">Ziziphusspina christi</italic>, showed antiproliferative property against a HepG2 cancer cell line [<xref rid="B120-molecules-27-08307" ref-type="bibr">120</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec8-molecules-27-08307"><title>8.
