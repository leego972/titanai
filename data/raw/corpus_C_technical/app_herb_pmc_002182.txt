As a natural plant product, <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> poses little toxicity and side effects to human health, and it could combine with other herbal medicines in Chinese medicine decoction for the treatment of diseases. Since the present chemical drugs and therapy could cause some side effects in patients, it is essential to develop natural-derived drugs and adjuvant supplements with fewer side effects for patients. Hence, phytochemicals and herbal therapeutics have gained lots of attention for investigation in various disease treatments. <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> might be a promising dietary supplement and functional food in the prevention and management of diseases, and its bioactivities and mechanisms of action are worthy of exploration [<xref rid="B7" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>]. This review summarized the updated knowledge of the phytochemical studies, bioactivities, health benefits, related mechanism of action, and safety of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic>. The current challenge and outlooks of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic>-related research are also discussed, providing new and critical viewpoints for developing medicinal and functional food in the future.</p></sec><sec id="sec2"><title>2. Bioactive Compounds</title><p>A large body of studies shows that <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> contains various bioactive compounds, such as polysaccharides, flavonoids, bibenzyls, and alkaloids [<xref rid="B4" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>, <xref rid="B7" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>–<xref rid="B9" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>] (<xref rid="fig1" ref-type="fig">Figure 1</xref>). The tissue analysis found that the stems, leaves, and protocorm-like bodies of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> had the highest content of polysaccharides, flavonoids, and alkaloids, respectively [<xref rid="B10" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>]. Among the bioactive compounds, polysaccharides are the major medicinal compound that is often utilized to investigate the therapeutic effects of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale.</italic> It is mainly isolated from the stems of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> with a yield rate of over 30% [<xref rid="B11" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>]. The chemical analysis showed that the polysaccharides mainly contained mannose and glucose with a structure of (1⟶4)-linked-<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>-<italic toggle="yes">D</italic>-mannopyranosyl and <italic toggle="yes">β</italic>-D-glucopyranosyl residues [<xref rid="B12" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>, <xref rid="B13" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>]. Dendronan® is a new polysaccharide <italic toggle="yes">O</italic>-acetyl-glucomannan isolated from <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> with a relatively detailed chemical structure, and it was identified as the ratio of mannose to glucose (6.9 : 1) [<xref rid="B14" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>]. However, some polysaccharides with large molecular weight or absence of certain chemical groups might have low biological activities, and thus, some modifications could be considered for improving the bioavailability of polysaccharides from <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic>, such as fermentation, degradation, or grafting [<xref rid="B15" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>, <xref rid="B16" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>]. Furthermore, the relationship between structural characteristics and biological properties of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> polysaccharides needs more in-depth investigation.</p><p>Additionally, the metabolic profile of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> found that leaves contained more flavonoids than other parts, and flavonoids were considered the important antioxidant source [<xref rid="B17" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>–<xref rid="B19" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>]. A total of 14 major phenolic compounds including 1 quercetin-type flavonol, rutin, and 13 apigenin-type flavones like apigenin 6-<italic toggle="yes">C</italic>-<italic toggle="yes">β</italic>-<italic toggle="yes">D</italic>-glucoside-8-<italic toggle="yes">C</italic>-<italic toggle="yes">α</italic>-L-rhamnoside were identified from the leaves of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic>. And the major flavonoid compound was rutin with a content of 1.33 to 2.89 mg/g from leaves [<xref rid="B19" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>]. Moreover, naringenin was the flavonoid compound only found produced in the stems of <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> [<xref rid="B10" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>].</p><p>Bibenzyl is one of the most active ingredients in <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic>, and the gigantol and dendrocandin are the most common bibenzyl compounds from <italic toggle="yes">Dendrobium officinale</italic> [<xref rid="B4" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>].
