# PMC: ethnobotany medicinal plants Africa Asia — Overview

FL measures the proportion of informants who report the use of a particular plant for a given condition, thus identifying plants that are both culturally valued and perceived as effective.</p><p id="Par184">Despite its utility, several authors, including Leonti and colleagues, have emphasized limitations in applying FL. These indices may be influenced by the number of informants, sampling bias, or social and cultural factors that do not necessarily reflect pharmacological efficacy [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR188">188</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR197">197</xref>]. High FL values could represent cultural popularity or ease of access rather than verified therapeutic action. Therefore, the application of FL should be interpreted cautiously. Nevertheless, we believe that the information provided by FL remains valuable because it highlights plants that are repeatedly used and trusted within the community, offering a culturally grounded starting point for pharmacological validation, conservation planning, and integration into health programs.</p><p id="Par185">In Sheikh District, informants commonly reported ailments including diarrhea, wounds, eye infections, common cold, toothache, jaundice, malaria, asthma, and tick infestations. The FL analysis revealed that <italic toggle="yes">Vachellia bussei</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Balanites aegyptiaca</italic> had the highest fidelity levels (94%), reflecting strong agreement among practitioners regarding their use for diarrhea and asthma, respectively (Table <xref rid="Tab9" ref-type="table">9</xref>). This indicates that these species occupy a central role in the local ethnomedicinal knowledge system and are considered highly reliable for their targeted ailments. Other notable FL values included <italic toggle="yes">Melia azedarach</italic> for toothache (83%), <italic toggle="yes">Aloe microdonta</italic> for jaundice (77%), and <italic toggle="yes">Commelina erecta</italic> for wound healing (73%).</p><p id="Par186">The prominence of <italic toggle="yes">Vachellia bussei</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Balanites aegyptiaca</italic> aligns with ethnobotanical patterns across East Africa. <italic toggle="yes">Vachellia</italic> species in Kenya and Tanzania are widely recognized for gastrointestinal and respiratory disorders, owing to their anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and astringent properties [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR123">123</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR124">124</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR186">186</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR187">187</xref>]. Similarly, <italic toggle="yes">Balanites aegyptiaca</italic> has been reported in Ethiopia and Nigeria as an effective remedy for digestive and respiratory conditions, reflecting a cross-regional convergence of traditional knowledge in areas with similar ecological and cultural contexts [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR126">126</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR128">128</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR136">136</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR171">171</xref>].</p><p id="Par187">Species with moderate FL values, such as <italic toggle="yes">Aloe pirottae</italic> for malaria (66%) and <italic toggle="yes">Zingiber officinale</italic> for the common cold (78%), indicate plants with broader medicinal applications, often used for multiple conditions. Conversely, lower FL values, such as <italic toggle="yes">Punica granatum</italic> for eye infections (40%), suggest either less consistent efficacy, variability in practitioner knowledge, or differences in treatment preferences. Comparable patterns have been documented in India, Pakistan, and China, where multi-purpose plants often show lower FL for individual ailments but maintain broad cultural and therapeutic relevance [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR149">149</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR153">153</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR156">156</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR170">170</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR185">185</xref>].</p><p id="Par188">FL analysis complements the Informant Consensus Factor (ICF) by pinpointing species that are specifically and consistently trusted for particular conditions. High-FL species are strong candidates for pharmacological investigation, as repeated independent use across multiple informants often correlates with the presence of bioactive compounds with reliable therapeutic effects [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>].
