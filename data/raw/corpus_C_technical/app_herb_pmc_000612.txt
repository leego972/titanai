In 2015, hexadecanoic acid, α-amyrin and 14-methyl-12,13-dehydro-sitosterol-heptadeconate were isolated from the leaves of <italic toggle="yes">X. strumarium</italic>, and their antioxidant potential was also evaluated. Antibacterial and Antifungal Effects</title><p>In 1983, Mehta et al. reported that the WEXFT possessed antimicrobial properties against <italic toggle="yes">Vibrio cholera</italic> [<xref rid="B109-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">109</xref>]. Later, a study in 1997 revealed that the xanthatin isolated from the leaves of <italic toggle="yes">X. strumarium</italic> had notable potent activities against <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococus epidermidis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus cereus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Klebsiella pneumoniae</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Pseudomonas aeruginosa</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Salmonella fyphi</italic> with minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) values of 31.3, 62.5, 31.3, 125 and 125 µg/mL, respectively [<xref rid="B110-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">110</xref>]. In addition, it is reported that MEXL (500 and 100 mg/mL) exhibited strong activity against <italic toggle="yes">K. pneumoniae</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Proteus vulgaris</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">P. aeruginosa</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Pseudomonas putida</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Salmonella typhimurium</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">B. cereus, Bacillus subtilis and S. epidermidis</italic> [<xref rid="B111-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">111</xref>]. In 2015, Chen et al. also reported that β-sitosterol and β-daucosterol isolated from the <italic toggle="yes">X. strumarium</italic> have significant inhibitory effects against <italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic>, with MIC values of 0.17 and 0.35 mg/mL, respectively [<xref rid="B112-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>]. By using the disc diffusion method, Devkota et al. determined the antibacterial activity of MEXL and WEXL, and results showed that the two extracts inhibited growth towards <italic toggle="yes">K. pneumoniae, Proteus mirabilis, E. coli, B. subtilis, Enterococcus faecalis</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic> at concentrations of 50, 100, 150, 200 and 250 mg/mL [<xref rid="B113-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>]. Moreover, Sharifi-Rad et al. revealed that EOXL can significantly suppress the growth of <italic toggle="yes">S. aureus, B. subtilis, K. pneumoniae and P. aeruginosa</italic> with MIC values of 0.5, 1.3, 4.8 and 20.5 µg/mL, respectively; additionally, EOXL (30, 60 and 120 mg/mL) also exhibited obvious antibacterial activity against Shiga toxin-producing <italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic> [<xref rid="B114-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>,<xref rid="B115-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">115</xref>]. Furthermore, Wang et al. revealed that WEX possessed antibacterial potentials against <italic toggle="yes">S. aureus</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">E. coli</italic> with MIC values of 31.25 and 7.81 mg/mL, respectively [<xref rid="B116-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">116</xref>]. Using the disk diffusion, the antibacterial activity of EOXF on <italic toggle="yes">Rathayibacter toxicus</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Pyricularia oryzae</italic> was evaluated, and the MIC values were 25 and 12.5 µg/mL, respectively [<xref rid="B108-molecules-24-00359" ref-type="bibr">108</xref>].
