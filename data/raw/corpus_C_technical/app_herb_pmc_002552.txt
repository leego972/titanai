/100 g fw. More recently, polyphenolic metabolites from pineapple
peel such as gallic acid, catechin, epicatechin, and ferulic acid,
which participated in the reduction of oxidative stress-related diseases,
were identified.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref273">273</xref></sup> Bromelain, a protease,
is found in pineapple stem, core, peel, and crown.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref274">274</xref></sup> Bromelain extracted from pineapple core was reported to

the stems, Maurer<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref276">276</xref></sup> reported that the
bromelain extracted from stems was 23.8 kDa. In terms of ascorbic
acid and carotenoid content, Freitas et al.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref270">270</xref></sup> reported that pineapple core has ∼2 times higher vitamin
C content, while the rind has ∼2.5 times higher β-carotene.</p><p>Kiwi pomace, which consists of a heterogeneous mixture of skins,
seeds, calyx, and pulp,<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref277">277</xref></sup> is the primary
byproduct from the kiwi juice industry, and it accounts for 20–40%
of the whole fruit.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref278">278</xref></sup> There is limited
information in the literature on the exploitation of kiwi fruit byproducts
such as seeds or peels. Therefore, the bioactive compounds and antioxidant
properties of various parts of the kiwi fruit have not been well-investigated. Zhu et al.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref277">277</xref></sup> reported TP in kiwi pomace
(skin not included) as 421 ± 12 mg GAE/g dw. They did not observe
anthocyanins, since the kiwi fruit is not red. Furthermore, flavan-3-ol
also was not determined. They reported the major phenolic compounds
in kiwi pomace as quinic acid, caffeic acid, and its derivatives,
followed by kaempferol derivatives and a small amount of quercetin
derivatives. Kheirkhah et al.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref279">279</xref></sup> also reported
the most abundant phenolic compounds in the kiwi pomace, which were
catechin, chlorogenic acid, <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-coumaric acid, protocatechuic
acid, and caffeic acid.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref279">279</xref></sup> Soquetta et
al.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref280">280</xref></sup> produced flour from the green kiwi
fruit skin of two varieties (Bruno and Monty). The flour made from
the Bruno variety showed higher DPPH values and levels of phenolic
compounds (1262.34 mg GAE/100 g flour), while the Monty variety had
higher FRAP values, vitamin C (189.06 mg/100 g flour), flavonoids
(486.47 mg/100 g flour), chlorophylls (12.13 mg/100 g flour), and
carotenoids (246.91 g/100 g flour). Deng et al.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref281">281</xref></sup> reported that the DPPH activity of the seeds of different
species of kiwi fruit was reported to change from 25.7 to 35.1 IC<sub>50</sub> mg/mL. Salama et al.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref282">282</xref></sup> also
studied the same species, but their study was on the in vitro antioxidant
activity in peel, which has been reported as 21–47 μM
TE/g. Duman and Özcan<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref283">283</xref></sup> reported
the major fatty acids in kiwi seed as palmitic, oleic, and linoleic
acids. Wang et al.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref284">284</xref></sup> reported the phenolic
compunds in peel, seed, and flesh. In seed, the major phenolics in
different cultivars were caffeic acid, quercetin, catechin, <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-hydroxybenzoic acid, and <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-coumaric acid.</p><p>Pomegranate peel accounts for 50% of the fruit, which is rich in
several compounds including phenolics, flavonoids, ellagitannins,
and proanthocyanidin compounds.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref285">285</xref></sup> Punicalagin
and punicalin, both of which include the polyphenolic chemical component
gallagic acid, the building block for many tannins, are ellagitannins
found in the pomegranate peel. Punicalagin is a pomegranate-specific
compound found in the seeds, peels, and juice.<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="ref286">286</xref></sup> Actually, the content of tannins and flavonoids is mostly
linked to biological characteristics of pomegranate peel.
