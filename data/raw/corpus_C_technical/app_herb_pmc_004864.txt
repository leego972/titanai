This suggests that OLFM4 could serve as a diagnostic marker for human cancers (Koshida et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B58">2007</xref>; Oh et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B79">2011</xref>). Reduced expression of the <italic toggle="yes">OLFM4</italic> gene inhibits cell growth and increases sensitization to hydrogen peroxide and tumor necrosis factor alpha-induced apoptosis in gastric cancer cells (Liu et al., <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="B68">2012</xref>). Down-regulation of the <italic toggle="yes">PLCD4</italic> gene (Table <xref ref-type="table" rid="T6">6</xref>), which encodes cytoplasmatic PLC, is apparently associated with catabolic activity of adaptogens.</p></sec><sec><title>The synergy/antagonism concept and considerations on dose-effect dependency</title><p>The mechanism of action of adaptogens in T98G cells was associated with deregulation of numerous genes. Even one single compound purified from the extracts, e.g., salidroside, triandrin, tyrosol from <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic>, schizandrin B from <italic toggle="yes">Schisandra</italic>, or eleutheroside E from <italic toggle="yes">Eleutherococcus</italic> affected the activity of 500–700 genes belonging to several different functional networks.</p><p>The approximate number of genes affected by any single compound in ADAPT-232 was on the same order as the number of genes affected by the entire mixture. Even if the gene expression profile contained the same genes (Figure <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F2">2</xref>), their expression remains of the same extent (the same fold range, Tables <xref ref-type="table" rid="T5">5</xref> and <xref ref-type="table" rid="T6">6</xref> and Supplementary Material). The total number of interactions in the molecular networks did not increase proportionally with the number of active compounds in the mixture. Further, the number of target genes affected by the complex mixture was decreased in some pathways as compared to single compounds. Figure <xref ref-type="fig" rid="F2">2</xref> shows that 735 deregulated genes overlap between the single-component compounds. However, they do not account for all 1056 genes affected by ADAPT-232, suggesting that deregulation of 321 genes by ADAPT-232 is due to synergic interactions. Thus, it is possible that some of ADAPT-232’s pharmacological effects are associated with these 321 genes, supporting the synergy concept for herbal mixtures. On the other hand, the complete pool of all genes deregulated by three single extracts is 2188, while ADAPT-232 deregulates only 1056 genes. This indicates that antagonistic interactions of the three components in the mixture may result in suppression of some effects (possibly negative) of the mono-drugs in fixed combination in ADAPT-232. This is also in line with synergy/antagonism concept for herbal mixtures.</p><p>Purified single substances revealed differential regulation of more genes than did the extracts, e.g., <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> extract affected more genes than salidroside, tyrosol, or triandrin, and ADAPT-232 affected more genes than <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Schisandra</italic>, or <italic toggle="yes">Eleutherococcus</italic> extracts (Tables <xref ref-type="table" rid="T5">5</xref> and <xref ref-type="table" rid="T6">6</xref>).</p><p>For example, schizandrin B at a concentration of 3 μM significantly down-regulated a number of genes (up to 12854 fold, Table <xref ref-type="table" rid="T7">7</xref>) which were not affected in the total extract (in which the concentration of schizandrin was comparable). Moreover, the gene expression profiles of schizandrin B and <italic toggle="yes">Schisandra</italic> extract were quite different (Table <xref ref-type="table" rid="T7">7</xref>). The same is true for other compounds as shown in Tables <xref ref-type="table" rid="T6">6</xref>–<xref ref-type="table" rid="T8">8</xref>. This data may support the reductionistic concept of drug discovery common in “western medicine” that is based on the isolation and application of active constituents rather than the use of complex
