Revisiting acute retinal pigment epitheliitis (Krill disease).

Fouad YA(1), Cicinelli MV(2), Marchese A(3), Casalino G(4), Jampol LM(5).

We reevaluate acute retinal pigment epitheliitis (ARPE) first described by Krill 
and Deutman in 1972, integrating a meticulous literature review with advanced 
multimodal imaging analyses. Our review included 98 eyes from 86 published cases 
diagnosed with ARPE. We scrutinized ARPE's clinical presentations, variability, 
and imaging characteristics, revealing that a large majority (90 %) of cases 
previously diagnosed as ARPE align more closely with other retinal disorders 
based on modern diagnostic criteria and imaging techniques. Only a small 
fraction (5 eyes) did not fit into any known categories, casting doubt on ARPE's 
distinct existence. This underscores the critical role of multimodal imaging in 
redefining our understanding of macular diseases and challenges the historical 
classification of ARPE as a unique clinical entity.
