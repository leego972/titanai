Obesity and hyperlipidemia</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> inhibited the activity of lipase in isolated mouse plasma <italic toggle="yes">in vitro</italic> and in the mouse gastrointestinal tube <italic toggle="yes">in vivo</italic> and can be used in treating or preventing lifestyle-related diseases such as hyperlipidemia and exogenous obesity [<xref rid="b14-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> extracts dose-dependently increased SOD activity, resulting in a reduced ROS level during adipogenesis [<xref rid="b96-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>]. Treatment with <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> extract inhibited the activities of proline dehydrogenase (PDH) and glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PDH) as well as lipid accumulation and ROS production in 3T3-L1 preadipocytes [<xref rid="b96-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>]. In addition, SOD activity in cells treated with <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> extract increased significantly during the differentiation of 3T3-L1 preadipocytes [<xref rid="b96-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>]. The inhibition of PDH and G6PDH prevented the proline oxidation required for critical ATP generation that is coupled with the antioxidant enzyme response via the proline-mediated pentose phosphate pathway, leading to the inhibition of adipogenesis [<xref rid="b96-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>]. The antiadipogenic effects of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> extract may disrupt proline-mediated energy generation and antioxidant enzyme response via the proline-mediated pentose phosphate pathway, resulting in the suppression of adipogenesis and lipid accumulation [<xref rid="b96-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>].</p><p>Tyrosol at 1.0 mg/mL significantly increased SOD activity during the differentiation of 3T3-L1 preadipocytes [<xref rid="b96-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>]. In addition, tyrosol inhibited the activities of PDH and G6PDH and lipid accumulation [<xref rid="b96-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> extract and tyrosol may be used to prevent obesity.</p></sec><sec sec-type="other"><title>15. Anticancer</title><p>Incubation with <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola imbricata</italic> aqueous extract at 100 mg/ mL and 200 mg/mL for 72 hours significantly reduced the proliferation of K-562 human erythroleukemic cells in a dose-dependent manner but not the proliferation of normal human peripheral blood lymphocytes or RAW-264.7 mouse macrophage cells [<xref rid="b97-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>]. In addition, <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola imbricata</italic> aqueous extract was observed to induce intracellular ROS in K-562 cells at 200 mg/mL and, thus, induce apoptosis [<xref rid="b97-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola imbricata</italic> aqueous extract arrested the cell cycle progression of K562 and NK cells in the G2/M phase in early and late periods of exposure [<xref rid="b97-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>].</p><p>Salidroside significantly reduced the proliferation of A549 human alveolar adenocarcinoma cells, inhibited cell cycle arrest in the G0/G1 phase, and induced apoptosis in A549 cells by reducing <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>p38 protein expression [<xref rid="b98-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">98</xref>]. Salidroside inhibited transforming growth factor-β-induced tumor invasion and suppressed protein expression [<xref rid="b98-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">98</xref>]. Salidroside inhibited intracellular ROS formation in a dose-dependent manner in A549 cells [<xref rid="b98-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">98</xref>]. Another study reported that salidroside significantly reduced tumor-induced angiogenesis in mice [<xref rid="b2-jfda-23-03-359" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>].</p></sec><sec sec-type="other"><title>16.
