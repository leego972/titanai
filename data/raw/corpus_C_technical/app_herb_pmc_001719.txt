[<xref rid="B14-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>], which revealed 16 volatiles. Phytochemicals from Leaves</title><p>Some flavonoid derivatives such as astragalin <bold>23</bold> [<xref rid="B27-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>], catechin derivatives (<bold>14</bold>, <bold>15</bold>), quercetin derivatives (<bold>3</bold>–<bold>5</bold>), myricetin derivatives (<bold>10</bold>–<bold>13</bold>), rutin <bold>6</bold>, naringenin <bold>42</bold>, taxifolin <bold>41</bold>, and luteolin derivatives (<bold>19</bold>–<bold>21</bold>) were identified in leaves collected in Egypt [<xref rid="B25-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>,<xref rid="B28-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>] and Saudi Arabia [<xref rid="B13-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>,<xref rid="B17-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>], as shown in <xref rid="molecules-28-04396-f001" ref-type="fig">Figure 1</xref>. HPLC-based studies conducted by Guneidy et al. [<xref rid="B28-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>], Elansary et al. [<xref rid="B13-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>], and Gumgumjee et al. [<xref rid="B17-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>] reported that crude extracts from leaves contained rich compositions of benzoic acid derivatives such as gallic acid <bold>25</bold>, syringic acid <bold>28</bold>, vanillin <bold>29</bold>, protocatechuic acid <bold>30</bold>, and <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-hydroxybenzoic <bold>31</bold> (<xref rid="molecules-28-04396-f001" ref-type="fig">Figure 1</xref>). Of note were cinnamic acid <bold>34</bold>, caffeic acid <bold>35</bold>, <italic toggle="yes">p</italic>-coumaric acid <bold>37</bold>, ferulic acid <bold>38</bold>, chlorogenic acid <bold>40</bold>, and other phenolic esters, including methyl gallate <bold>26</bold>, propyl gallate <bold>27,</bold> and ellagic acid <bold>44</bold>. </p><p>The sequential solvent polarity extraction of leaves collected in Australia provided a bioactive methanolic extract. Upon two successive purifications via column chromatography of this extract, we obtained (–)-epicatechin <bold>51</bold>, quercitrin <bold>4</bold>, myricitrin <bold>11</bold>, 2,4-di-<italic toggle="yes">t</italic>-butylphenol, (−)-pinitol <bold>49</bold>, and (3<italic toggle="yes">S,</italic>5<italic toggle="yes">S</italic>)-3-hydroxy-5-(2-aminoethyl)-dihydrofuran-2(3H)-one <bold>52</bold>. The latter three compounds were the first to be isolated from <italic toggle="yes">A. saligna</italic> growing in Australia and in other parts of the world [<xref rid="B26-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>].</p><fig position="anchor" id="molecules-28-04396-f001" orientation="portrait"><label>Figure 1</label><caption><p>Phenolic and flavonoid derivatives identified in flowers [<xref rid="B11-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>,<xref rid="B12-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>,<xref rid="B26-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>], leaves [<xref rid="B13-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>,<xref rid="B16-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>,<xref rid="B25-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>,<xref rid="B26-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>,<xref rid="B29-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>], and the bark [<xref rid="B18-molecules-28-04396" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>] of <italic toggle="yes">A.
