In studies of this kind, influences from the existence of some minor unidentified constituents with antimicrobial properties in the extracts cannot be neglected [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref060" ref-type="bibr">60</xref>]. The FT-IR analysis performed in this study revealed that oil of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds extracted with absolute ethanol is enriched with different kinds of phytoconstituents whose associated characteristic functional groups (antibacterial features) such as O–H (in phenolic compounds, flavonoids, and alcohols), C–H (for terpenes) and esters (C = O) could be responsible for the inhibition of some parts of the bacterial cell or account for antibacterial activity [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref013" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0272457.ref033" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>]. The antimicrobial activity of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds oil extracted using hexane may be attributed to the synergism effect of two of its bioactive compounds identified by GC-MS analysis which can exhibit antimicrobial activity, toluene and m-cymene [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref044" ref-type="bibr">44</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0272457.ref045" ref-type="bibr">45</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0272457.ref048" ref-type="bibr">48</xref>] which need to be confirmed by further studies.</p><p>Phytoconstituents in the different extracts and/or oil of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds can interact with the membranes of the bacteria and eventually cause disruption with the aid of lipophilic products (<xref rid="pone.0272457.g006" ref-type="fig">Fig 6</xref>). Concerning the mechanism of inhibition of bacterial growth, the bacterial cell membrane can be forced to break down and release the content of the cell. In general, the bacterial cells die due to such breakdowns which trigger inhibition of respiration and increase membrane fluidity and permeability of both bacterial stains [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref013" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>]. This study observed that different preparations of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds have different constituents occurring in varying amount and this could be the reason for the differential inhibitory activity against the bacteria. Literature reports revealed that the antibacterial activity depends on the extraction technique employed used to extract the bioactive phytochemicals from the seeds, the structure of the bioactive components in the extracts and the polarity of the bioactive components in the extracts [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref017" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0272457.ref025" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>, <xref rid="pone.0272457.ref039" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>].</p><p>Irregular zones of inhibition were observed in this study around the discs (<xref rid="pone.0272457.g006" ref-type="fig">Fig 6</xref>) and Khan et al. [<xref rid="pone.0272457.ref017" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>] had reported that it may be due to improper diffusion of the extracts or uneven growth of the bacteria at a particular area of the nutrient agar. Most of these sources of variations (i.e., the type of extractant used, extraction technique employed, the type of pathogenic bacteria strains used and variation due to error) were confirmed by the two-factor ANOVA statistical analysis done on mean values of replication of the zones of inhibition of different extracts and/or oil of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds and the results revealed that they were highly significant at p&lt;0.05. The antibacterial activity displayed by different preparations of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>. <italic toggle="yes">sativa</italic> seeds were consistent with both qualitative and quantitative investigations regarding their composition (Tables <xref rid="pone.0272457.t001" ref-type="table">1</xref> and <xref rid="pone.0272457.t002" ref-type="table">2</xref>), and structural characterization by FT-IR and GC-MS techniques (Figs <xref rid="pone.0272457.g003" ref-type="fig">3</xref>–<xref rid="pone.0272457.g005" ref-type="fig">5</xref>), verifying that the data of the zones of inhibition were accurate and reliable (<xref rid="pone.0272457.t004" ref-type="table">Table 4</xref>). The standard deviations for all the statistical analysis done in this study were lower, indicating that the values are clustered around the true mean of the set (Tables <xref rid="pone.0272457.t002" ref-type="table">2</xref> and <xref rid="pone.0272457.t004" ref-type="table">4</xref>).</p><p>Although this study offers strong insight into the phytochemical compositions and antimicrobial activity of <italic toggle="yes">N</italic>.
