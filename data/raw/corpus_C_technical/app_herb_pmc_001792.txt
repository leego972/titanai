# PMC: phytochemical bioactive compound anticancer — Overview

ref-type="bibr">191</xref>, <xref rid="B193" ref-type="bibr">193</xref>, <xref rid="B197" ref-type="bibr">197</xref>–<xref rid="B199" ref-type="bibr">199</xref>).</p><p>Scopoletin is a compound of interest in cancer research, as it has been shown to have apoptotic, cytotoxic, anti-proliferative, anti-angiogenic, and anti-metastatic activities in various cancer cell lines (<xref rid="B97" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>, <xref rid="B203" ref-type="bibr">203</xref>–<xref rid="B212" ref-type="bibr">212</xref>). Efforts are ongoing to develop synthetic derivatives of scopoletin and to characterize and improve its bioavailability and pharmacokinetic properties (<xref rid="B96" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>). Finally, a handful of other bioactivities have been reported for scopoletin, among them the promotion of melanogenesis (<xref rid="B296" ref-type="bibr">296</xref>–<xref rid="B298" ref-type="bibr">298</xref>) and osteoprotective (<xref rid="B292" ref-type="bibr">292</xref>, <xref rid="B293" ref-type="bibr">293</xref>), antitussive (<xref rid="B515" ref-type="bibr">515</xref>), gastrokinetic (<xref rid="B294" ref-type="bibr">294</xref>), and antimicrobial properties (<xref rid="B165" ref-type="bibr">165</xref>, <xref rid="B265" ref-type="bibr">265</xref>–<xref rid="B278" ref-type="bibr">278</xref>, <xref rid="B516" ref-type="bibr">516</xref>), as well as anti-aging effects in skin and lung fibroblasts (<xref rid="B289" ref-type="bibr">289</xref>, <xref rid="B290" ref-type="bibr">290</xref>).</p><p>It is notable that many of the same signaling pathways are modulated by scopoletin across the wide variety of experimental models and conditions in which it has been investigated. Scopoletin can activate the AMPK pathway (<xref rid="B181" ref-type="bibr">181</xref>, <xref rid="B182" ref-type="bibr">182</xref>, <xref rid="B186" ref-type="bibr">186</xref>, <xref rid="B263" ref-type="bibr">263</xref>, <xref rid="B514" ref-type="bibr">514</xref>) and the PI3K/AKT pathway (<xref rid="B184" ref-type="bibr">184</xref>, <xref rid="B186" ref-type="bibr">186</xref>, <xref rid="B188" ref-type="bibr">188</xref>, <xref rid="B514" ref-type="bibr">514</xref>), and inhibit the inflammatory TLR4/myeloid differentiation factor 88 (MyD88) and NF-κB pathways (<xref rid="B94" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>, <xref rid="B181" ref-type="bibr">181</xref>, <xref rid="B183" ref-type="bibr">183</xref>, <xref rid="B233" ref-type="bibr">233</xref>, <xref rid="B237" ref-type="bibr">237</xref>, <xref rid="B241" ref-type="bibr">241</xref>, <xref rid="B251" ref-type="bibr">251</xref>, <xref rid="B252" ref-type="bibr">252</xref>, <xref rid="B517" ref-type="bibr">517</xref>, <xref rid="B518" ref-type="bibr">518</xref>). There is also substantial evidence that scopoletin can inhibit the MAPK pathway in a variety of cell types (<xref rid="B199" ref-type="bibr">199</xref>, <xref rid="B235" ref-type="bibr">235</xref>, <xref rid="B237" ref-type="bibr">237</xref>, <xref rid="B241" ref-type="bibr">241</xref>, <xref rid="B252" ref-type="bibr">252</xref>, <xref rid="B290" ref-type="bibr">290</xref>, <xref rid="B297" ref-type="bibr">297</xref>).</p></sec><sec id="s4_1_3"><title>Esculetin</title><p>Like scoparone and scopoletin, esculetin’s well-documented antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties are central to its beneficial effects in biological systems (<xref rid="B226" ref-type="bibr">226</xref>, <xref rid="B350" ref-type="bibr">350</xref>–<xref rid="B355" ref-type="bibr">355</xref>, <xref rid="B384" ref-type="bibr">384</xref>, <xref rid="B519" ref-type="bibr">519</xref>). A broad range of inflammatory or oxidative conditions have been shown to be impacted by esculetin. These include lung injury and fibrosis (<xref rid="B356" ref-type="bibr">356</xref>, <xref rid="B357" ref-type="bibr">357</xref>, <xref rid="B520" ref-type="bibr">520</xref>); fibromyalgia (<xref rid="B521" ref-type="bibr">521</xref>); neuronal oxidative stress (<xref rid="B522" ref-type="bibr">522</xref>); psoriasis (<xref rid="B523" ref-type="bibr">523</xref>); arthritis (<xref rid="B358" ref-type="bibr">358</xref>); nociception (<xref rid="B359" ref-type="bibr">359</xref>); colitis (<xref rid="B360" ref-type="bibr">360</xref>); allergy, immunity, and asthma (<xref rid="B282" ref-type="bibr">282</xref>, <xref rid="B354" ref-type="bibr">354</xref>, <xref rid="B380" ref-type="bibr">380</xref>–<xref rid="B383" ref-type="bibr">383</xref>); and sepsis (<xref rid="B361" ref-type="bibr">361</xref>). The effects of esculetin on numerous cancer cell types have been extensively studied (<xref rid="B94" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>, <xref rid="B321" ref-type="bibr">321</xref>–<xref rid="B342" ref-type="bibr">342</xref>, <xref rid="B519" ref-type="bibr">519</xref>, <xref rid="B524" ref-type="bibr">524</xref>, <xref rid="B525" ref-type="bibr">525</xref>) and are the basis for efforts to develop novel therapeutics (<xref rid="B98" ref-type="bibr">98</xref>, <xref rid="B341" ref-type="bibr">341</xref>). Neuroprotective and behavioral actions of esculetin have also been described (<xref rid="B111" ref-type="bibr">111</xref>, <xref rid="B299" ref-type="bibr">299</xref>, <xref rid="B367" ref-type="bibr">367</xref>–<xref rid="B371" ref-type="bibr">371</xref>, <xref rid="B526" ref-type="bibr">526</xref>–<xref rid="B528" ref-type="bibr">528</xref>).
