Rosemary extract is a spice. It belongs to the <italic toggle="yes">Lamiaceae</italic> family and is also used in medicine for its many therapeutic properties and high antioxidant and antibacterial activity, thanks to the presence of active compounds: phenolic diterpenes (carnosic acid, but also carnosol, rosmanol and epi- and isorosmanol), flavonoids, and terpenoids. The healing of oral ulcers and gingivitis has been improved by its use in dentistry. <italic toggle="yes">Rosmarinus</italic> extract, a Lamiaceae spice with strong antioxidant and antibacterial properties, aids oral ulcer and gingivitis healing due to its phenolic diterpenes, flavonoids, and terpenoids. Ginger, <italic toggle="yes">Zingiber officinale Roscoe</italic>, a culinary spice, is widely used in phytotherapy. It has antimicrobial, antifungal, and pharmaceutical properties due to the presence of gingerol <italic toggle="yes">zingiber</italic> [<xref rid="B82-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">82</xref>,<xref rid="B83-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">83</xref>,<xref rid="B84-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">84</xref>,<xref rid="B85-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">85</xref>]. The mechanisms by which ginger <italic toggle="yes">zingiber</italic> inhibits the growth of oral bacteria that contribute to PD are still poorly understood [<xref rid="B34-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>,<xref rid="B73-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">73</xref>,<xref rid="B74-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">74</xref>,<xref rid="B75-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>,<xref rid="B76-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>,<xref rid="B86-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>,<xref rid="B87-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">87</xref>]. However, it was found that ginger reduces PR synthesis and that two highly alkylated extracts in particular inhibit three anaerobic Gram-negative bacteria that cause PD: <italic toggle="yes">Porphyromonas gingivalis</italic> ATCC (American Type Culture Collection) 53978, <italic toggle="yes">Porphyromonas endodontalis</italic> ATCC 35406, and <italic toggle="yes">Prevotella intermedia</italic> ATCC 25611 at low doses (6–30 µg/mL). It involves the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines, particularly IL-1β, PGE2 (linked to bone resorption and PD severity), and MMPs (especially MMP-8 and MMP-9), which degrade collagen and contribute to periodontal tissue destruction [<xref rid="B77-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>,<xref rid="B78-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">78</xref>,<xref rid="B79-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">79</xref>,<xref rid="B80-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>,<xref rid="B81-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>,<xref rid="B88-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">88</xref>,<xref rid="B89-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">89</xref>,<xref rid="B90-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">90</xref>,<xref rid="B91-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">91</xref>,<xref rid="B92-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">92</xref>,<xref rid="B93-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">93</xref>,<xref rid="B94-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>,<xref rid="B95-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>]. The triterpenoids in the CO<sub>2</sub> (carbon dioxide) extract of calendula flowers, including faradiol monoester, demonstrated predominant anti-inflammatory activity [<xref rid="B96-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>,<xref rid="B97-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>,<xref rid="B98-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">98</xref>,<xref rid="B99-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>,<xref rid="B100-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">100</xref>,<xref rid="B101-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">101</xref>,<xref rid="B102-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>,<xref rid="B103-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">103</xref>,<xref rid="B104-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">104</xref>,<xref rid="B105-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">105</xref>,<xref rid="B106-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>].
