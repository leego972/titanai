However, current data remain insufficient to establish whether <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> L. significantly influences ACTH or cortisol release.</p><p id="Par9">More than 140 compounds have been isolated from <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea L.</italic> [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR29">29</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR33">33</xref>]. Among these, salidroside (rhodioloside), trans-cinnamyl alcohol glycoside compounds (such as rosin, rosavin and rosarin), and tyrosol are considered the most critical constituents for its therapeutic activity [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR34">34</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR36">36</xref>]. Notably, rosavin is unique to <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea L.</italic> within the <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> genus, whereas salidroside and tyrosol are commonly found in other <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola</italic> species [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR37">37</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR38">38</xref>].</p><p id="Par10">Typically, preparations of Rhodiola rosea L. are standardized to contain 1% salidroside and 3% rosavin [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR39">39</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR41">41</xref>]. Salidroside and rosavin are generally regarded as the key adaptogenic compounds in herbal medicinal products and dietary supplements. Several preclinical [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR31">31</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR42">42</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR47">47</xref>] and clinical studies [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR48">48</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR52">52</xref>] have demonstrated that <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> root extracts may serve as effective natural remedies for improving mental and cognitive performance under stress. However, these studies have exclusively focused on root extracts, while no published research has yet examined the effects of the whole root powder.</p><p id="Par11">Root powder preserves the complete phytochemical spectrum of the plant [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR53">53</xref>], including minor compounds that may act synergistically rather than isolating individual molecules [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR33">33</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR54">54</xref>]. The powder used in this study was standardized to 3% salidroside, higher than typical extract formulations (1% salidroside, 3% rosavin), making it a unique preparation that could elicit different or stronger adaptogenic effects.</p><p id="Par12">To the best of our knowledge, this is the first experimental research to describe the anxiolytic and corticosterone-reducing effects of whole root powder standardized to 3% salidroside in a chronic stress model. Unlike prior work that often employed acute stress paradigms or tested extracts in healthy animals, our investigation specifically evaluated root powder under chronic mild stress conditions, a model more relevant to human stress-related disorders. Furthermore, administration in a gummy format ensured accurate, stress-free dosing and represents a practical delivery system translatable to human use. Together, these findings highlight that <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> root powder offers a minimally processed, sustainable, and effective alternative to standardized extracts, expanding the therapeutic potential of this adaptogenic plant for stress management.</p><p id="Par13">The aim of this study was to evaluate the potential effects of <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> L. root powder, with high level of salidroside (3%), on a murine model of chronic stress. For this purpose, a murine model of chronic stress was established using repeated mild stress exposure. The impact of daily <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea L.</italic> root powder administration during the stress period was then assessed. At the end of the experiment, stress levels were evaluated by measuring anxiety-like behavior and corticosterone levels. The results confirm that <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> L. root powder significantly modulates both physiological and behavioral markers of stress.</p></sec><sec id="Sec2"><title>Material &amp; methods</title><sec id="Sec3"><title>Animals</title><p id="Par14">The guidelines for animal welfare were approved by the Committee on Animal Research of the Université de Mons (ref RI-01501).</p><p id="Par15">8-weeks-old C57BL6 female mice were supplied by Charles River (agreement: C 69 208 1301).
