The backscattering coefficient of the sample, detected within the NA of the OCT system, has been extracted from the OCT amplitude after correcting for system parameters.<xref rid="r8" ref-type="bibr"><sup>8</sup></xref><sup>,</sup><xref rid="r28" ref-type="bibr"><sup>28</sup></xref><sup>,</sup><xref rid="r54" ref-type="bibr"><sup>54</sup></xref><sup>,</sup><xref rid="r128" ref-type="bibr"><sup>128</sup></xref><named-content content-type="online"><xref rid="r129" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="r130" ref-type="bibr"/></named-content><named-content content-type="print"><sup>–</sup></named-content><xref rid="r131" ref-type="bibr"><sup>131</sup></xref> Xu et al.<xref rid="r8" ref-type="bibr"><sup>8</sup></xref> and Liu et al.,<xref rid="r40" ref-type="bibr"><sup>40</sup></xref> respectively, added the backscattering coefficient and backscatter term (linearly related to the logarithm of the backscatter coefficient) to the attenuation coefficient, providing an approach to further differentiate atherosclerotic tissue components with similar attenuation coefficients.</p><p>The simultaneous measurement of attenuation and backscattering coefficients also allows the extraction of further parameters, including the anisotropy and the size of the scatterers. Kodach et al.<xref rid="r128" ref-type="bibr"><sup>128</sup></xref> calculated the ratio of backscattering coefficient to the total scattering coefficient (extracted from the OCT signal) as the phase function integrated over the NA (<inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math392"><mml:mrow><mml:msub><mml:mrow><mml:mi>p</mml:mi></mml:mrow><mml:mrow><mml:mi>NA</mml:mi></mml:mrow></mml:msub></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula>). They then built two calibration functions, including <inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math393"><mml:mrow><mml:msub><mml:mrow><mml:mi>p</mml:mi></mml:mrow><mml:mrow><mml:mi>NA</mml:mi></mml:mrow></mml:msub></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula> versus particle diameter and anisotropy versus particle diameter, aided by Mie theory. The OCT-estimated <inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math394"><mml:mrow><mml:msub><mml:mrow><mml:mi>p</mml:mi></mml:mrow><mml:mrow><mml:mi>NA</mml:mi></mml:mrow></mml:msub></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula> of an Intralipid sample was input into the <inline-formula><mml:math xmlns:mml="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" id="math395"><mml:mrow><mml:msub><mml:mrow><mml:mi>p</mml:mi></mml:mrow><mml:mrow><mml:mi>NA</mml:mi></mml:mrow></mml:msub></mml:mrow></mml:math></inline-formula> versus particle diameter function to decide the scatterer size, which was further input into the anisotropy versus particle diameter function to decide the anisotropy. A similar approach has been used by Schneider et al.<xref rid="r129" ref-type="bibr"><sup>129</sup></xref> for measuring the size of dispersed polystyrene nanoparticles. Levitz et al.<xref rid="r54" ref-type="bibr"><sup>54</sup></xref><sup>,</sup><xref rid="r130" ref-type="bibr"><sup>130</sup></xref> proposed the simultaneous calculation of attenuation and local reflectance, which were then fed into the calibration grid in the attenuation–local reflectance map to extract the anisotropy of developing collagen gels. To our knowledge, these methods for estimating the scatterer anisotropy and size have not yet been demonstrated on biological samples.</p><p>As described above, PS-OCT detects the polarization states of the reflected OCT light, aided by polarization diversity detection.<xref rid="r123" ref-type="bibr"><sup>123</sup></xref> Estimation of the birefringence and related polarization parameters (e.g., degree of polarization uniformity and optic axis orientation) represents an interesting alternative form of parametric imaging both with versions that integrate over similar axial regions as OCT attenuation imaging<xref rid="r72" ref-type="bibr"><sup>72</sup></xref><sup>,</sup><xref rid="r132" ref-type="bibr"><sup>132</sup></xref> and local versions.<xref rid="r133" ref-type="bibr"><sup>133</sup></xref><sup>,</sup><xref rid="r134" ref-type="bibr"><sup>134</sup></xref> Finally, retrieval of diattenuation—the polarization state-dependent differential attenuation—is also possible via PS-OCT but to the authors’ knowledge is thought to be a small effect and has not yet been widely investigated.<xref rid="r135" ref-type="bibr"><sup>135</sup></xref><sup>,</sup><xref rid="r136" ref-type="bibr"><sup>136</sup></xref> Also based on polarization detection, Yashin et al.<xref rid="r94" ref-type="bibr"><sup>94</sup></xref> recently used cross-polarization OCT to simultaneously measure the attenuation coefficients of the co- and cross-polarized detected light.
