Moreover, it has been reported that ethanol, methanol, and water broccoli seed extracts showed an inhibitory effect against Gram-negative bacteria, e.g., <italic toggle="yes">Staphylococcus aureus</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus subtilis</italic>, and Gram-positive bacteria, e.g., <italic toggle="yes">Salmonella typhimurium</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Escherichia coli</italic> [<xref rid="B77-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">77</xref>].</p><p>Isolation of antimicrobial proteins and peptides, identified as APPs, from seeds of <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> species is immensely increased owing to their remarkable antimicrobial activity with various mechanisms of action. These natural chemicals are being studied as anti-infectives [<xref rid="B166-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">166</xref>]. Several AMPs from <italic toggle="yes">Brassica</italic> seeds have been isolated with promising antimicrobial potential against numerous pathogens responsible for microbial infections. Indeed, the 8.5 kDa purified antifungal peptide, BGAP, from the crude extract of <italic toggle="yes">B. oleracea</italic> var. <italic toggle="yes">gongylodes</italic> seeds demonstrated a large antifungal spectrum, mainly against <italic toggle="yes">Colletotrichum higginsianum</italic> (IC<sub>50</sub>=17.33 μg/mL), <italic toggle="yes">Exserohilum turcicum</italic> (IC<sub>50</sub>=12.37 μg/mL), <italic toggle="yes">Magnaporthe oryzae</italic> (IC<sub>50</sub>=16.81 μg/mL), and <italic toggle="yes">Mycosphaerella arachidicola</italic> (IC<sub>50</sub>=5.60 μg/mL) [<xref rid="B167-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">167</xref>]. Another study found that broccoli seed peptides BraDef1 and BraDef2, identified as class I definsins, provided antimicrobial properties against fungi such as <italic toggle="yes">Colletotrichum gloeosporioides</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Alternaria alternata</italic> and against pathogenic bacteria strains such as <italic toggle="yes">Bacillus cereus 183</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Pseudomonas aeruginosa</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Listeria monocytogenes</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Vibrio parahaemolyticus ATCC 17,802,</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Salmonella typhimurium</italic> [<xref rid="B168-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">168</xref>]. Furthermore, a novel antifungal protein (15 kDa), a napin-like protein named broccoli napin (BoNap), was isolated from <italic toggle="yes">B. oleracea L</italic>. var. <italic toggle="yes">italica</italic> and shown to be responsible for blocking the germination of <italic toggle="yes">Fusarium culmorum</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Penicillium expansum</italic>’s spores with a minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) value of 2.31 µΜ. Moreover, BoNap was found to induce the permeabilization of mycelium membrane, trypsin-inhibitory properties, and the reduction of fungal contamination of cereals [<xref rid="B169-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">169</xref>]. <italic toggle="yes">Allergen Sin a1</italic>, the 14 kDa mustard Napin protein purified from <italic toggle="yes">B. hirta</italic> seeds (white mustard), was determined as a potent bioactive agent against a broad spectrum of microbial strains, especially <italic toggle="yes">Saccharomyces cerevisiae DSM 70449</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Zygosaccharomyces bailii Sa 1403,</italic> and Fusarium <italic toggle="yes">culmorum FST 4.05</italic>, with an MIC range between 3 μM and 6 μM, and with a noncytotoxic effect in mammalian cells. <italic toggle="yes">Sin a1</italic> is a stable protein, resistant to α-chymotrypsin digestion, high temperature, pH changes, and salts [<xref rid="B170-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">170</xref>]. The purified peptide, <italic toggle="yes">γ-thionin</italic> (BoT), from <italic toggle="yes">B. oleracea</italic> seeds was reported as a potent antifungal agent against <italic toggle="yes">Aspergillus niger</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Aspergillus flavus</italic> at 2 μM concentration and led to the death of the insects <italic toggle="yes">Tribolium castaneum</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Sitophilus oryzae</italic> at 0.12 μM concentration [<xref rid="B171-molecules-27-06008" ref-type="bibr">171</xref>].</p><p>Beyond their anti-cancer potential, glucosinolates, especially sinigrin (prop-2-enylglucosinolate), glucotropeolin (benzylglucosinolate), and gluconasturtiin (phenethylglucosinolate), isolated from mustard seed meal, are characterized as potent bioactive agents against different fungi such as <italic toggle="yes">Botrytis cinerea</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Fusarium oxysporum</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Aphanomyces euteiches var.
