Clinical Implications of Structure–Activity Correlations</title><p>The therapeutic efficacy of plant-derived compounds in managing periodontal diseases is largely determined by their chemical structure, which governs their bioavailability and biological activity. Understanding these structure–activity relationships can help elucidate the mechanisms through which natural compounds exert their antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant effects in periodontal therapy.</p><p><italic toggle="yes">Polyphenols</italic> (e.g., catechins from <italic toggle="yes">Camellia sinensis</italic>): the presence of multiple hydroxyl groups enables metal ion chelation and free radical neutralization, reducing oxidative stress in periodontal tissues. Additionally, catechins interact with bacterial membrane proteins, inhibiting bacterial adhesion and biofilm formation.</p><p>Ellagitannins (e.g., punicalagins from <italic toggle="yes">Punica granatum</italic>): These tannins act as potent MMP inhibitors, preventing the degradation of collagen and extracellular matrix components. They also suppress IL-1β and TNF-α production, thereby modulating inflammatory responses and limiting tissue destruction.</p><p>Terpenoids (e.g., carnosic acid from <italic toggle="yes">Rosmarinus officinalis</italic>): Due to their lipophilic nature, terpenoids integrate into bacterial membranes, increasing their permeability and leading to cell lysis. This mechanism underlies their broad-spectrum antimicrobial properties.</p><p>Flavonoids (e.g., quercetin from <italic toggle="yes">Cinnamomum verum</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">Zingiber officinale</italic>): Flavonoids regulate NF-κB signaling pathways, thereby reducing the transcription of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β, TNF-α, and PGE2. This anti-inflammatory action helps control gingival inflammation and periodontal tissue damage.</p><p>Polysaccharides (e.g., acemannan from <italic toggle="yes">Aloe vera</italic>): These compounds stimulate fibroblast proliferation and enhance collagen synthesis, promoting tissue regeneration and wound healing. Additionally, acemannan exhibits immune-modulatory effects, reducing inflammatory mediator release [<xref rid="B160-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">160</xref>,<xref rid="B161-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">161</xref>,<xref rid="B162-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">162</xref>,<xref rid="B163-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">163</xref>,<xref rid="B164-nutrients-17-00762" ref-type="bibr">164</xref>].</p><p>These structure–activity correlations highlight the pharmacological potential of phytochemicals in periodontal therapy. By targeting key molecular pathways involved in oxidative stress, bacterial proliferation, and inflammation, plant-based compounds serve as valuable adjuncts to conventional periodontal treatments. However, further studies are needed to standardize formulations, optimize bioavailability, and confirm long-term clinical efficacy.</p></sec></sec><sec sec-type="conclusions" id="sec5-nutrients-17-00762"><title>5. Conclusions</title><p>This systematic review collects various clinical studies that explore the use of natural remedies and supplements as complementary treatments for PD, including gingivitis and PD. The conclusions of this systematic review are as follows:<list list-type="simple"><list-item><label>-</label><p>Potential of Plant-Based Remedies: Natural extracts like <italic toggle="yes">Camellia sinensis</italic>, cinnamon, <italic toggle="yes">aloe vera</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">rosmarinus</italic> show promise in reducing inflammation, plaque, and bleeding in PD.</p></list-item><list-item><label>-</label><p>Effectiveness Compared to Conventional Treatments: While these remedies offer benefits, they often do not surpass conventional options like CHX or antibiotic gels.</p></list-item><list-item><label>-</label><p>Safer Alternative with Fewer Side Effects: Some plant-based treatments, such as cinnamon, have performed similarly to CHX but without side effects like dental staining.</p></list-item><list-item><label>-</label><p>Temporary and Variable Effects: The benefits of these natural remedies are often short-lived, and results vary across studies, highlighting the need for standardization.</p></list-item></list></p><p>Overall, this review supports the idea that some natural extracts could be a valuable aid in the management of PD, but further investigations, with larger sample sizes and longer follow-ups, are necessary to determine their definitive role as first-line treatments or as supplements to existing therapies.</p></sec></body><back><fn-group><fn><p><bold>Disclaimer/Publisher’s Note:</bold> The statements, opinions and data contained in all publications are solely those of the individual author(s) and contributor(s) and not of MDPI and/or the editor(s).
