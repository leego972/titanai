2016</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><sec id="Sec1"><title>Background</title><p>The healing of skin wounds represents an ongoing challenge in both human and veterinary medicine [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR1">1</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR2">2</xref>]. The care and management of both acute and chronic cutaneous wounds can be time consuming and may have significant health and economic consequences [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR2">2</xref>]. An emerging treatment modality involves the use of laser therapy, further described as low-level laser therapy (LLLT), which has been postulated to accelerate healing of damaged tissues and improve clinical outcomes in several independent research [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR3">3</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR4">4</xref>]. Despite the positive results demonstrated in previous reports, the effectiveness and underlying mechanisms of this treatment modality remain largely speculative and unresolved [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR4">4</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR5">5</xref>].</p><p>Wound healing was one of the first clinical applications of LLLT following its discovery by Endre Mester in 1967 [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR6">6</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR7">7</xref>]. It has been suggested that the use of this treatment modality on cutaneous wounds provides several benefits, including pain control, reduction of inflammation, modulation of the immune system, and acceleration of wound healing [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR6">6</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">9</xref>]. In recent years, the medical applications of LLLT in human medicine have expanded to include the treatment of many conditions such as neurological processes, musculoskeletal injuries, soft tissue injury, abscesses, dental disease, and various dermatologic ailments [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR9">9</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR10">10</xref>].</p><p>Low-level laser therapy causes low or imperceptible temperature changes to the treatment area [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR11">11</xref>]. At the cellular level, the effect of LLLT is thought to be secondary to the absorption of red and near-infrared light by cytochrome c oxidase, improving electron transport, production of adenosine triphosphate (ATP), release of nitric oxide, and the modulation of reactive oxygen species [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR10">10</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>]. Although LLLT is believed to result in increased cellular metabolism via activation of the cellular respiratory chain [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR12">12</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR13">13</xref>], other mechanisms of action have also been shown in recent literature which were associated to the effectiveness of this treatment modality [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR14">14</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR16">16</xref>].
