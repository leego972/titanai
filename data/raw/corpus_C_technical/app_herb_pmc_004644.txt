Adaptogens act as mild stressors, triggering adaptive stress response pathways of body cells regulated by the neuroendocrine-immune complex and activating the defense response of the organism after single or repeated administration in the appropriate dose range (<xref rid="pharmaceuticals-18-00261-f004" ref-type="fig">Figure 4</xref>). Under the stressor, we imply negatively affecting environmental factors of psychological, physical, viral, bacterial, and chemical origins.</p><p>Resistance to stress and survival depends on adaptability and the thresholds determining an organism’s innate tolerance to a given stress level. <xref rid="pharmaceuticals-18-00261-f005" ref-type="fig">Figure 5</xref> shows a hypothetical model of regulation homeostasis response to adaptogens.</p><p>The adaptogenic effect covers various pharmacological activities (adaptogenic, stress-protective, stimulating, and pleiotropic pharmacological profile) in stress-induced and aging-related disorders. Potential indications for use and health claims of adaptogens are stress-induced fatigue, mental and behavioral disorders, infectious diseases, and aging-associated disorders (<xref rid="pharmaceuticals-18-00261-f006" ref-type="fig">Figure 6</xref>). <xref rid="pharmaceuticals-18-00261-f006" ref-type="fig">Figure 6</xref> shows adaptive signaling pathways by which animals and human subjects respond to the challenges of food deprivation/fasting, running, and adaptogens.</p><p>The ability of some plant secondary metabolites to activate the adaptive stress response in the human body is one of the essential mechanisms of action of adaptogens. <xref rid="pharmaceuticals-18-00261-f005" ref-type="fig">Figure 5</xref> and <xref rid="pharmaceuticals-18-00261-f006" ref-type="fig">Figure 6</xref> illustrate the simple fundamental concept that mild stressors, including adaptogen supplementation, promote optimal health. Humans developed the ability to consume various bitter plant species, which exert mild noxious effects on cells, presumably by triggering cellular defense responses that protect cells against harmful stressors. It was hypothesized that some of the evolutionary metabolites of plants play a role in defense against various environmental stressors, including microorganisms, insects, UV, etc. At relatively small doses, they are not harmful in humans but induce mild adaptive stress responses in humans. Many natural compounds and botanicals exhibit dual (hormetic) dose-dependent reverse effects in low or high doses. For example, some plants are known as neurotoxic, e.g., <italic toggle="yes">Aconitum napellus</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Atropa belladonna</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Conium maculatum</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Oenanthe crocata</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Ricinus communis</italic> [<xref rid="B208-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">208</xref>], and are used in small doses in homeopathy. In contrast, other plants, including <italic toggle="yes">Ginkgo biloba</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Curcuma longa</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Panax ginseng</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Withania somnifera</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Centella asiatica</italic>, and other adaptogenic plants, have neuroprotective activity on the human brain, demonstrating antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and cognitive-enhancing properties, making them promising candidates for combating neurodegenerative diseases and improving brain function. Meanwhile, the same plant, e.g., <italic toggle="yes">Bryonia alba</italic> L., can be toxic (freshly collected in summer) or harmless (collected in fall and dried), depending on the harvesting season and processing methods of herbal substances, due to the metabolism of toxic cucurbitacin I converted to nontoxic metabolites tetrahydro cucurbitacin glucosides [<xref rid="B209-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">209</xref>].</p><p>One hypothesis of evolutionarily developed adaptive responses to stressors is that plants protect themselves against microorganisms, insects, pests, fungi, viruses, and hazardous environmental changes by biosynthesis of secondary metabolites in their most vulnerable parts [<xref rid="B210-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">210</xref>,<xref rid="B211-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">211</xref>,<xref rid="B212-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">212</xref>,<xref rid="B213-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">213</xref>,<xref rid="B214-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">214</xref>,<xref rid="B215-pharmaceuticals-18-00261" ref-type="bibr">215</xref>].
