# PMC: adaptogen stress response HPA axis — Overview

ref-type="table">Table 1</xref>, an upgrade from [<xref rid="B2-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>].</p><p>In the first definition of adaptogens dated 1958, a toxicologist, Lasarev, focused on the so-called “state of non-specific resistance of organism” [<xref rid="B4-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B5-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>] that is associated with the notions of “adaptability” [<xref rid="B6-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="B7-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>] defined by Georges Cunghilaim in 1943 and “stress”, Cannon [<xref rid="B8-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>] and Hans Selye [<xref rid="B9-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>,<xref rid="B10-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>]. In 1967, Brekhman and Dadimov implemented the term adaptogens in pharmacognosy, postulating that adaptogens are innocuous medicinal plants, normalizing body functions in response to various harmful environmental conditions [<xref rid="B11-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>,<xref rid="B12-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>], as shown in <xref rid="pharmaceuticals-18-01346-t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref>. The first adaptogens extensively studied were Ginseng, probably its reputation as a panacea for all diseases [<xref rid="B13-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">13</xref>,<xref rid="B14-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>], Eleutherococcus (Siberian ginseng), and later <italic toggle="yes">Rhodiola rosea</italic> (Golden root) due to their tonic effects [<xref rid="B15-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>,<xref rid="B16-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>,<xref rid="B17-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">17</xref>,<xref rid="B18-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">18</xref>,<xref rid="B19-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>,<xref rid="B20-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>,<xref rid="B21-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>,<xref rid="B22-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">22</xref>]. These postulates were supported by numerous publications during the last decades [<xref rid="B1-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B2-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="B3-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>], resulting in updated definitions of adaptogens, see <xref rid="pharmaceuticals-18-01346-t001" ref-type="table">Table 1</xref> and <xref rid="pharmaceuticals-18-01346-t002" ref-type="table">Table 2</xref> [<xref rid="B2-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="B23-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">23</xref>], included in textbooks of pharmacognosy and phytotherapy [<xref rid="B24-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">24</xref>,<xref rid="B25-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">25</xref>,<xref rid="B26-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">26</xref>,<xref rid="B27-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">27</xref>,<xref rid="B28-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">28</xref>,<xref rid="B29-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">29</xref>,<xref rid="B30-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">30</xref>,<xref rid="B31-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>,<xref rid="B32-pharmaceuticals-18-01346" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>], due to tremendous progress in biomedical sciences. However, new evidence is required to support the safety of some adaptogens, e.g., Withania, Bryonia, and some other putative adaptogens, such as Sideris, Eurocoma, Hypericum, etc., supporting Brekhmans’ postulations and current definitions of adaptogens.</p><table-wrap position="anchor" id="pharmaceuticals-18-01346-t001" orientation="portrait"><object-id
