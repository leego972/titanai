# PMC: low level laser therapy healing review — Overview

[<xref rid="B31-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>,<xref rid="B32-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">32</xref>,<xref rid="B33-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>,<xref rid="B34-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">34</xref>,<xref rid="B35-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">35</xref>,<xref rid="B36-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">36</xref>]. To control for potential systemic metabolic influences unrelated to wound healing, both healthy and diabetic non-ulcerated groups (I and IV) were included, allowing distinction of systemic metabolic effects from those specifically associated with mucosal ulceration and laser treatment.</p><p>In Phase 2, performed four weeks after diabetes induction, animals were reweighed, and glycemia was reassessed. Standardized buccal mucosal ulcers (6 mm in diameter) were created under general anesthesia (Ketamidor, Richter Pharma AG, Wels, Austria) as full-thickness excisions down to the muscle layer, ensuring consistent wound depth across all animals [<xref rid="B31-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>]. Mucosal disinfection was achieved using 0.12% chlorhexidine. Tissue was excised using a no. 15 scalpel blade. Hemostasis was achieved with sterile gauze. Photographs were taken immediately post-excision and on the day of sacrifice for wound area analysis using ImageJ 1.48 (NIH, Bethesda, MD, USA), with a millimeter scale included to minimize measurement error (<xref rid="medicina-61-01651-f002" ref-type="fig">Figure 2</xref>). Wound-healing percentage was calculated following the formula [<xref rid="B37-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>,<xref rid="B38-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>]<disp-formula>((wound area on day 0 − wound area on day X)/wound area on day 0) × 100%</disp-formula>
where X is the day of animal sacrifice.</p><p>Laser-treated animals (Groups III and VI) received LLLT (630–660 nm wavelength, 7 mW output power; Scorpion Dental Optima, Sofia, Bulgaria) using a 0.196 cm<sup>2</sup> probe at 6 J/cm<sup>2</sup> in continuous mode, applied every other day for 10 days. The protocol of 6 J/cm<sup>2</sup> applied every other day for 10 days was selected based on previous studies demonstrating effective wound healing in diabetic rodent models using comparable dosages and treatment schedules [<xref rid="B21-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">21</xref>,<xref rid="B37-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">37</xref>,<xref rid="B38-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">38</xref>,<xref rid="B39-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">39</xref>]. The irradiation time was calculated to be 168 s per session. Treatment was performed at 1 mm distance from the wound surface using a specially designed holder that ensured non-contact application and maintained a constant probe–tissue distance throughout irradiation.</p><p>In Phase 3, animals were sacrificed on days 3, 7, and 10 after the initiation of the second phase. At each time point, six animals per group were sacrificed. Afterward, blood samples were collected for biochemical analyses by cardiac puncture. Experimental animals from Groups I and IV, representing healthy and diabetic animals, respectively, served as control groups.</p></sec><sec id="sec2dot3-medicina-61-01651"><title>2.3. Biochemical Measurements</title><p>Blood collected in EDTA-coated tubes (Venosafe<sup>®</sup>, Terumo Europe, Leuven, Belgium) was centrifuged at 2500 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C, and plasma was stored at −20 °C until analysis.</p><p>The determination of total oxidative status (TOS) was based on the ability of oxidants in the sample to oxidize ferrous ions (Fe<sup>2+</sup>) in complex with o-dianisidine into ferric ions (Fe<sup>3+</sup>). In an acidic environment, ferric ions form a colored complex with xylenol orange [<xref rid="B40-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">40</xref>]. The assay was calibrated with hydrogen peroxide, and the results were expressed in μmol of H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub> equivalents per liter (μmol H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub> equivalents/L). The intra-assay and inter-assay coefficients of variation for TOS were 3.7% and 5.5%, respectively.</p><p>Total antioxidant capacity (TAC) was measured by ferric reducing ability of plasma (FRAP). The FRAP assay is a colorimetric method used to assess the radical scavenging ability of antioxidants [<xref rid="B41-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">41</xref>]. The assay was calibrated with FeSO<sub>4</sub>, and the results were expressed in μmol of FeSO<sub>4</sub> equivalents/L. The intra-assay and inter-assay coefficients of variation for TAC were 1.8% and 4.6%, respectively.</p><p>The oxidative stress index (OSI) was calculated from the TOS and TAC values according to the following formula: OSI = (TOS/TAC) × 100. The results are expressed in arbitrary units.</p><p>The activity of superoxide dismutase (SOD) (SOD; EC 1.15.1.1) was determined spectrophotometrically according to the method of Misra and Fridovich [<xref rid="B42-medicina-61-01651" ref-type="bibr">42</xref>].
