The aqueous extracts of roots led to programmed cell death, and thus inhibit the growth and development of apoptosis. They suppressed the growth of lymphoma and myeloma cell lines via disturbing normal expression level of <italic toggle="yes">Bcl</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">c-myc</italic> genes, while increased the expression level of cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor p27 (KIP1) [<xref rid="B121-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">121</xref>]. The results of 3-(4,5-dimethyl-2-thiazolyl)-2,5-diphenyl-2-H-tetrazolium bromide assay showed a lower rate at different doses. Flow cytometric analysis showed that baicalin induces apoptosis in a dose-dependent way. The aqueous extract of baicalin showed both inhibitory and angiogenic activities while the baicalein showed only anti-proliferative property. Quantitative PCR results showed the expression of 84 different angiogenesis-related genes at different doses of baicalin and baicalein. The low dose significantly increases angiogenic genes expression while the high concentration showed the opposite activity by decreasing angiogenesis and increasing the lethality. The low to high levels of baicalein decrease the level of gene expression of multiple angiogenic genes and decreased cell proliferation.</p><p>Sato et al. The findings of the study showed 100 µg/mL root extract retard the monolayer and anchorage-independent growth rate many times but do not affect the cell adhering ability. Scutellaria barbata</title><p><italic toggle="yes">Scutellaria barbata</italic>, the barbed skullcap is a key medicinal plant species of family Lamiaceae, used to treat inflammatory and cancer diseases [<xref rid="B128-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">128</xref>]. It is rich in important secondary metabolites like alkaloids, flavones, steroids, and polysaccharides [<xref rid="B129-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">129</xref>,<xref rid="B130-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">130</xref>]. In vitro studies showed positive activities against a vast range of cancers i.e., colon cancer, lung cancer, hepatoma, and skin cancer [<xref rid="B128-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">128</xref>]. </p><p>The apigenin and luteolin isolated from <italic toggle="yes">S. barbata</italic> gave cytotoxic activity against both human breast cancer cell line MDA-MB-231 and non-transformed breast cell line (MCF10A) [<xref rid="B131-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">131</xref>]. Similarly, scutellarein was found to possess strong anti-breast cancer activity demonstrated in MDA-MB-468 cell lines [<xref rid="B132-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">132</xref>]. Scutellarein increased the concentration of mitochondrial superoxide and peroxide while decreasing the level of glycolysis, retarding the growth of cancer cells by lowering ATP synthesis. Other secondary metabolites of <italic toggle="yes">S. barbata</italic> showed cytotoxic properties by leading ROX and DNA damage [<xref rid="B131-biomolecules-10-00047" ref-type="bibr">131</xref>]. The other important alkaloid of <italic toggle="yes">S. barbata</italic>, scutebarbatine A (SBT-A) also resulted in high antitumor and apoptosis activities in experimental A549 cells. </p></sec><sec id="sec3dot17-biomolecules-10-00047"><title>3.17.
