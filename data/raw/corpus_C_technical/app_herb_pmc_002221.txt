Introduction</title><p>Cancer remains one of the most pressing global health challenges and is currently the second leading cause of mortality worldwide after cardiovascular diseases [<xref rid="B1-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B2-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="B3-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B4-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B5-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>,<xref rid="B6-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>], accounting for ~19 million new cases and 10 million deaths in 2020 alone [<xref rid="B7-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>]. Epidemiological projections indicate that the global incidence may exceed 30 million new cases annually by 2040, underscoring the urgent need for innovative therapeutic and preventive strategies. Despite advances in surgery, chemotherapy, targeted therapies (e.g., EGFR and VEGF inhibitors), and immunotherapy, treatment efficacy remains limited for many aggressive and late-stage cancers due to toxicity, drug resistance, and disease recurrence [<xref rid="B8-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>]. Multidrug resistance alone contributes to up to 90% of chemotherapy failures, highlighting the need for alternative approaches that improve selectivity while minimizing adverse effects [<xref rid="B1-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="B2-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="B3-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="B4-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">4</xref>,<xref rid="B5-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">5</xref>,<xref rid="B6-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="B9-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>,<xref rid="B10-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">10</xref>,<xref rid="B11-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">11</xref>,<xref rid="B12-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>]. Plant-derived BACs have gained considerable attention as potential anticancer agents, given their longstanding role in drug discovery. Approximately 68% of small-molecule anticancer drugs developed between 1940 and 2014 were derived from or influenced by natural products [<xref rid="B8-molecules-31-01202" ref-type="bibr">8</xref>].
