Sixty patients with PD were randomly divided into the treatment group (BPJF+Madopar group, <italic toggle="yes">n</italic>=30) or the control group (Madopar group, <italic toggle="yes">n</italic>=30) and treated for 3 months. The total effective rate of the treatment group was 87.5%, whereas that of the control group was 77.5%, and this difference was significant (<xref rid="bib201" ref-type="bibr">Zhang, 2008</xref>). Another study observed the effect of Qishu decoction (contains <italic toggle="yes">Astragalus</italic>, prepared <italic toggle="yes">Rhizome of rehmannia</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Radix codonopsis</italic>, lyceum, donkey-hide glue, <italic toggle="yes">Angelica sinensis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Caulis Spatholobi</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Glycyrrhiza uralensis</italic>) on the treatment of NSCLC patients with leukopenia after chemotherapy. Two-hundred-forty patients with NSCLC were randomly divided into the treatment group (Qishu decoction group <italic toggle="yes">n</italic>=120) or control group (Leucogen and Batilol tablet group, both <italic toggle="yes">n</italic>=120). Both groups were treated for 10 days. The total effective rate in the treatment group was 91.5%, whereas that for the control group was 72.8%. This result suggested that the Qishu decoction can greatly improve bone marrow depression, promote blood-producing functions and recover leukocytes, thereby benefiting NSCLC patients (<xref rid="bib168" ref-type="bibr">Xiang and Han, 2013</xref>). The He shou wu and Rou cong rong decoction (composed of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Cistanche salsa</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Radix Astragali preparata</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Ligusticum wallichii</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Angelica sinensis</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Salviae miltiorrhizae</italic>) was demonstrated to enhance immunoregulation and improve the clinical symptoms in a study of 34-year-old inpatients with kidney deficiency and blood stasis KDBS (<xref rid="bib9" ref-type="bibr">Chen et al., 2006</xref>). The Shou wu ji li formula decoction (composed of <italic toggle="yes">Polygoni multiflori preparata</italic>, stir-baked <italic toggle="yes">Fructus tribuli</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Angelica sinensis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Fructus ligustri lucidi</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Ecliptae herba</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Rehmannia glutinosa libosch</italic>, duckweed, <italic toggle="yes">Fructus Xanthii</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Clematis chinensis</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Radix sileris</italic>, <italic toggle="yes">Semen Astragali Complanati</italic>, and <italic toggle="yes">Radix Glycyrrhizae preparata</italic>) combined with the Kaliziran tincture formula was effective for treating blood deficiency and wind sheng-type vitiligo, and there were fewer adverse reactions. The mechanism of action may be through an increase in tyrosinase activity and modulation of the immune system.
