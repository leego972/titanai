id="section1-2515841419899822"><title>Introduction</title><p>Glaucoma is characterized by progressive retinal ganglion cell (RGC) axonal loss.<sup><xref rid="bibr1-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">1</xref><xref rid="bibr2-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr3-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr4-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr5-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">5</xref></sup></p><p>Because RGC axonal thickness is greatest at the peripapillary retina,<sup><xref rid="bibr6-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">6</xref><xref rid="bibr7-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr8-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">8</xref></sup> optical coherence tomography (OCT) scans have been mainly used to measure the peripapillary retinal nerve fiber layer (pRNFL) thickness to estimate the glaucomatous structural loss. However, more recent studies demonstrated that glaucoma involves not only RGC axons but also their bodies and dendrites,<sup><xref rid="bibr9-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">9</xref><xref rid="bibr10-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr11-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr12-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">12</xref></sup> which are mainly located at the macula. Therefore, spectral domain-optical coherence tomography (SD-OCT) scans centered on the macula to measure various inner macular parameters (e.g. ganglion cell complex (GCC), ganglion cell-inner plexiform layer (GCIPL)) are also highly informative of glaucomatous damage, and, combined with optic nerve head (ONH) SD-OCT scans, provide the clinician with a comprehensive understanding of the disease stage.</p><p>In the last few years, optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA) was introduced in the clinical practice, allowing to image the vascular component of this disease, and, possibly, its role in the pathogenesis of glaucoma. Several studies reported decreased OCTA vessel density (VD) both in the peripapillary area and at the macula, corresponding to the location of RGCs’ neural loss in glaucoma.<sup><xref rid="bibr13-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">13</xref><xref rid="bibr14-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr15-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr16-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">16</xref></sup></p><p>The present review will summarize the most updated findings with regards to OCT and OCTA in glaucoma, highlighting their clinical use for detection and monitoring of the disease and their correlation to functional tests (such as visual field) widely employed in the asset of modern glaucoma clinics.</p><sec id="section2-2515841419899822"><title>Optical coherence tomography: detection of early glaucoma</title><p>It is well established that pRNFL is mainly affected in the inferior and superior quadrants in pre-perimetric and early glaucoma, whereas temporal and nasal quadrants are usually involved later on in the course of the disease.<sup><xref rid="bibr17-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">17</xref><xref rid="bibr18-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr19-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr20-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr21-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr22-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">22</xref></sup> Because of this pattern of pRNFL thinning, Wang and colleagues<sup><xref rid="bibr23-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">23</xref></sup> reported that the pRNFL temporal quadrant thickness missed 77% of the eyes showing early GCIPL thinning, suggesting that pRNFL analysis may overlook early glaucomatous macular damage.</p><p>At the same time, many studies reported that early superior and inferior pRNFL defects are frequently associated with initial GCIPL changes, which are preferentially located at the inferior-temporal and superior-temporal macular sectors.<sup><xref rid="bibr12-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">12</xref>,<xref rid="bibr24-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">24</xref><xref rid="bibr25-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr26-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr27-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">27</xref></sup></p><p>Furthermore, it was shown that GCIPL parameters have similar diagnostic accuracy compared to the pRNFL ones in detecting early glaucoma.<sup><xref rid="bibr28-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">28</xref><xref rid="bibr29-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr"/><xref rid="bibr30-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr"/>–<xref rid="bibr31-2515841419899822" ref-type="bibr">31</xref></sup></p><p>However, it remains unclear whether the glaucomatous damage becomes detectable at the macula or at the peripapillary region first.
