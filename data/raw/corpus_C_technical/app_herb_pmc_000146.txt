# PMC: herbal medicine pharmacology mechanism review — Overview

xlink:href="gr7_lrg.jpg"/></fig><fig id="f0040" position="float" orientation="portrait"><label>Fig. 8</label><caption><p>Chemical structures of new compounds after processed.</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="gr8_lrg.jpg"/></fig></p><sec id="s0025"><label>4.1</label><title>Stilbenes</title><p id="p0065">Stilbenes are the main characteristic components in <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic>. 2,3,5,4′-Tetrahydroxystilbene-2-O-β-<sc>d</sc>-glucopyranoside (<bold>1</bold>) was first isolated and identified from this plant in 1976 (<xref rid="bib186" ref-type="bibr">Yang, 1976</xref>), and another two stilbenes, which were identified as 2,3,5,4′-tetrahydroxystilbene-2-O-β-<sc>d</sc>-(2″-O-monogalloyl esters)-glucopyranoside (<bold>2</bold>) and 2,3,5,4′-tetrahydroxystilbene-2-O-β-<sc>d</sc>-(3″-O-monogalloyl esters)-glucopyranoside (<bold>3</bold>), were then isolated (<xref rid="bib118" ref-type="bibr">Nonaka et al., 1982</xref>). 2,3,5,4′-Tetrahydroxystilbene-2,3-di-O-β-<sc>d</sc>-glucopyranoside (<bold>4</bold>) was isolated from the ethyl acetate insoluble fraction of an ethanol extract of the plant (<xref rid="bib221" ref-type="bibr">Zhou et al., 1994</xref>). Two tetrahydroxystilbenes were isolated from this plant in 2000 and identified as 2,3,5,4′-tetrahydroxystilbene-2-O-(6″-O-α- <sc>d</sc>-glucopyranosyl)-β-<sc>d</sc>-glucopyranoside (<bold>5</bold>) (<xref rid="bib18" ref-type="bibr">Chen et al., 2000a</xref>) and 2,3,5,4′-tetrahydroxystilbene-2-O-(6″-O-acetyl)-β-<sc>d</sc>-glucopyranoside (<bold>6</bold>) (<xref rid="bib22" ref-type="bibr">Chen et al., 2000c</xref>). Another stilbene, which was identified as 2,3,5,4′-tetrahydroxystilbene-2-O-β-<sc>d</sc>-xyloside (<bold>7</bold>) (<xref rid="bib142" ref-type="bibr">Sun et al., 2013</xref>), was isolated from a 70% ethanol extract of <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic>. Five stilbene glycosides, which were identified as 2,3,5,4′-tetrahydroxystilbene-2-O-(4″-O-α-<sc>d</sc>-glucopyranosyl)-β-<sc>d</sc>-glucopyranoside (<bold>8</bold>), 2,3,5,4′-tetrahydroxystilbene-2-O-(6″-O-β-<sc>d</sc>-glucopyranosyl)-β-<sc>d</sc>-glucopyranoside (<bold>9</bold>), 2,3,5,4′-tetrahydroxystilbene-2-O-β-<sc>d</sc>-glucopyranosyl-4′-O-α-<sc>d</sc>-glucopyranoside (<bold>10</bold>), 2,3,5,4′-tetrahydroxystilbene-2-O-β-<sc>d</sc>-glucopyranosyl-5-O-α-<sc>d</sc>-glucopyranoside (<bold>11</bold>) and 2,3,5,4′-tetrahydroxystilbene-2-O-(2″-O-β-<sc>d</sc>-fructofuranosyl)-β-<sc>d</sc>-glucopyranoside (<bold>12</bold>), were isolated from <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic> in 2013 (<xref rid="bib71" ref-type="bibr">Li, et al., 2013b</xref>). Moreover, resveratrol (<bold>13</bold>), polydatin (<bold>14</bold>) (<xref rid="bib177" ref-type="bibr">Xu et al., 2009</xref>), rhaponticoside (<bold>15</bold>) (<xref rid="bib190" ref-type="bibr">Yi et al., 2007</xref>), <italic toggle="yes">cis</italic>-2,3,5,4′-tetrahydroxystilbene-2-O-β-<sc>d</sc>-glucoyranoside (<bold>16</bold>) (<xref rid="bib137" ref-type="bibr">Sun et al., 2009</xref>) and <italic toggle="yes">cis</italic>-2,3,5,4′-tetrahydroxystilbene-2-O-(6″-O-α-<sc>d</sc>-glucopyranosyl)-β-<sc>d</sc>-glucopyranoside (<bold>17</bold>) (<xref rid="bib170" ref-type="bibr">Xiao et al., 2002</xref>) were also isolated from <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic>. Four stilbene derivatives, denoted polygonumosides A-D (<bold>18</bold>–<bold>21</bold>), were isolated and identified from this plant in 2014 (<xref rid="bib181" ref-type="bibr">Yan et al., 2014</xref>). Other stilbenes, such as tetrahydroxystilbene-O-(malonyl)-hex, tetrahydroxystilbene-O-deoxyhex and tetrahydroxystilbene-O-(caffeoyl)-hex, were also found in <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic>, but their structures have not yet been identified (<xref rid="bib128" ref-type="bibr">Qiu et al., 2013</xref>).</p></sec><sec id="s0030"><label>4.2</label><title>Quinones</title><p id="p0070">Quinones are the other characteristic components in <italic toggle="yes">Polygonum multiflorum</italic>. Quinones and their derivatives have been isolated and identified, and most of them are anthraquinones. The predominant anthraquinones are emodin-type anthraquinones, including emodin (<bold>22</bold>), aloe-emodin (<bold>23</bold>), chrysophanol (<bold>24</bold>), physcion (<bold>25</bold>), rhein (<bold>26</bold>), 1,6-dimethyl ether-emodin (<bold>27</bold>), emodin-8-methyl ether (<bold>28</bold>), citreorosein (<bold>29</bold>), citreorosein-8-methyl ether (<bold>30</bold>), emodin-3- methyl ether (<bold>31</bold>) fallacinol (<bold>32</bold>), emodin-6,8-dimethylether (<bold>33</bold>) and 2-acetylemodin (<bold>34</bold>) (<xref rid="bib142" ref-type="bibr">Sun et al., 2013</xref>, <xref rid="bib72" ref-type="bibr">Li et al., 2013c</xref>, <xref rid="bib67" ref-type="bibr">Li and Lin, 1993</xref>, <xref rid="bib57" ref-type="bibr">Kato and Morita, 1987</xref>, <xref rid="bib159" ref-type="bibr">Wang et al., 2005b</xref>, <xref rid="bib17" ref-type="bibr">Chen et al., 1999b</xref>, <xref rid="bib228" ref-type="bibr">Zuo et al., 2008</xref>, <xref rid="bib211" ref-type="bibr">Zhang et al., 2006a</xref>).
