MOG is a component of the myelin sheath; thus, auto‐inflammation affects the perineural layers rather than the nerve axons [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0075" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0076" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>]. In MOG‐associated optic neuritis the peripapillary and macular vascular density positively correlates with visual acuity measures [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0068" ref-type="bibr">68</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0072" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>]. In studies comparing parameters between subjects with MOG‐IgG positive optic neuritis and healthy subjects, the former had statistically significant larger foveal avascular zone, as well as lower parafoveal and peripapillary capillary density. This was paralleled by statistically significant lower best corrected visual acuity, RNFL, and GCL count. Longitudinal OCTA studies have shown that the microvascular changes in MOG‐optic neuritis eyes can persist despite apparent clinical inactivity [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0072" ref-type="bibr">72</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0078" ref-type="bibr">78</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0079" ref-type="bibr">79</xref>]. This provides support for the pathogenic neuroaxonal energy demand theory that is paralleled in MS‐associated ON and suggests a role for OCTA for predicting the natural history of this subset of NMOSD subjects.</p><p>OCTA is helpful to distinguish inflammatory optic neuropathy from ischaemic optic neuropathy, which shows more vascular perfusion loss. In people affected by inflammatory optic neuropathy, it can help assess disease activity and visual prognosis based on microvascular integrity.</p></sec><sec id="ceo14544-sec-0010"><label>3.3</label><title>Dysthyroid Optic Neuropathy (<styled-content style="fixed-case" toggle="no">DON</styled-content>)</title><p>DON is a serious, sight‐threatening condition that complicates 5%–8% of thyroid‐associated orbitopathy (TAO). There are several factors that contribute to its pathogenesis. It is believed that soft tissue volume expansion within the orbital cavity in the context of autoimmune inflammation causes optic nerve compression and stretch at the orbital apex, with resultant venous congestion, arterial ischaemia, and impairs axoplasmic flow [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0080" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>]. It manifests most commonly as a central or paracentral scotoma with relative afferent pupil defect, with nerve fibre layer thinning, most commonly in the inferior and temporal quadrants [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0081" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0082" ref-type="bibr">82</xref>]. Analysis with OCT and OCTA of affected individuals in the acute setting demonstrates reduced peripapillary capillary density, in the absence of nerve fibre loss, which correlates to visual field defects, suggesting vascular anomalies precede structural changes in the natural history of DON [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0081" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>]. Of particular interest, there is an enlarged foveal avascular zone in those with TAO without evidence of DON or nerve fibre loss, which positively correlates to disease severity and intraocular pressure [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0082" ref-type="bibr">82</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0083" ref-type="bibr">83</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0084" ref-type="bibr">84</xref>]. An associated increased macular choroidal thickness is noted in these subjects, theorised to be related to venous congestion [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0082" ref-type="bibr">82</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0083" ref-type="bibr">83</xref>]. This suggests a role for OCTA in identifying those with thyroid eye disease who are at risk of DON, as the nerve fibre layer thickness closely correlates to visual acuity, even after surgical decompression, making early detection and prevention imperative [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0081" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0085" ref-type="bibr">85</xref>]. Dual OCTA may also be useful in prognostication following surgical decompression for thyroid eye disease, as vascular density following decompression may help to predict the post‐operative visual outcome [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0081" ref-type="bibr">81</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0085" ref-type="bibr">85</xref>].</p></sec></sec><sec id="ceo14544-sec-0011"><label>4</label><title>
<styled-content style="fixed-case" toggle="no">OCTA</styled-content> Findings in Congenital and Hereditary Aetiology</title><sec id="ceo14544-sec-0012"><label>4.1</label><title>Hereditary Optic Neuropathies</title><p>Autosomal Dominant Optic Atrophy (ADOA) is an inherited mitochondrial optic neuropathy, with prevalence in the population of 1 in 25 000 [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0086" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>]. Mitochondrial membrane dysfunction, most commonly because of OPA1 gene mutation, induces apoptosis in retinal ganglion cells, with preferential involvement of the papillomacular bundle [<xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0087" ref-type="bibr">87</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0088" ref-type="bibr">88</xref>, <xref rid="ceo14544-bib-0089" ref-type="bibr">89</xref>].
