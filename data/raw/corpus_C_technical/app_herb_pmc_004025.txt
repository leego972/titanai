Although it is well known that sufficient physiological levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS) are necessary for the processes of physiological adaptation to exercise training, overtraining, insufficient energy supply, or poor sleep hygiene may increase the ROS production, which in turn may have a negative impact on the time course of exercise-induced adaptations [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR140">140</xref>–<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR142">142</xref>]. For instance, it has been demonstrated that these chemicals cause structural changes in myofibrillar proteins, which have an impact on how well they function (e.g., a reduced sensitivity to intracellular Ca2+). </p><sec id="Sec15"><title>Effects of Ashwagandha in enhancing immune system</title><p id="Par123">There are several possible candidates in the exciting field of research on plant-derived immunomodulators. One of them, Ashwagandha, which is included in the Indian Ayurvedic tradition’s Rasayana class of drugs and has been extensively studied as an immunomodulatory agent. The components in WS mainly withanolide glycosides exert their immunomodulatory action by mobilizing and activating macrophages and induce proliferation in murine splenocytes [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR58">58</xref>]. The activation of immune-regulatory cells by WS extracts may serve multiple purposes, including modulation of antigen presentation, regulation of the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, and maintenance of a balanced cytokine profile that supports effective T cell responses [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR59">59</xref>]. Tharakan et al. reported that extracts derived from the root and leaf of WS can enhance immunoglobulin levels key elements of innate immunity as well as increase IFN-γ production and the population of CD3⁺ and CD4⁺ T cells in humans. These results suggest that the plant plays an important role in strengthening both innate and adaptive immune responses, which are vital for the recognition and effective defense against common bacteria, viruses, and allergens [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR34">34</xref>]. The results of the present study align with previous in vivo research demonstrating an increase in CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cell populations, along with elevated expression of T-helper 1 (Th1) cytokines. Likewise, in immune-suppressed mice, administration of WS extracts led to a dose-dependent enhancement of natural killer (NK) cell activity and a faster restoration of CD4⁺ T cells [<xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR149">149</xref>, <xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR150">150</xref>].
