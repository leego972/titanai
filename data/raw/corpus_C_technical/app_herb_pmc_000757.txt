Discussion</title><p>Herbal medicines in complementary and alternative medicine (CAM) were developed based on unique theories, such as KM and TCM, and have been used internally and externally for the treatment of various conditions over thousands of years.<sup>[<xref rid="R48" ref-type="bibr">48</xref>,<xref rid="R49" ref-type="bibr">49</xref>]</sup> In traditional medicine, external treatment is defined as a method of inducing a therapeutic effect through various stimulation methods, such as through the skin or the respiratory tract, without the use of medicine. It helps achieve therapeutic effects through chemical and physical actions at the lesion, as opposed to systemic treatmen.<sup>[<xref rid="R50" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>,<xref rid="R51" ref-type="bibr">51</xref>]</sup> Herbal medicine is applied to acupuncture points to regulate the meridian and correct the deviation of qi blood yin yang through the pharmacological action of herbal medicines and stimulation of acupuncture points for the prevention and treatment of diseases.<sup>[<xref rid="R52" ref-type="bibr">52</xref>]</sup> Its application is mainly focused on surgical diseases but includes various traditional medical diseases, such as those related to internal medicine, dermatology, ophthalmology, otolaryngology, gynecology, pediatrics, and neuropsychiatry.<sup>[<xref rid="R53" ref-type="bibr">53</xref>]</sup> HMEU in traditional medicine can help resolve the lesion and alleviate its symptoms by moisturizing, lubricating, cooling, and protecting the lesion.<sup>[<xref rid="R54" ref-type="bibr">54</xref>]</sup> In CAM, HMEU is considered cheaper and safer than that of conventional medicines, such as oral or topical medications.<sup>[<xref rid="R55" ref-type="bibr">55</xref>,<xref rid="R56" ref-type="bibr">56</xref>]</sup> While a variety of external skin preparations are being used because of the development of WM and new drugs and formulations, the diversification of formulations and product development of traditional external use preparations is insufficient.<sup>[<xref rid="R57" ref-type="bibr">57</xref>]</sup></p><p>Acupuncture-related therapies, such as acupuncture, electroacupuncture, fire needling, warm needling, pharmacopuncture, bloodletting, cupping, and herbal medicine, have been reported to be more effective than Western medical treatments by several systematic studies on the treatment effects of acute gouty arthritis symptoms.<sup>[<xref rid="R2" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>,<xref rid="R14" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>–<xref rid="R16" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>]</sup> However, in the case of HMEU, there is only literature evidence in Korea,<sup>[<xref rid="R2" ref-type="bibr">2</xref>]</sup> and no clinical studies were found. In TCM, HMEU is often applied clinically to relieve pain and symptoms in patients with acute gout; however, a systematic review is lacking. In this study, we attempted to establish a link between herbal medicine and evidence-based medicine through a systematic review and meta-analysis on the effects and safety of HMEU for acute gout. In addition, this study attempts to contribute to the evidence on the development of HMEU, which is currently lacking.</p><p>The number of participants in the 27 selected studies varied from 40 to 121, and the total number of participants was 1951. It has been shown that relatively large-scale clinical studies are being conducted. The participant ages ranged from 18 to 85 years, with the average age ranging from 30 to 50 years. The proportion of men was much higher, except for 4 studies in which no specific sex was indicated. Between 2016 and 2020, more men received more gout treatment than women in Korea. All the selected studies were conducted in China; therefore, further studies in other countries are needed.</p><p>There were more cases of HMEU treatment in addition to WM rather than of treatment with HMEU alone. HMEU were chiefly in a powdered form, and various types of herbal medicines were used. The treatment period was relatively short, ranging from 5 to 14 days, which is similar to the that described in studies on KM treatments, such as electroacupuncture, fire needling, and warm needling, for acute gout.<sup>[<xref rid="R15" ref-type="bibr">15</xref>,<xref rid="R16" ref-type="bibr">16</xref>]</sup></p><p>Five evaluation tools were used in this study: TER, uric acid level, VAS pain score, ESR, and CRP level. The review indicated that HMEU used alone or in combination with WM was more effective than WM in terms of TER in 24 studies, change in the uric acid level in 18 studies, and pain reduction in 17 studies.
