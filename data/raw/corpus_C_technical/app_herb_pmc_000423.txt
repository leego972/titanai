A single study assessed maternal death and stillbirth outcomes among users (n=205) and non-users (n=407) of castor oil to induce labour.<xref rid="R43" ref-type="bibr">43</xref> No maternal death occurred in either group, but one case of stillbirth (0.3%) was reported in the control group. Uterine rupture was reported by two studies in relation to castor oil and only one case was reported among exposed women in one of the studies.<xref rid="R43" ref-type="bibr">43 47</xref> Overall, no study found a significant difference in any of the three outcomes between users and non-users of herbal medicines for IOL.</p></sec></sec><sec sec-type="discussion" id="s4"><title>Discussion</title><p>We have found that herbal medicines for IOL are effective and there is no concrete evidence of association with adverse outcomes. On efficacy, we have found that women who used the herbal medicines were significantly more likely to give birth within 24 hours than their counterparts who did not use. This corroborates many in vitro studies around the world that have shown that some herbal medicines effectively induce uterine contractions.<xref rid="R13" ref-type="bibr">13 20 50</xref> For instance, studies in Malawi and Nigeria have established that some medicinal plants commonly prescribed by traditional healers to induce childbirth have oxytocic properties.<xref rid="R13" ref-type="bibr">13 20</xref> Previous reviews, however, found insufficient evidence for the effectiveness of herbal medicines for IOL.<xref rid="R51" ref-type="bibr">51 52</xref> This contradiction could be as a result of the differences in inclusion criteria. Most of the related reviews excluded non-experimental studies,<xref rid="R51" ref-type="bibr">51 52</xref> which are a common source of efficacy data due to safety issues surrounding RCTs for herbal medicines or pregnant women.<xref rid="R27" ref-type="bibr">27 28 53</xref> Whereas this allowed us to assess a wider evidence base than the previous reviews, we are also mindful of the biases inherent in observational studies. Therefore, a definite conclusion about the efficacy of herbal remedies for IOL cannot be put forward based on the present review.<xref rid="R54" ref-type="bibr">54–56</xref></p><p>On safety, we did not find any statistically significant difference in the rate of haemorrhage, caesarean section, assisted vaginal delivery, referral to neonatal intensive care unit, MSL, maternal death, stillborn and uterine rupture between participants in treated and control groups.
