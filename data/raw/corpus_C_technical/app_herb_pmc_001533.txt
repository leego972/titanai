Another notable bioactive compound is β-sitosterol, a plant sterol that contributes to cardiovascular health by lowering cholesterol levels through the inhibition of intestinal cholesterol absorption. In addition to its cardioprotective role, β-sitosterol has been linked to immune system modulation, as it enhances T-cell proliferation and reduces inflammation [<xref rid="B94-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">94</xref>].</p><p>Finally, pomolic acid, a pentacyclic triterpenoid isolated from <italic toggle="yes">C. angustifolium</italic>, has been extensively studied for its therapeutic potential, particularly in anticancer, antiviral, and anti-inflammatory applications. Research by Martins et al. demonstrated that pomolic acid effectively induces apoptosis and inhibits multidrug resistance mechanisms in prostate cancer cells [<xref rid="B95-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>]. Additionally, it exhibits significant cytotoxic effects against lymphocytic leukemia cells and HIV, with an efficacy comparable to 5-fluorouracil. Its mechanism of action includes the stimulation of AMP-dependent protein kinase, the suppression of cancer cell proliferation, and a reduction ininflammation. Importantly, the Ames test and SOS chromotest confirmed its lack of genotoxicity and mutagenicity, supporting its clinical safety profile [<xref rid="B48-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">48</xref>]. Recent studies further highlight pomolic acid’s selective cytotoxicity against glioma cells, including U-87 MG and primary glioma cell lines, demonstrating dose-dependent tumor inhibition [<xref rid="B96-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">96</xref>]. Beyond its anticancer effects, pomolic acid has also shown promise in renal fibrosis treatment. In an in vivo renal fibrosis model, it significantly reduced fibrosis through cadherin and α-SMA modulation while suppressing collagen deposition and extracellular matrix accumulation [<xref rid="B97-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">97</xref>].</p></sec><sec id="sec2dot3dot2-molecules-30-01186"><title>2.3.2. Bioactivity of <italic toggle="yes">C. angustifolium</italic> and <italic toggle="yes">C. latifolium</italic> Extracts</title><sec><title>Antioxidant Properties</title><p>The increasing use of plant species for phytotherapeutic applications has driven a surge in studies on their antioxidant properties, as these compounds play a vital role in counteracting oxidative stress [<xref rid="B98-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">98</xref>]. Medicinal plants, abundant in bioactive molecules like phenolic compounds, flavonoids, and terpenoids, are recognized for their strong antioxidant potential [<xref rid="B99-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>,<xref rid="B100-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">100</xref>,<xref rid="B101-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">101</xref>,<xref rid="B102-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">102</xref>]. Natural antioxidants have gained prominence as safer and healthier alternatives to synthetic ones [<xref rid="B103-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">103</xref>,<xref rid="B104-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">104</xref>,<xref rid="B105-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">105</xref>].</p><p><italic toggle="yes">C. angustifolium</italic>, a traditional medicinal plant, is distinguished by its abundant phenolic acids, flavonoids, and ellagitannins, which enhance its noteworthy antioxidant qualities [<xref rid="B80-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>]. Research indicates that growing methods and solid-phase fermentation (SSF) significantly affect the bioactive chemical composition in fireweed leaves. Evidence indicates that specific SSF parameters enhance the accumulation of certain bioactive compounds in fireweed [<xref rid="B80-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">80</xref>,<xref rid="B106-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">106</xref>]. For example, Lasinskas et al. found that natural fireweed samples exhibited high antioxidant activity (1319.16 M Trolox eq./g D.M.) [<xref rid="B76-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>]. Jariene et al. reported that the antioxidant activity initially declined after 24 h of SSF under aerobic (19.23%) and anaerobic (11.14%) conditions but increased after 48 h to levels higher than those of unfermented leaves (324.56 mM TEAC/100 g DW) under aerobic (15.50%) and anaerobic (14.27%) conditions [<xref rid="B75-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">75</xref>].</p><p>Additional research by Lasinskas et al. demonstrated annual fluctuations in the antioxidant activity of <italic toggle="yes">C. angustifolium</italic> leaves obtained from a biodynamic farm in Lithuania. In 2017, fermented leaves exhibited more antioxidant activity than unfermented leaves; conversely, in 2018, the reverse was noted. A significant association was observed between the antioxidant activity, quantified in mg 100 g<sup>−1</sup> DW Trolox equivalents, and the total polyphenol content, with variations dependent on fermentation length [<xref rid="B76-molecules-30-01186" ref-type="bibr">76</xref>].</p><p>Ecotypes of <italic toggle="yes">C.
