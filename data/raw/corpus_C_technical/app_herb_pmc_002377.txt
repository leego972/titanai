</p><p id="Par94">These findings align with other studies where encapsulation with cyclodextrins improved bioavailability. For example, Radic et al. (2020)<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR80">80</xref></sup> demonstrated that encapsulating olive pomace polyphenols enhanced antioxidant activity and gastrointestinal stability during digestion. Similarly<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR81">81</xref></sup>, reported improved drug bioavailability through cyclodextrin inclusion complexes. Encapsulation of ellagic acid with β-CD also increased its water solubility and controlled release due to the stabilization of its highly lipophilic structure<sup><xref ref-type="bibr" rid="CR82">82</xref></sup>. These studies support the efficacy of our encapsulation method in enhancing the stability and bioavailability of plant compounds.</p></sec><sec id="Sec20"><title>Results of encapsulation efficiency</title><p id="Par95">The encapsulation efficiency results demonstrated a distinct variation depending on both the type of cyclodextrin used and the plant part from which the extract was obtained. Specifically, β-cyclodextrin (β-CD) at 25 °C resulted in a higher encapsulation efficiency for the aerial part of <italic toggle="yes">L. bellidifolium</italic> (45.81%) compared to α-cyclodextrin (α-CD) at 40 °C (39.16%). Conversely, α-CD was significantly more effective for the root extract, achieving an encapsulation efficiency of 59.78% compared to 42.65% for β-CD. This differential behavior likely arises from the structural compatibility between the cyclodextrin cavity size and the molecular profile of the phytochemicals in different plant parts.
