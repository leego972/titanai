# PMC: phytochemical bioactive compound anticancer — Overview

Among the most notable species, <italic toggle="yes">C. oleifera</italic> is known for its high-quality oil used in cosmetics, <italic toggle="yes">C. sinensis</italic> is the main source of tea, and <italic toggle="yes">Camellia sasanqua</italic> has been utilized in the perfume industry [<xref rid="B14-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">14</xref>]. However, despite the rich composition and high bioactive potential of <italic toggle="yes">CJ</italic>, its commercialization has historically been limited compared to other species of the same genus. Nevertheless, this species contains a variety of active compounds, such as phenolic compounds, carotenoids, and terpenoids, which could have significant applications in different industrial fields [<xref rid="B19-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">19</xref>,<xref rid="B20-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">20</xref>,<xref rid="B31-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">31</xref>,<xref rid="B33-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">33</xref>,<xref rid="B50-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">50</xref>,<xref rid="B86-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">86</xref>,<xref rid="B87-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">87</xref>,<xref rid="B95-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">95</xref>,<xref rid="B101-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">101</xref>,<xref rid="B112-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">112</xref>,<xref rid="B113-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">113</xref>,<xref rid="B114-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">114</xref>].</p><p>In recent years, various scientific studies have promoted the revaluation of <italic toggle="yes">CJ</italic> through the development of new technological applications. Research has identified its potential in the production of dietary supplements, cosmetic ingredients, and pharmaceutical products, as well as in the creation of bioactive materials with antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. Thanks to these advances, a range of possibilities has opened up for their inclusion in multiple sectors (<xref rid="nutrients-17-03382-f002" ref-type="fig">Figure 2</xref>), which has improved both their commercial value and their integration into sustainable industrial practices.</p><sec id="sec5dot1-nutrients-17-03382"><title>5.1. Food Industry</title><p><italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> has great potential in the food industry due to its richness in bioactive compounds, such as carotenoids, polyphenols, flavonoids, saponins, and amino acids (<xref rid="nutrients-17-03382-f002" ref-type="fig">Figure 2</xref>). Therefore, this matrix is being investigated for the development of various food products, primarily beverages and dietary supplements. Among these is the development of tea-like infusions like the ones produced from the leaves of <italic toggle="yes">C. sinensis</italic>. These infusions have the commercial appeal of being rich in antioxidant compounds and promoting metabolic health, making them a healthy alternative herbal infusion [<xref rid="B111-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">111</xref>,<xref rid="B115-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">115</xref>]. Various <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> products are already on the market. For example, in recent years, the Areeiro Phytopathological Station (Pontevedra, Spain) developed a tea from <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> marketed under the name “Areeiro Tea”, which opens the door to experimenting with the use of <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> in functional beverages.</p><p>Furthermore, <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> has a rich composition from a nutritional (amino acids, minerals, fiber) and phytochemical (carotenoids, phenolic compounds) perspective, which is why this matrix has been investigated for the development of dietary supplements and food fortification with health-promoting properties, highlighting its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects [<xref rid="B99-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">99</xref>,<xref rid="B116-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">116</xref>]. For instance, carotenoids extracted from plant leaves are of particular interest in food and nutrition due to their dual role as natural pigments capable of enhancing or stabilizing the color of food products, and as bioactive compounds with provitamin A activity (in the case of <italic toggle="yes">β</italic>-carotene) and antioxidant capacity that may contribute to oxidative stability and health benefits related to the mitigation of oxidative stress [<xref rid="B117-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">117</xref>,<xref rid="B118-nutrients-17-03382" ref-type="bibr">118</xref>]. In this context, carotenoid-rich extracts from <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> represent a potential source of natural colorants and functional ingredients for incorporation into food formulations, where they could improve both the visual appeal and the nutritional value of products. Nevertheless, systematic evaluations of concentration–response effects, stability during processing (thermal, light and pH conditions), and in vivo bioavailability of <italic toggle="yes">CJL</italic> products remain scarce.
