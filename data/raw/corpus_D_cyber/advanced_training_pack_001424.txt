SECTION 1: ADVANCED PROBLEM SOLVING SKILLS
================================================================================

--- FRAMEWORK 1: FIRST PRINCIPLES THINKING ---
METHOD: Break down complex problems to fundamental truths
EXAMPLE 1: Battery Cost Problem
- Traditional: "Batteries cost $600/kWh"
- First Principles: What are batteries made of? (Cobalt, nickel, aluminum)
- What do these materials cost on commodity markets? (~$80/kWh)
- Why the difference? Manufacturing, assembly, distribution
- SOLUTION: Optimize manufacturing process, reduce assembly steps

EXAMPLE 2: Cybersecurity Breach Response
- Problem: "We were hacked, what do we do?"
- First Principles: What is a breach? (Unauthorized data access)
- How did they access? (Network, physical, social engineering)
- What did they access? (Data inventory, classification)
- How do we stop continued access? (Isolation, credential rotation)
- How do we prevent recurrence? (Gap analysis, control implementation)

--- FRAMEWORK 2: SYSTEMS THINKING ---
COMPONENTS: Elements, Interconnections, Purpose, Feedback loops
EXAMPLE: Supply Chain Disruption
- Elements: Suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, retailers, customers
- Interconnections: Orders, shipments, payments, information flow
- Feedback: Inventory levels trigger reorders, delays cause stockouts
- SOLUTION: Diversify suppliers, increase buffer inventory, real-time tracking

--- FRAMEWORK 3: INVERSION (AVOIDING FAILURE) ---
METHOD: Instead of "How to succeed?", ask "How to fail?"
EXAMPLE 1: Project Management
- How to guarantee project failure?
  * Unclear requirements
  * No stakeholder buy-in
  * Unrealistic timeline
  * Insufficient resources
  * Poor communication
- INVERSION: Do the opposite of each failure mode

EXAMPLE 2: Security Architecture
- How to guarantee breach?
  * Single point of failure
  * No monitoring
  * Weak passwords
  * Unpatched systems
  * No incident response plan
- INVERSION: Redundancy, comprehensive monitoring, MFA, auto-patching, IR plan

--- FRAMEWORK 4: SECOND-ORDER THINKING ---
QUESTION: "And then what?"
EXAMPLE 1: Price Reduction
- First order: Lower prices -> More customers
- Second order: More customers -> Higher demand -> Supply constraints
- Third order: Supply constraints -> Quality reduction -> Customer dissatisfaction

EXAMPLE 2: Remote Work Policy
- First order: Employees happy, reduced office costs
- Second order: Collaboration challenges, culture dilution
- Third order: Innovation decline, employee isolation, retention issues

--- FRAMEWORK 5: PROBABILISTIC THINKING ---
METHOD: Assign probabilities, update with new evidence (Bayesian)
EXAMPLE: Threat Assessment
- Initial: P(APT) = 5%
- Evidence: Custom malware found -> P(APT) = 30%
- Evidence: C2 beaconing to known APT domain -> P(APT) = 70%
- Evidence: Attribution to specific group -> P(APT) = 95%

DECISION MATRIX:
| Option | Probability | Impact | Expected Value |
|--------|-------------|--------|----------------|
| Isolate | 80% | High | 0.8 * 10 = 8 |
| Monitor | 50% | Medium | 0.5 * 5 = 2.5 |
| Ignore | 20% | Critical | 0.2 * 100 = 20 |

--- FRAMEWORK 6: MENTAL MODELS INVENTORY ---
1. Occam's Razor: Simplest explanation is usually correct
2. Hanlon's Razor: Never attribute to malice what can be explained by stupidity
3. Pareto Principle: 80% of effects from 20% of causes
4. Parkinson's Law: Work expands to fill time available
5. Law of Large Numbers: Large samples approximate true probability
6. Network Effects: Value increases with number of users
7. Diminishing Returns: Each additional unit yields less benefit
8. Critical Mass: Tipping point for self-sustaining reaction
9. Feedback Loops: Reinforcing (virtuous/vicious) or balancing
10. Emergence: Complex behaviors from simple interactions

GENERATIONS:

- SGSN/GGSN: Packet switching for GPRS/UMTS
- MME: Mobility Management Entity (LTE control plane)
- SGW/PGW: Serving/PDN Gateway (LTE user plane)
- AMF/SMF/UPF: 5G Core functions

- Full-size SIM: 85.6mm x 53.98mm x 0.76mm (ISO/IEC 7810 ID-1)
- Mini-SIM: 25mm x 15mm
- Micro-SIM: 15mm x 12mm
- Nano-SIM: 12.3mm x 8.8mm x 0.67mm

COMPONENTS:
- CPU: 8-16 bit microcontroller (ARM SC300, Infineon SLE78)

- OS: Java Card, MULTOS, Native
- Crypto: AES, DES, RSA, ECC hardware accelerators
- Standards: ISO/IEC 7816, ETSI TS 102 221, GlobalPlatform

3. MTP3: Network layer (routing, addressing)
4. SCCP: Signaling Connection Control Part (connection-oriented)
5. TCAP: Transaction Capabilities Application Part
6. MAP: Mobile Application Part
7. CAP: CAMEL Application Part

SS7 MESSAGE TYPES:
- IAM: Initial Address Message (call setup)
- ACM: Address Complete Message
- ANM: Answer Message
- REL: Release Message
- RLC: Release Complete
- SRI: Send Routing Information
- PRN: Provide Roaming Number
- UL: Update Location
- ISD: Insert Subscriber Data

SS7 ATTACK VECTORS:
- Location Tracking: SRI-for-SM to get VLR
- SMS Interception: UpdateLocation to rogue MSC
- Call Redirect: PRN manipulation
- DoS: Overload with fake messages
- Fraud: ISD to modify subscriber data

MITIGATION:
- SS7 Firewall: Filter suspicious messages
- Diameter for 4G/5G (more secure)
- SIGTRAN (SS7 over IP with better security)
- GT (Global Title) filtering
- B-number analysis

--- IMSI CATCHERS (STINGRAYS) ---
OPERATION MODES:
1. Passive: Monitor only, no transmission
2. Active IMSI Catcher: Force phones to connect
3. Active Man-in-the-Middle: Full interception

TECHNIQUE:
- Broadcast strong signal as legitimate carrier
- Phone connects to strongest signal (C1 criterion)
- Forced downgrade to 2G (no mutual authentication)
- Intercept calls/SMS/data

DETECTION:
- Unexpected cell towers
- Rapid LAC/CID changes
- Missing neighbor cells
- Encryption disabled suddenly
- Timing advance anomalies

COUNTERMEASURES:
- Force 4G/5G only (disable 2G/3G)
- Certificate pinning for IMS
- Network-based anomaly detection
- Legal IMSI catcher detection (fingerprinting)

SECTION 10: FACT CHECKING AND VERIFICATION
================================================================================

--- MISINFORMATION TYPES ---
DISINFORMATION: False information spread deliberately to deceive
MISINFORMATION: False information spread without intent to deceive
MALINFORMATION: Genuine information used to cause harm (doxing, revenge porn)

CATEGORIES:
- Fabricated content: 100% false, designed to deceive
- Manipulated content: Genuine info manipulated (deepfakes, photoshop)
- Imposter content: Impersonation of genuine sources
- False context: Genuine content with false context
- Misleading content: Misleading use of information
- False connection: Headlines not supported by content
- Satire/parody: No intent to cause harm but potential to fool

--- FACT-CHECKING METHODOLOGY ---
SOURCE TRIANGULATION:
- Find three independent sources confirming claim
- Check primary sources, not secondary reporting
- Verify credentials of quoted experts
- Look for original documents, not interpretations

REVERSE IMAGE SEARCH:
- Tools: Google Images, TinEye, Yandex
- Find original context of images
- Detect manipulation or out-of-context usage

VIDEO VERIFICATION:
- InVID: Verify video content and metadata
- Amnesty YouTube DataViewer: Extract thumbnails, reverse search
- Check upload history: Is this the original?
- Metadata analysis: EXIF data, creation dates

SOCIAL MEDIA VERIFICATION:
- Account age: New accounts more suspicious
- Follower analysis: Bot check (Bot Sentinel, Botometer)
- Posting patterns: Automated posting?
- Network analysis: Who do they follow/interact with?

DEEPFAKE DETECTION:
- Visual artifacts: Unnatural blinking, lighting inconsistencies
- Audio-visual sync: Lip movements not matching audio
- Face manipulation: Warping around edges
- Tools: Deepware Scanner, Sentinel, Microsoft Video Authenticator

--- CRITICAL THINKING TECHNIQUES ---
LATERAL READING:
- Leave site, check other sources about same claim
- Check Wikipedia (with caveats)
- Look for fact-checks on Snopes, Politifact, FactCheck.org

SIFT METHOD:
- STOP: Pause, don't share immediately
- INVESTIGATE THE SOURCE: Who is behind this?
- FIND TRUSTED COVERAGE: What do reliable sources say?
- TRACE CLAIMS, QUOTES, MEDIA: To original context

RED FLAGS:
- Emotional manipulation: Designed to provoke strong reaction
- Urgency: "Share before it's deleted"
- Conspiracy: Claims of cover-ups without evidence
- Anonymity: No named author or sources
- Too perfect: Fits narrative too cleanly
- Typos/poor grammar: Unprofessional presentation

BIASES TO AVOID:
- Confirmation bias: Seeking info that confirms beliefs
- Dunning-Kruger: Overestimating own knowledge
- Availability heuristic: Judging by recent examples
- Anchoring: Over-relying on first information received
- Groupthink: Conforming to group consensus
