# Tone Control

## Purpose
Tone is the emotional register and attitude the film takes toward its material — serious, playful, ironic, reverent, brutal.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Tone is set in the first 10 minutes and the audience calibrates all subsequent scenes against it
- Tone variation is welcome — relief and contrast require tonal shifts
- Tonal breaks are different from tonal variation — a break ruptures the audience's engagement
- Genre determines the acceptable range of tonal variation
- Every department — production design, costume, score, performance — carries tone

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Identify the film's tonal register before any creative decision
- Flag any scene where tone breaks rather than varies
- Check all departments for tonal consistency

## Scene-level checks
- Is this scene's tone consistent with or a deliberate variation from the film's established register?
- Is a tonal shift in this scene designed or accidental?

## Project-level checks
- Is the film's tonal register established clearly in the first 10 minutes?
- Are tonal variations intentional and managed?
- Is tone consistent across all departments?

## Warning signs
- Accidental comedy in a dramatic scene
- Tonal shifts that never recover
- Departments executing different tonal registers simultaneously

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Create a tone reference document shared with all departments
- In editing, review rough cut specifically for tonal breaks

## Example assistant output
> Scene 33 breaks your film's tone because the production design is whimsical in a scene that needs to be threatening. The desk lamp is a cartoon character. The motivational poster is ironic. Reshoot or replace with a different location.
