# Blocking

## Purpose
Blocking is the planned movement of actors through space — where they stand, move, sit, and interact within the frame. Blocking communicates character psychology, relationship dynamics, and power in purely visual language.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Blocking is a visual language — position and movement communicate meaning
- Characters who move have power; characters who are still are reacting
- Movement toward the camera feels aggressive; movement away feels withdrawn
- Blocking should arise from character psychology, not staging convenience
- The best blocking feels inevitable — as if the characters couldn't move any other way

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate blocking for psychological truth, not just visual cleanliness
- Check that blocking changes reflect shifts in character power and emotion
- Movement should be motivated — characters move because they want something

## Scene-level checks
- Does the blocking in this scene reflect the character psychology and power dynamics?
- Is every movement in this scene motivated by a character want or emotion?

## Project-level checks
- Is blocking planned with character psychology as the primary driver?
- Does blocking change appropriately as scene dynamics shift?

## Warning signs
- Static blocking that doesn't reflect the scene's emotional shifts
- Movement that contradicts character psychology
- Blocking that makes the camera's job easier but the actors' job harder

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Brief actors on the psychological motivation for each blocking move before rehearsing it
- Block the scene from character psychology first, then fit the camera to the blocking

## Example assistant output
> In your confrontation scene, both characters stand still for four minutes. Cinematically it's clean. Dramatically it's dead. Give Marcus the power through movement — he crosses to the window, controlling space. Ana doesn't move but turns her body away at the crucial admission — that's withdrawal through blocking. He moves to her at the end — the power has shifted. Give the camera something to follow that means something.
