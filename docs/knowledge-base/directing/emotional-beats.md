# Emotional Beats

## Purpose
An emotional beat is a distinct shift in the emotional register of a scene — a moment when the emotional temperature changes.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- A scene typically has 3–7 emotional beats — distinct shifts in emotional state
- Emotional beats are the points where the scene turns — the character wants something, gets or loses it, and responds
- Identifying emotional beats is essential for performance direction — actors play one beat at a time
- Emotional beats give editors specific cut points — cuts land on beat transitions
- The final emotional beat of a scene determines the audience's state as they enter the next scene

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Identify all emotional beats in a scene before shooting
- Brief actors on each beat transition — what changes, what the new objective is
- Plan coverage to ensure each beat is captured cleanly

## Scene-level checks
- How many distinct emotional beats does this scene have?
- Is each beat transition motivated by a character action or revelation?

## Project-level checks
- Are emotional beats planned for all key scenes?
- Do emotional beats connect across scenes?
- Is coverage designed to capture beat transitions?

## Warning signs
- Scenes played as one sustained emotion — no internal beats
- Coverage that doesn't capture the beat transitions — uneditable

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Write emotional beats into the shot list — not just camera positions but when beats occur
- In editing, cut at beat transitions, not at line ends

## Example assistant output
> Scene 18 has been shot as one sustained emotion: dread. But it has four beats: confidence, suspicion, realisation, calculation. You have footage for all four because the actor found them naturally. Cut at the transitions between beats, not at line ends. Beat 3 is your close-up. That's where the cut lands.
