# Power Shifts

## Purpose
A power shift is the moment in a scene when the balance of control moves from one character to another.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Every significant scene should contain at least one power shift
- Power shifts are caused by information revealed, action taken, or emotion displayed
- The character who reveals vulnerability gives power to the other
- Power shifts should be visible in performance, blocking, and camera
- The direction and timing of a power shift determines the scene's emotional meaning

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Identify the power shift in every scene before shooting
- Plan coverage to capture the shift: where are the cameras when the power moves?
- Brief actors on the power shift — it determines their performance arc through the scene

## Scene-level checks
- When does the power shift in this scene?
- Is the shift motivated by a character action or revelation?
- Does the camera placement capture the shift effectively?

## Project-level checks
- Does every significant scene contain at least one power shift?
- Are power shifts consistent with the film's larger power dynamic between characters?

## Warning signs
- Scenes with no power shift — static, dramatically inert
- Power shift that contradicts character psychology
- Coverage that misses the power shift — no close-up at the key moment

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Identify the power shift moment for every scene in the shot list
- Plan a close-up at the exact moment of power shift

## Example assistant output
> Scene 22: the power shift is when Marcus reveals he has the recording. You have a medium two-shot for that moment. That's wrong. You need his close-up as he reaches for the phone, then her close-up as she hears it. The shift needs to be on the face that loses power, not the face that gains it.
