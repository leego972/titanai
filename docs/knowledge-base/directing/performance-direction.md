# Performance Direction

## Purpose
Performance direction is the craft of guiding actors to deliver the emotional truth of a scene in a way that serves the film's vision.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Directors who give emotion as direction ('be sad') get generic results
- The best direction is specific: give the actor an action (a verb), a relationship, or a memory
- Actors need to know what they want in the scene (objective), what stops them (obstacle), and what they do about it (action)
- Performance direction must be given privately — correcting actors publicly damages performance
- Different actors require different direction styles — know your cast

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Never direct performance with emotion as the instruction — use action verbs instead
- Give actors objectives, obstacles, and actions for every scene
- Create a safe environment for risk — bad takes come from actors afraid to fail

## Scene-level checks
- Is this actor's performance serving the scene's intention?
- Is this performance emotionally truthful and character-consistent?

## Project-level checks
- Have all actors been briefed on their character's psychology, not just their lines?
- Is there a safe creative environment for performance exploration?

## Warning signs
- 'Be more sad/angry/happy' — emotion-as-direction, produces generic results
- Correcting actors publicly — damages trust and performance quality
- Over-directing — actors who are given every note cannot discover their performance

## Strong recommendation patterns
- For each scene, prepare three specific directions before the shoot — not 'what emotion' but 'what action'
- Brief actors one-to-one — never correct performance publicly

## Example assistant output
> Your note to the actress was 'be more desperate.' She played it louder and faster. Instead: 'in this scene, you're trying to convince him without letting him see how frightened you are.' Now she has an objective, an action, and an obstacle. The desperation will be in her eyes, not her volume.
