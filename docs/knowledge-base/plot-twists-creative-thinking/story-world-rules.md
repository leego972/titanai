# Story World Rules

## Purpose
Every story world — realistic or fantastical — operates by rules. The rules determine what's possible, what the protagonist can do, and what the audience expects. The 1B Brain must identify, protect, and apply world rules consistently.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- World rules establish the boundaries of what's possible in the story
- Rules are established in Act 1 and tested in Act 2 — they must never simply change to solve a plot problem
- Breaking world rules to solve a plot problem is a plot hole
- Fantasy and sci-fi rules must be internally consistent — they don't need to match reality, but they must match themselves
- The more rules a world has, the more discipline their application requires

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Identify the world's rules before advising on any plot development
- Flag any plot solution that requires bending or breaking established rules
- Test each rule: has it been established, and is it applied consistently?
- World rules that are introduced for the first time in Act 3 to solve a problem are deus ex machina

## Scene-level checks
- Does this plot development follow the established rules of the story's world?
- Is this world rule consistently applied — or does it change when plot requires it?

## Project-level checks
- Are all world rules established in Act 1 or Act 2?
- Are rules applied consistently throughout the film?
- Are there any Act 3 solutions that require new rules?

## Warning signs
- Rules that change in Act 3 to solve a plot problem — deus ex machina
- Rules that are inconsistently applied depending on plot need
- Rules established in Act 1 but forgotten by Act 3

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Create a world rules document and share it with all creative departments
- Test every Act 3 solution against the established rules — can it be done without new rules?

## Example assistant output
> Your Act 3 reveals that Ana can contact the dead — a rule never established in your film. The audience will feel cheated. Either establish this ability in Act 1 (even subtly — a scene where she speaks to an empty room), or find a solution to Act 3 that works within the rules you've already established.
