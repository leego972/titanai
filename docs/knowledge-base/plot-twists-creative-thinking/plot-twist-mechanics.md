# Plot Twist Mechanics

## Purpose
A plot twist is a sudden, unexpected story development that reframes what the audience thought they understood. The 1B Brain must evaluate plot twists for legitimacy, impact, and narrative integration.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- A legitimate twist is surprising AND inevitable in retrospect — both qualities are required
- Surprise alone (without inevitability) is a cheap shock; inevitability alone (without surprise) is predictable
- The audience must reinterpret prior scenes after a twist — that reinterpretation is the experience
- A twist must be seeded with clues the audience can retroactively find
- Twists that require characters to behave stupidly to work are structurally dishonest

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate every twist for: surprise + inevitability + retroactive coherence
- Check that clues are planted early enough to be discoverable — not in the scene before the twist
- A twist that breaks character consistency is a plot hole, not a twist
- The bigger the twist, the more setup it requires

## Scene-level checks
- Are there sufficient clues planted before this twist to make it feel earned in retrospect?
- Does the twist require a character to behave inconsistently with their established psychology?

## Project-level checks
- Are all major twists seeded at least one act before they land?
- Does the twist reframe prior events meaningfully — not just surprise?
- Does the twist serve the story's theme?

## Warning signs
- Twists that are surprising but not inevitable — cheap shocks
- Twists that require ignoring earlier established facts
- Twists introduced so late they have no narrative consequence
- Twist for twist's sake — no thematic resonance

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Create a twist-clue tracking document: every clue and its placement
- Test the twist by watching the film twice — does it hold?
- Ask: if the audience knew the twist before watching, would the film still work?

## Example assistant output
> Your twist in scene 38 is genuinely surprising but it requires Marcus to have lied about his background in scene 4. The problem: scene 4 doesn't play as a lie — it plays as truth. If you reseed scene 4 with micro-ambiguity (a pause, a look away, slightly too-smooth an answer), the twist becomes inevitable in retrospect rather than a retcon.
