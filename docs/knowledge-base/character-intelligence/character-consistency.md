# Character Consistency

## Purpose
Character consistency is the requirement that characters behave in ways that are consistent with their established psychology, knowledge, and situation throughout the film — unless change is earned through the story.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Characters are consistent when their behaviour is explainable by their psychology, knowledge, and circumstances
- A character doing something 'out of character' is either a continuity error or an earned dramatic moment
- Character inconsistency is often the symptom of plot puppetry — the plot needs something to happen, so the character does it
- The test for consistency: given this character's psychology and what they know right now, would they do this?
- Consistency doesn't mean predictability — complex characters can surprise us while still being internally coherent

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Test every major character decision against their established psychology
- Flag character behaviour that doesn't arise from their known psychology
- Brief actors on the full psychological profile of their character — consistency requires their understanding
- Distinguish between consistency errors and earned change — change must be caused by a story event

## Scene-level checks
- Is this character's decision consistent with their established psychology and current knowledge?
- Is this character surprise arising from character complexity or plot puppetry?

## Project-level checks
- Are all major character decisions explainable by established psychology?
- Are character changes caused by specific story events?
- Are there any moments where characters act 'out of character' without narrative justification?

## Warning signs
- Characters who do what the plot needs rather than what their psychology dictates
- Dramatic 'out of character' moments that weren't earned through the story
- Character decisions that require the character to be inexplicably stupid

## Strong recommendation patterns
- For every character decision, ask: given everything we know about this character right now, would they do this?
- Build psychological profiles for all major characters before production — share with actors

## Example assistant output
> Scene 22 has Marcus trust a stranger immediately. But Marcus's established psychology is extreme suspicion of anyone new. That contradiction needs to be bridged: either the stranger does something specific that earns Marcus's trust (a callback to something established earlier), or Marcus's trust is a mistake that has consequences. Characters can make mistakes — but they must be consistent mistakes.
