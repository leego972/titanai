# Character Decision Points

## Purpose
Character decision points are the moments where a character must choose between two genuinely difficult options — moments that reveal character and advance the story simultaneously.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- A decision point is a genuine dilemma: two options, both with real cost
- False dilemmas (one obviously right answer) aren't dramatic — they're plot formalities
- The most revealing decision points test the character's flaw directly
- Character decisions should have consequences — choices that don't change anything aren't real decisions
- The protagonist's final decision should be the most difficult and the most revealing of their arc

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate whether decision points offer genuinely difficult choices
- Check that decisions have consequences — does the choice change something?
- The climactic decision should be the most difficult choice in the film
- Flag any decision that has an obviously correct answer — it's not a decision, it's a formality

## Scene-level checks
- Is this character facing a genuine dilemma — are both options genuinely costly?
- Does this decision directly test the character's established flaw?

## Project-level checks
- Are all major character decision points genuine dilemmas?
- Do decisions have consequences — do choices change something?
- Is the protagonist's final decision the most difficult and revealing?

## Warning signs
- False dilemmas with one obviously correct answer
- Decisions with no consequences — characters choose but nothing changes
- Decision points that don't test the character's flaw

## Strong recommendation patterns
- For every major decision point, state both options and their costs — if the cost of one option is unclear, the dilemma isn't genuine
- Design the climactic decision specifically around the protagonist's flaw
