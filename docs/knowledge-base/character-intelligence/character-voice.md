# Character Voice

## Purpose
Character voice is the specific way each character speaks — their vocabulary, rhythm, syntax, and the kinds of things they say or avoid saying. Distinctive character voices make the dialogue feel alive and characters feel real.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Every character should be identifiable by their dialogue alone — without character names
- Voice is determined by: education level, regional origin, psychological state, social role, and personal history
- Characters under pressure revert to their most essential voice
- A character who speaks the same way in every situation has no voice — just generic dialogue
- Voice is also what characters don't say: their silences, avoidances, and non-answers are as distinctive as their words

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate dialogue for voice distinctiveness — could you identify the speaker without character names?
- Check that character voices are consistent throughout the film
- Brief actors and writers: voice comes from psychology, not affectation
- Voice should change under pressure — reveal something essential about the character

## Scene-level checks
- Is this character's dialogue voice distinct from all other characters in the scene?
- Is this character's voice consistent with their established psychology and background?

## Project-level checks
- Can all major characters be identified by dialogue alone?
- Are character voices consistent throughout the film?
- Do character voices change under pressure in ways that reveal character?

## Warning signs
- Characters who all sound the same — generic dialogue with interchangeable voices
- Voice inconsistency — characters speak differently in different scenes without psychological reason
- Voice that's all style and no psychology — accent or tick without underlying character logic

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Write all major characters' dialogue in separate documents — compare for distinctiveness
- Test: cover character names and read dialogue aloud. Can you identify the speaker?
