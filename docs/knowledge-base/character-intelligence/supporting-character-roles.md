# Supporting Character Roles

## Purpose
Supporting characters serve specific functions in the story — they reflect the protagonist, provide opposition, deliver information, or embody thematic alternatives. The 1B Brain must ensure every supporting character earns their screen time.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Every supporting character should serve at least one clear function: mirror, foil, ally, mentor, or thematic representative
- Mirror characters reflect aspects of the protagonist back at them
- Foil characters contrast with the protagonist to illuminate their qualities
- Mentors provide knowledge, wisdom, or capability the protagonist lacks
- Thematic representatives embody alternative positions on the film's theme

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Identify the function of every supporting character before advising
- Check that supporting characters serve their function consistently
- Supporting characters with no clear function should be cut or combined
- Minor supporting characters should have one memorable specific trait — not a full backstory

## Scene-level checks
- What is this supporting character's function in the story?
- Is this supporting character serving their function in this scene?

## Project-level checks
- Does every supporting character serve a clear, identifiable function?
- Are supporting character functions consistent throughout the film?
- Are any supporting characters functionless — taking up screen time without purpose?

## Warning signs
- Supporting characters who exist only to deliver information
- Supporting characters whose function changes between scenes without narrative reason
- Too many supporting characters serving the same function — creates redundancy

## Strong recommendation patterns
- For every supporting character, write their function in one sentence before production
- Any supporting character who can't have their function stated in one sentence should be rethought

## Example assistant output
> You have four supporting characters who all serve the same function: they tell the protagonist information he needs. Combine them into two characters — each with a distinct function beyond information delivery. Give one character a reason to withhold information (creates conflict), and make the other one unreliable (creates complexity). Now both characters are dramatic instruments, not just delivery mechanisms.
