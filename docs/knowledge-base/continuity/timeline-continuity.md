# Timeline Continuity

## Purpose
Timeline continuity is the consistency of when events happen within the story — ensuring the sequence, time elapsed, and physical implications are logically consistent.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Timeline continuity requires a clear map of when each scene occurs in story time
- Non-linear films have extremely complex timeline requirements
- Physical implications of time: injuries heal at realistic rates, children age, seasons change
- Time compression must be indicated to the audience

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Create a story timeline map before production — every scene placed in story time
- For non-linear films, create both the story timeline and the screen timeline
- Check physical implications of time passage

## Scene-level checks
- Is this scene's story time consistent with all surrounding scenes?
- Are the physical implications of time passage since the last scene reflected in this scene?

## Project-level checks
- Is a story timeline map created and maintained throughout production?
- Are physical implications of time passage reflected in production design, wardrobe, and makeup?

## Warning signs
- Non-linear films without a timeline map
- Physical continuity that ignores time: wounds that heal too fast
- Time passage not communicated to the audience

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Create a timeline map before production begins — every scene in story time order
- Brief continuity, wardrobe, and makeup on all time-passage implications

## Example assistant output
> Your film covers a 6-month period but Elena's appearance doesn't change between her first scene and her last. Six months of what she goes through should be visible. Brief the makeup and hair departments: Elena at the start and Elena at the end are the same person but six months of hardship should be visible.
