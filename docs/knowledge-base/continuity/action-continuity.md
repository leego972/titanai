# Action Continuity

## Purpose
Action continuity ensures that physical actions — movement, gesture, object handling — are consistent across all setups within a scene.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Action continuity requires identical action across all setups — same hand, same direction, same moment
- Actions that happen at cut points are highest risk — the cut reveals any discrepancy
- Actors must repeat actions precisely across multiple setups — this requires rehearsal

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Identify high-risk action moments — actions that happen at likely cut points
- Brief actors on action continuity requirements before shooting begins
- Record action state at key moments in the continuity notes

## Scene-level checks
- Are the actor's actions in this setup consistent with the master and previous setups?
- Are high-risk action moments photographed and noted for continuity reference?

## Project-level checks
- Are action continuity requirements identified before shooting each scene?
- Is the script supervisor monitoring action continuity throughout?

## Warning signs
- Complex actions that actors repeat differently each take
- Action continuity notes not taken
- Actors not briefed on action continuity requirements

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Brief actors on all action continuity requirements before the first take
- The script supervisor photographs action state at key moments

## Example assistant output
> Your glass of water on the desk is touched differently in every take. In the edit, you'll be forced to choose a single approach. Before scene 22's coverage: Marcus picks up the glass at his second line and puts it down at his fifth. That's the action. Every take does exactly that.
