# Story Continuity

## Purpose
Story continuity is the logical consistency of plot, timeline, and cause-and-effect across the full film.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Story continuity errors are the most damaging — they create plot holes
- Common errors: a character knows something they couldn't know, an object appears in the wrong place, an event happens in an impossible timeframe
- Non-linear structures multiply story continuity risks
- Every factual claim made about the story world must be consistent with all other claims

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Create a story continuity tracker: every established fact about the world, characters, and timeline
- Check story continuity at each production stage
- For non-linear films, create a timeline map

## Scene-level checks
- Is this character's knowledge in this scene consistent with what they've learned so far?
- Does this event follow logically from the established story timeline?

## Project-level checks
- Is there a story continuity tracker for the full film?
- Are non-linear timelines mapped and checked for consistency?

## Warning signs
- Characters who know things they couldn't know
- Events that happen before or after they should
- Story continuity checking only done at script stage

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Create a story continuity tracker before production begins
- Run a story continuity check during editing — new problems emerge in the cut

## Example assistant output
> Your story has a problem: Marcus is established to have left the city 15 years ago in scene 3. In scene 22, Elena says she saw him 10 years ago 'in this city.' Those facts are contradictory. Either Marcus returned after his initial departure (needs a line to establish this), or Elena's line needs to change.
