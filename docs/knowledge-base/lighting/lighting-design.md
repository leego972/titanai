# Lighting Design

## Purpose
Lighting design is the visual architecture of a film — the planned system of light and shadow that creates mood, communicates character, advances theme, and defines the world.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Lighting tells the audience how to feel before any other element — it sets emotional register instantly
- Every lighting decision should have a motivation: what is the source, and what story does it tell?
- The relationship between light and shadow is the primary visual language of a film
- Lighting design must be consistent within a film's visual world while varying scene to scene
- The director and DP must agree on the lighting bible before any shooting begins

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate lighting design for narrative motivation, not just technical quality
- Check that lighting is consistent with the film's established visual language
- Brief the DP: every major lighting change needs a motivated source
- For each scene, ask: what does the light say about this character's state right now?

## Scene-level checks
- Is this scene's lighting motivated by a narrative source and consistent with the established design?
- Does the lighting communicate the intended emotional register?

## Project-level checks
- Is there a clear lighting bible agreed between director and DP?
- Is lighting design consistent across similar emotional beats in the film?
- Does lighting evolve with the character's arc?

## Warning signs
- Lighting that's beautiful but doesn't serve the scene's emotional function
- Inconsistent lighting design — each scene looks like a different film
- Over-lit scenes that flatten all shadow

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Create a lighting bible before pre-production: establish rules for protagonist light, antagonist light, and key locations
- Review stills from each setup for lighting consistency before moving on
