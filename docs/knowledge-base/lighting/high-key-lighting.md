# High-Key Lighting

## Purpose
High-key lighting is a brightly lit style with minimal shadows and high overall illumination. It communicates safety, optimism, transparency, and control.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- High-key: high overall exposure, minimal shadows, low contrast ratio
- Communicates: safety, control, transparency, optimism, normality
- Used in: comedy, corporate sequences, false-security scenes, remembered happiness
- High-key can be ironic: a horrific scene lit high-key creates tonal dissonance

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate high-key for its narrative appropriateness
- High-key in a thriller creates unintended safety
- High-key used ironically can be powerful if intentional

## Scene-level checks
- Is high-key lighting appropriate for the emotional register of this scene?
- If high-key is used ironically, is the intention clearly supported by other elements?

## Project-level checks
- Is high-key lighting reserved for scenes where safety, optimism, or false security is the intention?

## Warning signs
- High-key lighting in dramatic or tense scenes — makes threats feel safe
- High-key used as a default for all indoor scenes — no lighting variation

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Reserve high-key for specific scenes that require its emotional language
- When high-key is used ironically, reinforce it with other tonal elements

## Example assistant output
> Scene 8 is lit high-key and cheerfully — which is correct, it's Ana's last happy moment. But scenes 9 through 14 are also high-key for no narrative reason. As soon as the threat enters in scene 9, start pulling fill and adding contrast. The lighting should tell the story before the plot does.
