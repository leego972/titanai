# Match Cut

## Purpose
A match cut is an edit that creates visual, thematic, or sonic continuity between two shots by matching their composition, movement, or subject across the cut.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- A match cut joins two shots based on visual or thematic similarity — the similarity creates the meaning
- Types: action match (same movement across different spaces), graphic match (similar shapes), sound match
- Match cuts must be planned — they require specific coverage in production

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Identify match cut opportunities in the script — they require pre-planned coverage
- Check that match cuts create meaning beyond visual interest
- Brief the editor on intended match cuts

## Scene-level checks
- Does this match cut create meaning — what does the comparison say about the two scenes?
- Is there coverage on both sides of the match cut that makes the edit possible?

## Project-level checks
- Are match cut opportunities identified and planned in pre-production?
- Do match cuts serve the narrative or thematic connections they're creating?

## Warning signs
- Match cuts planned but coverage not shot — impossible to execute in post
- Graphic match cuts that are visually satisfying but thematically meaningless

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Identify match cut opportunities in the script and brief the DP and editor simultaneously
- Plan the specific framing and movement needed on both sides of the match cut

## Example assistant output
> Your transition from Elena practicing her speech in the mirror (scene 14) to Elena facing the committee (scene 15) is a natural match cut: same composition, same angle, different situation. If you frame scene 14's mirror shot to match the committee coverage — same focal length, same head position — the cut becomes a statement: this is the moment she stops rehearsing and starts living it.
