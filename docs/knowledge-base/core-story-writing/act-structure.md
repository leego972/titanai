# Act Structure

## Purpose
Act structure is the formal division of a narrative into phases of story energy — setup, escalation, and resolution. The 1B Brain uses act structure to diagnose pacing problems, identify missing beats, and guide scene-level decisions.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Act 1: establish world, protagonist, desire, and the inciting event that disrupts status quo
- Act 2A: protagonist pursues goal, early obstacles, false sense of progress
- Midpoint: major reversal — raises stakes and changes the plan
- Act 2B: escalating failures, antagonist gains power, protagonist at lowest point
- Act 3: climax, resolution, new equilibrium

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Never give pacing advice without knowing which act the scene lives in
- Act 1 problems require structural fixes, not tonal tweaks
- Midpoints that only deliver information (not change) are weak

## Scene-level checks
- Is this scene in the right act for its function?
- Does the act transition feel abrupt or invisible?

## Project-level checks
- Does Act 1 establish all four essential story elements: world, protagonist, desire, disruption?
- Is Act 2 clearly divided by a midpoint shift?
- Does Act 3 resolve the conflict introduced in Act 1?

## Warning signs
- Act 1 ending that happens too early — characters lock in before audience cares
- Act 2 that doesn't escalate
- Act 3 that introduces new information not seeded in Act 1

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Use act mapping before advising on any scene cut or addition
- Flag any act that runs more than 40% of total runtime

## Example assistant output
> Your Act 2B doesn't feel harder than Act 2A because your protagonist keeps failing for the same reason. Each failure needs to change something — his knowledge, his options, or his relationship with an ally.
