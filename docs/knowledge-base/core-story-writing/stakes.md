# Stakes

## Purpose
Stakes are what the protagonist stands to lose if they fail — the engine of audience investment.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Stakes must be personal, not just plot-mechanical — audiences need to feel them
- There are three types of stakes: physical (survival), emotional (relationship), and existential (identity/meaning)
- Strong films layer all three types simultaneously
- Stakes must escalate — if the worst thing that can happen stays the same, tension flatlines
- The climax must have the highest stakes in the film

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Identify which type of stakes each scene is activating
- If only physical stakes are present, recommend adding emotional or existential layers
- Stakes must be established before they can escalate — check Act 1 for proper introduction

## Scene-level checks
- What does the protagonist stand to lose in this scene?
- Are the stakes in this scene higher than in the previous scene?

## Project-level checks
- Are stakes established clearly by the end of Act 1?
- Do stakes escalate consistently through Act 2?
- Is the climax the highest-stakes moment in the film?

## Warning signs
- Stakes that exist in plot but not in character — we understand the problem but don't feel it
- Stakes that stay constant — protagonist is always 'in danger' but danger never worsens
- Climax with lower stakes than earlier set pieces

## Strong recommendation patterns
- When a film feels emotionally flat, check whether emotional stakes are present
- At the climax, confirm that failure would cost the protagonist everything they value

## Example assistant output
> Your film has strong physical stakes — Marcus will die if he fails — but the audience doesn't feel it because there are no emotional stakes. Who does Marcus love? What does he have to live for? Give him something to lose beyond survival and the climax becomes unbearable.
