# Treatment

## Purpose
A treatment is a narrative document that describes the film in prose, scene by scene, with tone, character, and visual language — written before the screenplay.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- A treatment runs 10–30 pages for a feature film
- It describes every major scene in present tense, active prose
- Character interiority and visual language should be embedded in the prose
- A treatment is a development tool — it's written to be rewritten

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate whether the treatment's structure maps to sound story architecture
- Identify missing beats — scenes that are implied but not written
- Flag character inconsistencies that will cause problems in screenplay stage

## Scene-level checks
- Does this scene in the treatment have a clear dramatic function?
- Is the emotional truth of the scene communicated, not just the plot mechanics?

## Project-level checks
- Does the treatment have a clear three-act structure?
- Are all major characters introduced with sufficient specificity?
- Is the ending earned by everything that precedes it?

## Warning signs
- Treatments that skip Act 2 detail
- Character descriptions that are physical only — no psychology
- Endings that arrive without sufficient setup

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Use the treatment as a structural diagnostic before writing the screenplay
- If the treatment's tone doesn't match the intended film, fix it before the screenplay

## Example assistant output
> Your treatment reads cleanly but your Act 2 is five lines covering what should be 40 pages of screenplay. That's where the story lives. Flesh out the midpoint, the darkest moment, and the final push before you write a word of script.
