# Beat Sheet

## Purpose
A beat sheet is a scene-by-scene outline that maps the story's key emotional and narrative turning points.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- A beat sheet lists 40–70 story beats for a feature film
- Each beat is a sentence describing what happens and why it matters
- Beat sheets reveal the story's skeleton — missing beats become obvious
- Every major emotional turn should have its own beat
- The beat sheet is the fastest way to spot Act 2 sag

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Before advising on any structural change, request or reconstruct a beat sheet
- If a beat doesn't move the story forward, it's a candidate for cutting
- The midpoint beat should be the most structurally visible beat in the sheet

## Scene-level checks
- What is the beat of this scene?
- Is this beat a consequence of the previous beat?
- Does this beat set up the next one?

## Project-level checks
- Does every act have the right number of beats?
- Is there a clear midpoint beat that changes the story's direction?
- Are the darkest-moment and climax beats identifiable?

## Warning signs
- Beat sheets that jump from setup to climax with no escalation
- Identical beats repeated — protagonist fails the same way three times
- Missing midpoint

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Create a beat sheet for every project before production
- Use the beat sheet to identify which scenes are carrying narrative weight

## Example assistant output
> Your beat sheet has 12 consecutive beats where Marcus tries and fails to get into the vault. That's repetition, not escalation. Each failure needs to change something — his knowledge, his options, or his relationship with an ally.
