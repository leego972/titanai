# Story Architecture

## Purpose
Story architecture is the invisible skeleton that holds every scene, character, and decision together. Without it, films drift — scenes feel unconnected, momentum dies, and audiences disengage. The 1B Brain must understand architecture before advising on any production decision.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- A feature film is a three-act causal chain where every beat must earn its place
- Story architecture = premise + structure + escalating conflict + resolution
- Weak architecture cannot be fixed in post — it must be caught in development
- Architecture is separate from plot; it governs how story energy moves
- Every scene must either advance plot, reveal character, or raise stakes — ideally all three

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Always identify which act the current scene belongs to before giving advice
- If a scene has no causal consequence, flag it as potentially cuttable
- Check that the film's central question is established by the end of Act 1
- Ensure the midpoint shifts the story's direction meaningfully
- Confirm the climax answers the central dramatic question raised at the start

## Scene-level checks
- Does this scene move the story forward or just fill time?
- Is there a clear cause-and-effect chain connecting this scene to the next?

## Project-level checks
- Is the central dramatic question established clearly in Act 1?
- Does the midpoint represent a genuine shift in direction or stakes?
- Is the climax the direct result of everything built before it?

## Warning signs
- Scenes that entertain but don't advance story
- Act 2 that meanders without escalating the central conflict
- A climax that resolves a different problem than the one established in Act 1

## Strong recommendation patterns
- When a scene feels wrong, ask: what is its function in the architecture?
- Recommend the director map all scenes to their structural position before shooting

## Example assistant output
> Your Act 2 feels long not because you have too many scenes but because the escalation stalls around page 55. The conflict needs to intensify after the midpoint — your protagonist needs to face a new, worse version of their core problem.
