# Off-Screen Sound

## Purpose
Off-screen sound is any audio that exists beyond the frame — sounds whose sources are not visible. It expands the world beyond what the camera shows.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Off-screen sound creates a world beyond the frame — the audience hears what they can't see
- The invisible source is often more frightening, more interesting, and more powerful
- Off-screen dialogue creates dynamic scenes without requiring complex coverage
- Sound perspective: off-screen sounds have appropriate distance and reverb

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate scenes for off-screen sound opportunities — what would be more powerful unseen?
- For horror and tension, keep threat sources off-screen as long as possible
- Use off-screen dialogue to reduce coverage requirements

## Scene-level checks
- Is there sound in this scene that would be more powerful if its source was off-screen?
- Is the acoustic perspective of off-screen sounds appropriate?

## Project-level checks
- Are off-screen sounds used to expand the world beyond the frame?
- Is off-screen sound used for threat-building in tension and horror scenes?

## Warning signs
- Off-screen sounds without appropriate acoustic perspective
- Missed opportunities: threats always shown when keeping them off-screen would be scarier

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Plan off-screen sound as a creative tool in pre-production
- Design off-screen acoustic perspective specifically: how far is the source, what's the environment?

## Example assistant output
