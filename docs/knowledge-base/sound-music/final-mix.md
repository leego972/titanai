# Final Mix

## Purpose
The final mix is the process of combining all audio elements — dialogue, music, sound effects, ambience, foley — into the finished audio track of the film.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- The final mix sets the balance between dialogue, music, and effects
- Dialogue clarity is the primary rule: the audience must always understand what characters say
- Dynamic range: the difference between the quietest and loudest moments — determines emotional impact
- The final mix is a creative process, not just a technical one

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Approach the final mix as a creative process, not a technical sign-off
- Check that dialogue remains clear in all scenes
- Evaluate the dynamic range: are quiet moments quiet enough to make loud moments loud?

## Scene-level checks
- Does the audio mix in this scene prioritise dialogue clarity?
- Is the music-to-dialogue balance appropriate?

## Project-level checks
- Is the final mix approached as a creative process with director involvement?
- Is dialogue clarity maintained throughout?

## Warning signs
- Over-scored final mix — music buries dialogue
- Uniform loudness throughout — no dynamic range
- Director not present for the final mix — creative decisions made by technicians

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Director must be present for the final mix — it's a creative stage
- Test the mix on different playback systems: cinema, TV, laptop, headphones

## Example assistant output
> Your fine cut sounds great in the edit suite but your scene 22 dialogue is going to be inaudible in a cinema because of the music bed. In a cinema, at exhibition volume, that music bed becomes significantly louder. Pull it to -30dB in the cinema mix. Plan two mixes, not one.
