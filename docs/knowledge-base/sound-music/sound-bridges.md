# Sound Bridges

## Purpose
A sound bridge is a technique where the audio from the next scene begins before the image cut, or the audio from the previous scene continues over the first image of the next scene.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Sound bridges smooth transitions, create emotional connections between scenes
- The most powerful sound bridges are thematic — the sound of Scene B comments on Scene A
- Sound bridges can be used to compress time without disorienting the audience

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Identify scene transitions that would benefit from sound bridges
- Check that sound bridges are intentional — accidental sound overlap is confusing
- For emotional continuity across scene changes, use music bridges

## Scene-level checks
- Is this sound bridge creating emotional continuity or meaningful contrast?
- Is the return from the cutaway landing at the right dramatic moment?

## Project-level checks
- Are sound bridges planned for key scene transitions?
- Are accidental sound overlaps distinguished from intentional sound bridges?

## Warning signs
- Accidental sound overlap from sloppy editing
- Sound bridges that break audio consistency

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Plan sound bridges at key story transitions in pre-production
- Work with the editor and composer to design music bridges across emotional scene transitions

## Example assistant output
> Your transition from the funeral (scene 16) to the office party (scene 17) would be devastating with a sound bridge: let the party music enter 3 seconds before the image cut. The contrast between the grief of the funeral and the inappropriate cheerfulness of the incoming party is your transition.
