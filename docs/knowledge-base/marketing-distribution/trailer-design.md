# Trailer Design

## Purpose
A trailer is the film's primary marketing tool — a 90–150 second film designed to generate desire to see the full film.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Trailer structure: hook (first 10 seconds), world establishment (next 30 seconds), rising stakes and conflict (next 40 seconds), peak montage, title reveal
- Trailers don't follow the film's story structure — they create their own impression
- Music drives the trailer's emotional journey — often the first creative decision
- Trailers for different platforms have different specifications: theatrical (2.5 min), social (60 sec, 30 sec, 15 sec)

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate whether footage is trailer-ready — visually strong moments, clear stakes
- Brief the trailer editor: trailers are separate creative works, not film edits
- Plan multiple trailer lengths: theatrical, social formats, international

## Scene-level checks
- Is this footage strong enough for trailer use?
- Are the film's key selling points visible in the footage?

## Project-level checks
- Is trailer production planned as a separate creative process from the film edit?
- Are multiple trailer lengths planned for different platform requirements?

## Warning signs
- Trailers edited by the film editor without trailer craft experience
- Trailers that reveal key plot developments
- No versioning — single trailer for all platforms

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Hire a specialist trailer editor if possible — trailer craft is different from film editing
- Let the music drive the structure, then select images that serve the music and the meaning

## Example assistant output
> Your film's three best trailer moments are: scene 8 (Elena's confrontation in the rain), scene 22 (the recording reveal), and the Act 3 climax's first shot. Build your trailer around those three anchors. The music should peak at moment two and the title should land 5 seconds after moment three.
