# Press Kit

## Purpose
A press kit (or electronic press kit — EPK) is the collection of materials provided to journalists, programmers, and distributors to present the film professionally.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- A press kit contains: production stills, director's statement, synopsis (one-line, short, long), cast and crew bios, and director interview
- Production stills must be high-quality, cleared for publication, and represent the film's visual language
- The synopsis exists in three lengths: logline, short (one paragraph), long (one page)
- Director's statement is personal and specific — not a plot summary

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Help directors understand what belongs in a press kit and ensure all elements are prepared
- Check that production stills are high quality and represent the film's visual language
- Ensure all press kit elements are ready before any press or festival submissions

## Scene-level checks
- Is this press kit element of professional quality?
- Does the press kit represent the film's visual language and tone accurately?

## Project-level checks
- Is a complete press kit prepared before festival submissions or press outreach?
- Are production stills of publication quality?

## Warning signs
- Press kit assembled at the last minute with poor quality stills
- Director's statement that summarises the plot rather than articulating the vision
- No dedicated production stills

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Plan a production stills session specifically for press kit purposes
- Create a digital press page on the film's website before any public film activity

## Example assistant output
> Your press kit is missing one critical element: production stills. The behind-the-scenes photographs from your phone don't cut it. Plan a stills session: 2-3 hours on your most visually striking set, with a dedicated stills photographer working alongside your DP. You need 20-30 high-resolution stills that represent the film's visual language.
