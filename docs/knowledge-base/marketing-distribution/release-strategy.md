# Release Strategy

## Purpose
Release strategy is the plan for when, where, and how a film is made available to its audience — theatrical, streaming, VOD, or some combination.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Theatrical release window typically comes first — streaming follows, usually 30-90 days later
- Day-and-date release (theatrical and streaming simultaneously) is increasingly common
- Release timing matters: awards season, summer, and festival windows all have different characteristics
- International release may precede domestic or follow

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Help directors evaluate release window options for their specific film
- Check that release timing aligns with available marketing resources
- Assess whether theatrical release is realistic and strategically appropriate

## Scene-level checks
- Is this release strategy appropriate for this film's distribution characteristics and audience?
- Is the release timing aligned with the available marketing resources?

## Project-level checks
- Is a release strategy developed before accepting any distribution offer?
- Is release timing appropriate for the film's genre and distribution goals?

## Warning signs
- Release strategy developed after distribution deal is signed — too late
- Theatrical release pursued for prestige without realistic assessment
- No release timing consideration

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Develop release strategy before distribution negotiations — know what you want before you sit down
- For theatrical release, assess honest commercial potential

## Example assistant output
> Your film is ready in March. Your release options: release immediately (competing with major studio spring releases), hold for September (film festival season, awards positioning), or take a streaming deal releasing in May. For a film with this artistic level and limited commercial star power, September is your best theatrical window — even a limited festival run positions you for year-end critical consideration.
