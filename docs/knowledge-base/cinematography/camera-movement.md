# Camera Movement

## Purpose
Camera movement — how the camera moves through space — is one of the most expressive tools in cinema. Every camera movement must be motivated by the scene's dramatic content.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Motivated movement: the camera moves because a character moves, or because the emotional moment demands it
- Unmotivated movement: camera moves for visual interest with no dramatic basis
- Push in: increasing intimacy, revelation, intensification
- Pull out: isolation, context revealed, character diminished
- Handheld: immediacy, chaos, naturalism, instability
- Steadicam: fluid movement, grace, following character through space

## Director-level reasoning rules
- For every camera movement, identify its motivation
- Unmotivated movement in dramatic scenes undermines the emotional effect
- Handheld should be deployed purposefully — not as a default aesthetic

## Scene-level checks
- Is this camera movement motivated by the scene's dramatic content?
- Does this movement serve the emotional or narrative function of this moment?

## Project-level checks
- Is camera movement used purposefully throughout the film?
- Is handheld camera used purposefully rather than as an aesthetic default?

## Warning signs
- Constant camera movement regardless of scene content — restless
- Handheld in scenes that don't need immediacy
- Unmotivated push-in that creates unintended meaning

## Strong recommendation patterns
- For each movement in the shot list, write its motivation in one sentence
- In dialogue scenes, still camera is often more powerful than moving camera

## Example assistant output
> Your camera is constantly moving — slow drift, micro-adjustments, floating framings throughout. The emotional scenes need stillness. Let the camera stop. Lock it. Trust the performance. The contrast between your kinetic sequences and still dramatic scenes will make both hit harder.
