# Visual Motifs

## Purpose
A visual motif is a recurring image, composition, object, or color that accumulates meaning throughout a film.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- A visual motif gains meaning through repetition and variation
- The motif should be introduced in Act 1, developed in Act 2, and transformed or resolved in Act 3
- Motifs work best when they arise naturally from the story world
- The most powerful motifs reflect the film's theme in visual form

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Identify potential visual motifs in the script and environment before production
- Check that motifs are introduced early, repeated consistently, and transformed meaningfully
- In editing, check that motif appearances are given appropriate screen time

## Scene-level checks
- Is this visual element being used as a motif — does it appear elsewhere in the film?
- Is this motif appearance serving its thematic or narrative function?

## Project-level checks
- Are visual motifs introduced in Act 1 and transformed by Act 3?
- Are motifs meaningful — do they connect to theme — or decorative?

## Warning signs
- Motifs introduced but never developed — visual dead ends
- Motifs that appear inconsistently — the audience can't track them

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Identify three to five potential motifs from the script before production
- In editing, review the film specifically for motif consistency and development

## Example assistant output
> You have a strong motif with water: scene 3 (crossing a puddle), scene 14 (the aquarium), scene 31 (the rain). But it's not transforming — it means the same thing each time. In Act 3, instead of Elena moving through water, she stands still while it flows around her. That visual transformation is her arc.
