# Shot Sizes

## Purpose
Shot size is the primary vocabulary of visual storytelling — it determines how close the audience is to the subject, emotionally and physically.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Extreme Wide Shot (EWS): establishes environment, character is small or absent
- Wide Shot (WS): full character in environment
- Medium Shot (MS): waist up — the workhorse of dialogue scenes
- Medium Close-Up (MCU): chest to top of head — standard conversation framing
- Close-Up (CU): face fills the frame — emotion, reaction, intimacy
- Extreme Close-Up (ECU): eyes, mouth, hands — maximum intensity, used sparingly

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Know which shot size serves each dramatic moment
- Close-ups should be earned — used for emphasis, not as default
- Wide shots provide context; close-ups provide emotion — dramatic scenes need both

## Scene-level checks
- Is the shot size appropriate for the dramatic function of this moment?
- Is this close-up earned by the scene's emotional weight?

## Project-level checks
- Is there appropriate variety of shot sizes across the film?
- Are extreme close-ups used sparingly enough to retain impact?

## Warning signs
- Default close-up for all dialogue — emotionally claustrophobic
- Wide shot for emotionally intimate moments — creates unintended distance
- Extreme close-up overuse — loses impact through repetition

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Recommend shot sizes based on dramatic function, not visual preference
- Flag any sequence where shot size is uniform

## Example assistant output
> Your dialogue scenes default to close-up throughout. By scene 40, close-ups have lost their power. Use medium shots as your default for dialogue and reserve close-ups for moments of maximum emotional revelation. When you finally go to CU in scene 41, it will hit.
