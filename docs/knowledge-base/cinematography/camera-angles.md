# Camera Angles

## Purpose
Camera angle is the position of the camera relative to the subject — above, below, or level. Angle communicates power, psychology, and emotional relationship.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Eye level: neutral — the camera is a peer
- Low angle: the subject appears powerful, threatening, larger than life
- High angle: the subject appears small, vulnerable, diminished
- Dutch tilt (canted angle): psychological instability, unease
- Bird's eye: omniscient perspective, characters as elements of a larger pattern

## Director-level reasoning rules
- For every significant shot, know the angle and its communicative intention
- Angles should be consistent within a character's psychological state
- Angle changes on the same character signal a shift in their power or status

## Scene-level checks
- What does this camera angle communicate about the subject's power and status?
- Is this angle consistent with the character's psychological state in this moment?

## Project-level checks
- Is angle used consistently to represent power dynamics between characters?
- Are Dutch tilts used purposefully, not as visual decoration?

## Warning signs
- Dutch tilts used throughout a scene for atmosphere — loses meaning
- Low angle for a powerless character — contradicts intended meaning

## Strong recommendation patterns
- For characters with power, default to eye level or slight low angle
- Reserve Dutch tilts for specific psychological disruption — one per sequence maximum

## Example assistant output
> You're using Dutch tilts in eight consecutive scenes. By scene 30 they communicate nothing. Reserve the Dutch tilt for the moment where Marcus's world breaks: scene 33. One Dutch tilt in the right place hits harder than eight decorative ones.
