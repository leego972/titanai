# Depth of Field

## Purpose
Depth of field is the range of distance within which objects appear acceptably sharp. Control of depth of field is a primary compositional and narrative tool.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Shallow depth of field: subject sharp, background blurred — isolates subject, creates intimacy
- Deep depth of field: multiple planes in focus — context maintained, relationships visible
- Depth of field is controlled by aperture, focal length, and subject-to-camera distance
- Selective focus is a narrative tool — pulling focus transfers dramatic emphasis

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Recommend depth of field based on dramatic function, not aesthetic preference
- Shallow DOF in dialogue creates intimacy but removes context
- Brief the DP: selective focus should be used as a narrative tool

## Scene-level checks
- Is depth of field appropriate for this scene's dramatic function?
- Is selective focus used to direct audience attention to the right subject?

## Project-level checks
- Is depth of field used consistently within the film's visual language?
- Is selective focus used as a deliberate narrative tool?

## Warning signs
- Default shallow focus for every scene — loses communicative power
- Pulling focus at the wrong dramatic moment
- Shallow focus that obscures important environmental information

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Plan depth of field in the lookbook: shallow for intimacy, deeper for context
- Selective focus pulls should be planned and rehearsed

## Example assistant output
> Your scene 31 uses shallow focus throughout so Dr. Vance is always blurred in the background. When she speaks the key line, Marcus is in sharp focus reacting but she's soft. Pull focus to her for that line. The audience looks where the lens is sharpest — make sure that's the dramatic priority.
