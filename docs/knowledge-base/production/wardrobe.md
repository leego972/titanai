# Wardrobe

## Purpose
Wardrobe (costume design) is the clothing and accessories worn by characters — one of the most powerful and immediate communicators of character psychology, social status, and story arc.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Wardrobe communicates character before a word is spoken — color, fit, and condition tell a story
- Character arcs can be tracked through wardrobe changes — what characters wear reflects their internal state
- Wardrobe continuity is essential — clothing must be tracked shot by shot
- Color in wardrobe interacts with the cinematography and production design

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate wardrobe for character psychology — what does this character's clothing say about them?
- Check that wardrobe arcs align with character arcs
- Coordinate wardrobe colors with the production designer and DP

## Scene-level checks
- Does this character's wardrobe reflect their psychological state at this point in the story?
- Is the wardrobe consistent with the character's established psychology?

## Project-level checks
- Does wardrobe track with the character arc?
- Is wardrobe coordinated with production design and cinematography color palettes?
- Is continuity tracking in place for all hero wardrobe items?

## Warning signs
- Wardrobe that contradicts character psychology
- Character wardrobe that doesn't change despite significant character arc development
- Continuity errors in wardrobe — clothing that changes between setups

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Brief the costume designer on character psychology: what does this person wear to protect themselves?
- Create a wardrobe arc chart: how does each character's clothing change across the film?

## Example assistant output
> Elena armours herself against the world. Her Act 1 wardrobe: severe cuts, closed necklines, dark and controlled palette. As her armour breaks in Act 2, something slightly looser, a colour she wouldn't normally wear. By Act 3, the armour is gone but the confidence is different. The costume designer needs this arc, not just the aesthetic direction.
