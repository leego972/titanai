# Script Breakdown

## Purpose
A script breakdown is the systematic analysis of a screenplay to identify all the production elements it requires.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- A script breakdown translates story into production requirements — page by page
- Every scene is broken down by: cast required, location, props, wardrobe, effects, extras
- The breakdown drives the budget and schedule — inaccurate breakdowns create budget surprises
- Script revisions must trigger breakdown updates — outdated breakdowns are dangerous

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Verify that a thorough script breakdown has been completed before scheduling or budgeting
- Check that breakdown reflects the current draft
- Brief the producer: the breakdown is the foundation of the budget

## Scene-level checks
- Has this scene been broken down accurately — are all required elements identified?
- Are the breakdown's production requirements reflected in the schedule and budget?

## Project-level checks
- Is there a complete script breakdown for the current draft?
- Is the breakdown updated every time the script changes?

## Warning signs
- Breakdown based on an older draft
- Missing elements in the breakdown
- Production-heavy scenes scheduled without sufficient time

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Conduct the breakdown from the most current draft
- Update the breakdown after every significant script revision before re-scheduling

## Example assistant output
> Your script breakdown shows scene 28 requires a working helicopter and three motorcycles. That's the most production-heavy scene and it's currently scheduled for Day 12, your third day of shooting. Complex logistics scenes should be scheduled later when the crew is in sync. Move scene 28 to Day 18 or later.
