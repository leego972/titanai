# Storyboards

## Purpose
Storyboards are visual representations of the planned shots in a sequence — drawn panels that show framing, composition, camera angle, and movement.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Storyboards translate verbal shot descriptions into visual plans
- They're most essential for complex sequences: action, visual effects, multi-camera setups
- Storyboards communicate to the DP, AD, and department heads what's planned
- A storyboard is not a commitment — on-the-day discovery can and should override it

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Storyboard complex sequences before any other pre-production work
- Brief all department heads on storyboarded sequences
- Review storyboards with the DP before production

## Scene-level checks
- Does this storyboard sequence account for all the shots needed to edit the scene?
- Is the storyboard realistic — can the planned shots actually be executed on the day?

## Project-level checks
- Are complex sequences storyboarded before production begins?
- Are storyboards distributed to all relevant departments?

## Warning signs
- Storyboards created but not shared — the director's plan unknown to crew
- Storyboards that don't account for practical shooting constraints

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Storyboard sequences in order of complexity — most complex sequences first
- For visual effects sequences, treat the storyboard as a legal document

## Example assistant output
> Your action sequence in Act 3 has storyboards but they've only been seen by you and the DP. Your stunt coordinator has never seen them and doesn't know the camera's planned position for each stunt beat. Share the storyboards now. Every department will find at least one thing they need to know.
