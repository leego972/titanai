# Shot Lists

## Purpose
A shot list is a scene-by-scene, shot-by-shot document created by the director that specifies every planned camera setup.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- A shot list converts the director's creative vision into executable camera instructions
- It must be realistic — ambitious shot lists that can't be executed create schedule problems
- The shot list is a guide, not a contract — discovery on the day is part of filmmaking
- Shot lists should prioritise the KEY shot for each scene — everything else is secondary

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate shot lists for creative ambition vs production realism
- Check that the KEY shot for each scene is identified and prioritised
- Brief the DP on the shot list before each shooting day

## Scene-level checks
- Is the KEY shot for this scene clearly identified in the shot list?
- Is the shot list realistic given the available shooting time?

## Project-level checks
- Is a shot list created for every scene before the shooting day?
- Is the shot list shared with the DP and 1st AD?

## Warning signs
- Shot lists so ambitious they can't be executed — creates schedule disasters
- Shot lists not shared with DP — creative surprises on set

## Strong recommendation patterns
- For every scene, identify the KEY shot first — it must be protected above all others
- Mark each shot as MUST/WANT/IF TIME — clear priority hierarchy

## Example assistant output
> Your shot list for scene 22 has 14 setups. Your schedule gives you 4 hours. A setup takes minimum 15 minutes. 14 × 15 = 3.5 hours setup time alone, leaving 30 minutes for actual shooting. Cut to 8 setups and rank them: MUST, WANT, IF TIME.
