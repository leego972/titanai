# Budgeting

## Purpose
A film budget is the detailed financial plan that allocates funds to every department and production activity.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Film budgets are divided into above-the-line (creative talent) and below-the-line (production costs)
- Every creative decision has a cost implication
- The budget is set before production begins but is spent day by day
- Cost-to-completion is the running estimate of what the film will cost from this point forward

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Help directors understand the cost implications of their creative decisions
- Check whether creative decisions are within budget parameters
- Identify which creative decisions have the highest cost implications and assess alternatives

## Scene-level checks
- What is the estimated cost of this scene's planned approach, and is there a more budget-efficient alternative?
- Is this creative decision within the budget parameters set for this department?

## Project-level checks
- Is the budget sufficient for the production's creative ambitions?
- Is daily spending tracked against budget?

## Warning signs
- Creative decisions made without understanding cost implications
- Budget consumed unevenly — departments overspending early
- No contingency in the budget

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Brief directors on the cost structure of their production before making creative decisions
- Create a budget decision protocol: any creative decision above a threshold requires cost-impact assessment

## Example assistant output
> Your crane sequence is planned for 3 setup days. Your budget allows for 1. Three options: 1) Drone at $2,000/day — achieves a similar visual result. 2) Reduce to 1 crane day and redesign for a single key crane setup. 3) Rewrite the sequence to achieve the same effect without crane. Let's make the decision today so the schedule can adjust.
