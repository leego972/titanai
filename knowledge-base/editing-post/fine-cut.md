# Fine Cut

## Purpose
The fine cut is the near-final version of the film — where individual scenes are polished to their best performance selections, rhythm is refined, and the film approaches picture lock.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- The fine cut is where individual scene craft is perfected — performance selections, rhythm, timing
- Structural changes in the fine cut are expensive — avoid them unless absolutely necessary
- The fine cut is shown to producers, test audiences, and distributors

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Brief the director: fine cut is refinement, not restructuring
- Evaluate performance selections for each scene — is the best take being used?
- Create a fine cut change list before making edits

## Scene-level checks
- Is this scene at its best performance selection?
- Is the rhythm in this scene precisely right for its emotional function?

## Project-level checks
- Are all scenes at their best performance selections?
- Is the film's structure stable?

## Warning signs
- Structural changes in the fine cut stage — too expensive
- Fine cut shown without music and effects — creates false assessment
- Second-guessing structural decisions that were made correctly in the rough cut

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Complete the fine cut change list before making edits
- Evaluate each scene with temp music and effects before finalising

## Example assistant output
