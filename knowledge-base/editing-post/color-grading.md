# Color Grading

## Purpose
Color grading is the process of adjusting and enhancing the color, contrast, and luminance of the film to achieve the intended visual tone.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Color grading happens in two phases: primary (overall exposure and color balance) and secondary (specific area adjustments)
- The grade must be guided by the visual language established during production
- Grading is not correction — it's interpretation: turning technically correct images into emotionally correct ones
- Different scenes require different grades within a unified visual language

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Brief the colorist with a visual language document — reference stills, lookbooks, and tone descriptions
- Check that the grade is serving the film's narrative arc
- Evaluate grades specifically for emotional accuracy

## Scene-level checks
- Does the grade serve the emotional register of this scene?
- Is the grade consistent with the established visual language?

## Project-level checks
- Is the colorist briefed with a clear visual language document?
- Does the grade evolve appropriately across the film's emotional arc?

## Warning signs
- Grade that overrides visual language decisions made in camera
- Inconsistent grade between scenes
- Colorist working without a clear director's brief

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Create a grade brief: reference stills, emotional register descriptions, and what to avoid
- Grade with the director present — it's a creative stage, not a technical one

## Example assistant output
> Your grade brief says 'cinematic and warm.' That's insufficient direction. Write this instead: Act 1 — warm tungsten, slight golden tint on skin, moderate contrast, feels like home. Act 2 — progressively cooler, desaturated skin, higher contrast, feels clinical. Act 3 — the warmth returns but now it's harsh — overexposed, not soft. The grade is Elena's emotional arc expressed in color.
