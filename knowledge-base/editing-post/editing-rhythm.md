# Editing Rhythm

## Purpose
Editing rhythm is the pulse of cuts within and between scenes — the speed, pattern, and variation of edits that create pacing, tension, and emotional flow.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Editing rhythm is not uniform speed — it's the variation between fast and slow cuts
- Fast cutting creates urgency, chaos, and energy; slow cutting creates contemplation and weight
- Rhythm must match the scene's emotional register
- The rhythm of cuts should vary across the film's emotional arc

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate editing rhythm for its alignment with emotional register
- Check that rhythm varies appropriately across the film
- Brief the editor: rhythm is a creative tool, not a default setting

## Scene-level checks
- Does the cutting rhythm in this scene match its emotional register?
- Is there variation in rhythm within this scene?

## Project-level checks
- Does editing rhythm vary appropriately across the film's acts?
- Is there a signature editing rhythm that constitutes the film's visual style?

## Warning signs
- Uniform cutting rhythm throughout the film
- Fast cuts in emotional scenes — prevents the audience from feeling
- Slow cuts in action scenes — loses urgency

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Map editing rhythm to scene type: emotional drama = slower, action = faster, comedy = precise
- Review the rough cut specifically for rhythm consistency

## Example assistant output
> Your film has three consecutive action sequences all cut at the same fast rhythm. By the third, the cuts have lost energy. Design a rhythm arc: first is mid-speed, second is fast, third starts fast then slows as the protagonist regains control. The rhythm change in sequence three signals the shift before the outcome is clear.
