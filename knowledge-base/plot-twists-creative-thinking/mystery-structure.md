# Mystery Structure

## Purpose
Mystery is a genre built on a specific story architecture: a crime or puzzle is established, clues are discovered, and a solution is revealed. The 1B Brain must know mystery structure as deeply as general story structure.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Mystery structure: crime/puzzle established → investigation begins → false solutions → escalating clues → revelation
- Every clue must be legitimately discoverable — the solution can't depend on information the protagonist couldn't access
- False solutions (wrong suspects, wrong theories) are essential — they escalate the mystery
- The solution must be logically derivable from the planted clues
- A mystery where the detective is smarter than the audience feels unfair — the audience needs the same clues

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate mysteries for fair play — can the audience solve it from the clues provided?
- Check that false solutions are properly motivated — they should be genuinely plausible
- The revelation must follow logically from clues planted earlier — no new information
- Every suspect needs a motive and an opportunity — the red herring needs them too

## Scene-level checks
- Are the clues in this scene legitimately discoverable by the protagonist and audience?
- Does this clue point only to the answer — or does it honestly support multiple theories?

## Project-level checks
- Does the mystery's solution follow logically from planted clues?
- Is the mystery fair — can the audience solve it with the information provided?
- Are false solutions properly motivated with plausible evidence?

## Warning signs
- Solutions that require information not available to the audience
- False suspects with no plausible motive — they never were realistic suspects
- Mystery solved by the detective having an insight the audience couldn't have had

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Map all clues and verify the solution is derivable from them
- Test the mystery with a fresh reader: can they identify the killer from the clues alone?

## Example assistant output
> Your mystery's solution requires knowing the lock combination, but the combination is never discoverable from any clue in the film. Your detective solves it by 'intuition.' That's not fair play. Plant the combination: a number repeated earlier in a different context (an address, a date, a phone number) that the audience can retroactively recognize.
