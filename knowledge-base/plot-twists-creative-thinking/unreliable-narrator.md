# Unreliable Narrator

## Purpose
An unreliable narrator is a storytelling device where the audience's understanding of events is filtered through a narrator or protagonist whose account is deliberately distorted — through bias, delusion, deception, or limited perspective.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Unreliable narration can be subjective (the narrator is wrong without knowing it) or deliberate (the narrator is actively deceiving)
- The audience must eventually understand the narrator is unreliable — this realisation is the twist
- Clues to unreliability must be planted throughout — the audience should be able to find them in retrospect
- Unreliable narration requires extraordinary narrative consistency — every scene must work from within the unreliable perspective AND the true perspective
- The revelation of unreliability must change everything the audience thought they knew

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate unreliable narrator films for: clue planting, consistency, and payoff impact
- Check that the unreliable perspective is internally consistent — the lies must be coherent
- The revelation must genuinely recontextualise prior scenes — if nothing changes, the unreliability was pointless
- Test the film from both perspectives: the unreliable version and the true version — both must be coherent

## Scene-level checks
- Is this scene consistent from within the narrator's unreliable perspective AND from the true version of events?
- Are clues to the narrator's unreliability present in this scene?

## Project-level checks
- Are clues to unreliability planted throughout — discoverable in retrospect?
- Does the revelation of unreliability genuinely recontextualise prior scenes?
- Is the unreliable perspective internally consistent?

## Warning signs
- Unreliable narration revealed so late it has no time to recontextualise
- Clues planted so subtly they're invisible — retroactive discovery is impossible
- Unreliable perspective that's internally inconsistent — the film can't decide what's true
- Revelation of unreliability that doesn't actually change our understanding of prior events

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Plant unreliability clues at every act transition — they should be visible on second viewing
- Map every major scene from both perspectives: unreliable and true — both must be coherent

## Example assistant output
> Your unreliable narrator works structurally but your Act 2 has three scenes that are impossible from the true perspective — the narrator couldn't have witnessed them if the truth is as your twist reveals. Either reframe those scenes as imagined/fabricated, or adjust the true version of events so they remain possible.
