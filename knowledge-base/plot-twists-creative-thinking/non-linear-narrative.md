# Non-Linear Narrative

## Purpose
Non-linear narratives tell stories out of chronological order — through flashbacks, flash-forwards, parallel timelines, or fractured structures. The 1B Brain must evaluate non-linear structures for story purpose and audience comprehension.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Non-linear structure is a tool, not a style — it must serve the story
- The best non-linear films reveal information at the moment it has maximum emotional impact
- The audience must always know where they are in time — even if the structure is deliberately disorienting
- Non-linear structure multiplies continuity risks — timeline and character state must be tracked carefully
- The story must be coherent when reassembled chronologically

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Ask: why is this story being told non-linearly? If the answer is 'style,' reconsider
- Evaluate whether the non-linear structure creates more emotional impact than a linear telling
- Check that the audience always knows their temporal position — deliberate confusion is different from accidental confusion
- Map the complete chronological timeline and verify it's coherent

## Scene-level checks
- Does the non-linear structure of this scene reveal information at the moment of maximum emotional impact?
- Is the audience's temporal position clear — or is the confusion intentional?

## Project-level checks
- Is the non-linear structure serving a clear narrative purpose?
- Is the chronological timeline coherent when mapped?
- Are continuity implications of non-linear structure tracked?

## Warning signs
- Non-linear structure used for style rather than story purpose
- Non-linear structure that confuses without paying off the confusion
- Timeline errors created by non-linear structure — characters know things they shouldn't yet

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Create a two-column timeline: story time and screen time — verify coherence
- Ask: if this film were told linearly, would it be better or worse? The answer justifies the structure

## Example assistant output
> Your non-linear structure is creating a problem: the audience is given information in act 2 that makes scene 8 (which comes later on screen but earlier in story time) feel dramatically neutered. The flashback structure has spoiled its own setup. Either restructure the order, or withhold the key information from the act 2 sequence so scene 8 still has its impact.
