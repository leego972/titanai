## PRE-PRODUCTION

### Development
- **Script Coverage** — logline, synopsis, budget range estimate, marketability, comparable titles
- **Script Breakdown** — scene-by-scene analysis extracting cast, locations, props, wardrobe, VFX, stunts, special equipment
- **Budget Topsheet** — above-the-line (script, producer, director, cast), below-the-line (crew, equipment, locations, post), contingency (10% standard, 15% for action/VFX)
- **Scheduling** — strip board methodology, one-liner schedule, day-out-of-days, company moves
- **Green Light Criteria** — budget locked, financing committed, key attachments confirmed (director + lead cast), E&O insurance, chain of title cleared

### Financing Structures
- **Studio Financing** — studio funds 100%, owns all rights, producer receives fee + backend
- **Independent** — gap financing, pre-sales (territorial distribution rights sold before production), tax incentives (UK: HETV 25% credit, US: state incentives vary 15-40%), equity investment, co-productions
- **Soft Money** — government grants, film commissions, rebates — never counted as equity
- **Waterfall** — order of repayment: negative costs → deferred → investors → net profits

### Crew Hierarchy
- **Producer** — owns the project, responsible for financing, hiring, delivery
- **Line Producer** — translates creative vision into budget and schedule
- **UPM (Unit Production Manager)** — day-to-day operational control
- **1st AD** — runs the set, maintains schedule, safety coordinator
- **2nd AD** — manages cast, extras, call sheets, advance scheduling
- **2nd 2nd AD** — set PAs, background action, talent wrangling

### Casting
- **Casting Director** — talent relationships, audition process, offers
- **Offer vs Audition** — A-list talent always offer only; auditions for supporting roles
- **Deal Points** — fee, billing, approvals (hair/makeup/wardrobe/script), travel, accommodation (first class air threshold), per diem, credit position
- **Stunt Coordinator** — doubles casting, safety planning, rehearsal schedule

### Locations
- **Location Scout** — selects practical locations matching script requirements
- **Location Manager** — negotiates agreements, permits, parking, resident notifications
- **Film Permit** — required for any public filming; LA typically 3-5 days; NYC 1-2 days
- **Location Agreement** — fee, access hours, restoration clause, damage deposit
- **Studio vs Practical** — studio: control, cost efficiency at scale; practical: authenticity, logistical complexity

### Camera Department (Pre-Production)
- **DP (Director of Photography)** — visual language of the film, camera and lighting design
- **Camera Test** — lens tests, colour tests, VFX witness markers, wardrobe/makeup tests
- **Lens Selection** — spherical vs anamorphic, focal length suite, filtration package
- **Format Decision** — digital (ARRIRAW, REDCODE RAW, Sony XAVC) vs film (35mm, 65mm)
- **LUTs** — show LUT (creative intent), technical LUT (camera to display), on-set monitoring

## PRINCIPAL PHOTOGRAPHY

### Daily Operations
- **Call Sheet** — issued night before, lists all cast/crew calls, scenes, locations, special equipment
- **Production Report** — daily record: scenes shot, pages completed, scenes scheduled vs completed, overtime, incidents
- **One-Liner** — condensed schedule showing scene number, INT/EXT, day/night, cast, page count
- **Camera Reports** — roll number, scene, take, circle takes, any issues

### Camera Systems
- **ARRI ALEXA 35** — 4.6K sensor, 17 stops DR, native ASA 800/3200, ARRIRAW or ARRI ProRes
- **ARRI ALEXA Mini LF** — large format, 4.5K, 14+ stops, ideal for anamorphic work
- **RED MONSTRO 8K VV** — high resolution, high frame rate capability, REDCODE RAW
- **Sony VENICE 2** — 8.6K full frame, dual base ISO (800/3200), excellent skin tones
- **Blackmagic URSA Mini Pro** — budget productions, BRAW format, good DR for price
- **Film** — KODAK Vision3 250D (daylight), 500T (tungsten/low light), 200T; processed at FotoKem/Kodak Lab

### Lighting
- **Key Light** — primary source, establishes mood direction
- **Fill Light** — reduces shadow density, fill ratio determines contrast
- **Back/Rim Light** — separates subject from background, adds depth
- **Practicals** — lights visible in frame, must match scene logic
- **Color Temperature** — daylight 5600K, tungsten 3200K, LED variable
- **HMI** — daylight balanced (5600K), high output, flickerless at 50/60Hz
- **ARRI Orbiter** — LED fresnel, full spectrum, variable CCT 2000-20000K
- **Aputure 600d** — popular LED, daylight, bowens mount, wireless control
- **Lighting Ratios** — 2:1 (flat/commercial), 4:1 (dramatic/cinematic), 8:1+ (noir/horror)
- **Exposure Triangle** — aperture (depth of field), shutter (motion blur — 180° rule = double frame rate), ISO (noise)

### Sound
- **Production Sound Mixer** — primary sound recordist, equipment selection, boom supervision
- **Boom Operator** — positions boom microphone above/below frame
- **Utility Sound** — cables, walkie management, playback
- **Timecode** — LTC or MTC, jam sync at start of day, critical for multi-cam + VFX
- **Wild Lines** — dialogue recorded without camera for ADR prevention
- **Room Tone** — 30-60 seconds ambient recording every new location
- **Microphones** — Schoeps MK41 (hypercardioid), Sennheiser MKH50, DPA lavs (4060/4061)
- **Recorders** — Sound Devices 888, Zoom F8n Pro, Aaton Cantar X3

### VFX on Set
- **VFX Supervisor** — on-set representative for post-production needs
- **Witness Cameras** — record environment reference for CGI integration
- **Lidar Scan** — 3D scan of locations for matchmove/environment recreation
- **HDR Lighting Reference** — 360° HDR captures for accurate CG lighting
- **Greenscreen/Bluescreen** — colour choice depends on subject; blue for daylight/blondes, green for most other situations; 4 stops over subject exposure
- **Previz** — pre-visualisation animatics for complex sequences
- **Techvis** — technical visualisation — camera positions, lens choices, timing

### Script Supervision
- **Continuity** — tracks all elements across scenes for editorial matching
- **Lined Script** — marks which camera covered which dialogue
- **Editor's Log** — circle takes, recommendations, coverage notes sent to editorial daily

## DEPARTMENTS

### Art Department
- **Production Designer** — visual concept of the film, oversees all visual elements
- **Art Director** — manages art department execution under PD
- **Set Decorator** — dresses sets with props and décor
- **Props Master** — all hand props, hero props
- **Construction Coordinator** — builds sets, manages carpenter crew
- **Greens** — plants, trees, outdoor natural elements

### Wardrobe/Costume
- **Costume Designer** — character wardrobe concept
- **Key Costumer** — on-set costume maintenance and continuity
- **Breakdown** — artificial aging/distressing of costumes

### Hair & Makeup
- **Department Head Makeup** — designs and applies character makeup
- **SFX Makeup** — prosthetics, wounds, ageing, creature effects
- **Department Head Hair** — wigs, styling, continuity

### Stunts
- **Stunt Coordinator** — designs and supervises all stunts
- **Stunt Performer** — doubles or performs specific stunts
- **Safety Bulletin** — AMPTP/SAG-AFTRA requirements for stunt documentation
- **High Work** — rigging safety requirements, load calculations

### Special Effects (On-Set Practical)
- **SFX Supervisor** — practical physical effects: rain, wind, explosions, fire
- **Pyrotechnics** — licensed explosives use, safety permits
- **Atmospheric** — fog machines, dust, snow effects

## PRODUCTION SAFETY

### Safety Requirements
- **Safety Hotline** — anonymous reporting, required on union productions
- **Safety Meeting** — required before any stunt, pyrotechnic, or hazardous work
- **Covid Protocols** (when applicable) — zone system, testing cadence, on-set medic
- **Medical** — set medic required for stunts; hospital pre-clearance for remote locations
- **OSHA** — US federal requirements; heat illness prevention, noise exposure limits

### Union Requirements (US)
- **SAG-AFTRA** — performer contracts; daily/weekly rates, overtime, golden time
- **IATSE** — below-the-line crew; Local 600 (camera), 700 (editorial), 728 (lighting)
- **Teamsters** — Local 399 (transportation), drivers, picture cars
- **Turnaround** — minimum rest between wrap and next call; 12 hours standard, 10 for specific categories
