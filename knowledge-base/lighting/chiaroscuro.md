# Chiaroscuro

## Purpose
Chiaroscuro is the strong contrast between light and dark areas within a single image — used to create depth, drama, and psychological complexity.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Chiaroscuro: extreme contrast between bright and dark areas within the same frame
- Creates: depth, mystery, psychological complexity, moral ambiguity
- The subject is lit dramatically; the background recedes into darkness
- Used heavily in noir, thriller, psychological drama — any film about hidden truths

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate whether the film's genre and subject call for chiaroscuro
- Check that chiaroscuro is used purposefully
- Brief the DP: chiaroscuro requires precise light placement and hard sources

## Scene-level checks
- Does the chiaroscuro composition in this scene communicate meaning about who holds truth and who hides it?
- Is the contrast ratio in this scene creating the intended psychological complexity?

## Project-level checks
- Is chiaroscuro used consistently as part of the film's visual language?
- Does chiaroscuro deepen in scenes of moral ambiguity?

## Warning signs
- Chiaroscuro applied decoratively rather than narratively
- Chiaroscuro so extreme that subjects are unreadable
- Chiaroscuro that contradicts the practical lighting sources

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Establish chiaroscuro as a visual language principle if it's part of the film's DNA
- Reserve the most extreme chiaroscuro for the most dramatically complex moments

## Example assistant output
> Scene 27 is about a man deciding whether to betray his principles. Half-light him: the side of his face associated with his principles in light, the side associated with his temptation in shadow. As he makes his choice, light crosses his face — a subtle move as the key source shifts. His face becomes the visual language of his arc.
