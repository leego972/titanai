# Three-Point Lighting

## Purpose
Three-point lighting is the foundational lighting setup in film: key light (primary source), fill light (shadow control), and back light (separation from background).

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Key light: the primary, motivated light source — defines the subject's illumination direction
- Fill light: reduces shadow density from the key — controls contrast ratio
- Back light (rim light): separates subject from background — creates depth in the frame
- Three-point is a starting framework, not a formula — great cinematographers use it as a baseline to depart from
- The ratio between key and fill determines contrast: low ratio = flat, high ratio = dramatic

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate whether the scene's three-point setup is serving its dramatic function
- Check that the key light is motivated — does it match the practical source in frame?
- Adjust fill ratio to match the scene's intended contrast level

## Scene-level checks
- Is the three-point setup in this scene serving its emotional and narrative function?
- Is the key light motivated by a visible or implied practical source?

## Project-level checks
- Is three-point lighting used as a starting point that's adapted for each scene, not applied uniformly?
- Does the fill ratio vary to reflect the film's emotional range?

## Warning signs
- Three-point applied uniformly to every scene — all scenes look the same
- Fill light too strong — eliminates shadow and dramatic depth
- Back light so bright it's distracting

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Use three-point as a framework, then subtract fill for drama and tension
- Motivated key lights increase production value

## Example assistant output
> Your setup on scene 22 has fill at f/4 against a key at f/5.6 — barely a 1.5-stop ratio. The scene needs to feel threatening. Pull the fill to create a 3-stop ratio. Let half his face go dark. The scene is about what he's hiding — the lighting should confirm it before he speaks.
