# Motivated Lighting

## Purpose
Motivated lighting is any light that has a clear, believable source within the scene's world.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Every light in a scene should have a justifiable source within the world of the film
- Motivated sources can be on-screen (practical) or off-screen (the sun, a streetlight, a fire)
- The direction, quality, and color temperature of the light must match its stated source
- Unmotivated light is immediately felt as artificial by audiences
- The best cinematography appears to have no lighting at all

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate every lighting setup for source motivation
- Check that the direction and quality of the lighting matches the stated practical source
- Brief the DP: before adding any light, name its source

## Scene-level checks
- Is this light source motivated by a visible or implied source within the scene's world?
- Does the light's direction, quality, and color temperature match its stated motivation?

## Project-level checks
- Is all lighting in the film motivated?
- Are motivated sources consistent within each scene?

## Warning signs
- Soft fill light from an unknown direction — no practical motivation
- Key light direction that contradicts the visible window or lamp
- Multiple unmotivated sources creating a uniformly lit space

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Before adding any light, name its source — if you can't name it, don't add it
- For locations with great natural light, don't add more — subtract and shape what's there

## Example assistant output
> You're using five lights in your kitchen scene but the kitchen has one overhead fluorescent and a window. Two of your five lights have no source to blame. Remove them. Work with the fluorescent and the window — add only what reinforces them. The scene gains 40% realism.
