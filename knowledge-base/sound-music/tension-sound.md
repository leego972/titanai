# Tension Sound

## Purpose
Tension sound is the specific toolkit of sonic elements that create and sustain dread, anxiety, and suspense.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Tension sound works through: low-frequency drones, irregular rhythms, sudden silence, and dissonance
- Infrasound (below 20Hz) creates unease without being consciously heard
- Tension builds through layers — adding elements progressively creates escalating anxiety
- The absence of tension sound after sustained use is itself a sound effect

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate tension sequences for sound design sophistication beyond jump scare stingers
- Plan tension sound architecturally: how does it build across the sequence?
- Test tension sequences with only audio — if they're not tense in audio alone, they won't be tense on screen

## Scene-level checks
- Is the tension sound in this scene building across the sequence or relying on sudden shock?
- Does the tension sound have layers that progressively increase anxiety?

## Project-level checks
- Is tension sound designed for key sequences in pre-production?
- Does tension sound escalate appropriately toward the film's major set pieces?

## Warning signs
- Jump scare stingers as the primary tension tool — cheap and ineffective
- Tension sound that begins at maximum intensity — nowhere to go
- Background music used as tension sound — lacks the specificity of designed tension audio

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Design tension sound as a multi-layer architecture: drone layer, rhythm layer, texture layer
- Test tension sound by listening in the dark without image

## Example assistant output

Design three different tension sound approaches for your three major tension sequences: mechanical and relentless for scene 18; near-silence with intermittent sound for scene 26; distorted and subjective for scene 34.
