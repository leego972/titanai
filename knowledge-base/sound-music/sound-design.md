# Sound Design

## Purpose
Sound design is the creative and technical construction of the audio world of a film — every sound that isn't dialogue or music.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Sound design creates world, psychology, and emotion before the image registers
- The audience hears the film before they understand it — sound sets the emotional register
- Sound design operates at three levels: story level, atmosphere level, and subjective level
- What the audience doesn't hear is as important as what they do
- Sound perspective must match visual perspective

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Treat sound as a creative element equal to image in every scene evaluation
- Brief the sound designer before production — they need to understand the film's sonic world
- Plan production sound for quality — bad production audio limits post options severely

## Scene-level checks
- What sound design could make this scene more powerful or more true?
- Is the sound perspective in this scene matching the visual perspective?

## Project-level checks
- Is a sound design vision established before production?
- Is the sound designer part of the creative team, not just a post department?

## Warning signs
- Sound design treated as a post problem rather than a creative pre-production decision
- Production audio recorded poorly — limits what's possible in post

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Include the sound designer in pre-production creative meetings
- Create a sound design bible: what does this film sound like?

## Example assistant output
> Sound design note for scene 31: right now you're planning ambient interrogation room sound. That's illustration. What does the room sound like from inside Marcus's psychological state? The HVAC becomes a drone at exactly the frequency of anxiety. The distant voices become indistinct but threatening. Design it subjectively.
