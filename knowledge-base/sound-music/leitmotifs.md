# Leitmotifs

## Purpose
A leitmotif is a recurring musical theme associated with a specific character, relationship, location, or idea.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- A leitmotif is a short, distinctive musical phrase — not a whole cue — that returns throughout the film
- Associated with: a character, a relationship, a place, a theme, or an idea
- Each appearance gains resonance from all previous appearances
- Transformation of the leitmotif mirrors the story: the villain's theme can become the hero's at the climax

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Plan leitmotifs with the composer before any music is written
- Identify what each leitmotif is associated with and how it will transform across the film
- Check that leitmotifs are introduced early enough to accumulate meaning

## Scene-level checks
- Is this musical moment using an established leitmotif — and is it serving its association?
- Is a new leitmotif introduction timed correctly for maximum effect?

## Project-level checks
- Are leitmotifs planned for all major characters and relationships?
- Do leitmotifs transform appropriately as the story progresses?

## Warning signs
- Leitmotifs introduced so late they have no time to accumulate meaning
- Leitmotifs that don't transform — used identically throughout

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Develop the leitmotif plan with the composer in pre-production — before a single cue is written
- Create a leitmotif bible: what each theme is associated with and how it develops

## Example assistant output
> Your protagonist's leitmotif is a piano phrase in A minor. By Act 3, when she's finally free, it should resolve to A major — same notes, different mode. That harmonic transformation is her arc expressed in 8 seconds of music. Give the composer this direction now.
