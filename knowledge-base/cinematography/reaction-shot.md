# Reaction Shot

## Purpose
A reaction shot captures a character's response to another character's action or dialogue. Reaction shots are often the most important shots in a scene.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- The audience identifies with reactions, not actions — we watch the listener to know how to feel
- The most important line in a scene often isn't covered in close-up — the reaction to it should be
- Reaction timing in the edit is everything
- Reactions don't require the actor to 'do' anything — stillness is the most powerful reaction

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Plan reaction coverage for every significant dramatic moment
- Brief actors that reactions require no performance — just presence and truth
- Check that reaction shots cover the characters who are emotionally central

## Scene-level checks
- Is this reaction shot capturing the character who is most emotionally significant in this moment?
- Is the reaction's timing at the right dramatic moment?

## Project-level checks
- Are reaction shots planned for all key dramatic moments?
- Is reaction coverage prioritised appropriately in the shooting schedule?

## Warning signs
- Missing reaction shot on the key emotional moment
- Reactions that show the actor performing surprise rather than experiencing it
- Cutting to reaction too early or too late

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Plan reaction coverage in the shot list — don't leave it as an afterthought
- In editing, cut to the reaction at the moment of impact, not after
