# Coverage Planning

## Purpose
Coverage planning is the pre-production process of determining which shots are needed to edit a scene.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Coverage is not about shooting everything — it's about capturing the minimum needed to edit the scene
- Over-coverage creates false security and wastes production time
- Under-coverage creates editing emergencies — essential shots missing
- Every scene needs: establishing context, character relationship, emotional close-ups, and key inserts
- The director must know which shot is the KEY shot for each scene

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Review coverage plan for every scene before production days
- Identify the KEY shot — the shot the scene depends on — and protect it above all others
- Flag under-coverage before shooting — missing a close-up on the key moment is unrecoverable

## Scene-level checks
- Is the coverage plan for this scene complete?
- Is there a clearly identified KEY shot for this scene?

## Project-level checks
- Is coverage planning done in pre-production for every scene?
- Are there scenes with critical coverage gaps?

## Warning signs
- Coverage planned on set without pre-production thinking — results in random coverage
- Missing close-ups on key emotional moments
- No KEY shot identified — everything is equally unimportant

## Strong recommendation patterns
- For every scene, write the coverage plan in pre-production with a KEY shot identified
- On set, shoot the KEY shot first — before anything goes wrong

## Example assistant output
> You have 12 camera setups planned for your 2-page dialogue scene. That's over-coverage. Identify the KEY shot — probably a close-up of Elena when she makes the decision — and protect it. Then: master, two mediums, two close-ups. That's five setups. You'll shoot faster, perform better, and edit cleaner.
