# Close-Up

## Purpose
The close-up is cinema's most intimate and powerful frame — a magnification of the human face that allows the audience access to interior life invisible at any other scale.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- A close-up reveals what the character cannot say — it's the visual equivalent of inner monologue
- The power of a close-up is inversely proportional to how often it's used — restraint amplifies impact
- Close-ups must be earned by the scene's emotional context
- The timing of the close-up cut is as important as the composition
- The best close-ups show the actor thinking, not performing

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Use close-ups sparingly and purposefully
- Plan the KEY close-up for each scene in pre-production
- Brief actors that close-ups require stillness — the camera amplifies micro-expressions
- In editing, the close-up is a commitment — cut to it only when the performance justifies it

## Scene-level checks
- Is this close-up earning its intimacy?
- Is the actor's performance in close-up truthful enough to withstand the magnification?

## Project-level checks
- Are close-ups used selectively enough to retain impact?
- Is the timing of close-up cuts precise?

## Warning signs
- Close-up as default — every line in close-up eliminates impact
- Close-up at the wrong moment — before the dramatic peak
- Cutting to close-up on a line rather than on the reaction

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Identify in pre-production the five most important close-up moments in the film
- In editing, the close-up is earned — don't cut to it until the performance demands it
