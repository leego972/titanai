# Director's Vision

## Purpose
The director's vision is the unifying creative concept that governs every decision in a film — from casting to color grade.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Vision is not aesthetic preference — it's the specific way THIS director sees THIS story
- A vision statement should answer: what is this film about, how should it feel, and why is this director the right person to tell it
- Vision filters every decision: casting, location, music, pace, color — all tested against it
- Vision can evolve during production but must remain coherent
- A director who can't articulate their vision cannot lead a crew

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Ask the director: what is your single sentence that captures your vision for this film?
- Check every major creative decision against the stated vision
- Flag creative choices that contradict or dilute the vision

## Scene-level checks
- Is this scene's approach consistent with the director's stated vision?
- Does this creative choice serve the vision or contradict it?

## Project-level checks
- Is the director's vision clearly articulated before production begins?
- Are all department heads working from the same vision?
- Is the vision visible in the finished film?

## Warning signs
- Vision that changes fundamentally during production — crew loses creative north
- Vision so vague it's meaningless
- Vision that exists in the director's head but not in any creative document

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Help the director write a vision statement before pre-production begins
- Use the vision statement as a filter in every creative meeting

## Example assistant output
> Your vision statement says 'I want this to feel real and raw.' That's a starting point, not a vision. Real and raw like Cassavetes? Like the Dardennes? Pick a specific emotional register: 'I want the audience to feel like they're watching a family argument through the wall — close, uncomfortable, unable to look away.' Now your DP, editor, and actors all know where to go.
