# Production Risk

## Purpose
Production risk is the identification and management of everything that could go wrong — weather, equipment failure, location access, cast illness, schedule overruns, and budget exhaustion.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Every production has risks — identifying them in advance reduces their impact
- Risk categories: weather, cast, location, equipment, schedule, budget
- A risk register identifies risks, their probability, their impact, and the mitigation strategy
- Cover sets (interior alternatives for weather-dependent exterior scenes) are a standard risk mitigation tool

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Create a risk register before production begins — identify all known risks
- Check that high-probability risks have mitigation strategies
- Brief the 1st AD and producer on the risk register

## Scene-level checks
- What risks does this scene carry, and what's the mitigation strategy?
- Is there a contingency plan if this scene can't be executed as planned?

## Project-level checks
- Is a production risk register created and maintained throughout production?
- Do high-probability risks have documented mitigation strategies?
- Is there a cover set plan for weather-dependent exterior scenes?

## Warning signs
- Production risks identified too late — the problem becomes a crisis
- No mitigation strategies — the production has to improvise
- Weather-dependent exterior scenes without a cover set plan

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Create the risk register at the start of pre-production
- For every weather-dependent exterior scene, identify a cover set option

## Example assistant output
> Your exterior night shoot for scene 38 has no weather cover. If it rains, you lose the night. Identify a cover scene now — something that can be shot in the same location under cover, or an interior scene that can be pulled forward. Brief the 1st AD before you arrive on location at 6pm.
