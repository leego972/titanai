# Eyeline Continuity

## Purpose
Eyeline continuity ensures that characters appear to be looking at each other consistently across all coverage angles in a scene.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Eyeline continuity: when we cut between two characters, each should appear to be looking at the other
- The '180-degree rule' governs eyeline continuity
- Eyeline height must match between setups
- Breaking eyeline continuity is immediately felt by audiences even when they can't name the error

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Check eyeline consistency across all coverage setups for dialogue scenes
- Mark eyeline positions before coverage begins
- Review eyeline matches in dailies

## Scene-level checks
- Is this actor's eyeline consistent with the previous setup?
- Is the eyeline height appropriate for this character's height?

## Project-level checks
- Is eyeline continuity planned and monitored for all dialogue scenes?
- Are eyeline marks established before coverage begins?

## Warning signs
- Eyeline mismatches discovered in editing — no options remain
- No eyeline marks — actors improvising their look direction

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Mark eyeline positions with tape before each coverage setup begins
- For scenes with complex eyeline requirements (three or more characters), map eyeline positions before shooting

## Example assistant output
> Your three-person scene has a complex eyeline problem. Marcus looks at Elena in his coverage, but Elena looks slightly past Marcus's position in her coverage. When you cut from Marcus to Elena, she'll appear to be looking past him. Before you shoot Marcus's coverage, mark Elena's eyeline position precisely.
