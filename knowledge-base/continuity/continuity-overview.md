# Continuity Overview

## Purpose
Continuity is the consistency of all story and production elements across the film — ensuring that the world, characters, and events are internally coherent from scene to scene and shot to shot.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Continuity errors break audience immersion — they pull viewers out of the story
- Continuity operates at multiple levels: story, character, visual, and technical
- The script supervisor is the primary on-set continuity guardian
- Continuity errors are cheapest to prevent and most expensive to fix in post

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate films for all four levels of continuity: story, character, visual, and technical
- Brief the script supervisor on the director's continuity priorities
- Create a continuity risk register

## Scene-level checks
- Is this scene consistent with all previously established facts about this character, world, and timeline?
- Are all visual continuity elements tracked in this scene?

## Project-level checks
- Is a continuity system in place covering all four continuity levels?
- Is the script supervisor empowered to flag and address continuity concerns?

## Warning signs
- No continuity system — errors accumulate undiscovered
- Script supervisor not empowered to stop the shoot for continuity flags
- Continuity errors discovered in post when they can only be fixed with VFX

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Create a continuity system before production begins: people, processes, and tools
- Empower the script supervisor — their continuity call is not optional

## Example assistant output
> You have three shooting days on your main apartment set. Create a continuity Bible for the apartment before Day 1: photograph every surface, every prop, note every character's wardrobe state for every scene. Update it at the end of each day.
