# Technical Continuity

## Purpose
Technical continuity is the consistency of camera-related elements across setups — the 180-degree rule, screen direction, screen geography, and shot-to-shot spatial relationships.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- The 180-degree rule: establish an imaginary line between characters and always shoot from the same side
- Crossing the line reverses character screen positions — creates audience disorientation
- Screen direction: characters should travel in consistent screen directions between cuts
- Technical continuity errors in action sequences create spatial confusion

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Ensure the 180-degree rule is understood and applied by the DP
- For complex scenes, map the 180-degree axis before shooting
- Check screen direction for all moving subjects

## Scene-level checks
- Does this setup maintain the established 180-degree axis for this scene?
- Is screen direction consistent with the established geography?

## Project-level checks
- Is the 180-degree rule understood and applied throughout the production?
- Are complex scenes' axes mapped before shooting begins?

## Warning signs
- Axis violations discovered in editing — spatially disorienting cuts with no fix
- Screen direction inconsistencies in chase or action sequences

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Map the 180-degree axis for every scene with multiple characters before shooting begins
- For action sequences with complex geography, create a geography diagram

## Example assistant output
> Your chase sequence has a screen direction problem. In the establishing shots, Marcus runs left-to-right. In your chase coverage, the camera position changes and he briefly runs right-to-left in three shots. Either reframe those three shots so he runs consistently left-to-right, or insert a directional-change indicator.
