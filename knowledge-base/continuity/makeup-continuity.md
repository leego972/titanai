# Makeup Continuity

## Purpose
Makeup continuity ensures that each actor's makeup is consistent across all setups within a scene and between scenes that are continuous in story time.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Makeup continuity applies to: injury effects, aging effects, standard beauty makeup, and special effects makeup
- Injury makeup must progress realistically through the story timeline — wounds heal and evolve
- Special effects makeup has extremely complex continuity requirements
- Makeup continuity errors are among the most expensive to fix — they often require reshoots

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Create a makeup continuity plan for all injury and special effects makeup
- Photograph makeup state at the start of each scene and at story-time transitions
- Brief the makeup department on story timeline implications

## Scene-level checks
- Is this actor's makeup state consistent with their story timeline?
- Is makeup continuity documented and photographed for this scene?

## Project-level checks
- Is a makeup continuity plan in place for all injury and special effects makeup?
- Are story timeline implications of time passage reflected in makeup?

## Warning signs
- Injury makeup that doesn't evolve across the story timeline
- No makeup continuity photographs — matching by memory only

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Create a makeup continuity brief: what each character's makeup looks like at each point in the story timeline
- Photograph all injury and special effects makeup from multiple angles before the first take

## Example assistant output
> Marcus's black eye appears in scene 22 for the first time. Create a black eye continuity chart: Day of hit (scene 22): fresh, red, slightly swollen. 12 hours later (scenes 23-24): darker, more purple. 24 hours (scenes 25-26): blue-purple, swelling reduces. Brief your makeup artist on the chart.
