# Audio Continuity

## Purpose
Audio continuity ensures consistency of all sound elements — dialogue quality, background ambience, and effects — across all setups within a scene.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Audio continuity errors most often arise from inconsistent microphone placement between setups
- Room tone must match across all setups — if the background ambience changes, the cut is audible
- External noise contamination creates audio continuity problems
- ADR creates audio discontinuity risk if not carefully matched

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Monitor audio continuity throughout production
- Room tone must be recorded for every setup
- Flag any take with external noise contamination before accepting it

## Scene-level checks
- Is the audio quality in this setup consistent with other setups for this scene?
- Are there any background noise issues in this take that could create continuity problems?

## Project-level checks
- Is audio continuity monitored systematically throughout production?
- Is room tone recorded for every setup?

## Warning signs
- Audio discontinuity between setups — different room sound
- External noise contamination on takes that are then used
- Room tone not recorded

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Brief the production sound mixer: audio continuity is as critical as visual continuity
- Review audio in dailies specifically for continuity between setups — listen with headphones

## Example assistant output
> Your scene 18 has an audible ambient difference between the two shooting halves because the air conditioning was turned on during the lunch break. Your sound editor needs to know about this now — they can use room tone and processing to mitigate it, but only if they're warned. Alert them today.
