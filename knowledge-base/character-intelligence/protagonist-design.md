# Protagonist Design

## Purpose
The protagonist is the character through whose eyes the audience experiences the story — the one whose choices drive the narrative and whose journey constitutes the film's arc. Protagonist design is the most consequential character decision in filmmaking.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- The protagonist is the character the audience identifies with — their success or failure determines the audience's emotional experience
- A protagonist must be active — they drive the story through their choices
- A protagonist must have a clear want (goal) and a clear need (what they actually need)
- The audience must understand the protagonist's situation quickly — within the first 10 minutes
- Audience identification doesn't require likability — it requires understanding, empathy, and investment

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate whether the protagonist is clearly established within the first 10 minutes
- Check that the protagonist is active — are they making choices that drive the story?
- Distinguish between the protagonist's want and their need — both must be present
- The protagonist should be the character most changed by the story's events

## Scene-level checks
- Is the protagonist clearly established — do we know who they are and what they want?
- Is the protagonist active — driving the story through their choices?

## Project-level checks
- Is the protagonist established within the first 10 minutes?
- Is the protagonist active throughout — or do they become reactive?
- Are the protagonist's want and need clearly distinct?

## Warning signs
- Passive protagonists who react to events rather than drive them
- Protagonists established too slowly — audience hasn't connected before Act 1 ends
- Want and need conflated — the protagonist knows exactly what they need from the start

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Establish the protagonist's situation, want, and core characteristic within the first 10 minutes
- If the protagonist is becoming reactive in Act 2, add decision points that force them to choose

## Example assistant output
> Your protagonist Elena is reactive for the first 45 minutes of your film. Things happen to her. She responds. That's not a protagonist — that's a supporting character in someone else's story. Find three moments in Act 1 and Act 2 where Elena makes a choice that drives the story forward. Her decisions must have consequences. If removing her from the scene doesn't change what happens, she's not the protagonist.
