# Character Arc

## Purpose
A character arc is the transformation a character undergoes across the film — the internal change that occurs as a result of the story's events. The 1B Brain must evaluate character arcs for authenticity, motivation, and structural integration.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- A character arc is the journey from one internal state to another — what the character believes at the start vs the end
- Three types: positive arc (character overcomes flaw), negative arc (character succumbs to flaw), flat arc (character changes the world around them)
- The arc must be caused by the story's events — not arbitrary
- The arc must be visible in the character's final action — the climax should dramatise the arc's completion
- A character arc without an external story event causing it is just mood change, not character development

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Identify which type of arc each major character has before advising
- Check that the arc is caused by specific story events — trace the cause
- Evaluate whether the arc is complete — does the character arrive somewhere different from where they started?
- Test the arc: in the climax, does the character make a choice that would have been impossible at the start?

## Scene-level checks
- Has this character changed as a result of this scene — or is the scene not contributing to the arc?
- Is this arc development motivated by a specific story event?

## Project-level checks
- Is the arc clearly visible in the protagonist's final action?
- Are all major characters' arcs traceable to specific story events?
- Is the arc change significant enough to justify the film's runtime?

## Warning signs
- Arcs that happen without cause — character changes because the plot needs them to
- Arcs that are stated but not dramatised — character says they've changed but nothing demonstrates it
- Arcs so subtle they're invisible — the director knows about them, the audience doesn't

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Map each character's arc from start to finish before production begins
- Test the arc by stating start and end beliefs — the gap between them is the arc

## Example assistant output
> Elena's arc — from closed-off to open — is intellectually clear but emotionally invisible. We don't feel it because she never makes a choice that costs her openness. Find the moment where openness is genuinely risky for her and she chooses it anyway. That moment is the arc's completion, and without it, the arc isn't there.
