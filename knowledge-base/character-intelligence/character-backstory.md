# Character Backstory

## Purpose
Backstory is what happened to a character before the story begins — the experiences that formed their personality, worldview, and wound. The 1B Brain must help directors use backstory as an active story ingredient, not as exposition to be delivered.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Backstory is the 'why' behind who a character is at the story's start
- The best backstories contain a specific wound — a formative event that created the character's flaw or belief
- Backstory should be revealed through action and behaviour, not exposition
- Only backstory that's relevant to the present story needs to be shared with the audience
- Characters should know less about their own backstory than the director does — they live it, they don't narrate it

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate how backstory is being communicated — action vs exposition
- Check that backstory shared with the audience is earning its screen time
- Backstory that's delivered as a monologue is a structural warning sign
- The character's wound should be visible in their present behaviour before it's explained

## Scene-level checks
- Is this backstory revelation earning its screen time — is it necessary for the audience to understand?
- Is the backstory being communicated through action and behaviour rather than exposition?

## Project-level checks
- Is each character's backstory relevant to the present story?
- Is backstory visible in present character behaviour before it's explained?
- Is backstory delivered through action rather than exposition?

## Warning signs
- Backstory delivered as a monologue — character explains their own psychology
- Backstory that has no relevance to the present story
- Present behaviour that contradicts established backstory

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Design backstory to be visible in behaviour before it's explained — the audience should wonder why before they're told
- Brief actors on the full backstory — even elements the audience never learns

## Example assistant output
> Marcus's childhood trauma is explained in scene 14 through a 3-minute speech. That information needs to be visible in his behaviour in scenes 1-13 before it's articulated. If we've already sensed the wound from watching him, the explanation in scene 14 will resonate. If scenes 1-13 give us no sense of it, the explanation will feel like an insert.
