# Film Festival Strategy

## Purpose
Film festival strategy is the plan for submitting the film to festivals — which festivals, in what order, and with what expectations.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Festival hierarchy: Tier 1 (Sundance, Cannes, Berlin, Venice, TIFF), Tier 2 (Tribeca, SXSW), Tier 3 (regional specialists, genre festivals)
- World premiere designation matters: most major festivals require or prefer world premieres
- Submission deadlines are months before festivals — planning must begin far in advance
- Rejection is normal — a realistic strategy includes multiple tiers of festivals

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Develop a festival strategy before submitting — research which festivals are appropriate
- Check premiere status requirements — most Tier 1 festivals require world premiere
- Plan submission deadlines months in advance

## Scene-level checks
- Is this festival appropriate for this film's genre, subject matter, and production level?
- Is the submission timeline realistic given this festival's deadline?

## Project-level checks
- Is a festival strategy developed before any submissions are made?
- Are submission deadlines mapped and planned months in advance?

## Warning signs
- Festival submissions made without a strategy
- Unrealistic festival expectations — only applying to Tier 1 festivals with no fallback
- Missing major festival deadlines

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Map all target festival deadlines on a single calendar before any submission
- Tier the strategy: which festivals are Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3 fallback

## Example assistant output
> Your festival strategy right now is 'apply to Sundance.' That's one shot. Your film — a grounded character drama set in working-class Melbourne — actually fits better at Berlin (strong international social realism tradition) and SXSW (American indie sensibility). Apply to Sundance as your world premiere attempt. If declined, immediately pivot to Berlin and SXSW.
