# Audience Promise

## Purpose
The audience promise is the implicit contract established in the first 10 minutes of a film — the emotional experience, tone, genre, and world rules that the audience agrees to engage with.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- The audience promise is set by tone, genre, opening image, and inciting incident
- It tells the audience: here's the world, here's the character, here's what kind of story this is
- Breaking the promise must be intentional and the payoff must exceed the cost of the broken expectation
- The opening 10 minutes are the most important in the film for promise-setting

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Identify the audience promise from the first 10 minutes of the film
- Check whether the rest of the film delivers what the opening promises
- A tonal shift mid-film is a promise break — assess whether it's earned

## Scene-level checks
- Does this scene match the tone and register established in the opening?
- Is this scene delivering on the audience promise or drifting from it?

## Project-level checks
- Does the opening 10 minutes establish a clear audience promise?
- Is the promise delivered at the climax?
- Are any promise breaks intentional and justified?

## Warning signs
- Films that open as comedies and turn into tragedies without tonal preparation
- Opening sequences that don't reflect the actual film
- Genre-switching that alienates the audience

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Review the first 10 minutes specifically for promise-setting
- If a promise break is intentional, ensure it's set up within the promise itself

## Example assistant output
> Your opening 12 minutes promise a contained domestic thriller. By minute 45 you're in international conspiracy territory. That's a promise break. Either seed the larger conspiracy earlier so the expansion feels planted, or restructure to keep the domestic frame.
