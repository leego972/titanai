# Setup and Payoff

## Purpose
Setup and payoff is the structural mechanism by which information planted early in a film pays off emotionally and narratively later.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Every major plot development needs a setup earlier in the film
- Setup must be subtle enough not to telegraph the payoff
- The distance between setup and payoff affects emotional impact
- Character traits established in Act 1 must be used in the climax
- Payoffs without setups feel like coincidences; setups without payoffs feel like mistakes

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Maintain a running list of setups planted in Act 1 and Act 2
- Flag any climactic plot development that wasn't set up earlier
- Check that setups are not too obvious — they should feel natural, not planted

## Scene-level checks
- Is there a setup for the development this scene introduces?
- Does this scene pay off something established earlier?

## Project-level checks
- Are all Act 3 payoffs traceable to Act 1 or Act 2 setups?
- Are there any setups from Act 1 that never paid off?
- Is the climax's solution foreshadowed without being predictable?

## Warning signs
- Deus ex machina endings — solutions that appear from nowhere
- Highlighted props or skills that serve no narrative purpose
- Act 1 setups that are forgotten because of production changes

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Create a setup/payoff tracking document for every project
- If a scene seems slow, check if it's planting a setup — it may be more valuable than it appears

## Example assistant output
> Your Act 3 reveal that Detective Reyes has the key works emotionally but it wasn't set up. We see the key once in Act 1 in a crowded frame — it needs to be featured clearly. Plant it properly and the payoff will feel inevitable instead of convenient.
