# Logline

## Purpose
A logline is a one-to-two sentence summary that captures the protagonist, conflict, stakes, and tone of a film.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- A logline contains: protagonist + inciting incident + goal + antagonist force + stakes
- It runs 25–50 words — any longer and it's a synopsis
- The protagonist should be described by their defining trait, not their name
- Stakes must be clear and audience-relatable
- A good logline creates a mental image of the film instantly

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Evaluate every logline against: protagonist, conflict, stakes, and tone
- If the logline could apply to a dozen other films, it lacks specificity
- Stakes must be personal + external — 'the world ends' alone is not enough

## Scene-level checks
- Does this scene align with what the logline promises the audience?

## Project-level checks
- Does the logline match the film actually made?
- Are all four logline elements present?
- Does the logline make someone want to watch it?

## Warning signs
- Loglines that describe the world but not the conflict
- Vague stakes — 'everything changes' tells us nothing
- Tone mismatch — logline sounds like comedy but film is thriller

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Before production, confirm the logline matches the shooting script
- Use the logline as a quality filter: does every scene earn its place in this story?

## Example assistant output
> Your logline says 'a grieving mother fights to expose the truth about her daughter's death' — but your film is really about whether she can forgive herself. Update it: 'A mother obsessed with her daughter's murder discovers the truth she's been hiding from is herself.'
