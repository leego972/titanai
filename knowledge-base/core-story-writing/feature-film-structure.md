# Feature Film Structure

## Purpose
Feature film structure is the time-tested framework that organises audience experience across 90–130 minutes. The 1B Brain must know standard structures cold, recognise when a film is breaking them intentionally, and advise directors on structural health at every stage.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Three-act structure: Setup (25%), Confrontation (50%), Resolution (25%)
- Inciting incident lands at ~10–12% of total runtime
- First act break / lock-in at ~25% — protagonist fully committed
- Midpoint at 50% — false victory or false defeat that resets stakes
- Second act break at 75% — darkest moment or major reversal
- Climax resolves the central dramatic question
- Denouement is brief — 2–5 minutes maximum

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Map every scene to its structural position before commenting on pacing
- If Act 1 exceeds 30 minutes in a 100-min film, the setup is bloated
- Midpoints must shift something — not just reveal information
- The darkest moment must feel earned by prior escalation

## Scene-level checks
- Which structural beat does this scene serve?
- Is the inciting incident clear and located at the right time?

## Project-level checks
- Plot the runtime of each act — are proportions roughly 25/50/25?
- Is the inciting incident too late?
- Does the midpoint actually change the story's trajectory?

## Warning signs
- Inciting incident buried past 15 minutes — audience disengages
- Act 2 with no midpoint shift — feels like one long flat stretch
- Climax weaker than earlier set pieces

## Strong recommendation patterns
- When a film feels long, check structural proportions before cutting scenes
- Suggest a beat sheet review before the director commits to the shooting schedule

## Example assistant output
> Your climax at 1:18 feels underwhelming because your darkest moment at 1:05 isn't actually your darkest moment — your protagonist hasn't lost enough. Push the stakes higher at the 75% mark and the climax will feel inevitable.
