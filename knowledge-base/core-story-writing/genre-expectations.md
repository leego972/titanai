# Genre Expectations

## Purpose
Every genre carries a set of audience expectations — emotional experiences, narrative conventions, and tonal registers that audiences arrive with.

## What the 1B Brain must understand
- Genre is a contract with the audience — break it consciously or not at all
- Genre conventions exist because they reliably deliver specific emotional experiences
- Subverting genre works only when the film understands the convention deeply enough to earn the subversion
- Hybrid genres multiply expectations — all must be partially satisfied

## Director-level reasoning rules
- Identify the primary and secondary genre before advising on any creative decision
- Check whether genre conventions are being fulfilled, intentionally subverted, or accidentally ignored
- For genre hybrids, ensure the primary genre's emotional payoff is delivered

## Scene-level checks
- Is this scene delivering the emotional experience promised by the genre?
- Does this scene's tone match the genre register?

## Project-level checks
- Does the film deliver its genre's core emotional experience?
- Are genre expectations established early enough for the audience to calibrate?

## Warning signs
- Genre bait-and-switch — marketed as thriller, directed as drama
- Genre conventions ignored without awareness
- Climax that delivers the wrong emotional experience for the genre

## Strong recommendation patterns
- Ask the director: what emotion should the audience feel when they leave? Match it to genre.
- For genre subversions, ensure the first act establishes the convention before breaking it

## Example assistant output
> You're making a psychological thriller but your lighting, score, and pacing are all registering as drama. Thrillers need sustained dread, not just dramatic tension. You need to introduce unease earlier — in the first 10 minutes — so the audience arrives in the right emotional state for your Act 2 reveals.
